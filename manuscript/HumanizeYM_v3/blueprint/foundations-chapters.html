Foundations: independent writing instructions

These two chapters occupy 34 pages (18 + 16), including examples, but excluding bibliography. They implement Scott Outline I.1 and the elementary part of II.1. Read notation.md first. All source paths below are relative to the workspace root. A label is the authoritative locator; line numbers describe the supplied snapshot. Do not copy the sources' order of definition, which introduces terminal normalization before its construction.

Chapter 1. Lattice gauge theory and the continuum problem (18 pages)

Purpose and prerequisites

Assume basic compact Lie groups, Haar measure, distributions and probability. Introduce the actual finite model and what its continuum limit would mean. Give an informal description of the claimed construction, explicitly as the target of the later chapters. Reserve the full existence theorem until its objects and estimates have been built. Do not front-load a catalogue of Wightman, phase, OPE and RG definitions.

Sections

  1. Gauge normalization (3 pages). Fix a faithful realization of a closed group G ⊂ U(n_G) with simple nonabelian real Lie algebra; connectedness is not required. Use the inner product −Re tr(XY)/n_G, define d_g, C_g, b_0, κ_G=2b_0. State the standard invariant-form fact with a reference rather than a proof of Lie theory. Work out SU(n): C_g=2n², κ_G=11n²/(12π²). Explain that β=2n/g² in conventional SU(n) Wilson normalization; do not silently identify β with g⁻².

  2. Links, plaquettes and the Wilson law (4 pages). Define positive and reversed links, ordered path holonomy, site gauge action, plaquette orientation and probability Haar product. Define w(U)=1−Re tr(U)/n_G, χ(U)=||U−1||_F/√n_G, χ²=2w, E_s^W, Z_{s,β}, μ_{s,β}. Compactness gives existence for every real β, while the positivity statements require β⩾0. Give the one-line endpoint transformation of a path and its consequence for closed loops; do not expand four cancelling factors. Cite Wilson for lattice gauge formulation and gauge invariance. Use exact ODE parallel transport for the smooth-connection example, then give the plaquette expansion and the curvature-square normalization. Gauge-variant size of links is not a criterion for a small field.

  3. Geometry, scales and locality (3 pages). Λ_s=(Z/sZ)^4, centered representatives in (−s/2,s/2], physical placement, periodic sup distance. Introduce odd ratios L_m, D_0=1, D_{m+1}=L_mD_m, a_m=D_m⁻¹; increasing m is finer resolution, a block integration goes m+1→m. Physical periods R_p are increasing integer multiples of four and the site count is R_pD_m. Define finite link support and a fixed finite stencil; its physical diameter shrinks with a_N. State symmetry of centered odd blocks. Defer halo/cover-number estimates until polymer norms actually use them.

  4. Reflection positivity and chessboard estimates (4 pages). Define both site and link reflections on an even torus, including reversal of links crossing the axis. Set (θF)(U)=overline{F(ΘU)}. State the finite-volume inequality for bounded gauge-invariant F supported on links whose endpoints lie strictly on the permitted side; identify positivity at seams. Cite Osterwalder–Seiler and the chessboard theorem of Fröhlich–Israel–Lieb–Simon (with Seiler's gauge-theory application). Define a chessboard seminorm only for an actual compatible tiling: root of the expectation of all reflected copies, with absolute/conjugate convention spelled out. State the product bound in that notation. Check cut parity, tiling divisibility and support interior rather than hiding them in 'by reflection positivity'. No transfer-operator proof or repeated Cauchy–Schwarz proof is needed if the cited theorem covers the stated conventions. Do not claim arbitrary functions of seam links satisfy the same statement.

  5. Correlations and ordered limits (4 pages). A tentative label set suffices here; define a local insertion, n-point moment and smeared Riemann sum with a_N^{4n}. Define Conf_n, separated joint tests, physical period admissibility. State the exact quantifier order: fix G and b, then one family for all N,p; for each prescribed increasing volume-index sequence p there is one refinement q serving all arities, labels and separated tests, with first N→∞ at fixed volume along the full mesh sequence and then k→∞ along P=p∘q. Explain why a subsequence in N would be weaker and why a simultaneous diagonal is not the stated conclusion. Limit objects are separated correlations; no measure on continuum connections is asserted. Mention later phases, stress tensor and finite-order OPE in words only. State explicitly that a mass gap, uniqueness of the volume limit and independence of b are not part of the target.

Chapter 2. Local gauge-invariant observables (16 pages)

Purpose and prerequisites

Construct classical polynomial labels and finite-stencil lattice representatives before discussing renormalization. Inputs: only Chapter 1, finite-dimensional inverse function theorem, analytic ODE dependence, compact-group averaging. A field label is a finite polynomial specification, not a smooth sample of the eventual random field.

Sections

  1. Radial jets and three gradings (4 pages). Define radial/Fock–Schwinger gauge and formal jet A_μ(x)=Σ_{k≥0}A_{k,μ}(x), A_k homogeneous of polynomial degree k+1 with x·A_k=0. Engineering weight of its coordinate is k+2; its factor count is one. Define V_d=C[J]^{Ad(G)}_{weight≤d} and a nested real bihomogeneous basis b_{d,i}, including 1. Define weight w_i, factor count f_i, and count operator N_fac. The tag d specifies a truncation space and later physical normalization; it is neither necessarily w_i nor a scaling dimension. Examples: unit, curvature-square c_{F²}=¼Σ_{μν}<F_{μν},F_{μν}>, weight four and factor count two. Do not call the fields free because polynomial labels are expressed through classical jets.

  2. Taylor normalization on the lattice (3 pages). Define test holonomy by u′=u h A_μ(h(y+te_μ)), u(0)=1 and jet scaling sc_h J=(h^{k+2}A_k). Define Taylor map T_d by retaining weight⩽d. A local section P_d^loc:V_d→{finite-link polynomials} is a right inverse, preserves unit and conjugation and intertwines signed coordinate permutations. Distinguish O(4) acting on abstract jets from the finite signed-permutation group acting on lattice representatives; arbitrary lattice rotations are not defined.

  3. Construction and bounds for local sections (6 pages). Present one proof, using finitely many transported plaquette curvatures. Explain why their linearizations separate finite radial jets (polynomial circulation and radial identity); select a finite set. Apply an equivariant linear left inverse and the inverse function theorem, truncate the inverse, substitute invariant labels, average over lattice symmetries. State that every monomial in the representative of b_i has at least f_i curvature factors; global values are bounded because G is compact. For ordered left derivatives in directions bounded by t times a polynomial spatial weight, prove a uniform C_{d,q,p}t^{f_i} bound on small-field configurations. Define this specialized derivative norm locally, or import only the elementary definition agreed with the RG chapter. Constants are independent of torus side, placement and threshold; allow stencils wrapping around a small torus. The specific section construction is detailed in both supplied manuscripts; no original published theorem for exactly this construction has yet been identified. Do not replace this by a citation to the inverse function theorem alone: only that analytic step is standard.

  4. What renormalization still has to do (3 pages). Explain mixing with lower-weight labels, with F² able to acquire an additive constant. Introduce raw insertions P_d^loc[c](y;U); reserve the name 'renormalized insertion' until the terminal map exists in the observable-transfer chapter. Give the clover example and its coefficient ¼ over the 24 corner plaquettes. Explain that a fixed physical Wilson loop has a stencil growing with the cutoff and lies outside the theorem. Describe the two later obligations in words: invertible finite-dimensional normalization at a reference scale, and volume-uniform estimates. Defer exact equations of motion and null-ideal identification to the comparison/observable chapters, because Outline II.1 explicitly marks their identification as tentative.

Audit points

Do not identify the free equation ideal I_d with the interacting null space N_d(S). Scott's auxiliary equation sections involve a triangular map R_d, and the later rank argument is needed. Do not infer j_{d,e}[c]=[c] from V_d⊂V_e: terminal normalizations differ. Preserve the factor-count filtration in the inverse-function construction; taking an arbitrary polynomial section would lose the small-field bound. Check all averaging pullbacks/inverses with the row-transport convention from Chapter 1. Refresh jet/section notation at §3, because it may have been unused for four pages.
