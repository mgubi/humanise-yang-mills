Closure of the classical input (third pass)

The conditional algebraic matching proposition remains useful early in the chapter, but its common cubic/quartic symbol assumptions are now established for the actual stationary action in ch07:classical-uniform. This uses the already derived ratio-uniform weighted completed inverse, not the fixed-L tree-chart gap.

  • Full tangent T includes coarse external field, smooth fine W_a, and bounded centered scalar r_a. Differentiated stationarity gives D²S[Tu,(0,z)]=0 for all internal z.

  • Cubic fine-action vertices have at least one finite difference and quartic vertices need none. Exponential path division provides common coefficient sums. Three undifferentiated legs plus one difference or four undifferentiated legs cost mu4; residue L4 cancels it. For B(u,v)=D³S[Tu,Tv,internal], the internal factor is in normalized L2, so it is a dual weighted bound, not an entrywise bound. Penalty mixed derivatives are supplied by the actual centered-frame induction.

  • Differentiate internal gauge identity D_zS[R a]=0 twice along stationary tangents. DS(0)=0 and tangent orthogonality kill the derivatives of R, proving B R=0.

  • If C=H+F*F and w=C^-1 B, pairing with R a gives ⟨FR a,Fw⟩=0. Invertibility of FR yields Fw=0 and Hw=B. Hence the completed inverse represents the correct quotient inverse; a gauge change does not change the exchange pairing.

  • Exact stationary formulas E3=S3[T³] and E4=S4[T4]−sum_three_pairings B C^-1 B produce pre-L uniform bounds. Intermediate momentum is a sum of two external momenta, so a fixed strip reduction suffices. The finite-volume coefficient identity is exact, with one volume factor. This supplies the same-action cubic/quartic output construction without using the all-scale schedule or nonlinear integral theorem.

The trace-ideal paragraph has also been corrected to distinguish two separate mu² payments: m_mu X pays the frame derivative L², whereas Pf* M_mu Pf pays two mu^-1 weighted conversions. No term charges both costs simultaneously to one regulator factor.
