Real-parameter supplier checks

The finite-test argument uses one common dictionary and one fixed low-order coefficient projection. Local algebraic extensions to larger affine activity lines need not represent positive densities; their small logarithmic and forced factors are still controlled by polynomial interpolation and the convergent local expansion. Positivity is separately retained for the actual histories.

The coupling-band arithmetic is consistent: radius R=y^(1/256) produces relative coupling variation O(y^(-7/256)), smaller than delta=y^(-1/128). A lower reference coupling supplies 1<=a-6R|c|<=a+6R|c|<=1+delta. For either sign of the real energy q, choosing the node sign as stated makes the signed exponential dominate its coefficient norm; this does not replace the energy by its absolute value. The prefactor and projected Gaussian coordinate costs are local coordinate counts, paid by support reserve, not by the total volume.

The primitive exponent after the R^2 cost is -55/128, leaving the claimed -2/5 reserve. The later -3/8 and -1/4 bounds leave room for fixed derivative and projection factors. The uniform bounded cover number of the local projection support is explicitly retained; subpolynomial growth of that cover number alone would not suffice in an exponential weight. The 300-degree interpolation remainder has exponent -365/256, as stated. The order of choosing the small response allowance before the threshold is correct. The forced fusion estimate keeps one compulsory hard factor and its comparison with the output forced allowance is arithmetically consistent.

The bounded map from the moment vectors in c0 yields the actual power-series bound, rather than inferring analyticity from continuity. Its short-line and large-distance cases give the quoted Lipschitz constants with slack. The scalar correction is distinguished from the vacuum scalar throughout.

Coefficient paths and the free Gaussian limit

The coefficient readouts are fixed linear operations on common dictionaries. Cubic dependence is affine with quadratic data fixed; quartic dependence is affine after the cubic data are fixed; loop dependence is affine with the preceding data fixed. The extrapolation has norm at most33 inside the purchased radius64 domain and gives contraction1/16. Linear symmetry and moment constraints are preserved by that extrapolation.

Proposition ch05:horizon-limit supplies the actual nested quadratic rows and transports, including canonical positive roots with a strict direct-factor gap. It is stronger than a uniform finite-history bound and identifies cover coefficients at a strictly larger width. Thus the errors from preceding coefficient families tend to zero at every fixed level. Finite-depth truncation followed by increasing the truncation depth proves the sequential cubic/quartic/loop Cauchy statement. No quantitative rate or arbitrary new torus representative is substituted for this limit.

Common input, higher derivatives and zero arrays

Martin's compensated map is globally bijective near the base coarse field and holds the physical fine field fixed on the protected support, including fine masks and activity values. Consequently one more derivative of the smooth density, chart and Jacobian can supply equicontinuity of output derivatives without differentiating an incoming C^q activity q+1 times. This must be applied on the same protected read set and chart; the chapter states those restrictions and chooses the kernel/score order before stage constants. Outside the protected support the product vanishes on the required neighborhood. Direct path and finite projection terms are smooth independently.

At fixed scale and torus, the resulting finite-dimensional coarse domain admits a finite net in common protected charts. Uniform positive precision, converging stationary jets and polynomial Gaussian score domination justify convergence of the finite integrals and their derivative suprema. The fixed physical masks do not require differentiation or a measure-zero level-set claim. Infinite dictionaries are handled by reserved path/support tails, and by Cauchy value tails for input multiplicities; there is no assertion that finite arrays are dense in every derivative norm.

An auxiliary zero-terminal-array history begins with a positive bare path exponential. Each genuine step preserves positivity. Replacing its returned coupling by the next prescribed band coupling multiplies the represented density by a positive path exponential while leaving its activity gas unchanged. The same path, predictor, reference masks, stationary branch and local budgets remain available. This establishes the relevant positive scripts; it does not assert positivity for every element of the completed norm ball. Common-input continuity applies to these scripts with exactly the same returned arrays and scalar projection.

Coupled uniqueness checks

At fixed truncation depth, common-input cutoff continuity gives a Cauchy truncated history for prescribed convergent couplings. The activity contraction makes its difference from the full history exponentially small in depth. Diagonal compactness is needed only for scalar coupling coordinates, not for the activity norm balls. The prefix scalar identity is finite; the converging path corrections cancel. The cumulative allowance bound first makes the supremum discrepancy finite and then gives the strict inequalities D/32 and D/16, forcing D=0. The argument compares auxiliary scripts at the same finite cutoff before passing to limits, so it does not assume an undefined extension of the map to the completed space.
