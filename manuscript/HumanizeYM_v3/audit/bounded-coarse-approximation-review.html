Review of bounded coarse approximations

Source: Scott part2/E-bounded-real.tex; suppliers: Output Chapters 4 and 11. This records conditions for the new Chapter 13 supplier, not a review of a completed fragment.

  1. Conditional factorization requires full random-seed cones. The actual block kernel is a mixture over shared frame choices, not a product in coarse links conditional only on the fine field. Include the independent frame seeds, predictor seeds, and link-noise seeds, with every fine-link read and eligibility test, in each ancestor cone. Disjoint such cones give conditional independence given the terminal fine field; induction over the composed kernels proves the required product pullback identity. Merely disjoint coarse links do not suffice. A kernel patch has radius of order the convolution order times the next coarse spacing; sum these radii over the ancestor levels when proving the cell margin. The selected schedule must make this total radius tend to zero with the stopping mesh.

  2. Use the actual terminal charge in graph inversion. Scott bounds the terminal residual by 2(1+C_h) as if the trial had unit charge. The current general trial theorem gives 2(1+C_h)Q_h. Use the stronger last-step endpoint 

|e_j-J_h e_j| <= 2(1+C_h)Q_h L_(h-1)^(-1/8)

and purchase a finite pre-length stage floor making this at most 1/4. The guarded coarse polynomial has identity readout because its guard is identically one on the ordinary local chart. Its exact terminal representation must still retain the old selected large-field inventories. The normalized square-root allowance attachment estimate from the corrected equation calibration can make Q_h a stage-controlled amount; the larger unweighted reserve belongs separately in E_h. Establish that the guard has a strict plateau on the chart and that its full test stencil is used in the cone.

  1. Same history and bounds. Apply the terminal comparison to the effective density at h belonging to the actual history from N. Do not replace it by an independent Wilson terminal law. The matrix J_h and the recombination coefficients may depend on N,p. Their inverse and the finite product of reference-to-h column bounds must be uniform in N,p. Then the bounded polynomial at fixed h, the probability-kernel supremum contraction, and the recombination coefficient bound give a finite cutoff/volume-independent supremum. The root-error charge E_h must be bounded uniformly in N,p and grow subpower in a_h^{-1}; it need not be bounded uniformly in h.

  2. Replicate before marginalizing. A single-column root comparison is not by itself an all-copy signed-gas bound. Insert all signed marks before integrating, inject the coarse factors at h, and retain all joint modes transported from N to h. Disjoint replica cones confine principal connected components to one replica cell. Multicell modes require the stronger support exponent and the higher-mode induction of Chapter 11; their support reserve pays the increased residual weights. The replicated density identity and vacuum ratio can then give an error of the form C E_h product(column bounds) exp(-(tau_h-tau_m)/8). The subpower factor is dominated by this decay. This is the quantitative justification needed for the bounded approximation, beyond finite-dimensional graph inversion.

  3. Real words. Once the single-field centered-seminorm estimate is established, Scott's word argument is sound: take real parts, telescope products in distinct cells, bound by n delta (C+delta)^(n-1), and apply this to the four products in the two reflected-word differences. The large supremum bound never enters this replacement error. Ordered tails may depend on each fixed translated pair and their separation; a common cutoff threshold for all translation parameters is not asserted or needed. Set approximants to zero off their admissible tails to obtain the global supremum statement.

These requirements and the two concrete corrections (shared frame seeds; nonunit terminal charge) were sent to the parent before drafting the supplier.
