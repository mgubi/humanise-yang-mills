Draft files: chapters/01-lattice.tex, chapters/02-observables.tex. This audit concerns the supplied prose and limited literature checks; it does not certify the global construction or the Lean development. See foundations-literature.md for URLs and inspection status.

Chapter 1

Scope. Definitions of the finite model, symmetry, scale geometry, separated convergence; standard positivity inputs. No continuum-existence theorem is asserted. The coupling law is explicitly a later target. The quantifier order matches Scott thm:B and Martin ex-row-target, ex-volume-target.

Normalization checks. Gauge metric is −Re tr(XY)/n_G, plaquettes counted μ<ν once, inverse coupling multiplies that energy. SU(n) has C_g=2n² in this metric and β=2n/g². The factor a_N^{4n} in smearing is physical volume measure. Time last; axes 1…4. R_p is physical period and L_m is ratio; scales become finer with increasing m.

Standard results and their checks. Gauge invariance is given through the endpoint formula rather than a long proof and attributed to Wilson. The explicit Casimir identity is standard finite-dimensional Lie theory; no new proof included. The classical plaquette expansion is stated using exact ODE holonomy, not endpoint exponentials. Finite-volume RP is restricted to β⩾0, even torus, gauge-invariant bounded Borel functions, and links wholly in the specified positive sector, matching Martin prop:ex-lattice-finite-reflection. Both site/link reflection pullbacks include inversion. A restricted seam-free chessboard setting uses s=2qℓ, open cell interiors and nonnegative reflected copies |F|. No extension to arbitrary seam observables or arbitrary tilings is claimed.

Outstanding literature check. Exact original full-text coverage of these finite-torus positivity/chessboard conventions remains uninspected because publisher/author-copy fetches failed. Current references identify the standard source and the finite-torus manuscript implementation; before final acceptance verify the original statement or Seiler §2a, Theorems 2.1–2.2. This is an attribution/applicability check, not a detected counterexample. If a stated chessboard form requires an additional divisibility or tiling condition in the original, add it here before downstream use. The Cauchy–Schwarz chessboard proof has deliberately not been reproduced.

Dependency limitations. Scale schedules are defined, not claimed to satisfy later estimates. No common family existence, full rotation symmetry, mass gap, uniqueness, or coincident-point convergence is inferred. b is only described as the later block penalty; its kernel is not used.

Chapter 2

Local-section theorem. Sources: Scott part2/A-section.tex, lem:A1 / eq:A-average; Martin existence/local-sections.tex, finite sampler, equivariant recovery and eq:local-section-average. Inputs: fixed compact matrix G, finite d, radial jet space and invariant real bases. Output: finite period-independent stencil, linear polynomial section, correct weighted Taylor map, unit/reality/H₄ covariance, monomials of column i with at least f_i curvature factors. The argument was checked directly across both prose sources. No transfer theorem or continuum field-null statement enters.

Proof details checked. The derivative of a transported curvature at zero is its plaquette circulation since the zero curvature kills transport derivatives. Vanishing at every integer base point implies vanishing polynomial circulation; its top homogeneous part is dA_k, and radial contraction gives (k+2)A_k. Finite-dimensional separation gives finitely many measurements. Equivariant projection followed by inverse function theorem gives an inverse germ. Its Taylor polynomial has zero constant term, so substitution preserves the minimum factor count. Ordinary Taylor remainder has no low engineering-weight contribution since jet coordinates have positive weight ⩾2. H₄ averaging uses inverse action on coefficient labels and pullback on links. Curvature transforms by a sign/conjugation under signed lattice isometries. Repeating covering links on a small torus preserves the polynomial identity.

Uniform bounds. Sources: Scott lem:A-bounds; Martin eq:local-section-column-price. Threshold bound uses only the finitely many plaquettes actually read by a section; later support conventions may impose the stronger requirement that all plaquettes meeting the declared support are small. Directions are bounded by t(1+periodic distance)^p₀. A derivative of order k of a fixed path expression is O(t^k), uniformly in torus period and placement. Each curvature factor or its derivative is O(t) when 0<t⩽1; product rule gives t^{f_i}. Constants may depend on G, chosen sections, d,q,p₀, but not period, placement or threshold. Global polynomial bounds supply the separate large-field estimate. No summability over degrees, arities or cutoffs is claimed.

Literature. Exact local-section construction searched before proof; no older exact theorem was located in the searches recorded in foundations-literature.md. This is not a novelty claim. Ordinary analytic ODE parameter dependence and inverse-function steps are imported (Teschl2012 and Rudin1976); Rudin Theorem 9.24 is the inverse-function citation supplied by Scott, with analytic upgrade standard and not separately attributed as a new result. The full general analytic-theorem pages have not been independently inspected in this pass. All cited keys already occur in Scott's bibliography.

Further checks before final acceptance. Add an original radial/Fock–Schwinger-gauge reference if that formula is to carry historical attribution; no proof of the standard radial identity is included. Validate local sections against any exact downstream norm convention once the RG chapters fix it; the draft states its own complete specialized bound and does not assert an unspecified 'admissibility'. Compare derivative left/right ordering against downstream definitions. The source uses the same left convention.

Examples. Clover: 24 corner plaquettes, coefficient 1/4, each leading term h⁴|F_μν|²/2; gives c_{F²}=¼Σ_μν |F_μν|². Additive mixing is described but no terminal map assumed. The free equation ideal is explicitly not identified with the interacting null ideal. Grade-tag distinctions are retained.

Update: original chessboard theorem inspected (19 November)

Parent obtained the original FILS institutional preprint as /tmp/ym-fils.pdf, extracted /tmp/ym-fils.txt. Independently read Theorem4.1 and proof at extracted lines823–866 (PDF pp34–35, printed33–34), plus Theorems4.2–4.3. Theorem4.1 assumes a real-linear involution, cyclic multilinearity over2n slots, and the reflection Schwarz inequality; arbitrary positive integer n is allowed. For draft Chapter1 choose strips of widthℓ, identify their function spaces by translation, and take the involution reflecting local strip coordinates with link reversal. Translation invariance gives cyclicity; the finite-torus RP proposition gives Schwarz across opposite strip seams, because all observable links are strictly inside open cells. Apply the theorem axis by axis to absolute observables. The four reflections commute, yielding exactly the alternating reflected dissemination used in the draft seminorm. This closes the abstract chessboard applicability issue and removes any conjectural power-of-two restriction. The exact gauge-specific RP theorem's original full-text check remains separate. Chapter1 now cites Theorem4.1 and explains this hypothesis match rather than only citing §4.
