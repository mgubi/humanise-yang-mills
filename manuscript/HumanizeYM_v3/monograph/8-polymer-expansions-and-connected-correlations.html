Chapter 8
Polymer expansions and connected correlations

An effective density is useful only if it remains local after integration. Its logarithm should therefore be expressed as a sum of terms supported on connected sets, with a bound that does not grow with the surrounding volume. The cluster expansion provides this passage from local activities to connected terms. Its convergence is a standard result of statistical mechanics. The work specific to the gauge theory is to put the activities produced by the block integration within its hypotheses.

This chapter states the abstract results in the form needed for that application. It does not prove another cluster-expansion theorem. We use Kotecký–Preiss convergence[KP86], in the explicit rooted form of Ueltschi[Uel04, Theorem 1], and explain how support weights, activity derivatives and large-field constraints enter. The distinction between a general cluster estimate and a bound on the actual gauge-theory activities will remain explicit throughout.

8.1Activities, supports and incompatibility

Let \(\Lambda\) be a finite set of lattice sites. A polymer label \(\gamma\) belongs to a finite or countable set \(\mathcal{P}\), has a nonempty support \(X_{\gamma} \subset \Lambda\), and carries a complex number \(z_{\gamma}\), called its activity. Different labels may have the same support. The labels can retain information about an integration sector, derivative or local Taylor coefficient; they are not identified solely by their supports.

A symmetric, reflexive relation \(\gamma \nsim \eta\) specifies incompatibility. Reflexivity means that a compatible collection contains a label at most once. We assume

\(\displaystyle \gamma \nsim \eta \quad \Longrightarrow \quad X_{\gamma} \cap X_{\eta} \ne \varnothing .\) (8.1)

For the hard-core gas the implication is an equivalence. Some estimates below need only the displayed implication; the formula for deleting all polymers meeting a set will be stated for the hard-core case.

The polymer partition function is

\(\displaystyle Z_{\text{pol}} (z) = \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} A \subset \mathcal{P} \text{ finite}\\ A \text{ pairwise compatible} \end{array}$}}} \prod_{\gamma \in A} z_{\gamma} .\) (8.2)

The empty collection contributes one. This sum is a polynomial for finitely many labels. For countably many labels it is well defined whenever \(\sum_{\gamma} |z_{\gamma} | < \infty\), since its absolute sum is at most \(\exp (\sum_{\gamma} |z_{\gamma} |)\). The cluster criterion below implies this summability in finite volume. Complex or signed activities are allowed; they need not be probability weights.

We keep track of spatial size with a nonnegative function \(c (X)\) on nonempty subsets of \(\Lambda\). The needed properties are

\(\displaystyle |X| \leqslant h \hspace{0.17em} c (X), \qquad c (X \cup Y) \leqslant c (X) + c (Y) \quad \text{if } X \cap Y \ne \varnothing,\) (8.3)

where \(h > 0\) is fixed. One choice is the number of sites, with \(h = 1\). A useful multiscale choice is the minimum number of radius-\(B\) lattice balls whose union is connected and contains \(X\). In that case \(h = (2 B + 1)^4\) is an upper bound on the size of a ball, including on a torus. The symbol \(c\) will always denote this support-size function in this chapter, not a field coefficient.

For \(\alpha \geqslant 0\) define the site norm

\(\displaystyle \|z\|_{\alpha} = \sup_{x \in \Lambda} \sum_{\gamma : x \in X_{\gamma}} |z_{\gamma} | e^{\alpha c (X_{\gamma})} .\) (8.4)

It counts every polymer at each site in its support. A norm which counts only the chosen anchor of a polymer is different. Passing from an anchored bound to (8.4) requires a support-size estimate or a loss in the exponential weight. This distinction matters when checking a cluster criterion for an effective density.

8.2The logarithm and its convergence criterion

For a tuple \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}= (\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_n)\), form the graph on the occurrences \(1, \ldots, n\), joining distinct vertices whenever their labels are incompatible. Define

\(\displaystyle \varphi^T (\boldsymbol{\gamma}) = \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} H \text{ connected spanning graph}\\ H \text{ uses incompatibility edges} \end{array}$}}} (- 1)^{|E (H) |} .\) (8.5)

For \(n = 1\) its value is one; for a disconnected incompatibility graph it is zero. Repeated labels are allowed in this tuple, even though the gas (8.2) contains each label at most once. They are necessary in its logarithm: for a gas with one label, \(Z_{\text{pol}} = 1 + z\) and \(\log Z_{\text{pol}} = z - z^2 / 2 + \cdots\). The \(n\)! below compensates for ordering the occurrences; it is not part of the definition (8.5).

Theorem 8.1. (Standard polymer convergence criterion) Suppose \(A_{\gamma} \geqslant 0\) and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\eta : \eta \nsim \gamma} |z_{\eta} | e^{A_{\eta}} \leqslant A_{\gamma}, \qquad \sum_{\gamma} |z_{\gamma} | e^{A_{\gamma}} < \infty .\) (8.6)

Then the cluster series

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{L} (z) = \sum_{n \geqslant 1} \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_n} \varphi^T (\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \prod_{i = 1}^n z_{\gamma_i}\) (8.7)

converges absolutely and \(Z_{\text{pol}} (z) = e^{\mathcal{L} (z)}\). For a fixed label \(\gamma_1\), its absolute rooted series satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n \geqslant 1} \frac{1}{(n - 1) !} \sum_{\gamma_2, \ldots, \gamma_n} | \varphi^T (\boldsymbol{\gamma}) | \prod_{i = 2}^n |z_{\gamma_i} | \leqslant e^{A_{\gamma_1}} .\) (8.8)

Proof. This is the hard-core specialization of the cited convergence theorem: the pair interaction is \(- 1\) on incompatible pairs and zero otherwise. There is no need to assume \(Z_{\text{pol}} > 0\) before taking the logarithm. The series defines the branch continued from zero activity. For real activities it is real, and the exponential identity then gives \(Z_{\text{pol}} > 0\). For complex activities the conclusion is nonvanishing.\(\Box\)

The connected graph sum must be taken before its absolute value. Bounding the sum of the absolute values of all connected graphs would discard the cancellation on which convergence depends. The standard tree-graph inequality is one way to establish (8.8); we use the result rather than repeat its combinatorial proof.

8.3A spatially local bound with a geometric tail

The site norm from (8.4) gives a particularly transparent criterion. Suppose that, for some \(\alpha \geqslant 0\), \(\delta > 0\) and \(\gamma_0 > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \|z\|_{\alpha + \delta} \leqslant \gamma_0, \qquad 0 < q < 1, \qquad h \gamma_0 \leqslant q \delta .\) (8.9)

The parameter \(\delta\) is the additional exponential support decay; it will be spent to sum polymers that meet one another. The parameter \(q\) measures smallness and controls the number of occurrences in a cluster.

Write \(X (\boldsymbol{\gamma}) = \bigcup_i X_{\gamma_i}\). A direct weighted application of Theorem 8.1 yields, for every site \(x\) and integer \(k \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n \geqslant k} \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}: x \in X (\boldsymbol{\gamma})} | \varphi^T (\boldsymbol{\gamma}) | \prod_{i = 1}^n |z_{\gamma_i} | e^{\alpha c (X (\boldsymbol{\gamma}))} \leqslant \gamma_0 q^{k - 1} .\) (8.10)

This form is slightly stronger than the bound with an additional \((1 - q)^{- 1}\) obtained by separately summing estimates at each occurrence number. Either form is sufficient for the applications.

Here is the hypothesis matching for this standard adaptation. Apply the rooted theorem to the inflated activities \(\tilde{z}_{\gamma} = q^{- 1} |z_{\gamma} | e^{\alpha c (X_{\gamma})}\) and take \(A_{\gamma} = \delta c (X_{\gamma})\). By (8.1) and (8.3), the left side of (8.6) is bounded by \(q^{- 1} |X_{\gamma} | \gamma_0 \leqslant \delta c (X_{\gamma})\). For a nonzero connected coefficient the union weight is at most the product of the support weights. Designating one occurrence whose support contains \(x\) changes \(1 / n\)! to \(1 / (n - 1)\)! and gives an upper bound; the rooted sum is at most \(\gamma_0 / q\). Restoring the factor \(q^n\) on terms with \(n \geqslant k\) gives (8.10). Nonempty supports and finite \(\Lambda\) verify the total summability required by the theorem.

More generally, let \(\mathcal{Q} \subset \mathcal{P}\) be a distinguished set of labels with

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\gamma \in \mathcal{Q}} |z_{\gamma} | e^{(\alpha + \delta) c (X_{\gamma})} \leqslant b_{\mathcal{Q}} .\)

Replacing the condition \(x \in X (\boldsymbol{\gamma})\) by “at least one occurrence belongs to \(\mathcal{Q}\)” in (8.10) gives \(b_{\mathcal{Q}} q^{k - 1}\) on the right. This is useful when a cluster has to meet a prescribed insertion. An insertion selected in this way is a distinguished occurrence in the combinatorial expansion, not yet a renormalized quantum field.

The support norm is independent of \(| \Lambda |\). Summing the \(k = 1\) estimate over \(x\) bounds \(|\mathcal{L}(z) |\) by \(| \Lambda | \gamma_0\). Thus extensive free-energy bounds coexist with volume-independent estimates on connected terms meeting a fixed site. It is the latter estimates that preserve locality in a renormalization step.

8.4Deleting polymers and controlling local changes

Recall that \(Z_{\text{pol}}\) is the gas partition function (8.2), and \(X_{\gamma}\) is the full support of label \(\gamma\). For a set \(Y \subset \Lambda\), delete all labels whose supports meet \(Y\): put \(z^Y_{\gamma} = z_{\gamma}\) if \(X_{\gamma} \cap Y = \varnothing\) and zero otherwise. Under (8.9), both cluster series converge and

\(\displaystyle \frac{Z_{\text{pol}} (z^Y)}{Z_{\text{pol}} (z)} = e^{-\mathcal{L}_Y (z)}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_Y (z) = \sum_{n \geqslant 1} \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{X (\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \cap Y \ne \varnothing} \varphi^T (\boldsymbol{\gamma}) \prod_i z_{\gamma_i} .\) (8.11)

This is simply subtraction of two absolutely convergent logarithms. Its local bound is

\(\displaystyle |\mathcal{L}_Y (z) | \leqslant |Y| \gamma_0,\) (8.12)

and clusters with at least \(k\) occurrences contribute at most \(|Y| \gamma_0 q^{k - 1}\). Counting a cluster more than once when it meets several sites of \(Y\) only increases the upper bound.

The same mechanism controls changes of activities in a bounded region. If two families of activities agree away from \(Y\), their logarithmic cluster terms cancel unless a cluster contains a changed activity. A quantitative Lipschitz estimate additionally needs a bound on the difference of those activities, or a uniformly admissible interpolation between them. Agreement of supports alone gives locality of the difference, not its smallness.

8.5Parameters, derivatives and connected insertions

Suppose \(z_{\gamma} (u)\) is holomorphic in a complex parameter \(u\) on a disc and that (8.9) holds uniformly on each of its compact subdiscs. The cluster series then converges normally there. Consequently its sum is holomorphic and can be differentiated term by term. If the bound for a local cluster sum is \(M\) on \(|u - u_0 | < \rho\), Cauchy's estimate gives

\(\displaystyle | \partial_u^r \mathcal{L}_Y (u_0) | \leqslant r! \rho^{- r} M.\) (8.13)

There is no separate combinatorial proof for each derivative order. Several parameters are treated on a polydisc, with the product of the corresponding Cauchy factors. Only derivatives of fixed finite order are asserted unless additional uniform estimates are supplied.

One can introduce formal sources for bounded quantities \(F_j\) by the finite-volume generating function

\(\displaystyle Z (\boldsymbol{t}) = Z (0) \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E} \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( \sum_{j = 1}^r t_j F_j \right) .\)

The mixed derivative of \(\log Z\) at zero is the connected correlation (or cumulant) of the \(F_j\). For example,

\(\displaystyle \partial_{t_1} \partial_{t_2} \log Z (\boldsymbol{t}) |_{\boldsymbol{t}= 0} =\mathbb{E} (F_1 F_2) -\mathbb{E} (F_1) \mathbb{E} (F_2) .\)

The cluster representation expresses such derivatives through clusters carrying the distinguished source occurrences; see [Uel04, §3]. To use this representation in the gauge theory, the source-deformed activities must satisfy a uniform criterion on an actual complex neighbourhood of zero. Boundedness of the undeformed gas does not imply that estimate.

There is a second way to insert observables: differentiate the unnormalized density and expand the result directly. This is preferable if some activity can vanish, because division by that activity is then meaningless. Bounds are assigned to the inserted activity itself. The usual mnemonic “differentiate the logarithm of each activity” is not a valid definition in that situation.

8.6Large-field constraints and exact reassembly

An effective gauge density includes regions where a small-field expansion is not available. Fix a configuration of the coarse variables for the moment, and let \(D \subset \Lambda\) be the set of sites declared to be in the large-field region. In addition to the regular activities \(z_{\gamma}\), introduce large-field activities \(K_{\eta}\) with supports \(Y_{\eta}\) and nonempty sets \(I_{\eta} \subset D \cap Y_{\eta}\). The set \(I_{\eta}\) records the large-field sites assigned to that activity. Let \(\mathfrak{C} (D)\) consist of the allowed finite families \(B\) of such labels with disjoint supports and

\(\displaystyle \bigcup_{\eta \in B} I_{\eta} = D.\)

The exact rules for eligibility are part of the definition of an effective density; they may exclude some otherwise possible covers. When \(D\) is empty, the empty family is permitted.

For the simple overlap incompatibility rule, the constrained partition sum has the form

\(\displaystyle Z_D = \sum_{B \in \mathfrak{C} (D)} \left( \prod_{\eta \in B} K_{\eta} \right) Z_{\text{pol}} (z^{Y (B)}), \qquad Y (B) = \bigcup_{\eta \in B} Y_{\eta} .\) (8.14)

Applying (8.11) gives the exact factorization

\(\displaystyle Z_D = e^{\mathcal{L} (z)} \sum_{B \in \mathfrak{C} (D)} \left( \prod_{\eta \in B} K_{\eta} \right) e^{-\mathcal{L}_{Y (B)} (z)} .\) (8.15)

For finite sums this is algebra; the same identity holds for absolutely convergent sums. It is the basic reason that removing regular polymers near a large-field region can be absorbed into modified activities.

The expression \(\mathcal{L}_{Y (B)}\) need not split as a sum over individual members of \(B\). A connected regular cluster can meet two large-field supports. Such a cluster couples them, and the modified activity belongs on their union together with the cluster support. Grouping by connected components is the appropriate reorganization. An arbitrary product of independently corrected large-field activities would omit these terms.

Formula (8.15) does not by itself bound the sum over \(B\). That estimate requires suppression of large-field activities by the action, bounds on the number and geometry of eligible supports, and enough additional exponential support decay to absorb the regular clusters attached to them. These are analytic inputs to the nonlinear renormalization step. The abstract theorem bounds the regular cluster sum once those inputs have been established; it does not establish them.

Two support notions must be distinguished in this application. The set used in an exclusion or covering rule need not contain every link read by an activity. For example, a large-field condition at a boundary site can inspect a plaquette extending outside its assigned covering set. Disjoint covering sets therefore need not imply disjoint dependence on links. The abstract gas above needs the overlap implication (8.1) for its chosen exclusion supports; it does not assume statistical independence. When a local integration or factorization argument also requires full dependence supports, those enlarged sets and their geometric estimates must be specified separately. One must not silently replace the covering sets by enlarged sets in an exact covering identity.

8.7What an application must supply

The reusable conclusions of this chapter are the connected expansion (8.7), the local tail (8.10), parameter differentiation under a uniform complex-domain bound, and exact removal and reassembly formulas. They are statements about a specified family of activities. To apply them after a block integration one must give:

  1. an exact expansion of the integrated density, including the scalar factor and every large-field sector;

  2. supports containing every link and cutoff condition on which an activity depends, and a definite incompatibility relation;

  3. a site-norm bound with a strictly positive support-weight reserve, satisfying (8.9) uniformly in the period;

  4. corresponding estimates for the source-deformed or inserted activities whenever a connected observable is required;

  5. an absolutely summable bound for the constrained large-field families and the clusters attached to them.

These requirements explain why the cluster expansion is not the main obstruction in the construction. The general summation mechanism is already available in the literature. What must be proved for the Wilson model is that exact blocking, stationary integration, the large-field decomposition and the chosen observable normalization produce activities obeying those requirements on the same trajectory.
