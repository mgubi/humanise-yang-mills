Chapter 14
Reflection spaces, Ward identities and Euclidean symmetry

We first use reflection positivity in each coordinate direction separately. Continuous rotations are not assumed. The resulting Hilbert spaces and bounded insertions provide the positivity estimates needed to construct rotational Ward identities. Relativistic reconstruction requires additional arguments and is not a consequence of the Hilbert-space construction alone.

Throughout, \(S\) is the mixed correlation hierarchy of Chapter 13. Its finite-dimensional field spaces are \(F_d\), with the compatible inclusions and derivative maps constructed there. Statements using this calculus retain the actual marked estimates required in that chapter. All correlations in the present chapter are evaluated at distinct points or against test functions supported away from collisions.

14.1Reflection spaces for the coordinate axes

Fix an axis \(\mu\). A positive word \(w = ((x_i, A_i))_{i = 1}^n\) has \(x_i^{\mu} > 0\) and distinct points; the empty word is allowed. Let \(\theta_{\mu} w\) reflect the points across \(x^{\mu} = 0\), apply the signed coordinate reflection to the labels, conjugate them, and reverse their order. On formal finite linear combinations set

\(\displaystyle \langle v, w \rangle_{\mu} = S (\theta_{\mu} v \hspace{0.17em} w) .\)

The form is positive semidefinite by reflection positivity. Its null space is orthogonal to every vector by Cauchy–Schwarz. The quotient and completion give \(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{\text{OS}}\), with dense word vectors \([w]_{\mu}\) and unit vacuum \(\Omega_{\mu} = [\varnothing]_{\mu}\). This is the standard Osterwalder–Schrader quotient [OS73, Section3]. Only the quotient construction is used here, not the reconstruction theorem.

Let \(\tau_t\) mean translation by \(te_{\mu}\). On formal word sums put \(T_t c = [\tau_t c]_{\mu}\), \(t \geqslant 0\). Translation invariance implies

\(\displaystyle \langle T_s c, T_t d \rangle_{\mu} = \langle T_0 c, T_{s + t} d \rangle_{\mu} .\) (14.1)

Every fixed orbit \(T_t c\) is bounded: the two reflected word halves have a fixed positive separation and the separated-correlation bound of Chapter 13 is uniform as they move apart. If \(M = \sup_{t \geqslant 0} \|T_t c\|\), Cauchy–Schwarz in (14.1) gives \(M^2 \leqslant \|T_0 c\| M\). Thus \(\|T_t c\| \leqslant \|T_0 c\|\), including when \(c\) is null. The operators descend and extend to self-adjoint contractions \(E_{\mu} (t)\). Their semigroup law holds on the dense word span and hence everywhere. Correlation continuity gives strong continuity. The spectral theorem yields

\(\displaystyle E_{\mu} (t) = e^{- tH_{\mu}}, \qquad H_{\mu} \geqslant 0, \qquad \lim_{t \to \infty} E_{\mu} (t) = P_{\mu}^0 \quad \text{strongly},\) (14.2)

where \(P_{\mu}^0\) projects onto \(\ker H_{\mu}\).

Transverse translations, by \(y\) with \(y^{\mu} = 0\), preserve the reflected form and have inverse translations. They therefore give strongly continuous unitaries \(U_{\mu} (y)\), commuting with \(E_{\mu} (t)\). Both leave \(\Omega_{\mu}\) fixed. At this stage we do not claim that \(\ker H_{\mu}\) is one-dimensional, or that its vectors are fixed by transverse translations.

The commuting spectral measures of \(H_{\mu}\) and the three transverse momenta give a joint projection-valued measure \(P_{\mu}\) on \([0, \infty) \times \mathbb{R}^3\):

\(\displaystyle E_{\mu} (t) U_{\mu} (y) = \int e^{- tE + ip \cdot y} \hspace{0.17em} dP_{\mu} (E, p) .\) (14.3)

This is a coordinate half-space spectrum. No cone bound \(E \geqslant |p|\) has been proved or used. For \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} z > 0\), the same spectral integral defines the holomorphic contraction \(e^{- zH_{\mu}}\); its derivatives are bounded on every smaller right half-plane. This elementary heat analyticity will be used before any rotation action is constructed.

14.2Bounded insertions in a slab

A word \(w\) lies in a slab of width \(h\) if \(0 < x_i^{\mu} < h\) for each of its points. Its reflected slab word is \(w^{\sharp} = \tau_h \theta_{\mu} w\). Formally define \(I_w v = w \tau_h v\). Reflection and translation give

\(\displaystyle \langle [I_w c]_{\mu}, [d]_{\mu} \rangle = \langle [c]_{\mu}, [I_{w^{\sharp}} d]_{\mu} \rangle .\) (14.4)

This identity is on formal word sums; neither boundedness nor preservation of null vectors is assumed yet.

Proposition 14.1. (Slab norm bound) For labels of bounded degree and norm, a word of length \(n \geqslant 1\), with pairwise separation and slab margins at least \(r\), \(0 < r \leqslant 1\), defines a bounded operator \(B_h (w)\) on \(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{\text{OS}}\). There are constants depending only on the label bounds such that

\(\displaystyle B_h (w) [v]_{\mu} = [w \tau_h v]_{\mu}, \qquad \|B_h (w)\| \leqslant C^n (n / r)^{\kappa n} .\)

The constants are independent of the width and the exterior word.

Proof. Proposition 13.2 first gives the estimate at a width \(s \leqslant h\), with a slightly smaller buffer. Put \(T = I_{w^{\sharp}} I_w\) at that width and \(D = C^n (n / r)^{\kappa n}\). For every formal finite sum \(c\) its repeated-word estimate gives \(\|[T^j c]_{\mu} \| \leqslant C_c D^{2 j}\). By (14.4), \(T\) is symmetric for the possibly degenerate reflected form. Set \(b_j = D^{- 2 j} \|[T^j c]_{\mu} \|\) and \(M_c = \sup_{j \geqslant 0} b_j\). Then \(b_j^2 \leqslant \|[c]_{\mu} \| b_{2 j}\), so \(M_c \leqslant \|[c]_{\mu} \|\). In particular

\(\displaystyle \|[I_w c]_{\mu} \|^2 = \langle [c]_{\mu}, [Tc]_{\mu} \rangle \leqslant D^2 \|[c]_{\mu} \|^2 .\)

The operator preserves null vectors and extends boundedly. Increase the width to \(h\) by right multiplication by \(E_{\mu} (h - s)\).\(\Box\)

The empty word gives \(B_h (\varnothing) = E_{\mu} (h)\). Dense word matrix elements also prove the exact identities

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} B_h (w)^{\ast} & = B_h (w^{\sharp}), \hspace{3cm} \text{(14.5)} \\ B_{h + k} (w \tau_h v) & = B_h (w) B_k (v),\\ B_{h + t} (w) & = B_h (w) E_{\mu} (t), \qquad B_{t + h} (\tau_t w) = E_{\mu} (t) B_h (w) . \end{array}\)

Transverse covariance reads \(U_{\mu} (y) B_h (w) U_{\mu} (y)^{\ast} = B_h (w + y)\). These assertions concern bounded slab insertions, not unsmeared point fields acting on an arbitrary Hilbert vector.

Proposition 14.2. (Norm-smooth insertion positions) On the open configuration space inside a fixed slab, \(B_h (w)\) is smooth in operator norm. A position derivative replaces the corresponding field label by its derivative in \(F_{d + 1}\).

Proof. For a fixed configuration choose a smaller common buffer. The norm bound holds locally for the original tuple and each differentiated tuple. On dense word matrix elements the derivative identity is exactly the scalar derivative identity of Chapter 13. If a weak derivative \(DA (x) [v] = C (x) [v]\) on a dense domain is locally bounded in operator norm, the scalar mean-value inequality gives \(\|A (y) - A (x)\| \leqslant \sup_{[x, y]} \|C\| \hspace{0.17em} |y - x|\). Apply this first to every differentiated tuple, proving continuity of its operator. The same inequality after subtracting \(C (x) [y - x]\) proves norm differentiability of \(A\). Iteration proves all orders. This argument explains why weak differentiability alone would have been insufficient.\(\Box\)

14.3Comparing the mixed fields with free fields

The comparison is a statement about the actual marked construction, stronger than equality of dimensions of field spaces. We record its quantitative form and then derive it from the finite-cutoff comparison. For fixed arity and degree bounds, let \(M_k \to \infty\), \(r_k \to 0\) and \(\epsilon_k \to 0\). Suppose the same coefficient lifts in every slot give rescaled kernels \(S_k\), with, on a compact separated window,

\(\displaystyle |S_k | \leqslant A_k^n, \quad | \nabla S_k | \leqslant C_n V_k^n / \delta_k, \quad |S_k - S^0 | \leqslant \epsilon_k \quad \text{on its grid} .\) (14.6)

The gradient bound is required outside coordinate-equality strips of width \(4 \delta_k\); the grid has spacing \(s_k\), and \(S^0\) is the free correlation. The necessary small errors are

\(\displaystyle A_k^n \delta_k \to 0, \qquad V_k^n s_k / \delta_k \to 0, \qquad s_k = o (\delta_k) .\) (14.7)

For example the source construction uses \(r_k = a_k M_k^{\alpha_0}\), \(s_k = M_k^{- \alpha_0}\), \(A_k = CM_k^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) D}\), \(V_k = CM_k^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) D}\) and \(\delta_k = M_k^c\), with strictly negative exponents in (14.7). The marked coefficient inverse, Gaussian comparison and displacement estimates must all concern these same lifts. The following construction verifies this compatibility.

14.3.1Obtaining the three comparison bounds from one lattice family

Here are the scale choices behind (14.6). Fix the arity bound \(n\), maximum degree \(D\) and a compact configuration window \(K\) with positive point separation. Enlarge the finite label list to include its signed coordinate images and conjugates. All constants and all three exponents below are chosen for this one finite request. Choose positive numbers \(\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \alpha_2\) and \(c < 0\) with

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} \alpha_1 + 6 & < \alpha_0, & \alpha_2 + 5 & < \alpha_0 + c,\\ nD (\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) + c & < 0, & nD (\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) - \alpha_0 - c & < 0. \hspace{3cm} \text{(14.8)} \end{array}\)

For example first choose \(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1 = 7\), next \(- c > 7 nD\), then \(\alpha_0 - \alpha_2 > 5 - c\), and finally increase all three \(\alpha_i\) together until the last inequality holds and all are positive. Increasing their common part does not alter the preceding inequalities.

At an admissible comparison depth \(k\) put \(H_k = \exp ((\log \log \hat{\beta}_k)^2)\) and set

\(\displaystyle r_k = a_k H_k^{\alpha_0}, \qquad s_k = H_k^{- \alpha_0}, \qquad \delta_k = H_k^c .\)

Thus \(r_k s_k = a_k\): grid rounding occurs on the actual construction lattice. Use one common polynomial extraction \(T\) from Section 11.12.1. If \(v\) is a fixed real polynomial representative of a free quotient field, its lift \(u_{k, N, p}\) solves

\(\displaystyle S_{g / \theta} Tu_{k, N, p} = W_{H_k^{\alpha_0}} v.\)

Here \(S_t\) multiplies a monomial with \(f\) curvature factors by \(t^f\), whereas \(W_{\rho}\) multiplies one of scaling dimension \(j\) by \(\rho^j\); \(g = \beta_{N, p, k}^{- 1 / 2}\) and \(\theta\) is the local normalization used for \(T\). These two dilations have different purposes. The inverse bound in (11.26) gives bounded lifts at each fixed \(k\), uniformly in the cutoffs and periods. Their real, unit and signed symmetry identities are exact. No separate choice is made for different spectator words.

For clarity, the finite-arity use of the corrected-source estimate is as follows. For a subset \(J\) of the slots write \(G_J\) for its free moment at the rescaled positions, and define

\(\displaystyle R_{\varnothing} = 1, \qquad R_I = \prod_{i \in I} I_i - \sum_{\varnothing \ne J \subseteq I} G_J R_{I \setminus J} .\)

These are precisely the coefficients of the actual squarefree source polynomial divided by the free moment polynomial. The latter has constant coefficient one and hence an exact inverse in the finite squarefree algebra. For the limiting Gaussian variables every nonempty coefficient has expectation zero. The extraction of coefficients of source monomials, covariance comparison and marker-weight restoration of Proposition 11.10 apply to this entire finite algebra, including its joint incoming terms. With \(q = \log H_k / (16 n)\) and marker amplitude \(e^{2 q}\), the output normalization is at most \(e^q\) per reflected cell, and extraction gives

\(\displaystyle \|R_I \|_{\text{cell}} \leqslant e^{q - 2 q |I|} \leqslant H_k^{- 1 / (16 n)} \quad (\varnothing \ne I \subseteq \{1, \ldots, n\}) . \) (14.9)

The stage coupling threshold is chosen after this fixed arity and its source constants. The estimate is on the original positive partition function and includes every reflected copy. Uniformity on \(K\) uses positive point separation in the covariance estimate; it does not require a positive lower bound on each coordinate difference. Unwinding the finite recursion and applying the chessboard inequality therefore gives the grid error \(C_K H_k^{- 1 / (16 n)}\) at generic grid points. Distinct coordinates there differ by at least \(a_k\) in physical units, so the assignments to lattice sites require no rounding.

Apply the same coefficient map and the same finite comparison request also at exponents \(\alpha_1\) and \(\alpha_2\). At its own normalization a single insertion has chessboard seminorm at most \(1 + |G_{\{i\}} |\). Changing its scaling dilation to \(\alpha_0\) costs at most \(CH_k^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_j) D}\). Set

\(\displaystyle A_k = CH_k^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) D}, \qquad V_k = CH_k^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) D} .\)

The comparison cells have sides at most \(a_k H_k^{\alpha_j + 5}\). Consequently the first cells are \(o (r_k)\), and the second are \(o (r_k \delta_k)\), by (14.8). The chessboard inequality gives \(|S_k | \leqslant A_k^n\) on \(K\). The positional modulus of Section 11.7.1, applied with physical margin \(r_k \delta_k\), gives \(| \nabla S_k | \leqslant 4 nV_k^n / \delta_k\) outside the coordinate strips. Include the finitely many coordinate hybrids used in rounding in the excluded strips; their total volume is still \(O (\delta_k)\). The physical windows lie inside the smallest period for large \(k\), so the periodic and ordinary separations agree.

Take an ordered limit point of the lifts at each fixed \(k\). The uniform bounds pass to the already chosen continuum hierarchy. Their exponents give exactly (14.7), since

\(\displaystyle A_k^n \delta_k = CH_k^{nD (\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) + c}, \qquad V_k^n s_k / \delta_k = CH_k^{nD (\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) - \alpha_0 - c} .\)

Both tend to zero, and \(s_k / \delta_k \to 0\) follows from the second inequality. A diagonal sequence over the finite requests gives a single comparison sequence. The bounded coefficient limits do not alter the chosen correlation hierarchy or its order of limits.

The coordinate-equality strips have volume \(O (\delta_k)\) on a fixed window. Their contribution to any test function of supremum norm at most one is \(O ((A_k^n + 1) \delta_k)\). Outside them, rounding to the grid and (14.6) cost \(O (V_k^n s_k / \delta_k)\) plus the free modulus of continuity. It follows that

\(\displaystyle \sup_{\|f\|_{\infty} \leqslant 1, \text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} f \subset K} \left| \int f (x) (S_k (x) - S^0 (x)) \hspace{0.17em} dx \right| \longrightarrow 0.\) (14.10)

This is local \(L^1\) convergence; it includes coordinate ties which are not collisions. Testing against each fixed \(f\) separately would be weaker than the displayed assertion.

The lifts can be made exactly compatible with inclusions. Write \(i_d : F_d^0 \to F_{d + 1}^0\) for the free inclusion, \(j_d\) for the physical inclusion and \(L_d\) for a real, hypercubic-equivariant left inverse of \(i_d\). From provisional maps \(U_{k, d}\) define recursively

\(\displaystyle \tilde{U}_{k, d + 1} = j_d \tilde{U}_{k, d} L_d + U_{k, d + 1} (I - i_d L_d) .\)

Exact inclusion identities of correlations preserve (14.10) in every slot. The unit is fixed at degree zero. Free finite detecting families from Chapter 3 make these maps eventually injective; equality of dimensions in Chapter 13 makes them isomorphisms. For each finite request the tail is chosen once, before the test function. A single sequence for all requests is obtained only by explicitly diagonalizing arity, degree, compact windows and errors; the numerical comparison level must be chosen after those finite requests.

Proposition 14.3. (Derivative transport and smooth comparison) For the comparison maps just described,

\(\displaystyle D_{k, d, a} := r_k U_{k, d + 1}^{- 1} \partial_a U_{k, d} \longrightarrow \partial_a^0\)

in finite-dimensional operator norm. Rescaled correlations converge in \(C^j\) on every compact separated window, for every fixed \(j\), also for convergent moving labels.

Proof. Choose free tested correlations giving an injective map \(L : F_{d + 1}^0 \to \mathbb{C}^q\). Their corresponding maps \(L_k\) converge to \(L\) by (14.10); hence \(\|L_k v\| \geqslant c \|v\|\) eventually. Integration by parts in the insertion position gives \(L_k D_{k, d, a} v \to L \partial_a^0 v\). No bound on \(D_{k, d, a}\) was assumed: subtracting the limiting derivative and using the lower bound supplies it and its convergence. Moving labels are handled by expansion in fixed finite bases. Every fixed derivative of the correlation error consequently tends to zero locally in \(L^1\). Multiply by a smooth cutoff supported away from collisions. Integration by parts gives a Fourier bound by \((1 + |p|)^{- M}\) times the sum of its \(L^1\) derivative norms through order \(M\). Choose \(M > 4 n + j\) and apply Fourier inversion.\(\Box\)

14.4The conserved stress line

Let \(\mathcal{K}^0\) and \(\mathcal{K}\) be the real spaces of centered, real, symmetric, hypercubic-covariant degree-four tensor arrays in the free and mixed field spaces. Centered means that each component has zero expectation. Let \(\mathcal{V}^0, \mathcal{V}\) be the degree-five covariant vector spaces. The free divergence calculation of Chapter 3 has rank two. To check that its target is the full covariant vector space, note that degree five permits only colour-contracted \(F \partial F\). Sign parity and permutations leave the two expressions

\(\displaystyle V_b^{(1)} = \sum_c : F_{bc} \partial_b F_{bc} :, \qquad V_b^{(2)} = \sum_{c < d, c, d \ne b} : F_{cd} \partial_b F_{cd} :,\)

with colour summation understood. The apparent further contractions \(\sum_{c, d} F_{bd} \partial_c F_{cd}\) and \(\sum_{c, d} F_{cd} \partial_c F_{bd}\) reduce to these: use the Maxwell equation in the first and the Bianchi identity in the second. The two independent rows of correlation pairings in (3.19) show that these vectors are independent and in the divergence image. Thus free divergence is onto, with kernel spanned by \(T^0\).

Center the componentwise map \(U_{k, 4}\) after transport. It gives an isomorphism \(C_k : \mathcal{K}^0 \to \mathcal{K}\): its inverse transports back and centers there, and preservation of the unit makes the two scalar corrections cancel. Let \(V_k\) be the corresponding vector isomorphism. The derivative comparison gives

\(\displaystyle V_k^{- 1} r_k \text{div} \hspace{0.17em} C_k \longrightarrow \text{div}^0 .\)

Compose with a fixed right inverse of free divergence. The result tends to the identity and is invertible for large \(k\), so physical divergence is onto. Rank–nullity therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \dim \ker (\text{div} |_{\mathcal{K}}) = 1.\) (14.11)

This step uses the full low-degree rank computation, not just the existence of a free conserved tensor.

Choose a nonzero \(J\) on this line. Normalize its pulled-back arrays \(r_k^4 U_{k, 4}^{- 1} J\) to have the norm of \(T^0\) and take a convergent subsequence on that finite-dimensional sphere. Smooth comparison at arity one ensures that its limit is centered. Derivative transport ensures conservation. It must therefore be \(T^0\) or \(- T^0\). Changing the sign of \(J\) once gives positive numbers \(c_k\) with

\(\displaystyle U_{k, 4}^{- 1} (c_k^{- 1} r_k^4 J) \longrightarrow T^0 .\) (14.12)

This fixes an orientation of the physical line. It does not yet fix its Ward normalization, and no positive lower bound for \(c_k\) has been asserted.

14.5Shrinking shells and local charge fields

Choose \(\chi \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R}^4)\) equal to one near an insertion \(x_0\) and zero near all spectators. For a Euclidean Killing field \(v (y)\), either constant or \(K (y - x_0)\) with \(K^{\textsf{T}} = - K\), set

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{Q}^J_v (A ; \boldsymbol{B}) = - \int \partial_{\mu} \chi (y) v_{\nu} (y) S (J_{\mu \nu} (y), A (x_0), \boldsymbol{B}) \hspace{0.17em} dy.\) (14.13)

Repeated indices are summed. Conservation and symmetry of \(J\) show that the value is independent of the cutoff as long as it separates the same distinguished insertion from the spectators. Indeed the difference is the integral of a total divergence in a collision-free region, and \((\partial_{\mu} v_{\nu}) J_{\mu \nu} = 0\).

The actual two-mark estimate needed here has the following form. If \(A \in F_d\), let \(\varkappa = 0\) for constant \(v\) and \(\varkappa = 1\) for linear \(v\). Put \(D = d + 1 - \varkappa\). For a finite family of separated spectator correlation functionals \(\ell_i\) on \(F_D\) and coefficients \(g_i\) annihilating that space, there must be \(\rho_m \downarrow 0\) such that

\(\displaystyle \sup_{1 / 4 \leqslant |z| \leqslant 2} \left| \sum_i g_i \rho_m^{3 + \varkappa} S (J_{\mu \nu} (x_0 + \rho_m z), A (x_0), \boldsymbol{B}_i) \right| \longrightarrow 0.\) (14.14)

Here the same correction coefficient at the distinguished insertion must work for every spectator in the finite family. The power \(3 + \varkappa\) is the four-dimensional volume minus one cutoff derivative plus the degree of the Killing field.

The mixed local replacement estimate of Proposition 13.9 supplies this assertion directly, without using phase decomposition or rotational Ward identities. Apply it to the two-field cluster \((J_{\mu \nu}, A)\), of total degree \(d + 4\), and truncate at \(D = d + 1 - \varkappa\). Its replacement field \(C_D (\rho, z) \in F_D\) is chosen by fixed probes before the exterior spectator. For every spectator in the finite family the residual is bounded uniformly on \(1 / 4 \leqslant |z| \leqslant 2\) by \(C \rho^{D + 1 - (d + 4) - \epsilon}\). The same coefficients therefore cancel under \(\sum_i g_i \ell_i = 0\). After multiplying by the shell factor,

\(\displaystyle \rho^{3 + \varkappa} \left| \sum_i g_i S (J_{\mu \nu} (x_0 + \rho z), A (x_0), \boldsymbol{B}_i) \right| \leqslant C \rho^{1 - \epsilon} \longrightarrow 0, \qquad 0 < \epsilon < 1.\)

This proves (14.14) for every sufficiently small radius, and hence along any sequence \(\rho_m \downarrow 0\). The full compact annulus is allowed because the local replacement proof already removed its lattice-grid restriction by derivative bounds. Only point collisions are excluded; coordinate ties cause no additional restriction. This use of local replacement precedes its phase-uniform operator-product formulation.

Proposition 14.4. (Representation of the local flux) There are unique linear maps \(p_b : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\) and \(Y_d (K) : F_d \to F_d\) such that the constant and linear fluxes (14.13) equal the correlations with \(p_b A\) and \(Y_d (K) A\) at \(x_0\), respectively.

Proof. Every finite linear dependence among the spectator functionals on \(F_D\) annihilates the flux: shrink the shell, rescale its integration variable and use (14.14). Choose a finite separating subfamily of spectator functionals. Its injective map from \(F_D\) has finite-dimensional range; the preceding annihilator property says that the corresponding flux vector lies in that range. It therefore specifies a unique field, and adjoining any other spectator correlation functional proves the identity for it as well. Continuity extends the identity from generic grid configurations to every separated configuration. Linearity follows from uniqueness.\(\Box\)

Differentiating the shell identity with the distinguished insertion, using conservation to move the cutoff and symmetry to remove the Killing derivative, gives

\(\displaystyle Y_{d + 1} (K) \partial_b A = \partial_b Y_d (K) A + \sum_c K_{cb} p_c A, \qquad \partial_b p_c A = p_c \partial_b A.\) (14.15)

Equivalently one can differentiate after moving the whole local shell with \(x_0\); the derivative of \(K (y - x_0)\) supplies the displayed constant flux term. Faithfulness of separated spectator correlation functionals turns these response identities into field identities. The same argument shows compatibility with inclusions, reality and signed coordinate symmetries; both charges annihilate the unit.

14.6Coordinate spectral measures and the angular boundary

We recall that \(P_{\mu}\) in (14.3) is the joint measure of nonnegative heat energy and transverse momentum. For a point field, translate its insertion by \(ue_{\mu}\), \(u > 0\), into the positive half-space and define the undamped measure

\(\displaystyle d \nu_{\mu}^A (E, p) = e^{2 uE} d \langle [A (ue_{\mu})], P_{\mu} (E, p) [A (ue_{\mu})] \rangle .\)

The semigroup law makes this independent of \(u\). It is finite on \(E \leqslant M\); it need not have finite total mass. Word measures are defined after translating the entire word beyond its extreme point. Polarization defines mixed measures. For integrable nonnegative \(g_1, g_2\) and \(|f|^2 \leqslant g_1 g_2\), spectral Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \int f \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^{AB} \right|^2 \leqslant \int g_1 \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^A \int g_2 \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^B .\) (14.16)

Positive damping and the separated derivative bounds imply all polynomial transverse moments. One direct justification takes centered differences of the reflected two-point function: their spectral multiplier is \((2 \sin (tp_j / 2) / t)^{2 r}\). Fatou's lemma and the integral remainder for finite differences give the moment bound as \(t \to 0\). Further heat damping gives energy moments. For point fields, integration over small damping times against a sufficiently high power of time also gives

\(\displaystyle \int (1 + E)^{- D} |p_j |^{2 r} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^A < \infty\) (14.17)

for some \(D\) depending on \(A, r\).

14.6.1What opposite reflection signs control

If a field or word combination \(A\) has reflection signs \(\varepsilon_i = \pm 1\) in axes in a set \(I\), its two-point kernel has, in time direction \(i\), the spectral sign \(\varepsilon_i\). For an even frequency profile \(f\) use

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{L}f (E) = \int e^{- E |t|} \left( \int e^{ipt} f (p) \hspace{0.17em} dp \right) dt = 2 E \int \frac{f (p)}{p^2 + E^2} \hspace{0.17em} dp.\)

At \(E = 0\) the limiting value is \(2 \pi f (0)\). To compare representations in different axes without an arbitrary collision extension, subtract the low moments of the profiles. Choose even \(\sigma \geqslant 0\) of integral one supported in \(1 \leqslant |v| \leqslant 2\), and the Lagrange polynomials \(L_j\) for the nodes \((j + 2)^2\), \(0 \leqslant j < m\). Define the annular correction by

\(\displaystyle \int (\mathcal{C}_{m, \rho} f) (q) g (q) \hspace{0.17em} dq = \int \sigma (v) f (p) \sum_{j < m} L_j ((p / (\rho v))^2) g (\rho v (j + 2)) \hspace{0.17em} dp \hspace{0.17em} dv.\)

Then \(f -\mathcal{C}_{m, \rho} f\) has its first \(2 m\) moments zero. Interpolation of \((p^2 + E^2)^{- 1}\) gives its exact response

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{R}_{m, \rho} f (E) = 2 E \int \frac{f (p)}{p^2 + E^2} \int \sigma (v) \prod_{j < m} \frac{(\rho v (j + 2))^2 - p^2}{(\rho v (j + 2))^2 + E^2} \hspace{0.17em} dv \hspace{0.17em} dp.\) (14.18)

For \(f \geqslant 0\) supported in \([- \rho, \rho]\) this is nonnegative. If \(f_w (p) = w^{- 1} f (p / w)\) with \(f = 1\) near zero and \(0 < w \leqslant 1 \leqslant \rho\), it is bounded below by \(c_m / (E + w)\) on \(0 \leqslant E \leqslant 1\). For large \(E\) its decay can be made arbitrarily high by increasing \(m\). The upper bound, unlike the low-energy lower bound, holds also for growing \(w\): it requires only \(\rho \geqslant 1\) and \(\text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} f_w \subset [- \rho, \rho]\). For \(E \leqslant 1\) use \(\|f_w \|_{\infty} \leqslant C / w\); for \(E \geqslant 1\) the product in (14.18) is bounded by \(C_m \min (1, (\rho / E)^{2 m})\). Thus \(\mathcal{R}_{m, \rho} f_w (E) \leqslant C (w^{- 1} + \rho^D) (1 + E)^{- D}\) for any fixed \(D \leqslant 2 m\). Together with (14.17) this gives, for fixed \(r\),

\(\displaystyle \int \mathcal{R}_{m, \rho} f_w (E) |p_j |^{2 r} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_i^A \leqslant C (w^{- 1} + \rho^D) .\)

Choose \(D\) from the damped moment first, and then \(m\) sufficiently large; \(D\) does not grow with the later subtraction order.

We now give the precise comparison. For a word combination \(A\), assume its offsets have zero coordinates in \(I\) and that reflection in axis \(i\) acts by \(\varepsilon_i\) for \(i \in I\). These geometric restrictions permit arbitrarily small positive placements in any of the chosen time axes, so the integrated moment bound (14.17) holds in each. They will be relaxed for the line axis below. For even nonnegative fixed profiles \(f_j \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R})\) write \(f_{j, v} (p) = v^{- 1} f_j (p / v)\); in particular the normalization preserves its integral, while its supremum costs \(v^{- 1}\). For a product of profiles \(g = (g_k)_{k \in I}\) define

\(\displaystyle \text{Resp}_i (g) = \int \mathcal{L}g_i (E) \prod_{k \in I \setminus \{i\}} g_k (p_k) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_i^A (E, p) .\)

The same even product test function of the unreflected pair kernel has spectral values \(\varepsilon_i \text{Resp}_i (g)\) in every axis \(i\). First require sufficiently many vanishing moments in two axes, so the test function vanishes to the required order at the collision. This makes the comparison an equality of convergent integrals of the original kernel. If \(I\) omits axes, insert in each omitted frequency coordinate a profile \(h (p / T)\) with \(h = 1\) near zero, and let \(T \to \infty\); the damped moment bounds justify this limit. Thus opposite signs give \(\text{Resp}_i (g) = - \text{Resp}_j (g)\).

Let \(a_i =\mathcal{C}_{m, \rho_i} g_i\) and \(a_j =\mathcal{C}_{m, \rho_j} g_j\), where each radius contains the support of its profile and \(m\) exceeds the necessary collision orders. Apply the signed equality to the product with residual profiles in both \(i\) and \(j\). Expanding it gives

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \text{Resp}_i (g_i - a_i ; g_{\ne i}) & \leqslant \text{Resp}_i (g_i - a_i ; g_j \mapsto a_j) + \text{Resp}_j (g_j - a_j ; g_i \mapsto a_i) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(14.19)} \end{array}\)

Here the semicolon leaves every unmentioned profile unchanged. The omitted term is \(- \text{Resp}_j (g_j - a_j ; g_{\ne j}) \leqslant 0\), because its temporal residual response and all its transverse profiles are nonnegative. The corrections \(a_i, a_j\) themselves can have signs. They occur only as transverse profiles on the right, so replacing them by nonnegative majorants gives an upper bound. This explains precisely where positivity is used.

We claim that when both signs occur in \(I\), for \(\lambda \in e^{\mathbb{N}}\), \(q < 0\), \(\beta_j \leqslant 0\), and every \(\delta > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \int_{E \leqslant 1} \frac{\prod_{j \in I \setminus \{i\}} f_{j, \lambda^{\beta_j}} (p_j)}{E + \lambda^q} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_i^A = O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta}) .\) (14.20)

The following induction proves the claim while keeping the order of parameter choices explicit. Choose a walk \(i_0, \ldots, i_N\) through \(I\) with opposite signs at consecutive entries, starting at \(i_0 = i\), with every axis visited before its final entry. Put \(V_s = \{i_0, \ldots, i_{s - 1} \}\). Fix \(K \geqslant 0\) bounding the magnitudes of all negative width exponents. Induct backwards on \(s\) to find \(a_s > 0\) such that

\(\displaystyle \int \mathcal{R}_{m, R_0 \lambda^{\eta}} f_{i_s, \lambda^{\omega_{i_s}}} (E) \prod_{j \ne i_s} f_{j, \lambda^{\omega_j}} (p_j) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{i_s}^A \leqslant C \lambda^{\delta} . \) (14.21)

Here \(0 \leqslant \omega_j \leqslant a_s\) on \(V_s\), \(- K \leqslant \omega_j \leqslant a_s\) elsewhere, \(0 \leqslant \eta \leqslant a_s\) and \(\omega_{i_s} \leqslant \eta\). The fixed \(R_0 \geqslant 1\) contains the support at the current radius; \(m\) may be any sufficiently large fixed subtraction order. Products here and below range over \(I\).

At \(s = N\) all widths are at least one, hence the transverse suprema are bounded. The residual moment estimate with moment order zero bounds the left side by \(C (\lambda^{- \omega_{i_N}} + \lambda^{\eta D})\). Choose \(a_N D < \delta\). For the step from \(s + 1\) to \(s\) write \(i = i_s\), \(j = i_{s + 1}\) and \(p_{\ast} = K |I \setminus \{i, j\}|\). Choose, in this order,

\(\displaystyle \alpha = a_{s + 1}, \qquad 2 r \alpha > p_{\ast} + \max (K, \delta) + 1, \qquad D = D (A, r), \qquad 0 < a_s < \min \{\alpha, \delta / (D + 1)\} .\)

Only after these choices enlarge \(m\) to handle the moment exponent \(D\) and the collision orders. Correct \(g_i\) at radius \(R_0 \lambda^{\eta}\) and \(g_j\) at a fixed multiple of \(\lambda^{\alpha}\). The correction in \(i\) has a fixed nonnegative annular majorant of width \(\lambda^{\eta}\), since its coefficients involve only factors \(\lambda^{2 r' (\omega_i - \eta)} \leqslant 1\). The second term of (14.19) is therefore covered by the next induction step, with the previously current axis now having nonnegative width exponent. The correction in \(j\) is bounded by \(C_r \lambda^{- 2 r \alpha} |p_j |^{2 r}\) on its annulus; its coefficients are bounded because \(\omega_j < \alpha\). The remaining narrow suprema cost at most \(\lambda^{p_{\ast}}\). The first term is consequently at most

\(\displaystyle C \lambda^{p_{\ast} - 2 r \alpha + \max (- \omega_i, \eta D)} \leqslant C \lambda^{p_{\ast} - 2 r \alpha + \max (K, a_s D)} = O (\lambda^{\delta}) .\)

This proves (14.21). Finally take \(s = 0\), \(\omega_i = q\), \(\omega_j = \beta_j\), and \(\eta = 0\). The lower bound \(\mathcal{R}_{m, R_0} f_{i, \lambda^q} (E) \geqslant c_m / (E + \lambda^q)\) on \(E \leqslant 1\), with a bump \(f_i = 1\) near zero, gives (14.20). No continuous rotation was used.

14.6.2The separate diagonal-stress estimate

For \(J_{\mu b}\) with \(\mu \ne b\), the two odd tensor indices give opposite signs and (14.20) applies. A diagonal stress component has only positive signs, so it requires conservation. We describe the contact subtraction needed just for this argument; it is not a positive extension of the correlation hierarchy.

Let \(K_{ab, cd} (x) = S (J_{ab} (x), J_{cd} (0))\) for \(x \ne 0\), and choose an integer \(D\) above the collision orders of these kernels and their first derivatives. For one-dimensional Schwartz test functions let \(\Pi_D\) retain Taylor orders less than \(D\), multiplied by a fixed even cutoff. On product test functions define

\(\displaystyle R_{ab, cd} (h) = \int K_{ab, cd} (x) \left( \prod_i h_i (x_i) - \prod_i \Pi_D h_i (x_i) \right) dx.\)

Each term of the telescoping product difference has a factor \(O (|x_i |^D)\), ensuring integrability. Using the same cutoff in every axis preserves signed tensor symmetries and exchange of the index pairs. Conservation makes its divergence a distribution supported at zero with finitely many derivatives. Inversion removes its even orders. Here is an explicit subtraction of the possible linear orders. Put \(\chi_0 (x) = \prod_j \chi (x_j)\), using the even Taylor cutoff above, and set

\(\displaystyle q_{ab, cd} = \sum_i R_{ib, cd} (\partial_i (x_a \chi_0)), \qquad R'_{ab, cd} = R_{ab, cd} - q_{ab, cd} \delta_0 .\)

Indeed \(\sum_i \partial_i R_{ib, cd}\) evaluated on \(x_a \chi_0\) equals \(- q_{ab, cd}\), whereas the divergence of \(q_{ib, cd} \delta_0\) has the same value. Thus their difference has no linear derivative of \(\delta_0\). To check the tensor symmetries, reflection parity makes \(q_{ab, cd}\) vanish unless each coordinate index occurs an even number of times. The remaining index patterns are \(aa, aa\), \(aa, bb\) and \(ab, ab\) (including their within-pair reversals); coordinate permutations, within-pair symmetry of \(R\), and exchange of its two pairs give the same symmetries for \(q\). The subtraction is therefore compatible with all the tensor identities. We henceforth rename \(R'\) as \(R\). Its Fourier divergence polynomial starts in degree three.

Write \(\hat{R}\) for this corrected functional and \(F_{b, cd} = \sum_i p_i \hat{R}_{ib, cd}\). For a chosen time axis \(\mu\), set

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} Q_0 & = \hat{R}_{\mu \mu, \mu \mu},\\ Q_1 & = \sum_{j, k \ne \mu} p_j p_k \hat{R}_{k \mu, j \mu},\\ Q_2 & = \sum_{j, k, r, s \ne \mu} p_j p_k p_r p_s \hat{R}_{sk, rj} . \end{array}\)

Conservation on the two index pairs gives \(Q_1 = p_{\mu}^2 Q_0 + D_1\) and \(Q_2 = p_{\mu}^2 Q_1 + D_2\), where the polynomials \(D_1, D_2\) start in degrees four and six. Sufficiently many temporal moment subtractions annihilate them exactly. On a fixed temporal annulus the transverse responses of \(Q_0, Q_1, Q_2\) are bounded by \(O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta})\) times respectively \(1, W^2, W^4\), if the transverse widths are at most \(W\). For a diagonal entry, divide its annular temporal profile by \(p_{\mu}\) and put that derivative on the label; the resulting vector has mixed signs. Odd profiles are corrected by \(p^{- 1} \mathcal{C}_{m, \rho} (pf)\), and their residual Laplace absolute value is bounded by \(C (E + w)^{- 1} (1 + E)^{- 2 m}\). Thus (14.20) and spectral Cauchy–Schwarz apply. Removing one transverse moment restriction at a time terminates after three axes. The annular coefficients remain bounded because all shrinking width exponents are nonpositive.

For a fixed even annular \(\sigma\), evaluate \(Q_r\) against \(g_{v, r} (p) = v^{- 1} (p / v)^{- 2 r} \sigma (p / v)\), so \(p^2 g_{v, r + 1} = v^2 g_{v, r}\). Compare the widths \(W\) and \(2 W\) and subtract their fixed annular corrections. Write \(c_r (t) = W^{- 2 r} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} Q_r (g_{tW, r})\), with the transverse profiles fixed, and let \(d_r\) be the same normalized real response to \((I -\mathcal{C}_{m, 8}) (g_{W, r} - g_{2 W, r})\). Conservation gives \(c_{r + 1} (t) = t^2 c_r (t) + O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta} W^2)\) for \(t = 1, 2\). The correction has zero constant moment and higher even moments \(O (W^2)\), so \(d_r = c_r (1) - c_r (2) + O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta} W^2)\). Eliminating \(c_0 (1), c_0 (2)\) from these three relations gives

\(\displaystyle 4 d_0 = 5 d_1 - d_2 + O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta} W^2) .\)

The corrected dyadic profile is signed, so its positivity needs one further observation. For fixed correction nodes \(A_j = (8 v (j + 2))^2\), the function

\(\displaystyle R_E (z) = \frac{1}{z + E^2} \prod_{j < m} \frac{A_j - z}{A_j + E^2}\)

is positive and decreasing for \(0 \leqslant z \leqslant 16\), since \(|v| \geqslant 1\) and \(A_j \geqslant 256\). The dyadic response is the integral of \(2 E [R_E (W^2 p^2) - R_E (4 W^2 p^2)]\) against the nonnegative annular profile \(p^{- 2 r} \sigma (p)\) and the correction profile. For \(W \leqslant 1\) it is therefore nonnegative. On \(E \leqslant 1\) the product factors have a fixed positive lower bound; retaining the difference of the two resolvents gives the lower bound \(cEW^2 / (E + W)^4\) for \(r = 0\). Conservation with the sufficiently many zero moments now gives \(d_0 \geqslant 0\), \(d_1 \leqslant 0\), \(d_2 \geqslant 0\): each temporal multiplier \(p_{\mu}^2\) becomes \(- E^2\) in the Laplace response, with the contact terms killed exactly. Therefore, for a nonnegative transverse product profile \(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\),

\(\displaystyle \int_{E \leqslant 1} \frac{EW^4}{(E + W)^4} \mathcal{K}_{\lambda} (p) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^{J_{\mu \mu}} = O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta} W^4) .\) (14.22)

The integrand vanishes at \(E = 0\). The zero-energy part must be kept separate in the subsequent Cauchy–Schwarz estimate.

14.6.3Vanishing angular flux at infinity

For \(K = E_{ba} - E_{ab}\) and spectator positions on a coordinate line \(\mathbb{R}e_l\), choose anisotropic exponents \(\omega_a = \omega_b = - 100\), \(\omega_l = - 150\) when \(l \not\in \{a, b\}\), and \(\omega_j = - 300\) on remaining axes. Let \(w_j = \lambda^{1 + \omega_j}\), \(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda} = \prod_{j \ne \mu} w_j^{- 1} \psi (p_j / w_j)\), with fixed even nonnegative \(\psi = 1\) near zero, and put

\(\displaystyle I_{\mu} (A ; q) = \int_{E \leqslant 1} \frac{\mathcal{K}_{\lambda} (p)}{E + \lambda^q} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^A .\)

Project the spectator word to odd parity in \(a, b\) and even parity in the other axes, calling the result \(B\). For a reflected line axis this is a finite combination of words on the same line. The mixed-sign estimate gives

\(\displaystyle I_{\mu} (B ; - 3000) = O (\lambda^{\gamma_{\mu}}), \quad I_{\mu} (J_{\mu b} ; \omega_{\mu}) = O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta}) \quad (\mu \ne b), \quad \gamma_l = 1, \quad \gamma_{\mu} \leqslant 150.\)

For the line time axis this requires a genuine temporal gap. Choose a nonnegative real even smooth profile \(\sigma_0\), let \(c = \sigma_0 \ast \sigma_0\), and normalize \(\Phi = \hat{c} = (\hat{\sigma}_0)^2\) so \(\Phi (0) = 1\). Thus \(\Phi \geqslant 0\). If translating the word by \(u\) puts it in the positive half-space, choose an even cutoff \(\zeta = 1\) on \([- 2 u - 1, 2 u + 1]\), supported in \([- 2 u - 2, 2 u + 2]\). Remove from \(c_w\) the profile with Fourier transform \(\zeta (t) \Phi (wt)\). Its inverse-Fourier supremum is bounded uniformly in \(w\), whereas the residual has positive response

\(\displaystyle \int e^{- E |t|} (1 - \zeta (t)) \Phi (wt) \hspace{0.17em} dt \geqslant c_u / (E + w) \quad (0 \leqslant E \leqslant 1) .\)

For the lower bound integrate on \(R < t < R + \gamma / (E + w)\), with \(R = 2 u + 2\), \(wR \leqslant \gamma\) and \(\Phi \geqslant 1 / 2\) on \([- 3 \gamma, 3 \gamma]\). Apply the sign-transfer inequality using this residual and an opposite-sign transverse axis. Its correction term is bounded by the three-axis mixed-sign estimate and the uniform supremum above; its other term is bounded using a sufficiently high transverse moment, now with fixed damping \(e^{- 2 uE}\). The parity pattern has both signs among the transverse axes and a sign opposite to the line axis, so this proves any positive exponent, in particular \(\gamma_l = 1\). For a different time axis use the three transverse-to-line axes and bound the omitted line profile by its supremum. With width \(\lambda^{1 + \omega_l}\) this costs exponent \(- (1 + \omega_l) + 1 = - \omega_l \leqslant 150\). These arguments keep the word measures fixed and include their zero-energy mass; an arbitrary frequency bump would not have the positive temporal-gap response used here. The diagonal estimate is (14.22) with \(W = \lambda^{- 99}\). The crude bound \(I_{\mu} (J_{\mu \mu} ; 0) = O (\lambda^{900})\) follows from finite mass on \(E \leqslant 1\) and the profile suprema. Outside \(E \leqslant 1\) and \(|p_j | \leqslant w_j\), positive damping and Schwartz decay make the relevant test functions \(O (\lambda^{- M})\) for every fixed \(M\).

Take even \(\eta \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R})\) equal to one near zero, put \(\ell (t) = t \eta (t)\), and define

\(\displaystyle f_{ab} (y) = \lambda^{100} \ell (\lambda^{- 100} y_a) \prod_{j \ne a} \ell' (\lambda^{\omega_j} y_j), \qquad V_b = f_{ab}, \quad V_a = - f_{ba} .\)

The other components of \(V\) vanish. Near all insertions \(V (y) = Ky\). Conservation and the local shell identities express the total raw angular response as \(- \int \partial_{\mu} V_c S (J_{\mu c}, \boldsymbol{A})\). The terms \(\partial_a f_{ab} J_{ab}\) and \(\partial_b f_{ba} J_{ba}\) cancel exactly. In the remaining terms, reflection projects the spectators to \(B\). Fourier scaling supplies \(\lambda^3 \mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\) and the temporal derivative supplies \(e^{- E / \lambda^{\omega_{\mu}}}\). Apply (14.16). For the diagonal term split at \(E = \lambda^{- 3000}\), using (14.22) above this value and the crude bound below it. The worst resulting exponents, including the prefactor \(\lambda^{100}\), are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c|c} \mu = l \not\in \{a, b\} & 100 + 3 - 150 + (1 + 1) / 2 = - 46\\ \mu \not\in \{a, b, l\} & 100 + 3 - 300 + (150 + 1) / 2 = - 121.5\\ \mu = b, E \geqslant \lambda^{- 3000} & 100 + 3 + (150 + 1 - 396) / 2 = - 19.5\\ \mu = b, E < \lambda^{- 3000} & 100 + 3 - 1500 + (150 + 900) / 2 = - 872. \end{array}\)

The exchanged column has the same bounds. Every term tends to zero, whereas the total shell response is independent of \(\lambda\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle \sum_i \{ S (\ldots, Y (K) A_i, \ldots) + (Kx_i)_b S (\ldots, p_b A_i, \ldots) \} = 0\) (14.23)

for every separated configuration on a coordinate line. This argument used coordinate positivity and conservation, not a cone spectrum or a pre-existing rotation action.

14.7Normalization and propagation of the Ward identities

Translate a collinear configuration in its line direction in (14.23) and subtract the original identity. With a plane generator mixing that line and a transverse direction one obtains the translation Ward sum for \(p_b\). The commutation law (14.15) gives its positional derivatives as well. The usual positional derivatives \(\partial_b\) obey the same identities.

The finite vector correlation functionals of Theorem 3.6 have a one-dimensional common kernel in the free vector-map space, spanned by \(\partial^0\). Adjoin a linear functional nonzero on that vector; the matrix of correlation pairings becomes injective. Smooth comparison transports this injectivity to the mixed finite-dimensional vector-map space. Thus its Ward kernel has dimension at most one. It contains both \(p\) and \(\partial\), so \(p = c_d \partial\) at every sufficiently large degree. Inclusions and nonzero free differentiation identify these constants across degrees and extend the identity to the lower ones. Call the common constant \(c_{\ast}\). Changing variables in the shell integrals and using (14.12) gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{r_k}{c_k} U_{k, d + 1}^{- 1} p_b U_{k, d} \to \partial_b^0, \qquad \frac{1}{c_k} U_{k, d}^{- 1} Y_d (K) U_{k, d} \to L_d^0 (K) .\) (14.24)

A finite separating family of correlation pairings justify these operator limits, even if the operator norms were not known in advance. Since \(r_k U_{k, d + 1}^{- 1} \partial_b U_{k, d} \to \partial_b^0 \ne 0\) in some degree, the first limit gives \(c_{\ast} \ne 0\) and \(c_k \to c_{\ast}\). In particular \(c_{\ast} > 0\), since \(c_k > 0\). Put \(L_d (K) = c_{\ast}^{- 1} Y_d (K)\). Then

\(\displaystyle L_{d + 1} (K) \partial_b = \partial_b L_d (K) + \sum_c K_{cb} \partial_c .\) (14.25)

Every positional derivative of the normalized angular Ward sum is therefore another such sum with a differentiated label.

14.7.1Analyticity supplied by a positive Gram kernel

The missing step from a line to an open set is analyticity, not smoothness. We establish exactly the analyticity needed. For \(x^a > 0\) let \(\Psi_A (x) = [A (x)]_a\) in the coordinate reflection space. The curve \(t \mapsto \Psi_A (x + te_b)\) is smooth for \(b \ne a\): its weak derivative against every word vector is \(\Psi_{\partial_b A} (x + te_b)\), which is continuous by translation continuity. Integrating this continuous vector curve and using the dense word span proves strong differentiability. Iterate for higher orders. In the normal direction the curve is analytic away from the reflection plane, since it is a positive-time heat orbit.

Let \(F_{A, B} (x) = S (A (x), B (0))\). Its restriction to \(ue_a\), \(u > 0\), is analytic by that heat argument. In the plane \((a, b)\) with \(Ke_a = e_b\), \(Ke_b = - e_a\), define

\(\displaystyle Q_{A, B} (u, z) = F_{e^{- \theta L (K)} A, e^{- \theta L (K)} B} (\rho e_a), \quad \rho = (u^2 + z^2)^{1 / 2}, \quad \theta = \arctan (z / u) .\)

This is analytic for \(u > 0\): the label spaces are finite-dimensional and the radial functions are analytic. It solves the angular identity because \((u \partial_z - z \partial_u) \theta = 1\) and the same operator kills \(\rho\). It agrees with the actual \(F\) at \(z = 0\). All transverse jets of the actual angular expression vanish there, by (14.23) and (14.25). For \(H = F - Q\), differentiating the angular expression \(n\) times in \(z\) gives at \(z = 0\)

\(\displaystyle u \partial_z^{n + 1} H_{A, B} = - \partial_z^n (H_{L (K) A, B} + H_{A, L (K) B}) + n \partial_u \partial_z^{n - 1} H_{A, B} .\)

The last term is omitted at \(n = 0\). Induction proves equality of all transverse jets of \(F\) and \(Q\), simultaneously for all labels.

Equality of jets has not yet proved equality of functions. The positive Gram identity supplies the decisive bound. If a smooth Hilbert-valued curve \(v (t)\) satisfies \(\langle v (s), v (t) \rangle = A (t - s)\) and the derivatives of \(A\) at zero agree with an analytic function, then

\(\displaystyle \|v^{(n)} (t)\|^2 = |A^{(2 n)} (0) | \leqslant CR^{- 2 n} (2 n) ! \leqslant C (2 / R)^{2 n} (n!)^2 .\)

Hilbert-space Taylor remainders tend to zero for \(|h| < R / 2\), uniformly in \(t\). Apply this to the single-insertion curve above: its Gram kernel is \(F_{A, A^{\dagger_a}} (2 x^a e_a + (t - s) e_b)\), whose jets are those of \(Q\). Thus single-insertion vectors are analytic along every transverse coordinate as well.

If one insertion is a strict maximum in some coordinate, separate it from the spectator word by a coordinate hyperplane. Its moment is a matrix element of this analytic single-insertion curve against a fixed word vector. Strict minima are handled by the existing signed reflection. Hence the Ward sum is analytic along any coordinate segment on which the moving point stays a strict extremum in some coordinate. This uses no integration of the spin operators into a rotation group.

14.7.2A finite path through separated configurations

Configurations with distinct first coordinates are dense. Starting from one, choose the point with smallest first coordinate among those not yet moved. Move it in the second coordinate until it exceeds all other second coordinates; it remains strictly first in the first coordinate. Next move it in the first coordinate beyond all other points, remaining extreme in the second. Finally set its other coordinates to zero, remaining extreme in the first. Repeat with the remaining points. Every segment is separated and of the analytic kind just proved. The result lies on the first coordinate axis. There all Ward sums and all their derivatives vanish. Reverse the path: the one-variable identity theorem on each segment propagates the identity and its derivative identities. Continuity then gives, on every separated configuration,

\(\displaystyle \sum_i \{ S (\ldots, L (K) A_i, \ldots ; x) + (Kx_i)_b \partial_{x_i^b} S (\boldsymbol{A}; x) \} = 0.\) (14.26)

This is why replacing analyticity by smoothness in the propagation argument would leave a gap.

14.8The rotation action and the normalized tensor

Commuting two identities (14.26) shows that the internal defect \([L (K), L (M)] - L ([K, M])\) has zero correlation sums. It is real, alternating, filtration preserving, kills the unit, and has the signed covariance of a pair of rotation generators. The finite internal-defect correlation functionals of Theorem 3.7 are injective on this defect space. Their injectivity transfers under the smooth finite-dimensional comparison, exactly as for the vector correlation functionals above. The defect is therefore zero. Injective inclusions extend this conclusion from any sufficiently large degree to the lower ones.

One must still exclude a representation only of the universal cover of \(SO (4)\). Here the free comparison gives an exact intertwiner. Choose the six plane generators and, at one degree, write their free matrices as \(C_i\) and their transported mixed matrices as \(B_{k, i}\). By (14.24) these converge to \(C_i\). On the matrix space put

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_{k, i} (X) = B_{k, i} X - XC_i, \qquad \mathcal{C}_k = \sum_i \mathcal{A}_{k, i}^2 .\)

The bracket identities make \(\mathcal{C}_k\) commute with every \(\mathcal{A}_{k, i}\). At the limit an invariant inner product makes \(\mathcal{C}\) self-adjoint and negative semidefinite, with kernel the commutant of the free action. Its zero eigenvalue is isolated. Finite-dimensional spectral projections around that eigenvalue give real projections \(P_k \to P\) commuting with all \(\mathcal{A}_{k, i}\). They also commute with the signed symmetries on matrix space. The restrictions of \(\mathcal{A}_{k, i}\) to \(\text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} P_k\) tend to zero. Every plane generator is a commutator of two others, so their maximal norm satisfies \(m_k \leqslant 2 m_k^2\). Eventually \(m_k = 0\). Consequently \(f_k = P_k I \to I\) is invertible and \(B_{k, i} f_k = f_k C_i\). The intertwiner \(U_{k, d} f_k\) is real and preserves the signed action. Conjugating the free \(O (4)\) action by it gives the required genuine orthogonal-group representation. This finite-dimensional argument also fixes the periodicity of plane rotations, rather than assuming it from Lie brackets.

Theorem 14.5. (Euclidean rotations) Under the actual marked comparison and annular estimates used above, there are continuous complex-linear representations \(R_d\) of \(O (4)\) on \(F_d\), extending the signed coordinate symmetries, preserving reality and the unit, and compatible with inclusions. Their infinitesimal generators are \(L_d\), their derivatives transform as vectors, and

\(\displaystyle S (R_{d_i} (g) A_i ; gx) = S (\boldsymbol{A}; x), \qquad g \in O (4) .\)

They are isomorphic in each degree to the free label representations.

Proof. The preceding construction supplies the representations. The function

\(\displaystyle t \longmapsto S (R (e^{tK})\boldsymbol{A}; e^{tK} x)\)

has zero derivative by (14.26). Plane rotations and signed reflections generate \(O (4)\). Covariance, the fact that inserting the identity leaves correlations unchanged, and the exact derivative and inclusion identities show that the corresponding label identities have zero responses against every spectator. Faithfulness of the field quotient proves their equality. Thus the independently chosen finite-degree intertwiners need not themselves commute with inclusions; the resulting actions do.\(\Box\)

Let \(J_0 = c_{\ast}^{- 1} J\). With probability Haar measure on \(O (4)\) set

\(\displaystyle T_{ab} = \int_{O (4)} \sum_{i, j} g_{ai} g_{bj} R_4 (g) (J_0)_{ij} \hspace{0.17em} dg.\) (14.27)

Proposition 14.6. (Normalized stress tensor) The tensor \(T\) is real, centered, symmetric, conserved and nonzero. It transforms as a tensor under \(R_4\). Its constant and linear local fluxes are respectively the positional derivative and \(L_d (K)\). Along the same comparison sequence, \(U_{k, 4}^{- 1} (r_k^4 T) \to T^0\).

Proof. Haar invariance gives the tensor law. All other linear properties pass through the integral, conservation using vector covariance of derivatives. In a transformed shell change variables by \(g\) and use correlation covariance; its actions are \(\partial_b\) and \(L_d (K)\). Averaging preserves these normalized actions. If \(T = 0\), all tested positional derivatives would vanish, contradicting smooth comparison with a free differentiated quadratic field. Finally \(T\) belongs to the same centered conserved line as \(J\). Its translation action forces \(J = c_{\ast} T\), and (14.12) with \(c_k \to c_{\ast}\) proves the comparison limit.\(\Box\)

The complexified finite-dimensional rotation representations also specify the label representations for the later continuation to Lorentz transformations. No Lorentz action on a physical Hilbert space, uniqueness of the vacuum, or positive extension of collision kernels has been constructed in this chapter.
