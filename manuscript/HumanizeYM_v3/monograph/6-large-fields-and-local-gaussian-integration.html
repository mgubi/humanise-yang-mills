Chapter 6
Large fields and local Gaussian integration

A block transformation integrates every configuration. The usefulness of small-field coordinates therefore depends on a bound for their complement. This chapter separates two mechanisms. Selected large plaquette fields and large block average discrepancies retain a deterministic positive energy. Failures of smooth local cutoffs in the remaining coordinates are controlled by simultaneous Gaussian tail estimates. These mechanisms use different estimates and must be combined under the same integral.

We work at one adjacent pair of scales. The algebraic decompositions below are exact. The energy and differentiated integral estimates use uniform bounds for the chosen path rows and actual sector matrices. We construct these bounds for the same local integral below; the flat Gaussian identities alone would not supply them.

6.1Selected tests and the local energy

Fix a stage, a block ratio \(L\), the penalty \(b > 0\), and the fine and coarse path data of (4.20). Throughout this chapter distances are coarse lattice distances, except that path cutoffs use true fine length. Write

\(\displaystyle t_f = t_{m + 1}, \qquad t_c = t_m, \qquad B = B_m, \qquad R_{\ast} = \lfloor B / 100000 \rfloor, \qquad t_{\text{hi}} = \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}^{- 49 / 100} .\)

The thresholds \(t_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 2} M_m^{\text{cut}}\) and \(B_m = \lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_m)^2 \rceil\) were defined in (4.15). At fixed \(L\), as \(\hat{\beta}_m \to \infty\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{t_f}{t_c} \longrightarrow 1, \qquad B^n \frac{t_f}{t_{\text{hi}}} \longrightarrow 0, \qquad B^n \frac{t_{\text{hi}}^2}{t_f} \longrightarrow 0 \quad (n \text{ fixed}) . \) (6.1)

The coarse torus period is at least \(100 (B + 1)\). All constants below are independent of that period.

For a configuration pair \((U, V)\) and the block average \(Q (U)\) of Chapter 4, put

\(\displaystyle r_p^f = \chi (U_p), \qquad r_p^c = \chi (V_p), \qquad n_e = \chi (V_e Q_e (U)^{- 1}) .\)

Here \(\chi\) is the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt distance from the identity. A selection \(A = (A_f, A_n, A_c)\) consists of fine plaquettes, coarse mismatch edges, and coarse sites. Its size is the sum of the three cardinalities. Its location contains the projected corners of its plaquettes, the endpoints of its edges, and its sites. Write \(A^{[r]}\) for the coarse \(r\)-neighborhood of that location. Fix \(W > 0\). A selection is consistent if, on all tests meeting \(A^{[5 B]}\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} & r_p^f \geqslant t_f / 4 \quad (p \in A_f), & & r_p^f \leqslant t_f / 2 \quad (p \not\in A_f),\\ & n_e \geqslant Wt_f \quad (e \in A_n), & & n_e \leqslant 2 Wt_f \quad (e \not\in A_n), \end{array}\)

and \(A_c\) is exactly the set of coarse sites incident to plaquettes with \(r_p^c > t_c\) in that neighborhood. Every chosen frame tag must have positive mass. The overlaps in the first two threshold pairs permit a partition with smooth cutoffs; consistency does not prescribe a unique selection.

For the exact integral use a global allocation before forming components. Choose a smooth function \(\vartheta_f\) equal to zero below \(t_f / 4\) and to one above \(t_f / 2\), with values in \([0, 1]\), and an analogous \(\vartheta_n\) for the thresholds \(Wt_f, 2 Wt_f\). Give a fine selection the weight

\(\displaystyle \prod_{p \in A_f} \vartheta_f (r_p^f) \prod_{p \not\in A_f} (1 - \vartheta_f (r_p^f)),\)

and use the analogous product for mismatch edges. Summing over all selections gives one at every configuration. Take \(A_c\) to be the actual coarse large-field set. Each nonzero global allocation obeys the consistency inequalities on every test, not merely within \(5 B\). The weaker local definition above suffices for the core energy estimate; later component factors retain the global allocation's restrictions on their larger dependence regions. This refinement adds local eligibility tests without discarding any part of the original integral.

An allocation sector fixes the selection, its assignment to connected components of its neighborhoods, a rooted spanning tree in every block, and all frame and block average tags. Tree links are set to the identity. These choices fix the coordinates and supports before differentiation. The set \(D \subset A_f \cup A_n\) consists of tests exceeding \(t_{\text{hi}}\). Let \(H\) be the output anchors at distance at most \(4 B / 5\) from \(D\). Local counting gives

\(\displaystyle |H| \leqslant KB^4 |D|, \qquad |A^{[2 B]} | \leqslant KB^4 |A| . \) (6.2)

The constants may depend on the fixed stage and ratio.

6.1.1An exact completion of squares

Let \(F (g) = P_{\mathfrak{g}} (g - g^{- 1}) / 2\), with the orthogonal projection taken in the fixed trace metric. Direct and path curvature rows are grouped as

\(\displaystyle X_f (p) = \left( \sqrt{c_{\text{dir,f}}} F (U_p), L_{f, R_{\ast}} (U, p) \right), \quad X_c (p) = \left( \sqrt{c_{\text{dir,c}}} F (V_p), L_{c, R_{\ast}} (V, p) \right) .\)

The \(L_{\ast, R_{\ast}}\) are the path sums of (4.20), truncated at true fine length \(R_{\ast}\). Let \(\chi_p^{\text{rel}}\) denote the relaxed small-field test of (4.18). Define capped coarse rows and local kinetic energies by

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} v_c (p) & = \left( \alpha_p \sqrt{c_{\text{dir,c}}} F (V_p), \chi_p^{\text{rel}} (V) L_{c, R_{\ast}} (V, p) \right), & \alpha_p & = \min (1, t_c / r_p^c),\\ K_f (p) & = \text{clip}_{t_f} (r_p^f) + \tfrac{1}{2} \chi_p^{\text{rel}} (U) |L_{f, R_{\ast}} (U, p) |^2, & & \\ K_c (p) & = \text{clip}_{t_c} (r_p^c) + \tfrac{1}{2} \chi_p^{\text{rel}} (V) |L_{c, R_{\ast}} (V, p) |^2 . & & \end{array}\)

Set \(\alpha_p = 1\) when \(r_p^c = 0\). The clip is the Borel quadratic/linear function in (4.17), with its respective scale coefficients. In particular \(|v_c (p) | \leqslant Ct_f\).

For clarity the square-completion input can be stated without choosing a particular row factorization. Suppose the flat fine, penalty, and complement maps \(M_f, M_n, N\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle M_f^{\ast} M_f + M_n^{\ast} M_n + N^{\ast} N = I, \qquad M_f^{\ast} X_f^{\text{lin}} + M_n^{\ast} Z^{\text{lin}} = X_c^{\text{lin}} . \) (6.3)

The superscript denotes linearized curvature and \(Z^{\text{lin}} = \sqrt{b} (v - Qa)\), with the linear block average appropriate to the chosen fine/coarse pair. For the selected quadratic data these identities follow from the transport and positive-defect identities of Chapter 5. Here is their row realization, including the exact alignment premise. Let \(C_{\nu}\) map curvature to the direct and path rows actually used at scale \(\nu \in \{f, c\}\), and require \(C_{\nu}^{\ast} C_{\nu} = \textsf{H}_{\nu}\), where \(\textsf{H}_{\nu}\) is the chosen modified curvature metric. This is the quadratic coefficient condition of (5.28); it is not a conclusion for an arbitrary path action. Put \(\textsf{B}_{\nu} = \textsf{H}_{\nu}^{- 1}\) and \(P_c = C_c \textsf{B}_c C_c^{\ast}\). The positive direct row makes \(C_c\) injective, so \(P_c\) is an orthogonal projection. Equation (5.19) supplies an exponentially local row \(K\) with

\(\displaystyle K^{\ast} K = \textsf{B}_c - T^{\ast} \textsf{B}_f T - b^{- 1} d_{1, c} d_{1, c}^{\ast} .\)

Choose

\(\displaystyle M_f = C_f \textsf{B}_f TC_c^{\ast}, \qquad M_n = b^{- 1 / 2} d_{1, c}^{\ast} C_c^{\ast}, \qquad N = \left( \begin{array}{c} I - P_c\\ KC_c^{\ast} \end{array} \right) .\)

Direct multiplication gives the first identity in (6.3). For the second, use \(X_f^{\text{lin}} = C_f d_{1, f} a\), \(X_c^{\text{lin}} = C_c d_{1, c} v\), and \(T^{\ast} d_{1, f} = d_{1, c} \mathcal{Q}\). The last identity is (5.17) with the actual block average of (5.24): its extra coarse gradient has zero curl. The metric modification does not change these identities on curls. Thus both row identities hold for the same aligned action rows and actual block average. The selected path construction imposes this alignment: in Section 7.7 it chooses \(C_c = (\sqrt{c_{\text{dir}}} I, \rho)^T\) with \(\rho^{\ast} \rho = \textsf{H}_c - c_{\text{dir}} I\), and realizes these very rows by transported plaquettes. Cubic and quartic matching terms do not change their Hessian, while the separate loop correction is not part of the classical metric. The update in Theorem 9.2 uses precisely this construction. At Wilson initialization the direct and additional rows have coefficients \(\sqrt{c_{\text{dir}}}\) and \(\sqrt{1 - c_{\text{dir}}}\), so their Gram matrix is \(I\); the terminal fine metric is unmodified (\(\zeta_f = 0\)). The first coarse modification and every later one obey (5.19). Thus alignment is an invariant of the selected construction, although it remains a necessary premise when applying the estimate to other path actions. Transport coefficients along their fixed coordinate routes using the replicated coarse field, and discard routes of fine length greater than \(R_{\ast}\). Denote the resulting maps by \(\Phi, \Phi_n, \mathcal{N}\) and put \(Z (e) = \sqrt{b} F (V_e Q_e^{- 1})\). Their adjoints below are the actual transported adjoints.

The rows \(\Phi v_c\) and \(\Phi_n v_c\) are the transported linear comparison terms. Throughout this chapter, a comparison term means one of these specified rows, not an independently chosen approximation.

Define six anchored quantities:

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} E_f & = K_f + \tfrac{1}{2} | \Phi v_c |^2 - \langle \Phi v_c, X_f \rangle,\\ E_n & = \tfrac{b}{2} n_e^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} | \Phi_n v_c |^2 - \langle \Phi_n v_c, Z \rangle,\\ E_c & = \langle v_c, X_c \rangle - \tfrac{1}{2} |v_c |^2 - K_c,\\ E_N & = \tfrac{1}{2} |\mathcal{N}v_c |^2,\\ E_L & = \langle v_c, \Phi^{\ast} X_f + \Phi_n^{\ast} Z - X_c \rangle,\\ E_D & = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle v_c, (I - \Phi^{\ast} \Phi - \Phi_n^{\ast} \Phi_n -\mathcal{N}^{\ast} \mathcal{N}) v_c \rangle . \hspace{3cm} \text{(6.4)} \end{array}\)

Every expression is evaluated at its corresponding plaquette, edge, or complement-row anchor. On summing over the whole torus, the cross terms and squares cancel exactly:

\(\displaystyle \sum (E_f + E_n + E_c + E_N + E_L + E_D) = \sum K_f + \frac{b}{2} \sum_e n_e^2 - \sum K_c . \) (6.5)

This equality is algebraic and does not assume flatness. The local energy \(\mathcal{E}_A\) is instead the sum of the six terms at anchors in \(A^{[2 B]}\). Cancellation across its boundary is unavailable. The two signed defects \(E_L, E_D\) must therefore be estimated locally.

6.1.2Direct bounds and the signed comparison

Assume the transported maps have absolute row and column sums bounded by \(CL^{- 2}\) and \(CL^2\) for \(\Phi\), respectively, and by \(C\) for \(\Phi_n, \mathcal{N}\). Bounds involving \(L^{- 2}\) below require \(C\) fixed before choosing \(L\). Put

\(\displaystyle R_f = \sum_{p \in A_f : r_p^f > t_f} r_p^f, \qquad R_n = \sum_{e \in A_n} n_e .\)

The direct fine term, when \(r_p^f \leqslant t_f\), is at least \(\tfrac{1}{2} (\sqrt{c_{\text{dir,f}}} r_p^f - | \Phi v_c (p, 0) |)^2\). Since \(| \Phi v_c | \leqslant CL^{- 2} t_f\), a selected plaquette in this range contributes at least \(c_{\text{dir,f}} t_f^2 / 128\) for large \(L\). Above \(t_f\) the linear clip retains a multiple of \(t_f r_p^f\).

There is one additional loss: a path row whose relaxed test fails is no longer part of a square. Such a row based at \(p\) has a witness \(q\) with

\(\displaystyle r_q^f > t_f \exp (\text{dist} (p, q) / R^{\text{rel}}) .\)

For a fixed witness the number of possible bases is at most \(C (1 + R^{\text{rel}} \log (r_q^f / t_f))^4 \leqslant Cr_q^f / t_f\). All witnesses lie within \(B / 50\) for sufficiently large reference coupling. They belong to the selected set by consistency. Split path endpoints into those with distance from the identity at most \(t_f\) and the rest. The row bound handles the former, and the column bound handles the latter, giving

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{omitted rows } p} |L_{f, R_{\ast}} (U, p) | \leqslant CR_f .\)

Multiplication by \(| \Phi v_c | \leqslant CL^{- 2} t_f\) absorbs this loss into the linear clip after increasing \(L\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle \sum E_f \geqslant \frac{c_{\text{lin,f}}}{2} t_f R_f + \frac{c_{\text{dir,f}}}{128} t_f^2 |\{p \in A_f : r_p^f \leqslant t_f \}| . \) (6.6)

The mismatch square similarly gives, for sufficiently large \(W\) after \(L\),

\(\displaystyle \sum E_n \geqslant \frac{b}{8} \sum_{e \in A_n} n_e^2 . \) (6.7)

The coarse path contribution cancels exactly in \(E_c\). The direct part is \(c_{\text{dir,c}} (\alpha_p - \alpha_p^2 / 2) |F (V_p) |^2 - \text{clip}_{t_c} (r_p^c)\). The squared normal displacement from the tangent space is \(O (\chi (g)^4)\), so \(|F (g) |^2 \geqslant \chi (g)^2 - C \chi (g)^4\) near the identity. Choose fixed \(\delta > 0\) so that also \(|F (g) |^2 \geqslant 3 \chi (g)^2 / 4\) below \(\delta\). The clip coefficients satisfy \(c_{\text{dir,c}} > 16 c_{\text{lin,c}}\), and hence

\(\displaystyle E_c (p) \geqslant \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} - Ct_c^4, & r_p^c \leqslant t_c,\\ c_{\text{dir,c}} t_c r_p^c / 4, & t_c < r_p^c \leqslant \delta,\\ - c_{\text{lin,c}} t_c r_p^c, & r_p^c > \delta . \end{array} \right. \) (6.8)

The remaining ingredient is genuinely about the chosen nonabelian paths and block average. Its quantitative form is recorded explicitly.

Proposition 6.1. (Local signed comparison from compatible rows) Assume (6.3), the stated transported row and column bounds, and a common positive exponential bound for the uncut path kernels, with their coefficients aligned with the curvature rows used in those identities. Then, on every consistent sector for the block average of Chapter 4, there are \(C, K\) fixed before \(L, W\) and \(Z = Z_{j, L, W}\) such that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \sum_H (E_L + E_D) & \geqslant c_{\text{lin,c}} t_c \sum_{r_p^c > \delta} r_p^c - Ct_f (L^{- 2} R_f + R_n) - Zt_f^2 B^4 |D|, \hspace{3cm} \text{(6.9)} \\ \sum_{A^{[2 B]} \setminus H} (E_L + E_D) & \geqslant - \frac{a}{4} t_f^2 |A| . \hspace{3cm} \text{(6.10)} \end{array}\)

Every coarse plaquette with \(r_p^c > \delta\) lies in \(H\) and their number is at most \(K |D|\). Here

\(\displaystyle a = \min \{c_{\text{lin,f}} / 8, c_{\text{dir,f}} / 128, b / 32, c_{\text{dir,c}} / 64\} .\)

Proof. In a lifted patch of radius \((j + 7) L\), let \(\mathcal{P}_y (U) = \sum_q m_y (q) r_q^f\) be the sum of fine plaquette distances from the identity with the actual route multiplicities. The required comparison is

\(\displaystyle r_p^c \leqslant \sum_{e \in \partial p} n_e + C_j L^{- 2} \mathcal{P}_y (U), \qquad \sup_q \sum_y m_y (q) \leqslant K_j . \) (6.11)

This estimate does hold for the precise branches of Chapter 4, as the following argument shows. It is a first-power bound; comparison of squared frame energies alone would give an inadequate square-root bound.

The first-power block average estimate is Lemma 4.4; its comparison uses the same macro-frame and rooted paths as here.

The same bound localizes coarse plaquettes with \(r_p^c > \delta\). If every fine or mismatch test in their fixed macro-patch is at most \(t_{\text{hi}}\), (6.11) bounds \(r_p^c\) by \(C_{j, L} t_{\text{hi}} < \delta\) after increasing the coupling threshold. Thus each such plaquette has a test in \(D\) in that patch. The fixed patch fits inside the \(B\)-neighborhood used to define \(H\), and bounded macro-patch multiplicity gives the asserted count \(K |D|\). Separating the small-distance contribution from the selected excess gives \(r_p^c \leqslant C_{j, L, W} t_f + \Delta_p\) with \(\sum_p \Delta_p \leqslant C (L^{- 2} R_f + R_n)\). The constant multiplying the excess must remain independent of \(L, W\). Applying the transported row and column bounds then gives (6.9); the positive displayed term is obtained by absorbing its upper bound into the enlarged loss constants. It is not a new positivity assertion for \(E_L\).

Outside \(H\), all fine plaquette distances from the identity and block average mismatches in the surrounding fixed macro-patches are at most \(t_{\text{hi}}\). The unselected tests obey this too, since \(2 Wt_f \leqslant t_{\text{hi}}\) eventually. The verified macro-comparison bounds coarse plaquette distances from the identity there by \(C_{j, L, W} t_{\text{hi}}\). Choose a coarse axial gauge on a box of radius \(B / 2\). Its sum of bond distances from the identity is bounded by \(C_{j, L, W} B^5 t_{\text{hi}}\). To compare a fine link with the replicated coarse link, join its two endpoints to their block roots along the actual tree. All tree transports are the identity. The resulting walk lies in two neighboring blocks. In a macro axial gauge its distance from the identity is bounded by the route length times the sum of bond distances from the identity in the macro-box. Comparing a crossing walk with the block average adds the two endpoint frame errors already bounded in Lemma 4.4, and then the mismatch \(n_e\). Consequently, with a constant depending on the fixed tree family and \(L\),

\(\displaystyle \chi \left( U_e (\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} V)_e^{- 1} \right) \leqslant C_{j, L} \mathcal{P}_y (U) + n_{e_c}\)

for crossing edges; omit the mismatch for internal edges. Any tree in a fixed block has uniformly bounded path length (at most \(L^4 - 1\)), which suffices here because this constant is chosen after \(L\). This is not a pre-\(L\) excess estimate. The parent lift of the coarse gauge preserves these distances between corresponding links. It therefore places both fine and coarse logarithms in a common chart, throughout radius \(2 B / 5\), with bound \(\delta_{\text{loc}} = C_{j, L, W} B^{10} t_{\text{hi}}\).

The derivative of the actual rooted block average also matches the flat identity. Near the identity all selected frame and block average branches are minimizing; the redundant tags do not change their values. The finite-patch minimizing frame is smooth there by the same nondegenerate implicit equation used in Lemma 4.1. Write \(h_{y, z} (\exp (\epsilon a)) = 1 + \epsilon H_{y, z} (a) + O (\epsilon^2)\) and \(\bar{H}_y = \sum_z w_z^{\text{av}} H_{y, z}\). Differentiating (4.5), and then its nearest-point block average, gives

\(\displaystyle (\mathcal{Q}a)_{y, \mu} = (\mathcal{A}a)_{y, \mu} + \bar{H}_y (a) - \bar{H}_{y + e_{\mu}} (a),\)

where \(\mathcal{A}\) averages the straight length-\(L\) link sums with the same weights. Thus \(\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{A}\) is a coarse gradient. Its curl vanishes exactly. This verifies the block average identification needed in (6.3). Their aligned row realization was derived above from (5.17) and (5.19); the incoming path action must still satisfy the exact quadratic coefficient condition in (5.28). Second-order Taylor bounds are uniform in the period because each block average reads a fixed finite patch at fixed \(j, L\).

Set \(\varepsilon_{\text{loc}} = (1 + R_{\ast}) \delta_{\text{loc}} + e^{- \tau R_{\ast}}\), where \(\tau > 0\) is a common path decay rate. Subtracting the flat identities (6.3) before estimating terms gives the required bounds

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} | \Phi^{\ast} X_f + \Phi_n^{\ast} Z - X_c | & \leqslant C \delta_{\text{loc}} \varepsilon_{\text{loc}},\\ | (I - \Phi^{\ast} \Phi - \Phi_n^{\ast} \Phi_n -\mathcal{N}^{\ast} \mathcal{N}) v_c | & \leqslant Ct_f \varepsilon_{\text{loc}} . \end{array}\)

Indeed a transport of length \(s\) differs from its flat value by at most \(Cs \delta_{\text{loc}}\); the omitted tails have size \(Ce^{- \tau R_{\ast}}\). The count (6.2) and the limits (6.1) yield (6.10). This completes the verification of Proposition 6.1. It uses the aligned flat identities for the chosen path family, not merely a decay estimate for a flat Fourier kernel.\(\Box\)

Proposition 6.2. (Energy bound under the local comparisons) Under the hypotheses of Proposition 6.1, choose \(L\) sufficiently large, then \(W\) sufficiently large, then the reference coupling sufficiently large. Uniformly in the period and consistent sector,

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{E}_A (U, V) \geqslant \kappa t_f^2 |A|, \qquad \kappa = a / 2 > 0.\)

The threshold may depend on \(j, L, W\); \(a\) is fixed by the direct and linear clip coefficients and \(b\).

Proof. The bounds (6.6) and (6.7) leave \(at_f^2\) per selected fine or mismatch test, \(at_f t_{\text{hi}}\) per test in \(D\), and enough linear excess to absorb \(Ct_f (L^{- 2} R_f + R_n)\). The choices are in that order: increase \(L\) for the fine excess, and \(W\) for the mismatch excess.

Assign each coarse large-field site to one incident plaquette with distance from the identity above \(t_c\). A plaquette has at most four such sites. Since \(t_f \leqslant 2 t_c\) eventually, the middle range of (6.8) contributes \(4 at_f^2\) per plaquette and therefore \(at_f^2\) per assigned site. The exceptional plaquettes with distance from the identity above \(\delta\) number at most \(K |D|\); their negative energies cancel the positive term in (6.9). Their missing site contributions cost at most \(4 aKt_f^2 |D|\). Also \(E_N \geqslant 0\). The total remaining losses are bounded by

\(\displaystyle (4 aK + ZB^4) t_f^2 |D| + Ct_c^4 KB^4 |A| + \frac{a}{4} t_f^2 |A| .\)

Increase the coupling threshold until \((4 aK + ZB^4) t_f^2 \leqslant at_f t_{\text{hi}}\) and \(Ct_c^4 KB^4 \leqslant at_f^2 / 4\). Equation (6.1) justifies both choices. The first loss is absorbed by the positive energy contribution retained for \(D\), leaving at least \(at_f^2 |A| / 2\).\(\Box\)

6.2The actual sector Gaussian

Fix the sector and the frozen free links \(Y\). Tree links are not integration variables. Active coordinates are logarithms of the remaining free links outside the selected \(10 B\)-neighborhood. Linearize the retained square terms at the actual replicated coarse field, including their actual block average and fixed cutoffs. Write their affine stack as

\(\displaystyle Q_Y (u) = \tfrac{1}{2} | \textsf{D} u + b_Y |^2, \qquad \textsf{A} = \textsf{D}^{\ast} \textsf{D}, \qquad f_Y = \textsf{D}^{\ast} b_Y . \) (6.12)

If hypercubic orientations are averaged, the stack uses the square roots of their weights. A row is retained when its fixed stencil meets an active edge. This rule differs from retaining rows whose anchors are a specified distance from the selected set. A comparison between the corresponding matrices requires a proof. Identity coordinates may be adjoined for inactive variables, with their trivial integrals normalized to one.

The stencil contains the block average patch and every endpoint of a retained path. Its coarse radius is at most \(R_{\ast} + 5 + r_{\text{pred}} \leqslant B / 50\) for large coupling. Thus a retained stencil lies in the small-field region when its active edge is outside the selected \(10 B\)-neighborhood. This geometric observation permits local estimates at the actual field; it does not by itself prove a lower bound on \(\textsf{A}\).

6.2.1Transferring a local tree estimate to the actual rows

The gap needed below can be obtained without identifying actual and flat matrices. The geometric comparison just proved implies a local inequality on the specified block-tree slice:

\(\displaystyle |a_e | \leqslant C_L \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_e} | (d_f a)_p | + |\mathcal{Q}a (e_c) | . \) (6.13)

Here \(\mathcal{P}_e\) is a fixed macro-patch of fine plaquettes, \(e_c\) is the coarse edge in the direction of \(e\), and \(\mathcal{Q}\) is the derivative of the actual block average at the identity. The inequality is for arrays vanishing on the chosen tree, extended by zero on frozen edges. For internal edges the last term may be omitted. Its nonlinear origin is worth specifying: join the endpoints of \(e\) to their block roots along the same tree, compare the resulting transport with the block average in a common macro-frame, and bound the discrepancy by the macro-patch plaquette sum. Differentiating this comparison at the identity gives (6.13). The route constants and endpoint bounds are those proved above for the same trees and tags. No coordinate change to an unrelated tree is made.

The tree comparison (5.26) applies to these coordinates: the replica is varied by left multiplication on free bonds, tree coordinates are zero, and the block average is the actual rooted block average of (5.24). Thus the selected direct and block average rows are exactly its derivatives, multiplied by their fixed positive square weights. Each such derivative differs, in one common gauge on its macro-patch, from its flat derivative by at most \(\varepsilon\) times the sum of input norms on its fixed stencil. This follows from continuity of the finite-patch derivative on the minimizing branch once clearance and small coarse plaquettes put that entire patch in a small common gauge. Insert the comparison in (6.13), square, and sum over active edges. Fixed stencil multiplicities give

\(\displaystyle \|a\|_2^2 \leqslant C_L \sum_p |D_p a|^2 + C_L \sum_e |D_e^{\text{pred}} a|^2 + C_L \varepsilon^2 \|a\|_2^2 .\)

All norms here are counting norms, without division by volume. The row selector introduces no error: an omitted row has a stencil disjoint from the active coordinates, hence annihilates their zero extension. For \(C_L \varepsilon^2 \leqslant 1 / 2\) and positive lower direct and penalty coefficients, this proves \(\textsf{D}^{\ast} \textsf{D} \geqslant c_L I\). The other rows add nonnegative squares. The gauge may depend on the edge at which the local inequality is applied, but it is fixed during differentiation; invariance of the norm on \(\mathfrak{g}\) permits the subsequent sum.

This provides the precise route from a local tree comparison to the actual matrix gap. It leaves no need to compare whole determinants of actual and flat matrices. The identification here is local and preserves the original forest; row selection only removes terms annihilating the zero extension. Their constants may depend on the fixed ratio \(L\), unlike the excess constant in (6.11), which is needed before \(L\) is chosen.

6.2.2Local inverse columns

For an edge coordinate \(i\), let \(E_i\) be its two projected endpoints. Two useful symmetric windows are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \Omega_i^{\text{sec}} & = \{i' : \max_{x \in E_i, y \in E_{i'}} \text{dist} (x, y) \leqslant B / 20\},\\ \Omega_i^{\text{loc}} & = \{i' : \min_{x \in E_i, y \in E_{i'}} \text{dist} (x, y) \leqslant B\} . \end{array}\)

Restrict them to the active coordinate set. Both contain a ball of radius \(B / 60\) about \(i\) for large \(B\). Use one prescription throughout a given integral and define

\(\displaystyle P_{i' i} =\boldsymbol{1}_{i' \in \Omega_i} (\textsf{A} [\Omega_i, \Omega_i]^{- 1})_{i' i}, \qquad \Gamma = \tfrac{1}{2} (P + P^{\ast}), \qquad m_Y = - P^{\ast} f_Y . \) (6.14)

The matrix \(\Gamma\) is finite range. Symmetry alone does not make it a covariance; positivity follows from the following comparison.

Proposition 6.3. (Window estimates from actual matrix bounds) Assume \(cI \leqslant \textsf{A} \leqslant CI\) and an exponentially weighted Schur bound \(\sup_i \sum_{i'} e^{\tau d (i, i')} | \textsf{A}_{ii'} | \leqslant C\), with the analogous column bound. Assume bounded coordinate multiplicity per coarse site. Then, for some \(\eta, c' > 0\), uniformly in the active set, period, and principal compression,

\(\displaystyle \|P - \textsf{A}^{- 1} \|_{\eta} + \| \Gamma - \textsf{A}^{- 1} \|_{\eta} \leqslant C' e^{- c' B} . \) (6.15)

Here \(\| \cdot \|_{\eta}\) is the maximum of the exponentially weighted row and column sums. For sufficiently large \(B\), \(c_1 I \leqslant \Gamma \leqslant C_1 I\). Fixed-order derivatives obey corresponding bounds if the actual matrix has them, with all windows and selected coordinate sets held fixed.

Proof. The weighted conjugation argument of Section 5.8 gives exponential inverse bounds for \(\textsf{A}\) and all its principal compressions. The constants are uniform because a principal compression preserves the gap and the weighted bound. The column \(Pe_i\) solves \(\textsf{A} Pe_i = e_i\) inside \(\Omega_i\). Outside that window its residual crosses a distance at least \(B / 60\) from \(i\). The convolution of two exponentially decaying kernels therefore gives \(\| \textsf{A} P - I\|_{\eta} \leqslant C' e^{- c' B}\) after decreasing the decay rate. Multiplication by \(\textsf{A}^{- 1}\) proves the first estimate in (6.15); adjunction proves the second. Operator norm is bounded by Schur norm, so comparison with \(\textsf{A}^{- 1} \geqslant C^{- 1} I\) proves positivity.

For derivatives, use \(D \textsf{A}^{- 1} = - \textsf{A}^{- 1} (D \textsf{A}) \textsf{A}^{- 1}\) and its iterates, also on the compressions. Every term in a differentiated residual still crosses the window boundary. Polynomial spatial weights are moved along a kernel using \(1 + d (o, i') \leqslant (1 + d (o, i)) (1 + d (i, i'))\) and absorbed by a smaller exponential rate. These operations leave the factor \(e^{- c' B}\) at any fixed order.\(\Box\)

6.2.3The source and derivatives on a fixed sector

The source in (6.12) uses a bounded extension of the frozen logarithms. This detail is essential. Let \(v_Y\) be the patched, smoothly clipped frozen displacement, extended by zero off the frozen coordinates, with \(|v_Y |_{\infty} \leqslant 2 d\) and \(d \leqslant rt_f\), for fixed \(r\). If \(b (V)\) is the row value at the replicated field and \(\textsf{D}_{\text{all}} (V)\) is its derivative on all free coordinates, including the frozen ones, the source is

\(\displaystyle b_Y = b (V) + \textsf{D}_{\text{all}} (V) v_Y, \qquad f_Y = \textsf{D}^{\ast} b_Y . \) (6.16)

The row weights are included in both terms. The original integrand is preserved by putting the difference from this affine square into the local correction factors. No estimate below replaces an arbitrary frozen Haar logarithm by a small logarithm.

Here is why the source is uniformly small. A retained row has an active coordinate outside the \(10 B\) neighborhood of the selected set. Its whole stencil has radius at most \(B / 50\); hence its anchor, endpoints, and fixed macro-patches have more than \(9 B\) clearance for large \(B\). All coarse tests used by the row are therefore small. In the common local gauge, the four-link curvature formula, the absolute path-coefficient bounds, and the smooth block average branch give

\(\displaystyle |b (V) |_{\infty} \leqslant C_b t_f, \qquad \| \textsf{D}_{\text{all}} (V)\|_{\infty \to \infty} \leqslant C_a .\)

The comparison term is extended without its large-field indicator when the field is varied. Thus (6.16) implies \(|b_Y |_{\infty} \leqslant (C_b + 2 rC_a) t_f\), uniformly over the frozen Haar variables. The column bound for \(\textsf{D}\) now gives

\(\displaystyle |f_Y |_{\infty} + |m_Y |_{\infty} \leqslant Ct_f, \qquad | \textsf{A} m_Y + f_Y |_{\infty} \leqslant Ct_f e^{- cB} . \) (6.17)

The second inequality uses \(\textsf{A} m_Y + f_Y = (I - \textsf{A} P^{\ast}) f_Y\) and the window estimate. In particular \(g^{- 1} (\textsf{A} m_Y + f_Y)\) remains exponentially small for \(g = \beta^{- 1 / 2}\) on the coupling band: \(g^{- 1} t_f = M_f^{\text{cut}}\) up to fixed constants grows slower than \(e^{\epsilon B}\).

For clarity, the corresponding derivative assertion has a different scale. For any fixed allowed order \(k\), use dimensionless variation parameters whose coarse link increments and clipped frozen displacements have derivatives bounded by \(C_k t_f\). Keep the selector, forest, and windows fixed. Differentiating a path word costs a fixed power of its length. The exponential path bound absorbs that power after decreasing its decay rate. A derivative with anchor \(o\) can occur only on a word whose dependence region contains \(o\); its distance to the row is bounded by the word length plus the fixed block average radius. Consequently the same argument bounds the derivatives of \(\textsf{D}\) in exponentially weighted row and column norms, uniformly in volume. Products give the bounds for \(\textsf{A} = \textsf{D}^{\ast} \textsf{D}\). All fixed positive-order derivatives of \(b_Y, f_Y, m_Y\) are \(O (t_f)\) in these normalized directions, and the residual derivatives retain the factor \(e^{- c_k B}\). Unscaled derivatives instead carry the corresponding powers of \(t_f^{- 1}\).

These derivatives exist on a common neighborhood, not just on the set satisfying the large-field indicator inequalities. Indeed the row formulas with the event indicator omitted extend smoothly there. If their first derivative norm is at most \(C\), a ball of radius \(c_L / [2 (1 + C)]\) preserves the actual gap \(c_L / 2\). Every principal compression remains invertible on that ball. Inverse differentiation and the proof of Proposition 6.3 therefore apply with fixed selectors even if nearby fields change their large-field indicators. The radius need not be uniform in arbitrary unnormalized coordinates.

6.2.4The exact integral and its normalization

Let \(n\) be the real dimension of the active coordinate space. For any \(m \in \mathbb{R}^n\), positive covariance \(\Gamma\), and integrable factor \(F\), the ordinary Gaussian change of variables \(u = m + gz\) gives

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} e^{- \beta Q_Y (u)} F (u) \hspace{0.17em} du & =\mathcal{C}_Y \int F (m + gz) \exp \{\eta \cdot z + \tfrac{1}{2} z^{\ast} (\Gamma^{- 1} - \textsf{A}) z\} \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_{\Gamma} (z),\\ \mathcal{C}_Y & = (2 \pi g^2)^{n / 2} (\det \Gamma)^{1 / 2} e^{- \beta Q_Y (m)},\\ \eta & = - g^{- 1} (\textsf{A} m + f_Y) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(6.18)} \end{array}\)

This is the usual finite-dimensional completion of squares with an arbitrary reference covariance. We use it as an identity, not an approximation. In particular \(m = m_Y\) and (6.14) do not remove the linear or quadratic residual in the exponent.

In a sector integral \(F\) includes the chart indicators, the Haar density of every active link, the branch masses, incoming activities, and all remaining action terms. It is extended by zero off its original chart. The frozen variables are subsequently integrated with probability Haar measure \(dY\). When \(\Gamma\) depends on \(Y\), the resulting reference law is

\(\displaystyle dY \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_{\Gamma (Y)} (z) .\)

It is a conditional Gaussian law, generally not a product of an independent Haar field and a fixed Gaussian. The positive scalar \(\mathcal{C}_Y\) must be retained with the Haar Jacobians and relative determinants. A change of tree coordinates preserves this combined integral, not its determinant factor in isolation. Zero-dimensional active sectors have determinant one and no Gaussian variables.

6.3Differentiation on a common dependence region

The cutoffs and large-field indicators in the sector definition are fixed Borel tests. Their indicators are not differentiated. Even on a fixed sector an incoming large-field activity can be merely measurable. Differentiating it as a function of its fine links would be unjustified. A local change of variables instead keeps those physical fine links fixed.

Write an active physical link as \(U_e = \exp (u_e) (\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} V)_e\). At a base field \(V_0\), set \(u = m_Y (V_0) + gz\). Within a protected logarithmic chart define

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} u_V (e) & = \log \hspace{-0.17em} \left( \exp (u_e) (\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} V_0)_e (\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} V)_e^{- 1} \right),\\ z_V & = g^{- 1} (u_V - m_Y (V)) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(6.19)} \end{array}\)

Then \(\exp (u_V (e)) (\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} V)_e = U_e\) exactly. The following extension makes this a legitimate change on the whole Gaussian space.

Choose a fixed logarithm radius \(d_{\ast} > 0\) and a smooth radial function \(\chi\) equal to one on \(|u| \leqslant d_{\ast}\) and zero on \(|u| \geqslant 2 d_{\ast}\). For a group element \(M\) sufficiently near the identity put

\(\displaystyle F_M (u) = u + \psi (M, u), \qquad \psi (M, u) = \chi (u) \{\log (\exp (u) M) - u\} .\)

The logarithm is used only on the support of \(\chi\). At \(M = 1\), \(\psi = 0\) and \(D_u \psi = 0\). Compactness of that support permits one common neighborhood of \(1\) on which \(| \psi | < d_{\ast} / 2\) and \(\|D_u \psi \| < 1 / 2\). For every target \(v\), the map \(u \mapsto v - \psi (M, u)\) is a contraction of the complete Euclidean space, so \(F_M\) is bijective. Its derivative is invertible, and the inverse function theorem gives a smooth inverse. Its determinant is positive by continuity from \(M = 1\). All fixed parameter derivatives of \(\psi\) and of this determinant are bounded uniformly in \(u\), since they vanish outside a fixed compact set. These are the usual finite-dimensional contraction and inverse-function arguments.

Apply this map separately on each active bond with \(M_e (V) = (\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} V_0)_e (\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} V)_e^{- 1}\), and set

\(\displaystyle T_V z = g^{- 1} \{F_{M (V)} (m_Y (V_0) + gz) - m_Y (V)\} .\)

The without the event indicator extension of \(m_Y\) described above is used off the valid sector. The map is globally smooth and invertible, and its Jacobian is the product of the positive bond Jacobians; the factors \(g\) cancel. If a protected factor is supported where \(|u_V (e) | \leqslant 8 a_{\ast} t_f\) and \(8 a_{\ast} t_f \leqslant d_{\ast} / 2\), then \(|u_e | < d_{\ast}\) by the displacement bound. There \(F_M\) equals the logarithmic map and holds the physical link fixed. Nested cutoffs, with the smaller support inside \(|u_V | \leqslant 4 a_{\ast} t_f\), leave a strict margin. Thus a protected measurable factor is constant on a neighborhood of the base parameter whenever it contributes; it does not have to be differentiated. Cutoff remainders must likewise vanish on a neighborhood, rather than only at the base point.

Use one coordinate change on the union \(I\) of all coordinates read by the product under consideration. If \(\Gamma_I\) is its marginal covariance, the density ratio contributed by that change is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{R}_I (V, z) = | \det D_z z_V | \hspace{0.17em} \frac{(\det \Gamma_I (V))^{- 1 / 2} e^{- z_V^{\ast} \Gamma_I (V)^{- 1} z_V / 2}}{(\det \Gamma_I (V_0))^{- 1 / 2} e^{- z^{\ast} \Gamma_I (V_0)^{- 1} z / 2}} . \) (6.20)

The marginal is the principal covariance block \(\Gamma_I\), whose inverse is generally not a principal block of \(\Gamma^{- 1}\). Applying separate changes to overlapping factors would not reproduce this common ratio.

The familiar derivative formulas

\(\displaystyle D \log \det \Gamma_I = \text{tr} (\Gamma_I^{- 1} D \Gamma_I), \qquad D \Gamma_I^{- 1} = - \Gamma_I^{- 1} (D \Gamma_I) \Gamma_I^{- 1}\)

show that the logarithmic derivative of the Gaussian part is

\(\displaystyle - \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (\Gamma_I^{- 1} \dot{\Gamma}_I) + \tfrac{1}{2} z^{\ast} \Gamma_I^{- 1} \dot{\Gamma}_I \Gamma_I^{- 1} z - z^{\ast} \Gamma_I^{- 1} \dot{z} .\)

Higher fixed derivatives are polynomials in the coordinates, with coefficients controlled by the window estimates and the coordinate change. Ordinary Gaussian moment formulas, in particular Isserlis' formula [Iss18], then give polynomial bounds in \(|I|\) for fixed derivative order. The trace costs a factor proportional to \(|I|\); it is not uniformly bounded independently of the dependence region. For local factors with \(s\) anchors one typically has \(|I| \leqslant CB^4 s\).

A factor's dependence region includes every cutoff test, block average path, window matrix entry and frozen coordinate needed to evaluate it. This region is different from the exclusion support \(Y_i\) in Definition 4.5. Disjoint exclusion supports imply no independence. Even disjoint coordinate sets give conditional Gaussian independence only when their cross covariance vanishes. Integration over shared frozen variables can destroy that independence after conditioning is removed.

6.3.1Differentiation under the common Gaussian law

There is a useful distinction between the bounds used to justify differentiation and those used to estimate the resulting derivatives. The first may depend on the fixed finite volume. Since \(\psi\) has compact support and the frozen logarithms are clipped, \(T_V z - z\) and its fixed parameter derivatives are bounded uniformly in \(z\) and in the frozen variables on one neighborhood. If \(\Gamma_I (V_0)^{- 1} \geqslant \lambda I\), shrink that neighborhood so \(\Gamma_I (V)^{- 1} \geqslant \lambda I / 2\). The Gaussian density ratio and its first \(k\) derivatives are then bounded by

\(\displaystyle C \hspace{0.17em} p_{\Gamma_I (V_0)} (z)^{- 1} e^{- \lambda |z|^2 / 8} (1 + |z|)^{2 k},\)

with a finite constant \(C\). Multiplication by the reference Gaussian density makes this integrable. The Jacobian has bounded derivatives. For the local correction factors, the endpoint cutoffs ensure bounded arguments whenever the factor is nonzero. Hence their derivatives are also bounded on that neighborhood. Dominated differentiation follows. For a quadratic correction this last assertion requires both endpoints of every nonzero matrix term to carry the protecting cutoff; it is not a consequence of a cutoff at only one endpoint.

At the base point \(T_{V_0} z = z\) and the ratio is one. Its differentiated logarithm is a polynomial of degree at most two in \(z\) and the derivatives of \(T_V z\), and repeated exponential differentiation gives a fixed-degree polynomial bound. Matrix products are estimated in row and column norms; a trace costs \(|I|\). Therefore, if \(s\) counts the factor anchors and \(|I| \leqslant CB^4 s\), there are fixed integers \(r_k, m_k\) such that

\(\displaystyle |D^j \mathcal{R}_I (V_0, z) | \leqslant A_k (\hat{\beta}_f) (1 + s)^{m_k} (1 + |I| + \sum_{i \in I} |z_i |)^{r_k}, \quad j \leqslant k, \) (6.21)

where \(A_k\) is subpower, meaning \(A_k (x) \leqslant C_{\epsilon} x^{\epsilon}\) eventually for each \(\epsilon > 0\). In this statement the normalized variation derivatives of \(g^{- 1} m_Y\) and of \(g^{- 1} \psi\) must have subpower bounds; these follow from (6.17) and \(t_f / g \asymp M_f^{\text{cut}}\). The relevant marginal inverses have uniform operator bounds; when row bounds are used, a polynomial factor in \(|I|\) suffices and is already allowed in (6.21). No assertion about independence of the coordinates is involved.

6.4Simultaneous tail estimates

A cutoff remainder is bounded but need not be pointwise small. Its smallness comes from a large-coordinate event. An event label specifies one particular cutoff and its anchor; repeated appearances of that event label must be counted once. Suppose each remainder with event label \(w\) vanishes unless

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{E}_w = \{\exists i : d (i, w) \leqslant 2 B, |z_i | \geqslant T\}, \qquad T = \xi t_f / (2 g), \) (6.22)

for fixed \(\xi > 0\). Verifying this implication uses the actual mean, block average response, and event indicators. A covariance estimate by itself cannot prove it.

Lemma 6.4. (Joint tails with bounded local multiplicity) Let \(z\) be centered Gaussian with covariance \(C \leqslant MI\). Suppose an event label has at most \(N\) possible witness coordinates, and each coordinate can witness at most \(Q\) distinct event labels. For \(r\) distinct event labels, if \(T \geqslant M / 2 + HQ\) with \(H > 0\), then

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{P} \left( \bigcap_{w = 1}^r \mathcal{E}_w \right) \leqslant (2 Ne^{- H})^r .\)

No independence between the events is assumed.

Proof. This is the exponential Markov bound for a Gaussian, with the local multiplicities kept explicit. For any distinct coordinate set \(J\) and signs \(\sigma_i\), the variance of \(\sum_{i \in J} \sigma_i z_i\) is at most \(M |J|\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{P} \{|z_i | \geqslant T (i \in J)\} \leqslant 2^{|J|} \exp \{(M / 2 - T) |J|\} .\)

6.5Weighted bounds for the primitive local factors

We now retain the individual correction labels, including repeated paths, instead of estimating only their sum as an operator. Fix a derivative order \(k\), a support exponent \(\alpha > 0\), and a halo multiplier \(h\) large enough to contain every window and protected variation used above. For a support \(S\), write \(c_B (S)\) for its connected covering number by \(B\)-balls. A family of nonnegative amplitudes \(a_{\lambda}\) has containing-site bound \(\epsilon\) if

\(\displaystyle \sup_x \sum_{\lambda : x \in S_{\lambda}} e^{\alpha c_B (S_{\lambda})} a_{\lambda} \leqslant \epsilon .\)

6.5.1Gaussian correction series

Put \(D = \textsf{A} \Gamma - I\). The window estimates give exponentially small weighted entry and fixed derivative envelopes for \(D\). For the interpolating precision \(\textsf{A}_s = I + s (\textsf{A} - I)\), \(0 \leqslant s \leqslant 1\), the same argument applies uniformly: its gap is at least \(\min (1, c_L)\) and its weighted bounds are uniform. Let \(P_s\) be its local inverse columns and \(E_s = \textsf{A}_s P_s - I\). The exact inverse and trace expansions are

\(\displaystyle \textsf{A}_s^{- 1} = P_s \sum_{n \geqslant 0} (- E_s)^n, \qquad \Gamma^{- 1} - \textsf{A} = \sum_{n \geqslant 0} (- D)^{n + 1} \textsf{A} .\)

All series converge in weighted matrix norm. Integrating \(\text{tr} ((\textsf{A} - I) \textsf{A}_s^{- 1})\) gives \(\log \det \textsf{A}\). The \(n = 0\) term is its local diagonal normalization; the remaining words are \((\textsf{A} - I) P_s (- E_s)^{n + 1}\). The second determinant series comes from \(\log \det (I + D) = \log \det \textsf{A} + \log \det \Gamma\). This determinant identity is legitimate even if \(D\) is not symmetric: \(I + D = \textsf{A} \Gamma\) is similar to a positive matrix, and the convergent real logarithm series agrees with its positive determinant.

Each word is indexed by its ordered sequence of bonds, with the intermediate Lie algebra indices contracted in each matrix product. For each fixed derivative order \(k\), the corresponding derivative of the amplitude is bounded by

\(\displaystyle C_k (B) e^{- cB} \{C_k (B) e^{- cB} \}^n,\)

with possibly a smaller positive \(c\). The endpoint cutoffs in a quadratic word bound each Gaussian endpoint and its normalized derivatives by a subpower function. A linear residual has one such endpoint. Inactive endpoints give a zero germ because both precision and covariance have identity inactive blocks. Thus all these estimates hold for the protected factors on the entire sample space.

For completeness, the sum over word supports is also small. Consecutive bonds have distance at most \(3 B + 2\). There are at most \(K = C_L (1 + B)^4\) possible next bonds. A word of length \(n + u\), for fixed \(u\), has support cover at most \((n + u + 1) s_h\), where \(s_h\) depends only on the halo multiplier. If that support contains a prescribed site, its starting bond has at most \(C_L (1 + B)^4 (n + u + 1)^4\) possible locations. Fixed derivatives cost an additional polynomial in \(n + u\). Absorb these polynomials by \(C_k 2^{(k + 4) n}\). The containing-site sum is consequently bounded by

\(\displaystyle C_k (B) e^{- cB} \sum_{n \geqslant 0} \{C_k (B) e^{- cB} 2^{k + 4} e^{\alpha s_h} K\}^n .\)

Its ratio is at most \(1 / 2\) eventually. The sum is smaller than \(x^{- A}\) for every prescribed \(A > 0\). This proves smallness of the individual determinant and inverse-correction labels with their full supports, including repeated bonds, rather than merely a small operator norm.

at large coupling. This is the needed bound on the extracted energy. Dropping \(X_H\) before proving the annular estimate would be invalid.

The remaining local normalization, Haar, tag, and frozen-coordinate costs are bounded by \(CB^a (1 + \log x)^a n = o (\beta t_f^2) n\). The component amplitude consequently satisfies

\(\displaystyle a_{\text{comp}} \leqslant \exp \{- \kappa' \beta t_f^2 n\}\)

for a fixed positive \(\kappa'\) smaller than the retained energy coefficient. Fixed derivatives of its smooth extension on a fully small-field coarse neighborhood add polynomial costs per component and are absorbed by a further decrease of \(\kappa'\). The physical Borel indicator is held fixed. This assertion is where the retained cutoff factors are nonzero, under the precise core/annulus allocation just specified; where a compulsory cutoff factor vanishes the full component factor is zero. Connected selections with prescribed root have at most \(\{C (1 + B)^a \}^n\) possibilities: list them by a rooted spanning tree, using their finite-range adjacency. This entropy and \(e^{\alpha c_B (S)} \leqslant e^{C \alpha n}\) are absorbed by the same remaining positive energy bound. A component's physical Borel indicator is held fixed; the derivative assertion concerns its smooth extension on the stated small-field coarse neighborhood.

Finally replace each small logarithm \(f_{\lambda}\) by \(e^{f_{\lambda}} - 1\). For \(|f_{\lambda} | \leqslant 1\), the exponential partition formula preserves its amplitude up to a constant depending on \(k\). The previous bounds furnish the pointwise product jets (6.25); derivative costs polynomial in the total support size are absorbed by a small further decrease of the support exponent. Warnings carry the joint-event factors proved above. Equation (6.26) then gives a product majorant under the one actual conditional Gaussian, followed by the frozen Haar probability integral. This is the local integration estimate required before the connected expansion. Incoming large-field activities retain their given amplitudes and participate in this same product bound; they are not claimed to become pointwise small by Gaussian integration.

6.5.4Comparison with the linear local integral

The estimates also give the small parameter needed to compare a local nonlinear integral with its linear Gaussian counterpart. Fix the same forest, coordinate set, windows, and clean coarse field throughout the comparison. Write \(\Gamma_{\text{lin}}\) for the window covariance formed from the corresponding flat rows, and \(v\) for the tree-straightened linear relative response on the common clean neighborhood. Taylor expansion of the actual rows and the principal resolvent identity give, on those windows,

\(\displaystyle \| \Gamma - \Gamma_{\text{lin}} \| \leqslant CB^a t_f, \qquad |m_Y - v|_{\infty} \leqslant CB^a t_f^2 + CB^a t_f e^{- cB} .\)

The second estimate is the local mean transfer proved above; the first is valid in weighted row norms, or with a harmless local polynomial factor in an unweighted matrix norm. Both have fixed-order versions under the same available path derivative bounds. In particular the mean shift in Gaussian units costs

\(\displaystyle g^{- 1} |m_Y - v|_{\infty} \leqslant CB^a g (M_f^{\text{cut}})^2 + CB^a M_f^{\text{cut}} e^{- cB} .\)

Thus it is of size \(x^{- 1 / 2 + o (1)}\), rather than merely bounded.

The physical coordinate comparison uses the same tree chart. Its nonlinear map and its first-order Taylor map differ by

\(\displaystyle CB^a (t_f + g|z|)^2 \leqslant C' B^a (t_f^2 + g^2 |z|^2)\)

on their protected common chart. Dividing by the fine small-field radius gives \(CB^a (t_f + (g^2 / t_f) |z|^2)\). All its fixed Gaussian moments are \(x^{- 1 / 2 + o (1)}\) on a region with polynomial-in-\(B\) dimension. This is a Taylor estimate with polynomial moment control, not a uniform bound on unbounded samples; the chart-complement events are controlled separately.

For an interpolation of the positive covariances and of the centered means, differentiation of its common Gaussian density ratio therefore has local polynomial moments bounded by \(x^{- 1 / 2 + o (1)}\), with the appropriate polynomial factor in the dependence-region size. Indeed the logarithmic derivative is the trace, quadratic, and linear score displayed after (6.20); each coefficient contains one of the covariance or normalized mean differences just estimated. Integrating this derivative compares the two laws. For an inherited activity with only \(k\) available derivatives, this comparison must be implemented by holding its physical fine links fixed with the protected change of variables. Differentiate the common density, Jacobian, smooth cutoff factors, and at most the available \(k\) activity derivatives. Taylor expansion of \(D^k E (U_{\text{nonlinear}}) - D^k E (U_{\text{linear}})\) would require an unavailable \((k + 1)\)st derivative and is not justified here. The physical-map estimate above supplies bounds on the compensating map and its density scores; it is not permission to lose an extra derivative of the incoming activity. This export concerns a local region whose entire window neighborhoods are clean. It does not compare a seeded sector to an unseeded law, does not replace the tree response by a different gauge response, and does not identify separately chosen event indicators. Such replacements need their own tail or support decomposition.

6.6What must be checked for a concrete step

The local integral estimate now follows from the actual gap, clipped source, cancellation of the aligned comparison terms, strict margins in the event inequalities, and the primitive bounds above. Its hypotheses are the adjacent pair's compatible full quadratic identities, actual rooted block average, exponential path bounds through the requested derivative order, and the incoming activity bounds with a strictly larger support exponent than the one used in the conclusion. These are incoming data, not estimates that can be created by selecting a scale schedule. All matrices, comparison terms, forests, tags, and frozen sources in the argument belong to that same pair.

The chapter controls local amplitudes and their integrals. Reconstruction of the exact block density additionally requires assigning every nonlinear row, determinant contribution, Haar factor, and event indicator exactly once. In particular the two near sets for local determinant terms and Haar terms need not coincide, and the flat determinant scalar is extracted only once for the whole forest. Those identities, and the subsequent connected regrouping, are distinct from the analytic bounds proved here.
