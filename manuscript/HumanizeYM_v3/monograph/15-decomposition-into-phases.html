Chapter 15
Decomposition into phases

A translation-invariant correlation family can be a mixture of states with different long-distance behavior. We separate this mixture using operators on the zero-energy subspace of its reflection Hilbert space. A phase will be a character of a commutative operator algebra, not a set of gauge configurations. Its correlations are obtained by evaluating compressed whole words.

The inputs are the common field spaces \(F_d\) and separated correlation family of Chapter 13, bounded slab operators with their adjoint and concatenation laws, and orthogonal covariance of that same family. The latter is stronger than the original lattice hypercubic covariance. We also require the bounded real lattice approximations and corrected moment estimates for separating families specified below. The assertions retain these analytic hypotheses; a decomposition theorem for an abstract state alone does not establish them.

15.1The zero-energy subspace

Use time coordinate four. Write \(\mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}\) for the mixed reflection Hilbert space, \(\Omega\) for its unit vacuum, \(E (t) = e^{- tH}\) for the heat semigroup, and \(U (y)\) for spatial translations. Let \(P_0\) be the projection onto \(\mathcal{H}_0 = \ker H\). The spectral theorem gives \(E (t) \to P_0\) strongly as \(t \to \infty\).

Every spatial translation acts trivially on \(\mathcal{H}_0\). Here the orthogonal covariance input is essential. For two separated clusters put \(F_{v, w} (x) = S (v \hspace{0.17em} \tau_x w)\). At sufficiently large time separation this is a matrix element of \(U (\vec{x}) E (x^4 - c)\) between fixed vectors. Hence a fixed temporal displacement changes it by a quantity tending to zero, uniformly in arbitrary spatial displacements. If \(b^4 > 0\), choose an orthogonal map \(K\) with \(Kb = |b| e_4\). Then \((Ke_4)^4 = b^4 / |b| > 0\). Rotate the clusters and apply the temporal statement along \(tKe_4\). This proves \(F_{v, w} (te_4 + b) - F_{v, w} (te_4) \to 0\). For spatial \(y\) use \(b = y + e_4\) and remove the extra unit time shift. Taking strong heat limits and using the density of word vectors gives

\(\displaystyle U (y) P_0 = P_0 . \) (15.1)

This conclusion concerns the ground space of the mixture; it does not say that this space is one dimensional.

15.2Compression and separation of clusters

For a word \(w\) placed inside a slab of width \(h\), let \(B_h (w)\) be its bounded insertion operator and define

\(\displaystyle M (w) = P_0 B_h (w) |_{\mathcal{H}_0} .\)

Increasing the width appends a heat operator, and shifting the word in time inserts one on the other side. Both disappear after compression. Spatial placement disappears by (15.1). Thus \(M (w)\) is defined for any distinct-point word independently of its slab placement, with

\(\displaystyle M (\varnothing) = 1, \qquad M (w) \Omega = P_0 [w], \qquad S (w) = \langle \Omega, M (w) \Omega \rangle .\)

For positive-time words also put \(Y_w = B_h (w) |_{\mathcal{H}_0}\), which maps into the full reflection Hilbert space. The slab adjoint and concatenation laws give

\(\displaystyle M (\theta v \hspace{0.17em} w) = Y_v^{\ast} Y_w, \qquad M (\theta v) = M (v)^{\ast} . \) (15.2)

If successive time gaps between translated words tend to infinity, their correlation tends to the corresponding ordered product of compressed operators:

\(\displaystyle \lim_{j \to \infty} S (\tau_{z_1 (j)} w_1 \cdots \tau_{z_k (j)} w_k) = \langle \Omega, M (w_1) \cdots M (w_k) \Omega \rangle, \quad (z_{i + 1} (j) - z_i (j))^4 \to \infty . \) (15.3)

The spatial parts of the displacements can be arbitrary. Indeed, if \(\psi_j \to \psi\) and \(t_j \to \infty\), then \(U (y_j) E (t_j) \psi_j \to P_0 \psi\) by contraction, strong heat convergence and (15.1). Iterating this fact through the bounded slab operators proves (15.3).

Proposition 15.1. (The compression algebra) The norm-closed unital star algebra \(\mathfrak{A}\) generated by all \(M (w)\) on \(\mathcal{H}_0\) is commutative and separable. The vector \(\Omega\) is cyclic and separating for it.

Proof. We give the particular geometric commutativity argument. Place four words at

\(\displaystyle 0, \quad j (e_4 + 2 e_1), \quad j (2 e_4 + e_1), \quad 3 j (e_4 + e_1) .\)

Exchanging coordinates one and four interchanges the time order of the middle clusters. Covariance and (15.3) therefore exchange the two middle operators, initially with that orthogonal map applied to all four words. Apply the same cluster limit to four words placed on the ray \(j (e_4 + e_1)\), which the exchange fixes. This removes the common transformation of the labels and internal positions. Thus

\(\displaystyle \langle \Omega, M (w_1) [M (w_2), M (w_3)] M (w_4) \Omega \rangle = 0.\)

The vectors \(M (v) \Omega = P_0 [v]\) span a dense subspace of \(\mathcal{H}_0\); reflected words give the adjoints in the left slot. The bounded commutator is therefore zero. Density proves cyclicity. If \(A \Omega = 0\) for \(A \in \mathfrak{A}\), commutativity gives \(AB \Omega = 0\) for all \(B \in \mathfrak{A}\), hence \(A = 0\). Norm continuity of slab operators, finite-dimensional label spaces, and countable dense position sets give a countable generating family, hence separability.\(\Box\)

Compression is not multiplication of finite-separation words. In fact

\(\displaystyle M (\theta v \hspace{0.17em} w) - M (v)^{\ast} M (w) = Y_v^{\ast} (1 - P_0) Y_w . \) (15.4)

The right side usually survives at finite separation. It is the whole-word identity (15.2), rather than a product of scalar phase expectations, that will give phase reflection positivity.

15.3Characters and the representing measure

Let \(\Xi\) be the space of unital star characters of \(\mathfrak{A}\). The commutative Gelfand representation identifies \(\mathfrak{A} \simeq C (\Xi)\) isometrically; see the original [GN43, Section 2, Lemma 1]. The compact space \(\Xi\) is metrizable because \(\mathfrak{A}\) is separable. The positive normalized vacuum functional has a unique representing probability measure \(\nu\) by the Riesz theorem; for this application see [Lan98, Theorems 2.4.1 and 2.8.2]. It has full support. Otherwise a nonzero continuous function supported in a nonempty open null set would correspond to an \(A \ne 0\) with \(\|A \Omega \|^2 = \int | \xi (A) |^2 \hspace{0.17em} d \nu = 0\), contradicting separation.

Define

\(\displaystyle S^{\xi} (w) = \xi (M (w)), \qquad S (w) = \int_{\Xi} S^{\xi} (w) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\xi) . \) (15.5)

Every fixed separated value is continuous in \(\xi\). This is a representation of the mixed correlations by characters of the ground algebra. Vacuum uniqueness in these phases will be proved separately.

We shall repeatedly use the following consequence of full support. If \(A \in \mathfrak{A}\) and \(\langle \Omega, (A^{\ast} A)^j \Omega \rangle \leqslant C^{2 j}\) for all \(j\), then

\(\displaystyle \|A\| = \sup_{\xi \in \Xi} | \xi (A) | \leqslant C. \) (15.6)

This is the usual limit of \(L^{2 j}\) norms on a probability space, combined with full support for a continuous function. In applications, the moment bound comes by taking widely separated copies of the same lattice expression, applying the chessboard bound at fixed copy number, and then taking the ordered limits and the cluster limit. The order of these operations matters.

15.4Symmetries in each phase

Symmetry of a mixture need not imply symmetry of every component. Here an additional lattice variance estimate gives the required operator identity. Write \(\vartheta\) for linear time reflection on positions and labels, without conjugation. For a generic real grid word \(w\) (distinct projections in each coordinate), set \(Z = M (w) - M (\vartheta w)\). The adjoint law gives \(Z^{\ast} = - Z\), so

\(\displaystyle a := \langle \Omega, Z^2 \Omega \rangle = - \|Z \Omega \|^2 \leqslant 0.\)

Corollary 13.6, using the complete residual estimate of Lemma 13.4, gives the following bounded real approximation. For widely translated lattice copies \(f_i\) of \(w - \vartheta w\), and every \(\varepsilon > 0\), there are real \(g_i\) with \(|g_i | \leqslant R_{\varepsilon}\), uniformly in cutoff, volume and copy position, such that

\(\displaystyle | \langle g_i g_j \rangle - \text{Re} \langle f_i f_j \rangle | \leqslant \varepsilon \quad (i < j)\)

on the ordered cutoff tails at fixed translation and fixed pair. There is no corresponding assumed bound on \(f_i^2\). Variance positivity for \(q + 1\) copies gives

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \left\langle \left( \sum_{i = 0}^q g_i \right)^2 \right\rangle \leqslant (q + 1) R_{\varepsilon}^2 + 2 \sum_{i < j} (\text{Re} \langle f_i f_j \rangle + \varepsilon) .\)

First take the ordered cutoff limits at fixed \(q\) and translations, then send their separation to infinity. Equation (15.3) gives \(0 \leqslant (q + 1) [R_{\varepsilon}^2 + q (a + \varepsilon)]\). Send \(q \to \infty\) and then \(\varepsilon \downarrow 0\). Thus \(a = 0\), and separation of \(\Omega\) gives \(Z = 0\). The cofinal grids are chosen after the degree and lower-level request. Generic configurations are dense in the full separated configuration space. Norm continuity and multilinearity extend the identity to every word.

Orthogonal covariance of the mixture induces simultaneous invariance of compressed product moments whenever a cluster-separating ray has positive time before and after rotation. Such a ray exists unless time is reversed; compose with \(\vartheta\) in that case. On the dense vectors \(M (w) \Omega\) this gives a unitary orthogonal action, under which time reflection now acts as the identity. Its conjugates, all hyperplane reflections, therefore act trivially as well. Since these generate \(O (4)\),

\(\displaystyle M (Kw) = M (w) \qquad (K \in O (4)) . \) (15.7)

Character evaluation proves symmetry in every phase. The proved lattice approximation estimate is indispensable to this conclusion.

15.5Positivity, field identities and null spaces

For positive words \(w_i\), (15.2) gives the positive operator

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i, j} \overline{c_i} c_j M (\theta w_i \hspace{0.17em} w_j) = \left( \sum_i c_i Y_{w_i} \right)^{\ast} \left( \sum_j c_j Y_{w_j} \right) \geqslant 0.\)

It belongs to \(\mathfrak{A}\). Positivity agrees with ambient operator positivity in a unital C\(\ast\)-subalgebra, and characters are positive; see [Lan98, Section 2.6]. Hence each phase is reflection positive, with a Gram form that need not have rank one. Norm-smooth slab dependence gives smooth phase correlations and their uniform separated bounds. Field inclusion, derivative and stress identities pass to phases when written as operator identities, tested against all exterior words before character evaluation. For a stress shell the weights avoid collisions, so the norm-continuous operator integral is defined. The expectation identity alone would not suffice.

A mixed null field has zero slab operator, by testing against the dense word vectors. It is therefore null in every phase: \(N_d (S) \subset N_d (S^{\xi})\). The reverse inclusion uses the quantitative separating families from the same lattice family. Apply Proposition 11.10 with two marks per comparison cell. At a fixed sufficiently deep comparison level \(k\), its corrected coefficients for one and two insertions have chessboard seminorm at most \(B_k = H_k^{- 1 / 32}\), uniformly in cutoff and period. Choose a simultaneous coefficient limit for the finitely many bounded lifts of the separating family at this fixed \(k\), on a refinement of the already selected correlation limits. For every fixed integer \(j\), put \(2 j\) copies of a corrected word and its adjoint at widely separated translations. The chessboard bound is \(B_k^{2 j}\): the two-mark estimate is used with arbitrary replica census, not rerun with arity \(2 j\). Its constant is chosen before \(j\); only the cutoff and translation thresholds depend on that fixed number. The repeated-word identity and the cluster limit (15.3) identify the limiting product with \(\langle \Omega, (\Delta^{\ast} \Delta)^j \Omega \rangle\). Taking successive deeper levels \(k = k_{\ell}\) therefore supplies compressed errors for one and two insertions \(\Delta\) with

\(\displaystyle \langle \Omega, (\Delta^{\ast} \Delta)^j \Omega \rangle \leqslant \gamma_{\ell}^{2 j} \quad (j \geqslant 1), \qquad \gamma_{\ell} \longrightarrow 0. \) (15.8)

Here one may take \(\gamma_{\ell} = B_{k_{\ell}}\), enlarging it by a fixed finite row constant if necessary. The free row moments are bounded, so \(\gamma_{\ell} \to 0\). These are estimates uniform in the number of reflected copies, stronger than a bound on the mixed mean. The finite reference rows use fixed free labels and convergent noncollision configurations; their one-point means are uniformly bounded. To make the pair correction explicit, if the reference one-point means are \(s, s'\) and the reference connected pair is \(c\), use

\(\displaystyle \Delta_1 = M (u) - s 1, \quad \Delta'_1 = M (v) - s' 1, \quad \Delta_2 = M (uv) - sM (v) - s' M (u) + (ss' - c) 1.\)

Equation (15.6) bounds each error by \(\gamma_{\ell}\) in every character. Therefore the phase pair differs from \(ss' + c\) by at most \(\gamma_{\ell} (1 + |s| + |s' |)\). The phase matrices of correlation pairings converge uniformly in \(\xi\) to the injective free matrix of correlation pairings. Since they factor through \(V_d / N_d (S^{\xi})\), its dimension is at least \(\dim \mathcal{M}_d^{\text{Max}}\). The inclusion of null spaces gives the opposite bound through Proposition 13.1. Consequently

\(\displaystyle N_d (S^{\xi}) = N_d (S) \quad \text{for every } \xi . \) (15.9)

All phases have the same field filtration. Neither abstract character decomposition nor positivity alone proves this equality.

15.6Selecting a phase with a unique ground vector

Construct the reflection Hilbert space and heat semigroup of each phase using its separated hierarchy. Let \(P_{0, \xi}\) denote its ground projection. For a positive word \(w\), the nonnegative heat matrix elements decrease to \(\|P_{0, \xi} [w]_{\xi} \|^2\) and are dominated by the continuous zero-time Gram value. Dominated convergence and (15.5) yield

\(\displaystyle \int \|P_{0, \xi} [w]_{\xi} \|^2 \hspace{0.17em} d \nu = \|P_0 [w]\|^2 = \int |S^{\xi} (w) |^2 \hspace{0.17em} d \nu .\)

Their difference is \(\|P_{0, \xi} [w]_{\xi} - S^{\xi} (w) \Omega_{\xi} \|^2\). It vanishes almost everywhere. Rational positive configurations with basis labels form one countable family dense in every phase reflection space, by Gram continuity. Intersect its full-measure sets. Outside the resulting null set,

\(\displaystyle \ker H_{\xi} =\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\xi} . \) (15.10)

Choose and retain one \(\xi_{\ast}\) in this full-measure set. At this stage we have not eliminated exceptional phases or shown that \(\nu\) is a point mass.

The slab bounds are uniform in all phases, including exceptional ones. For the formal iterates \(v_j = (A_{w^{\dagger}} A_w)^j v\), the mixed maps satisfy \(Y_{v_j} = (B_h (w)^{\ast} B_h (w))^j Y_v\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \|[v_j]_{\xi} \|^2 = \xi (Y_{v_j}^{\ast} Y_{v_j}) \leqslant \|Y_v \|^2 \|B_h (w)\|^{4 j} .\)

The repeated-power argument gives bounded phase slab operators with \(\|B_h^{\xi} (w)\| \leqslant \|B_h (w)\|\), and their identities and derivatives follow on the dense word span. These uniform bounds are the input for relativistic reconstruction and for the later removal of exceptional phases. No spectral gap has been obtained here.

15.7Sharp separated bounds in every phase

Recall that (13.15) bounds the mixed fixed-arity correlation by the sum of degrees plus any positive excess. The estimate is also uniform in the phase \(\xi\). To see this without bounding phase evaluations by the mixed expectation of the same word, apply the preceding chessboard argument to arbitrarily many separated copies of that whole word and their adjoints. The cell scales are chosen from the residue patterns and separations of one fixed word, not from the total number of copies. Copies placed far apart occupy distinct cells and have the same one-word factor on the right of the chessboard inequality. Passing to the separated-cluster limit yields the same bound to the corresponding power for the moments of its compressed operator \(M (w)^{\ast} M (w)\). The norm formula of (15.6) and the character bound then give

\(\displaystyle \sup_{\xi \in \Xi} |S_n^{\xi} (\boldsymbol{A}; x) | \leqslant C_{\boldsymbol{A}, n, \epsilon} r^{- \sum_i d_i - \epsilon} . \) (15.11)

Thus the fixed-arity scaling cost is the sum of degrees plus an arbitrarily small excess. No uniform constant as arity tends to infinity is asserted; the repeated-word estimate remains the appropriate statement for that different purpose.
