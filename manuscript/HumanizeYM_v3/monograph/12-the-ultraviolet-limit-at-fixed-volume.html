Chapter 12
The ultraviolet limit at fixed volume

We keep the physical period \(R_p\) fixed and let the ultraviolet cutoff \(N\) tend to infinity. The level \(m\) has spacing \(a_m = D_m^{- 1}\); blocking proceeds from \(m + 1\) to \(m\). The purpose of this chapter is to compare different cutoffs on one fixed configuration space. Compactness by itself would give only a subsequence. The additional ingredient is an estimate for stopping the marked evolution at an intermediate level, with a constant independent of that level.

The common trajectory of Chapter 10 and the marked estimates of Chapter 11 supply the stability and finite-difference estimates used here. We state their precise forms to distinguish a change of cutoff from comparison of two insertions under one fixed transformation.

12.1Fixed-level convergence data

Fix finitely many labels \(v_i \in V_{d_i}\) and distinct physical origins. At a fixed level \(m\) above their reference levels, use one dictionary of local supports and large-field inventories for all cutoffs \(N > m\). All small-field event indicators and eligibility criteria in this dictionary are fixed at level \(m\). A large-field profile is the sum of activities having one particular support and prescribed set of large-field sites; the original activity list can be infinite even though the number of such profiles on a fixed finite torus is finite.

The required background convergence consists of Cauchy convergence of \(\beta_{N, p, m}\), of the path coefficients in their exponentially weighted sums, and of the regular and large-field activity arrays in the norms of Section 4.5. The scalar multiple of the density need not converge. For the marked transformation we need fixed-input continuity across cutoffs: at each fixed level, applying the cutoff-dependent marked map to any fixed admissible remainder graph gives Cauchy output graphs and coefficient maps. This condition is stronger than contraction for two graphs acted on by the same map. For the common construction, Theorem 10.3 supplies the background limits. The marked estimates and fixed-input comparison in Section 11.4.1 supply the corresponding single-insertion maps. We keep the needed forms explicit to show how the two comparisons enter this argument.

Here is a useful way to obtain the required path convergence. For canonical path indices \(a\) of length \(\ell (a)\), suppose the coefficients \(c_{N, a}\) converge individually and obey a common bound at width \(w > v\). Then

\(\displaystyle |c_{N, a} - c_a | e^{v \ell (a)} \leqslant 2 Ce^{- (w - v) \ell (a)} .\)

The canonical indices enumerate displacement vectors and finitely many plaquette directions, rather than all walks of a given length. For a row their length is \(|x|_1 + 4\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}^4} e^{- \delta (|x|_1 + 4)} = e^{- 4 \delta} \left( \frac{1 + e^{- \delta}}{1 - e^{- \delta}} \right)^4 < \infty .\)

For an atom of degree \(q\) the analogous sum has \(q - 1\) displacement vectors and exponent \(4 (q - 1)\). Dominated convergence therefore gives convergence in the smaller width \(v\). A strict width reserve makes coordinatewise coefficient convergence sufficient for the actual path-action norm; convergence of an unrelated finite-volume matrix would not do so.

12.2Contraction of the complete marked remainder

For a fixed degree, let \(\mathcal{R}_m\) denote the space of remainder arrays at level \(m\). Its seminorm is the infimum of the sum of the regular derivative majorants and the large-field value majorants multiplied by \(\kappa_m^{- 1}\), where \(\kappa_m = (\epsilon_m^{\text{for}})^{1 / 2}\) is the square root of the actual level-\(m\) large-field bound, as in Section 11.4.1. We keep the arrays themselves, without identifying different arrays merely because their evaluations agree. Evaluation of the regular sum and of each large-field profile is a bounded linear map in this seminorm. This follows first for any admissible majorant and then by taking its infimum.

An admissible graph \(G : V_d \to \mathcal{R}_m\) obeys \(\|Gx\|_{\mathcal{R}_m} \leqslant s_m \|x\|_1\), where \(s_m \geqslant 1\) is fixed by the finite-degree construction. Set

\(\displaystyle d_m (G, H) = \sup_{x \ne 0} \frac{\|(G - H) x\|_{\mathcal{R}_m}}{s_m \|x\|_1} .\)

Thus any two admissible graphs have distance at most two. Write \(I_G\) for the coefficient map of one marked step and \(J_G\) for its unreparametrized remainder map. Its output graph is \(\Phi G = J_G I_G^{- 1}\).

Lemma 12.1. (Graph contraction) Suppose the marked step maps admissible graphs into admissible graphs, and the common-support marked difference estimates give, for \(a = d_{m + 1} (G, H)\),

\(\displaystyle \|I_G - I_H \| \leqslant \delta_m a, \qquad \|(J_G - J_H) x\|_{\mathcal{R}_m} \leqslant \delta_m a \|x\|_1, \qquad \|I_G^{- 1} \| \leqslant s_{m + 1} .\)

If \(4 \delta_m s_{m + 1} \leqslant 1\), then \(d_m (\Phi G, \Phi H) \leqslant a / 2\).

Proof. For \(y = I_G^{- 1} x\), use \(J_H = (\Phi H) I_H\) to write

\(\displaystyle (\Phi G - \Phi H) x = (J_G - J_H) y + (\Phi H) (I_H - I_G) y.\)

Its norm is at most \((1 + s_m) \delta_m as_{m + 1} \|x\|_1\), which is at most \(as_m \|x\|_1 / 2\).\(\Box\)

The difference estimates are for the complete regular and large-field remainder on common supports. Linearity of a single insertion allows its local polynomial part to cancel. A contraction only for the regular part would not prove this lemma for the actual inserted density. For the actual transformation, (11.8) gives ball invariance and the inverse bound, while (11.9) gives these two differences with \(\delta_m = C_d \epsilon_m s_{m + 1}\). The selected ratio and thresholds for the starting scales impose \(4 \delta_m s_{m + 1} \leqslant 1\). The fine and coarse remainder norms retain their distinct factors \(\kappa_{m + 1}^{- 1}\) and \(\kappa_m^{- 1}\). Weighted path continuity at a strict width reserve and activity-norm continuity in Section 11.4.1, together with Theorem 10.3, give the fixed-input cutoff continuity as well.

Proposition 12.2. (Forgetting the terminal graph) For the selected trajectory, the graph contraction and fixed-input cutoff continuity just verified imply that the selected graphs and coefficients normalized at one reference level are Cauchy at every fixed level.

Proof. Fix \(m\) and a positive integer \(h\). Replace the selected graph at \(m + h\) by one fixed admissible graph and propagate down \(h\) steps. The resulting graphs are Cauchy as \(N \to \infty\): fixed-input continuity and contraction give this by finite induction. Their distance from the actual graphs is at most \(2^{1 - h}\). First let \(h\) be large and then let both cutoffs tend to infinity. This proves the Cauchy assertion without an infinite product of inverse maps.

The coefficient at its reference level is the prescribed label. For each subsequent fixed level solve the finite-dimensional relation \(x_{m, N} = I_{m, N} x_{m + 1, N}\). The maps are Cauchy and their inverses uniformly bounded at this fixed level. The identity

\(\displaystyle \|I_{m, N}^{- 1} x_{m, N} - I_{m, M}^{- 1} x_{m, M} \|_1 \leqslant C_m \left( \|x_{m, N} - x_{m, M} \|_1 +\|I_{m, N} - I_{m, M} \| \hspace{0.17em} \|I_{m, M}^{- 1} x_{m, M} \|_1 \right)\)

proves the assertion by finite induction. Evaluation of \(G_{m, N} x_{m, N}\) is Cauchy by the graph bound and its bounded evaluation maps.\(\Box\)

Consequently the complete functions multiplying a single source \(f_{i, N, m} (U)\) and large-field profiles linear in the sources \(k_{i, N, m, Q} (U)\) converge uniformly in \(U\). Here \(Q\) is a support together with its exact prescribed set of large-field sites. Multiplication by a fixed Borel event indicator preserves uniform convergence; no continuity of the event indicator as a function of \(U\) is required.

12.3The scalar normalization cancels

Write \(\rho_{N, m}\) for the actual positive density at the fixed period, \(s_{N, m}\) for its extensive scalar term, and

\(\displaystyle \tilde{\rho}_{N, m} = e^{- s_{N, m}} \rho_{N, m}, \qquad Z^0_{N, m} = \int \tilde{\rho}_{N, m} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{m, p},\)

where \(\mu_{m, p}\) is product probability Haar measure. Background convergence makes \(\tilde{\rho}_{N, m}\) uniformly Cauchy. Indeed, curvatures are bounded on the compact group, so weighted path convergence implies uniform action convergence. Summing a rooted activity norm over the finitely many sites gives uniform convergence of the regular sum and every large-field profile. The exact-cover gas is a finite polynomial in the latter profiles and fixed eligibility indicators. Its product with the exponential of the regular action therefore converges uniformly.

The limit of \(Z^0_{N, m}\) is strictly positive. Choose a fixed link ball \(\mathcal{U}_m\) about the identity small enough that every inspected plaquette satisfies the level-\(m\) cutoff. On this set there are no large-field sites, so the exact-cover sum is one. The action and the regular sum are bounded above by a constant \(A_{m, p}\) uniformly on the cutoff tail. Since Haar measure assigns positive mass to every nonempty open link ball,

\(\displaystyle Z^0_{N, m} \geqslant e^{- A_{m, p}} \mu_{m, p} (\mathcal{U}_m) > 0. \) (12.1)

Nonnegativity of the full density justifies restricting the integral to \(\mathcal{U}_m\). Positivity of each partition function alone would not imply a positive limit.

Let \(z_1, \ldots, z_n\) be commuting variables with \(z_i^2 = 0\), and write \(z_S = \prod_{i \in S} z_i\). The exact marked density at level \(m\) has regular terms linear in the sources \(\sum_i z_i f_i\), higher regular entries \(H_X\), and large-field factors

\(\displaystyle K_Q + \sum_i z_i k_{i, Q} + K_Q^{\geqslant 2} .\)

Every term in \(H_X\) and \(K_Q^{\geqslant 2}\) contains at least two distinct source variables. All insertion origins belong to its support. Keep the complete terms linear in the sources and set only these higher entries to zero. Call the resulting density \(\mathscr{X}^0_{N, m} (z, U)\). The expression

\(\displaystyle C_{N, p, m} (v ; x) = \frac{\int [z_{\{1, \ldots, n\}}] e^{- s_{N, m}} \mathscr{X}^0_{N, m} (z, U) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{m, p}}{Z^0_{N, m}} \) (12.2)

is Cauchy in \(N\) for fixed \(m\). Its numerator is a finite polynomial in uniformly convergent grouped profiles and functions multiplying a single source, multiplied by a uniformly convergent exponential. Its denominator has the positive limit in (12.1). The same scalar has been removed from both. In particular, deleting terms involving at least two insertions does not mean deleting the large-field profiles linear in the sources.

12.4A stopping estimate uniform in the stopping level

Recall that \(B_m\) is the support radius from (4.15) and \(\text{cov}_{B_m} (X)\) counts a connected cover of \(X\) by radius-\(B_m\) boxes. Suppose the physical origins belong to one common lattice and are distinct modulo \(R_p\). Their separation at level \(m\) is at least \(\eta / a_m\) for some \(\eta > 0\). Since \(a_m B_m^2 \to 0\), choose a fixed base level \(r\) so that separation is at least \(B_m^2\) for every \(m \geqslant r\). Enlarge \(r\) to include all reference levels.

We use the following marked norm conclusion. Terms involving at least two insertions have rooted majorant sums at most one at support exponent \(\sigma_m\). The transfer accepts exponent \(\sigma_{m + 1} - 4\) and restores exponent \(\sigma_m\). It retains all inventories and insertion origins. Moreover, after transfer to the fixed base level, normalized coefficient extraction is bounded by

\(\displaystyle C_r = \frac{\exp \{| \Lambda_{r, p} |q_r + n (x_r + y_r + 2)\}}{\prod_i w_i} . \) (12.3)

Here \(q_r\) is the vacuum normalization bound; \(w_i > 0\) are fixed source weights; \(x_r\) bounds the weighted regular sizes of the terms linear in the sources; and \(y_r\) bounds their weighted large-field profile sums. Such weights can be chosen uniformly on the cutoff tail: the fixed-level coefficients linear in the sources are bounded, so scale each weight down to its positive allowed size. Crucially all four quantities in (12.3) are evaluated at \(r\), not at the later stopping level \(m\). These statements concern the complete actual marked transfer. They follow from the estimate for terms involving at least two insertions (11.11) and the normalized chessboard bound (11.17).

Lemma 12.3. (Removal of terms involving at least two insertions) Under these marked norm and transfer bounds, for each \(m > r\) and all sufficiently large \(N\),

\(\displaystyle |M_{N, p} (v ; x / a_N) - C_{N, p, m} (v ; x) | \leqslant 2 C_r e^{- B_m / 64} .\)

The constant \(C_r\) is independent of \(m, N\); the cutoff threshold can depend on \(m\).

Proof. A support containing two origins separated by \(B_m^2\) has cover number at least \(B_m / 64\). Multiply every term of source degree at least two, and only those entries, by a complex scalar \(u\). For \(|u| < S_m = e^{B_m / 64}\) its majorant still fits exponent \(\sigma_m - 2\), since

\(\displaystyle |u| e^{(\sigma_m - 2) \text{cov}_{B_m} (X)} \leqslant e^{\sigma_m \text{cov}_{B_m} (X)} .\)

Transport the entire modified marked density to \(r\). The support reserve allows its first and subsequent steps, and the constant and coefficients linear in the sources are unchanged. If a source monomial contains \(q\) origins, the stronger geometric bound \(B_m q \leqslant 64 \text{cov}_{B_m} (X)\) also absorbs a change of each source weight by at most \(e^{B_m / 128}\). This is the weight reserve needed when using the marked transfer.

Let \(F_{N, m} (u)\) be the normalized integral of its full-source coefficient. Exact probability-kernel integration preserves that integral and its vacuum denominator. The fixed-base estimate gives \(|F_{N, m} (u) | \leqslant C_r\) throughout the disk. The source algebra is nilpotent; after grouping the finitely many support and prescribed-site profiles, \(F_{N, m}\) is a polynomial in \(u\), hence holomorphic. For \(S_m > 2\), Cauchy's derivative estimate on circles of radius \(S_m / 2\) about points of \([0, 1]\) gives \(|F_{N, m} (1) - F_{N, m} (0) | \leqslant 2 C_r / S_m\). These two values are the actual cutoff moment and (12.2), respectively.\(\Box\)

Proposition 12.4. (Cauchy convergence on common lattice points) For the selected trajectory, the correlations converge along the whole cutoff sequence at every configuration of distinct points belonging to one common lattice.

Proof. Given an error tolerance, first choose \(m\) so that the two stopping errors in Lemma 12.3 are small. Keep \(m\) fixed. The stopped expression (12.2) is Cauchy as both cutoffs tend to infinity. The triangle inequality proves the assertion. No new subsequence is chosen.\(\Box\)

12.5Smoothness and all lattice differences

The remaining passage is a standard discrete version of the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem and convergence of derivatives; compare [Rud76, Theorems 7.17 and 7.25]. We record the precise application, since the rounded lattice functions are not continuous. Let \(U \subset \mathbb{R}^D\) be open, \(a_N \downarrow 0\), and let \(F_N\) be functions on \(a_N \mathbb{Z}^D\). Write

\(\displaystyle \Delta_{N, j} F_N (x) = a_N^{- 1} (F_N (x + a_N e_j) - F_N (x)) .\)

Assume every fixed iterated difference is bounded on each compact subset of \(U\), eventually in \(N\), and the rounded values are Cauchy on a dense subset. Then there is \(F \in C^{\infty} (U)\) such that all rounded iterated differences converge locally uniformly to the corresponding derivatives of \(F\).

Only the discrete-to-continuous adaptation needs explanation. For nearby lattice points, telescoping along coordinate edges gives \(|G_N (y) - G_N (x) | \leqslant C |y - x|_1\) whenever the first differences of \(G_N\) are bounded on a slightly larger compact neighborhood. Rounding adds at most \(2 DCa_N\) to this modulus. A finite net from the dense Cauchy set therefore proves locally uniform convergence of \(F_N\). Bounded second differences give the discrete Taylor estimate

\(\displaystyle |G_N (y) - G_N (x) - \sum_j (y_j - x_j) \Delta_{N, j} G_N (x) | \leqslant C |y - x|_1^2 .\)

For fixed small \(h > 0\), compare \(\Delta_{N, j} G_N (x_N)\) to the secant with interval \(h_N = a_N \lceil h / a_N \rceil\). The error is at most \(Ch_N\). The secants converge first as \(N \to \infty\); then \(h \to 0\) proves that the first differences are Cauchy. Their own first-difference bounds give locally uniform convergence. Passing to the displayed Taylor inequality identifies their limits as the derivatives of the limit of \(G_N\). Iteration proves the assertion for every finite order. All paths and shifted stencils lie in the enlarged compact neighborhood.

Apply this to periodic lifts on

\(\displaystyle U_{p, n} = \{(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in (\mathbb{R}^4)^n : x_i - x_j \not\in R_p \mathbb{Z}^4 \text{ for } i \ne j\} .\)

The union of the nested physical lattices is dense here. Its points supply Proposition 12.4; the all-order separated finite-difference bounds supply the other hypothesis. Points sharing one or several coordinate projections are allowed. Only coincidences of the full four-dimensional points modulo the period are excluded. The limiting function, denoted \(M^{\text{UV}}_{p, v}\), is smooth and periodic on this domain. The difference hypothesis is supplied by Theorem 11.13: on a compact subset of \(U_{p, n}\) the periodic separation has a positive lower bound, and any fixed shifted difference stencil stays in a slightly larger separated compact set on the cutoff tail.

12.6The fixed-volume conclusion

Theorem 12.5. (Full ultraviolet limit) For the common selected trajectory with the strengthened stage requirements of Chapters 10 and 11, the correlations have the smooth full-sequence limit \(M^{\text{UV}}_{p, v}\) at every fixed physical period. For every \(f \in C_c^{\infty} (U_{p, n})\) supported in an admissible period cell and every finite coordinate list \(\alpha\),

\(\displaystyle \lim_{N \to \infty} a_N^{4 n} \sum_y f (a_N y) \Delta_{N, \alpha} M_{N, p} (v ; y) = \int f (x) \partial^{\alpha} M^{\text{UV}}_{p, v} (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx.\)

Here differences in the summand are divided by their physical step lengths. Their shifted sites must lie in the slightly enlarged separated set, which holds on a cutoff tail.

The last assertion is a Riemann-sum consequence of locally uniform convergence. Fixed forward, backward or centered stencils give the same limit: telescope each stencil as a finite average of shifted unit forward differences. The averaging weights sum to one and the shifted points approach the same point.

The convergence threshold in this theorem may depend on \(p\). Bounds on compact separated sets that were uniform in \(p\) remain uniform for the limiting kernels, by passage to the limit. These two quantifier statements serve different purposes: the former removes the ultraviolet cutoff; the latter permits the subsequent infinite-volume extraction. Finite reflection inequalities also pass to the limit whenever every correlation in their finite quadratic form is supported away from collisions. Neither this argument nor reflection positivity asserts an unsubtracted limit at coincident points. Distributional extensions and reconstruction require their own arguments.
