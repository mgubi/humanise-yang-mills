Chapter 13
Continuum correlations and local fields

The ultraviolet limit leaves a theory on each fixed physical torus. We now let its period grow. The construction may select a subsequence of volumes, but it does not select a new subsequence of ultraviolet cutoffs for the correlations. Local fields are then defined by their responses in all separated correlations. Polynomial labels that give the same responses represent the same field.

We use the full fixed-volume limit of Theorem 12.5, the volume-uniform finite-difference bounds of Theorem 11.13, and the equation comparisons and the comparisons using separating families in Chapter 11. Compactness gives the volume extraction; the separate equation inclusion and rank bounds identify its local fields.

13.1One subsequence of volumes

Recall that \(\text{Conf}_n\) is the open set of pairwise distinct points in \((\mathbb{R}^4)^n\), without any restriction on their coordinate projections. Let \(p (k)\) be any strictly increasing sequence of volume indices. On every compact subset of \(\text{Conf}_n\), the periodic kernels \(M^{\text{UV}}_{p (k), v}\) are eventually defined with separation bounded below independently of \(k\). Each of their derivatives is uniformly bounded there. The cutoff threshold used to prove the bound may depend on the fixed volume; after taking that ultraviolet limit it disappears from the bound.

Arzelà–Ascoli and diagonal extraction [Rud76, Theorem 7.25] give one refinement \(P (k) = p (q (k))\) and a multilinear family \(S_n\) such that

\(\displaystyle M^{\text{UV}}_{P (k), v} \longrightarrow S_n (v ; \cdot) \quad \text{in } C^{\infty}_{\text{loc}} (\text{Conf}_n) . \) (13.1)

The diagonal family is countable: arities, degree lists, basis-label lists, derivative lists, and compact exhaustions of the configuration spaces. Multilinearity extends its conclusions to all labels. Integration by parts identifies the extracted derivative limits with the derivatives of \(S_n\). No diagonalization over all smooth test functions is needed. For \(v = (v_1, \ldots, v_n)\) and \(f \in C_c^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_n)\) the order of limits is

\(\displaystyle \lim_{k \to \infty} \lim_{N \to \infty} a_N^{4 n} \sum_y f (a_N y) M_{N, P (k)} (v ; y) = \int f (x) S_n (v ; x) \hspace{0.17em} dx. \) (13.2)

The sum ranges over ordered \(n\)-tuples of lattice sites in the period \(R_{P (k)}\) for which \(a_N y\) lies in the support of \(f\), and \(dx\) denotes Lebesgue measure on \((\mathbb{R}^4)^n\). The inner limit is the whole cutoff sequence, taken down each column of Figure 13.1. Every further volume refinement preserves (13.1) and (13.2).

Figure 13.1. The two limits for separated correlations. Each column shows one periodic coordinate at successive lattice spacings (schematic). The full cutoff sequence at that period gives the torus kernel and the limit for every separated test supported inside its period cell. The framed kernels illustrate a subsequence of the prescribed volumes. One such choice gives convergence in \(C^{\infty}_{\text{loc}} (\text{Conf}_n)\) for all arities and label lists, hence for every separated test function.

Permutation symmetry, conjugation, the distinguished unit and hypercubic covariance pass from the finite correlations by these limits. Translation by an arbitrary real vector is obtained by approximating it on the same lattice; the uniform first-difference bound controls the rounding error. Full orthogonal covariance will require a separate argument.

13.2Separated cells and errors in local observables

The chessboard seminorm of Section 1.4 bounds an observable by its repeated reflected copies in a tiling. For local observables \(F_1, \ldots, F_n\) interior to distinct cells of the same translated tiling \(\mathcal{T}\), the chessboard inequality gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle \prod_i F_i \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant \prod_i \|F_i \|_{\mathcal{T}} . \) (13.3)

It is the lattice inequality already cited from [FILS78], with the constant observable in every unused cell. The estimate does not grow with the distances between the cells.

Here is the required geometric placement. For a tiling of physical side \(a\), the translations in one coordinate bringing one of \(n\) anchors within distance \(w\) of a boundary have total length at most \(2 nw\) modulo \(a\). If \(2 nw < a\), a translation avoids all these strips; a lattice translation does so for sufficiently fine cutoff. Choose one in each coordinate. Anchors with pairwise maximum-coordinate distance greater than \(a\) cannot share a cell. Hence if the centered local bounds apply at scale \(k\) with margin \(3 a_k\), and \(6 na_k < a_m\), anchors separated by more than \(a_m\) admit distinct side-\(a_m\) cells with the required margins.

We shall use an error family \(E_{N, p}\) with the following property: for any fixed arity and sufficiently small fixed cell side, its chessboard seminorm is at most \(\eta_{N, p} \to 0\) in the ordered limits, uniformly over placements satisfying a specified interior margin. The cell side can be chosen after the arity and a requested lower scale, before the cutoff. The spectator insertions have uniform centered seminorm bounds on sufficiently fine fixed scales. Place the error and all spectators in distinct cells, choosing a still finer scale for the spectators' margins. Equation (13.3) yields

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle E_{N, p} (y_i) \prod_{h \ne i} O_{N, p} (v_h, y_h) \right\rangle \right| \leqslant C (v, \delta) \eta_{N, p}, \) (13.4)

for configurations separated by \(\delta > 0\). The weighted number of lattice points in the fixed compact support of a test function is bounded. The same error therefore vanishes after separated smearing. This conclusion uses the stated order of choices for the error norm; pointwise smallness at one configuration would not suffice.

13.3Fields as equivalence classes of labels

A label includes its degree space: the same formal polynomial in \(V_d\) and \(V_e\) can have different cutoff normalizations. For \(c \in V_d\), a fixed spectator list \(v\), and a separated smooth test function \(f\), define the one-slot response

\(\displaystyle L_{v, f} (c) = \int f (x_0, x) S_{n + 1} (c (x_0), v (x)) \hspace{0.17em} dx_0 \hspace{0.17em} dx.\)

Let

\(\displaystyle N_d (S) = \bigcap_{v, f} \ker L_{v, f}, \qquad F_d = V_d / N_d (S) . \) (13.5)

The intersection permits every finite spectator list, of arbitrary degrees. Smoothness makes this equivalent to vanishing in every pointwise separated correlation. Permutation symmetry ensures that nullity has the same meaning in every slot. The quotient \(F_d\) is finite dimensional, but its dimension has not yet been identified.

We use the bounded equivariant idempotents \(\Pi_{d, N, p}\) of Section 11.12, with kernel \(\textsf{Z}_{N, p, d} P_{N, p, d} S_{t_N} R_d \mathcal{I}_d\). Choose a common ordered limit \(\Pi_d\) after a coefficient refinement. More precisely, diagonal extraction over the countably many degrees and volumes gives one increasing sequence \(N = n (j)\) along which the bounded coefficient arrays converge at each fixed volume. A further volume refinement makes those limits converge as well. All coefficient limits below use these refined ordered tails. This auxiliary selection does not change the already existing full cutoff limits of the correlations. Write \(r_d = \dim \mathcal{M}_d^{\text{Max}}\) for the dimension of the free Maxwell quotient in Chapter 3. The construction gives:

  1. the ranks of \(\Pi_{d, N, p}\) are at most \(r_d\);

  2. every bounded family in \(\ker \Pi_{d, N, p}\) has vanishing separated responses in the ordered limits;

  3. there are finite matrices of limiting separated responses, evaluated on comparison labels, that approach an invertible \(r_d \times r_d\) free matrix of correlation pairings.

The first statement is exact rank equality, since every map defining the finite equation space is invertible. For the second, Lemma 11.7 makes its chessboard seminorms tend to zero; bounded rows have uniformly subpower inverse terminal coordinates, and (13.4) supplies every spectator response. For the third, Proposition 11.11 gives the actual finite-cutoff matrices with error tending to zero as their comparison depth increases. Include their finitely many bounded label columns at each of the countably many comparison depths in the same coefficient refinement. At each fixed depth, their ordered limits preserve the matrix estimate by multilinearity and the uniform correlation bounds. An injective rectangular matrix of correlation pairings has an invertible square minor; retain those rows.

Proposition 13.1. (Identification by two rank bounds) For this selected continuum family, \(N_d (S) = \ker \Pi_d\) and \(\dim F_d = r_d\).

Proof. For fixed \(c\), the bounded family \((1 - \Pi_{d, N, p}) c\) has vanishing responses. Taking limits gives \(L_{v, f} ((1 - \Pi_d) c) = 0\) for all \(v, f\), so \(\ker \Pi_d \subset N_d (S)\). Converging idempotents have eventually constant ranks: if their norm difference is less than one, each is injective on the other's range. Thus \(\dim F_d \leqslant \text{rank} \hspace{0.17em} \Pi_d \leqslant r_d\). Each comparison matrix factors through \(F_d\), since its rows are separated responses. Matrices sufficiently close to the invertible free matrix of correlation pairings have rank \(r_d\). Hence \(\dim F_d \geqslant r_d\). Equality of dimensions and the kernel inclusion prove the result.\(\Box\)

13.3.1Representatives independent of the other insertions

Approximate inclusion and derivative labels need not stay bounded. The following finite-dimensional observation explains the normalized null estimate required to replace them by fixed vectors. It also avoids choosing a separate representative for every test function.

Let \(V\) be finite dimensional, \(\Pi_u \to \Pi\) idempotents, and \(L_{i, u} \to L_i\) linear functionals, along a proper filter of indices. Assume \(\ker \Pi = \bigcap_i \ker L_i\). If vectors \(x_u\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle L_{i, u} (x_u) \to t_i, \qquad L_{i, u} \left( \frac{x_u - \Pi_u x_u}{1 +\| \Pi_u x_u \|} \right) \to 0 \quad \text{for every } i, \) (13.6)

there is one \(z \in \text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} \Pi\) with \(L_i (z) = t_i\) for all \(i\). To see this, put \(b_u = (1 +\| \Pi_u x_u \|)^{- 1}\) and \(z_u = b_u \Pi_u x_u\). A common cluster point \((b, z)\) satisfies \(b + \|z\| = 1\), \(\Pi z = z\) and \(L_i (z) = bt_i\). If \(b = 0\), then \(z \in \ker \Pi \cap \text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} \Pi\), a contradiction. The vector \(z / b\) is the desired representative. Compactness is used only in one fixed finite-dimensional space; all response identities hold at the same cluster point because they hold along the filter.

In the application, the filter is generated by ordered tails: \(p = P (k)\) with \(k \geqslant k_0\), and \(N = n (j)\) with \(j \geqslant j_0 (k)\). The normalized null condition is available for every admissible coefficient family in the sense of Section 11.12. Indeed, if \(x_{N, p}\) has subpower inverse terminal coordinates, then \(x / (1 +\| \Pi x\|)\) has the same property, whereas \(\Pi x / (1 +\| \Pi x\|)\) is bounded and therefore admissible. Their difference lies in the exact finite equation kernel. Lemma 11.7, followed by (13.4), proves (13.6) for all spectators on the same tail. This argument applies even when the original \(x_{N, p}\) are unbounded.

13.4Inclusions and derivatives

For each basis label \(c \in V_d\), the protected trial construction of Section 11.8 gives approximate representatives in \(V_e\) for \(e \geqslant d\) and in \(V_{d + 1}\) for a physical lattice difference. To check the inclusion trial, multiply the original cutoff observable by \(a_N^e\); its local representative is \(a_N^{e - d}\) times the degree-\(d\) section, so its derivative and value bounds remain subpower. For a difference, the extra factor \(a_N\) cancels the inverse step, and the fixed translated-stencil cost is bounded by the coupling-threshold requirements used in Theorem 11.13. Trial descent yields admissible coefficients and vanishing chessboard seminorm errors, uniformly in period. The preceding paragraph gives their normalized null estimates. All representatives and comparison errors are chosen before the spectators and test functions. Applying the preceding finite-dimensional observation gives fixed linear maps on labels, \(\hat{\iota}_{d, e}\) and \(\hat{\partial}_{d, \mu}\), such that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} S (\hat{\iota}_{d, e} c (x_0), v (x)) & = S (c (x_0), v (x)), \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.7)} \\ S (\hat{\partial}_{d, \mu} c (x_0), v (x)) & = \partial_{x_0^{\mu}} S (c (x_0), v (x)) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.8)} \end{array}\)

For the derivative target, exact torus summation by parts is

\(\displaystyle a_N^{4 n} \sum_y f (a_N y) \Delta_{N, i, \mu} M_{N, p} (y) = a_N^{4 n} \sum_y \frac{f (a_N y - a_N e_{i, \mu}) - f (a_N y)}{a_N} M_{N, p} (y) .\)

The quotient on the right tends uniformly to \(- \partial_{i, \mu} f\); the zero-order separated bound controls its error. This identifies the distributional derivative target. Smoothness gives (13.8) pointwise.

These maps descend to unique maps \(\iota_{d, e} : F_d \to F_e\) and \(\partial_{\mu} : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\) with the displayed responses. They preserve null classes by their definition. The inclusions are injective and satisfy

\(\displaystyle \iota_{d, d} = 1, \qquad \iota_{e, f} \iota_{d, e} = \iota_{d, f}, \qquad \partial_{\mu} \partial_{\nu} = \partial_{\nu} \partial_{\mu} .\)

Each identity follows by testing against every spectator and using (13.5); the derivative maps also commute with inclusions. Conjugation and hypercubic covariance descend in the same way. The union of the \(F_d\) is therefore a filtered space of local fields, with derivatives raising the filtration by one. No multiplication of two fields at the same point has been defined by this construction.

13.5Reflection positivity and separated power bounds

Let a word mean a finite list of labels and positions, with correlation denoted \(S (w)\). Write \(w^{\ast}\) for conjugation of its labels and reversal of its order. Time is coordinate four. Let \(\theta w\) reflect that coordinate and act on its labels by the corresponding signed transformation, as well as applying \(\ast\). For words with strictly positive times, lattice bond reflection positivity passes to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i, j} \overline{c_i} c_j S (\theta w_i \hspace{0.17em} w_j) \geqslant 0.\)

It also passes for joint smooth test functions supported on positive-time separated configurations. Indeed their lattice supports stay away from both reflection planes on the ordered tail. The lattice plane \(x^4 = a_N / 2\) tends to zero, and the uniform separated bound controls the moving test functions. Taking the two limits preserves positivity of each finite matrix. Pointwise positivity follows by concentrating smooth bumps. This uses the finite lattice result of Section 1.4; it asserts no positivity for unspecified extensions at coincident points.

For later use take the centered chessboard estimate \(\|O\|_k \leqslant Ca_k^{- (d + 1)}\), uniform on a bounded set of labels of degrees at most \(d\) and every sufficiently fine fixed level \(k\). It follows by choosing \(\epsilon = 1\) in Proposition 13.3, whose proof below uses only the bound for reflected tilings of Chapter 11 and the selected schedule. Slow growth of the block ratios gives a constant \(c > 0\) with

\(\displaystyle a_{k + 1} \geqslant ca_k^2 . \) (13.9)

The first scale below a length \(r \leqslant 1\) is consequently at least a constant times \(r^2\). Choose a cell side below \(r / 2\), then a centered scale below that side divided by \(6 (n + 1)\). The latter is at least \(c_n r^4\). The separated-cell estimate yields

\(\displaystyle |S_n (v ; x) | \leqslant C_{n, v} \hspace{0.17em} r^{- 4 (d + 1) n}, \qquad \min_{i \ne j} |x_i - x_j |_{\infty} \geqslant r. \) (13.10)

The constant is independent of the diameter of the configuration. Applying the same argument to the fixed degree-raised labels in (13.8) gives a polynomial separated bound for every derivative.

13.6Repeated reflected words

The preceding bound cannot be used with arity tending to infinity without controlling its constants. We need a more specific estimate. Let \(w\) have \(n \geqslant 1\) insertions, degrees at most \(d\) and coefficient norms at most \(R\), pairwise separation at least \(r\), and times in \([r, h - r]\), where \(0 < r \leqslant 1\). We call \(r\) its buffer in the slab of width \(h\). For a positive-time exterior word \(v\), set

\(\displaystyle A_w v = w \hspace{0.17em} \tau_{se_4} v, \qquad w^{\dagger} = \tau_{se_4} \theta w, \qquad \|v\|_{\text{OS}}^2 = S (\theta v \hspace{0.17em} v) .\)

At this point \(A_w\) acts only on words; boundedness on a Hilbert space is not assumed.

Proposition 13.2. (Uniform bound for repetitions) Under the centered chessboard bounds and their interior-cell comparison used above, choose \(0 < s \leqslant h\) so that \(w\) still has buffer \(r / 2\). There are \(C = C (d, R)\) and \(\kappa = 4 (d + 1)\) such that, for every exterior word \(v\),

\(\displaystyle \|(A_{w^{\dagger}} A_w)^j v\|_{\text{OS}}^2 \leqslant B_v \hspace{0.17em} [C^n (n / r)^{\kappa n}]^{4 j}, \qquad j \geqslant 0.\)

Here \(s, C, \kappa\) are chosen before \(v\), and \(B_v < \infty\) is independent of \(j\).

Proof. Choose a cell scale \(a_m < r / 32\) as the first permitted such scale, and \(s = a_m \lfloor h / a_m \rfloor\). Equation (13.9) gives \(a_m \geqslant c_1 r^2\). Choose a centered scale \(a_k < \alpha (r / n)^2\), with \(20 \alpha < c_1\). Again taking the first such scale gives \(20 na_k < a_m\) and \(a_k \geqslant c_2 (r / n)^4\).

The expanded reflection form contains \(4 jn\) main insertions and two exterior copies. Since \(s\) is a multiple of \(a_m\), the main points have at most \(2 n\) residue patterns modulo the side-\(a_m\) tiling, independently of \(j\). The strip count therefore places all main points in distinct cells with margins \(5 a_k\), using constants independent of the number of repetitions.

Choose finer aligned cell and centered scales for the finitely many exterior points, with exterior cell side at most \(a_k\). Their two copies have times \(\pm (2 js + t)\), so a choice depending on their minimum positive time and internal separation works for every \(j\). A further translation smaller than the exterior cell side adjusts their margins while leaving the main margins at least \(3 a_k\). The odd integer ratios preserve the fine cell grid under coarse reflections. Apply the chessboard inequality first to the coarse cells, regarding all exterior factors in one coarse cell as a single observable, then to the fine cells for those factors. If \(X\) bounds the exterior centered seminorms, the result is

\(\displaystyle X^{2 |v|} (C_0 a_k^{- (d + 1)})^{4 jn} .\)

Take \(B_v = \max (1, X^{2| v|})\) and use the lower bound on \(a_k\). Every use of the lattice inequality is at fixed \(j\) before taking the ordered limits. A cutoff threshold uniform in \(j\) is unnecessary.\(\Box\)

This estimate is the input for bounded slab insertions in the reflection Hilbert space. Its derivation precedes that operator construction and does not use orthogonal covariance or a phase decomposition. The equation comparison, admissible trial encoding, estimates for finite separating families and centered estimates all refer to the same selected family. Full orthogonal covariance and products at coincident points remain separate constructions.

13.7Sharp fixed-arity short-distance bounds

For a fixed number of insertions the loss in (13.10) can be reduced to an arbitrarily small excess over the sum of their degrees. This uses the normalized integral over the reflected tiling, not the stronger Gaussian comparison used for rank detection. We keep all incoming joint marked components; replacing them by a product of terms linear in the sources would invalidate the estimate.

Write \(\tau_m = \log (1 / a_m)\), so \(\hat{\beta}_m = \bar{\beta} + \kappa_G \tau_m\). Let \(C_j \geqslant 1\) include the inverse-column, finite-section and source constants at stage \(j\). The stage ratio and coupling threshold may be chosen to satisfy

\(\displaystyle \frac{\log C_j}{\log \mathscr{L}_j} \longrightarrow 0, \qquad B_{i + 1}^2 \leqslant 2 B_i^2, \qquad \frac{(L_i B_i^2)^4 q_{i + 1}^{\text{site}}}{\tau_{i + 1}} \longrightarrow 0. \) (13.11)

Here \(B_i\) is the support-block radius, and \(q_i^{\text{site}} \geqslant 1\) is the per-site normalization cost of (11.16); it is not a marked density or the scalar coupling correction. For the admissible path-action representation one can use \(q_i^{\text{site}} \leqslant C_{J (i)} (1 + \log \hat{\beta}_i)\). Indeed the scalar-free exponent has upper bound \(C_{J (i)} V\), while on the event that every link \(U\) has normalized Hilbert–Schmidt distance \(\chi (U)\) at most \(\hat{\beta}_i^{- 1 / 2} / 4\) it has lower bound \(- C_{J (i)} V\) and all large-field indicators vanish. The leading positive path quadratic is bounded there by \(C_{J (i)} \hat{\beta}_i \rho^2 V\) with \(\rho = \hat{\beta}_i^{- 1 / 2}\); exponential path summability bounds the path lengths in this estimate. Signed regular costs and the absolute activity site sum have their admissible bounds. The Haar-ball argument of Section 11.7 therefore costs only \(C_{J (i)} V + 2 D_G V \log \hat{\beta}_i\), where \(D_G\) is an ambient real matrix dimension for the gauge group.

These requirements are compatible with sufficiently large coupling thresholds. At each fixed stage \(B_i = O_j ((\log (2 + \tau_i))^2)\) and \(q_i^{\text{site}} = O_j (1 + \log (2 + \tau_i))\), so the last quotient in (13.11) tends to zero at its fixed ratio. Choose the ratio after \(C_j\), then increase the coupling threshold until that quotient is at most \(1 / j\) for both adjacent-stage choices. The same threshold choice gives the displayed bound on consecutive \(B_i\), \(\log L_i = o (\tau_i)\), and \(\log C_{J (i + 1)} = o (\tau_i)\) at transitions. Choose each ratio against both adjacent-stage column constants, which are fixed beforehand. These are conditions on the single selected schedule; no new schedule is chosen after fixing a correlation.

13.7.1A normalized local insertion

Fix a scaling-dimension bound \(d\) and a ball of radius \(R\) in the coefficient space of \(V_d\). Set

\(\displaystyle E_k = C_{d, R}^{\text{sec}} C_{J (k)} \prod_{i < k} C_{J (i + 1)}, \qquad \omega_k = a_k^d \hat{\beta}_k^{- w_d} E_k^{- 1} .\)

The fixed section constant \(C_{d, R}^{\text{sec}}\) includes the coefficient radius and all its signed and conjugated images. Choose the fixed integer \(w_d\) large enough to absorb the finite powers of the inverse small-field threshold in the degree-\(d\) section and its counterpart constrained to cover the prescribed large-field sites, as well as the source attachment bound. Multiplication of the single-insertion inverse bounds gives the factor \(E_k\); the scaling normalization is exactly \(a_k^{- d}\). Thus multiplication of the complete source by \(\omega_k\) makes the exponential bound for its regular term linear in the sources less than three and the bound for its large-field term linear in the sources less than one. Its remaining normalized size can be placed below the attachment bound by increasing \(w_d\).

The bounds in Chapter 11 for terms involving at least two insertions retain every incoming source term and every original eligibility event. Decreasing all mark weights only decreases their absolute mode sums. The source attachment inequalities at the intervening levels propagate this chosen small weight; they require the same inverse-column and threshold powers already included in \(E_k, w_d\). Consequently (11.17), with \(x = 3, y = 1\), gives

\(\displaystyle \|O_{N, p}^{(d)} (c)\|_{\text{cell} (m)} \leqslant \omega_k^{- 1} \exp ((a_m / a_k)^4 q_k^{\text{site}} + 6) . \) (13.12)

It holds on a full cutoff tail uniformly in period and in the coefficient ball, whenever the reflected anchors at the stopping level have spacing at least \(B_k^2\). Their spacing stays adequate at finer levels because \(L_i \geqslant 3\) whereas \(B_{i + 1}^2 / B_i^2 \leqslant 2\).

The logarithm of \(E_k\) is \(o (\tau_k)\): split its sum into a finite initial segment and a tail where \(\log C_{J (i + 1)} \leqslant \epsilon \log L_i\), then let \(\epsilon \downarrow 0\). The separate terminal column is absorbed by the same ratio bounds and \(\log L_{k - 1} = o (\tau_k)\). Also \(\log \hat{\beta}_k = o (\tau_k)\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle 6 - \log \omega_k = d \tau_k + o (\tau_k) . \) (13.13)

This is the reason the prefactor from the reflected tiling does not introduce a fixed extra scaling degree.

13.7.2The stopping scale and centered estimate

Choose \(k = k (m)\) as the first level \(k \geqslant m\) with \(B_k^2 \leqslant a_m / a_k\). It exists because \(B_k\) grows subexponentially in \(\tau_k\), whereas \(a_m / a_k = e^{\tau_k - \tau_m}\). By minimality,

\(\displaystyle (a_m / a_k)^4 q_k^{\text{site}} \leqslant (L_{k - 1} B_{k - 1}^2)^4 q_k^{\text{site}} = o (\tau_k) .\)

If the first level already qualifies the same conclusion follows from the fixed-stage cost bound. Since \(q_k^{\text{site}} \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \tau_k - \tau_m = \log (a_m / a_k) \leqslant (a_m / a_k)^4 q_k^{\text{site}} = o (\tau_k) .\)

It follows that \(\tau_k / \tau_m \to 1\). Combining this with (13.12) and (13.13) proves the following statement.

Proposition 13.3. (Sharp centered bound) For every scaling-dimension bound \(d\), coefficient radius \(R\) and \(\epsilon > 0\), there is \(C_{d, R, \epsilon}\), fixed before the cell scale \(m\), such that

\(\displaystyle \|O_{N, p}^{(d)} (c, y)\|_{\text{cell} (m, y)} \leqslant C_{d, R, \epsilon} a_m^{- d - \epsilon}, \qquad \|c\| \leqslant R.\)

For each fixed \(m\) this holds for all sufficiently large cutoffs, uniformly in \(p, y\) and in signed and conjugated images. The insertion is interior in that cell on the same tail.

Proof. For all sufficiently large \(m\), the logarithm of the right side of (13.12) is at most \((d + \epsilon) \tau_m\) by the preceding limits. For the finite remaining set of \(m\), use its selected fixed stopping level and take the maximum of the finite constants. The fixed lattice stencil becomes interior at every fixed physical cell scale as \(N \to \infty\). Translation covariance makes the threshold independent of \(y\).\(\Box\)

13.7.3Arbitrary separated points

The slow ratio condition gives a sharper first-below-scale estimate than (13.9). For any fixed \(c > 0\), let \(m (r)\) be the first level with \(a_m < cr\). Then

\(\displaystyle \frac{\log (1 / a_{m (r)})}{\log (1 / r)} \longrightarrow 1 \quad (r \downarrow 0) . \) (13.14)

Indeed \(a_{m - 1} \geqslant cr > a_m = a_{m - 1} / L_{m - 1}\), and the logarithm of the overshoot is at most \(\log L_{m - 1} = o (\tau_{m - 1})\). Any fixed finite iteration of first-below choices has the same property.

For a fixed arity \(n\) and mutually separated anchors, first choose a cell side below a fixed small multiple of their minimum separation \(r\). Translate its boundaries away from all anchors. Then choose a centered scale smaller than this side divided by \(6 (n + 1)\). The interior-cell comparison and the chessboard inequality apply. Both chosen scales have logarithm \((1 + o (1)) \log (1 / r)\) by (13.14). Apply Proposition 13.3 with a sufficiently small excess to each degree separately. We obtain, for every \(\epsilon > 0\),

\(\displaystyle |S_n (A_1, \ldots, A_n ; x) | \leqslant C_{\boldsymbol{A}, n, \epsilon} r^{- \sum_i d_i - \epsilon}, \qquad 0 < r \leqslant 1, \quad A_i \in F_{d_i}, \quad \min_{i \ne j} |x_i - x_j |_{\infty} \geqslant r. \) (13.15)

The same bound is uniform over bounded sets of fixed raw representatives. Its constant is independent of the diameter of the configuration. For fixed derivatives, replace the differentiated field by its degree-raised label; their total order is added to \(\sum_i d_i\).

13.8Bounded real approximations for separated words

The later phase argument needs real approximants bounded uniformly in the cutoff. Compactness of the link space at each cutoff is insufficient: the physical normalization of a local field grows as the mesh shrinks. We instead stop at one fixed coarse level and pull back a bounded coarse function through the probability block kernels.

We record the precise marked estimate used in this construction. It is the local-trial comparison for reflected tilings, with every term involving at least two insertions retained, not only its one-column coefficient estimate. At a coarse level \(h\), let \(J_h\) map the coefficients of a trial in the chosen basis to the coefficients of its selected level-\(h\) polynomial. Write \(C_h\) for the prescribed coefficient-column bound, \(Q_h\) for the ordinary norm bound of a normalized local trial, and \(E_h\) for its complete bound, including the complementary large-field part. For the selected trajectory these bounds and all fixed coefficient products are subpower in \(a_h^{- 1}\). The last-step endpoint estimate and chessboard seminorm estimate for the residual required here are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \|J_h - I\|_{1 \to 1} & \leqslant 2 (1 + C_h) Q_h L_{h - 1}^{- 1 / 8} \leqslant \tfrac{1}{4}, \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.16)} \\ \| \text{trial residual} \|_{\mathcal{C}_k} & \leqslant C_{k, m, d} E_h e^{- (\tau_h - \tau_m) / 8} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.17)} \end{array}\)

Here \(k, m\) are fixed before \(h\); \(\mathcal{C}_k\) is the centered cell tiling at level \(k\) from Section 13.2. Both estimates must concern the history restricted from the actual cutoff \(N\), uniformly in \(N \geqslant h\) and the physical period.

The first inequality is the endpoint comparison used in Proposition 11.6. Its final \(1 / 4\) is an additional finite stage requirement: require \(L_{h - 1}^{1 / 8} \geqslant 8 (1 + C_h) Q_h\). For the normalized small-field polynomial the local smooth cutoff is identically one on the ordinary chart, so its ordinary derivative constant is fixed before this block choice. Its global value is controlled by the square-root large-field bound, by a larger coupling threshold as in Section 11.11. This does not replace \(E_h\) by \(Q_h\) in the second estimate.

The second inequality requires more than a small coefficient linear in the sources. We prove its complete marked version next. The source weight is increased across successive scales; it is not increased by its entire final factor on an already formed coarse component.

Lemma 13.4. (The coarse-trial residual on reflected tilings) On the selected histories, after the finite coupling-threshold requirements specified below, (13.17) holds with all incoming regular and large-field terms involving at least two insertions retained. Its constant is independent of the outer cutoff, the period, and the number of replica cells.

Proof. Fix the degree ceiling before the schedule. At level \(i\), choose a positive number \(\zeta_i\) so that a term linear in the sources with complete normalized size at most \(\zeta_i\) fits every local attachment and adjacent comparison bound of Chapter 11. Include the finite signed symmetry orbit in these bounds. More explicitly, if \(D_i\) is the complete adjacent comparison constant and \(u_i > 0\) the terminal attachment bound, require

\(\displaystyle C_i \zeta_i \leqslant 1, \qquad 2 D_i \zeta_{i + 1} \leqslant 1, \qquad e^{81 \sigma_i + 162} Z_i^{\text{val}} \zeta_i / u_i \leqslant 1.\)

Here \(Z_i^{\text{val}}\) bounds the global section and its complementary cutoff part; this symbol denotes a bound, not a partition function. Also decrease \(\zeta_i\) to put the incidence sum for terms linear in the sources below the small constant in (11.11). These choices concern normalized coefficients, before the physical factor \(a_i^{- d}\).

These positive upper bounds can be chosen with \(| \log \zeta_i | = O_j (1 + \log \hat{\beta}_i)\) on every fixed stage. Indeed the section derivatives, inverse small-field thresholds, and adjacent comparison constants have finite powers of \(\hat{\beta}_i\), with stage-dependent constants. The terminal attachment bound may be taken as \(u_i = \tfrac{1}{4} \hat{\beta}_i^{- 1 / 2}\); the original large-field suppression is reserved separately in the attachment estimate and need not be inverted to define \(u_i\). The factor \(e^{81 \sigma_i + 162}\) is constant on each stage. Taking the minimum of this finite list of reciprocal bounds gives such a choice. At transitions between adjacent stages use the minimum of both lists. All requirements below therefore follow by increasing the coupling thresholds, since \(B_i\) grows as a positive constant times \((\log \hat{\beta}_i)^2\) on a fixed stage.

Choose \(0 < \phi_i \leqslant 1\) such that

\(\displaystyle \phi_i L_i^{d + 1} \max (1, C_{i + 1}) \zeta_i \leqslant \zeta_{i + 1}, \qquad \phi_i^{- 1} \leqslant e^{B_i / 128} . \) (13.18)

For example take the minimum of one and the ratio in the first inequality. The logarithm of its inverse is bounded by a finite stage constant times \(1 + \log \hat{\beta}_i\), plus \((d + 1) \log L_i\), and hence is eventually below \(B_i / 256\). Any finite local incidence bound \(\delta_i > 0\) can simultaneously be included so that \(\delta_i \phi_i e^{B_i / 128} \geqslant 1\): its inverse has at most another fixed power of \(\hat{\beta}_i\). This verifies the comparison and attachment bounds as well as the displayed, weaker upper bound on \(\phi_i^{- 1}\). These are countably many finite degree requests to the single schedule of coupling thresholds.

Put \(G (h, i) = e^{(\tau_h - \tau_i) / 8}\) and choose

\(\displaystyle w = \frac{\zeta_m a_m^d G (h, m)}{2 E_h}, \qquad \omega_i^{\text{res}} = w \prod_{j = m}^{i - 1} \phi_j .\)

The residual coefficients from (11.18), with the fixed harmless column factors included in \(E_h\), satisfy \(c_i + |x_i | \leqslant E_h / G (h, i)\). Since the ratio of \(a_i^{- d} / G (h, i)\) at successive levels is \(L_i^{d + 1 / 8}\), (13.18) gives

\(\displaystyle \omega_i^{\text{res}} a_i^{- d} (c_i + |x_i |) \leqslant \omega_i^{\text{res}} a_i^{- d} E_h / G (h, i) \leqslant \zeta_i / 2 \quad (m \leqslant i \leqslant h) . \) (13.19)

In particular the negative normalized local insertion used in the subtraction has normalized cost at most \(\zeta_h\) at \(h\). Above \(h\), its inverse coefficient bounds grow by at most \(C_{i + 1}\) per step; the same bound recurrence controls these factors and the physical factor \(L_i^d\). Thus its terminal source satisfies the joint attachment bound at \(N\), and its complete push to \(h\) is available at the weights \(\omega_i^{\text{res}}\).

Here is the support calculation for its terms involving at least two insertions, including those formed above \(h\). Open the centered cells at \(m\) with spacing at least \(B_m^2\). This spacing persists because \(L_i \geqslant 3\) and the adjacent support radii satisfy the schedule of (13.11). Apply reweighting on the incoming fine support \(X\). If it contains \(r \geqslant 2\) distinct marked ancestors, then \(B_{i + 1} r \leqslant 64 c_{B_{i + 1}} (X)\) by (11.10). Since \(B_i \leqslant B_{i + 1}\) and \(\omega_i^{\text{res}} / \omega_{i + 1}^{\text{res}} = \phi_i^{- 1}\), one factor from the incoming exponential support weight gives

\(\displaystyle (\omega_i^{\text{res}} / \omega_{i + 1}^{\text{res}})^r e^{- c_{B_{i + 1}} (X)} \leqslant e^{B_i r / 128 - c_{B_{i + 1}} (X)} \leqslant e^{- c_{B_{i + 1}} (X) / 2} .\)

To make this argument iterable, let \(A_i > 4\) be the full exponent maintained in the norm for terms involving at least two insertions at level \(i\), and impose

\(\displaystyle 10 \cdot 15^4 (A_i + 9) \leqslant L_i (A_{i + 1} - 4) .\)

Reserve four units of the incoming \(A_{i + 1}\) norm for this weight increase, site conversion, and attachments. The support contraction of Chapter 11 converts the remaining exponent \(A_{i + 1} - 4\) into the full outgoing exponent \(A_i\), including its local reserves. Thus the estimate does not lose two exponent units at each successive step. Choosing the bounds for terms linear in the sources small enough makes (11.11) preserve the bound one in these full norms for both arrays of terms involving at least two insertions. This calculation applies to every cardinality and period. A large-field array is treated as one incoming factor with its complete original prescribed set of large-field sites, not as a product of its coefficients linear in the sources. The terminal joint construction reserves the original large-field decay for the complementary factors. Hence there is no uncontrolled reweighting by \(w^r\) at the intermediate level \(h\).

At that level insert the positive local trial in every cell. If \(\mathscr{A}_h\) is the complete pushed negative source, the new polynomial is

\(\displaystyle \mathscr{A}_h \exp_{\text{nil}} \left( \sum_v z_v F_v \right) .\)

In its local representation this adds \(F_v\) only to the regular entry linear in the sources. All regular terms involving at least two insertions and all original large-field terms remain unchanged. The scalar part is exactly the coefficient of the identity in the global exponential. The nonconstant part multiplied by the smooth cutoff has zero complementary profile because its small-field restriction is included in its definition. Linearity makes the new term linear in the sources precisely the residual whose coefficients satisfy (13.19); its adjacent regular and large-field comparisons have bounds at most one by the choice of \(\zeta_i\). Descend this whole polynomial to \(m\). Its regular terms linear in the sources have bound at most two, and the large-field terms linear in the sources have bound at most one, and its two incidence bounds for terms involving at least two insertions are at most one.

Let \(\textsf{P}_{h, N}\) push densities from level \(N\) to level \(h\) by successive applications of (4.9) along the chosen history. Its adjoint \(\mathcal{P}_{N, h}\) pulls observables back from \(h\) to \(N\). Let \(\mathcal{I}\) index the cells of the reflected tiling and let \(- U_v\) denote the terminal insertion used for cell \(v\). In the product above the coefficient of \(\prod_{v \in \mathcal{I}} z_v\) at level \(h\) is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{A \subseteq \mathcal{I}} \textsf{P}_{h, N} \left( \rho_N \prod_{v \not\in A} (- U_v) \right) \prod_{v \in A} F_v .\)

Duality and the disjoint-cone identity (13.20) identify its integral with the original partition function times the expectation of \(\prod_v (\mathcal{P}_{N, h} F_v - U_v)\). No product of separately pushed densities for single insertions has replaced it. Equation (11.17) now yields

\(\displaystyle \|\mathcal{P}_{N, h} F - U\|_{\mathcal{C}_k} \leqslant w^{- 1} \exp ((a_k / a_m)^4 q_m^{\text{site}} + 5) = \frac{2 e^{(a_k / a_m)^4 q_m^{\text{site}} + 5}}{\zeta_m a_m^d} E_h e^{- (\tau_h - \tau_m) / 8} .\)

The prefactor depends only on the fixed stopping data. This proves (13.17), once the cones have the room verified below. All identities and estimates use the original selected history.\(\Box\)

Proposition 13.5. (Coarse bounded approximation) Use the selected histories with the stage requirements above. Fix a scaling-dimension bound \(d\), a real reference coefficient \(c\), a sufficiently fine centered tiling \(k\), and \(\delta > 0\). There are real observables \(\mathcal{B}_{N, p} (c, y)\) with a finite bound independent of \(N, p, y\), such that

\(\displaystyle \|\mathcal{B}_{N, p} (c, y) - O_{N, p} (d, c ; y)\|_{\mathcal{C}_k (y)} \leqslant \delta\)

on the ordered cutoff and volume tails. Both observables are interior to the same cell. The construction includes their signed and conjugated images with the same bound.

Proof. Fix \(m \geqslant k\) with \(B_m^2 \leqslant a_k / a_m\). At a finer level \(h\), use the coarse trial

\(\displaystyle V_h (v ; U) = a_h^{- d} (v_0 +\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{small-field stencil}} P_d^{\text{loc}} [S_{1 / t_h} v^{\circ}] (U)) .\)

The scalar part is retained exactly. The full inspected stencil includes the links needed by the relaxed small-field condition. For fixed \(h\) this is a bounded measurable function, with bound uniform in the outer cutoff and period: its coefficient range and allowed couplings are compact, and its section is a fixed polynomial in compact group variables.

Let \(\mathcal{K}_{N, h} (U, dV)\) be the composition of the one-step probability kernels \(k^{(L_i)}_{\beta} (U, V) \hspace{0.17em} dV\) of (4.8), in order from \(i = N - 1\) down to \(h\) and with the coupling of each step, and set \(\mathcal{P}_{N, h} F (U) = \int F (V) \mathcal{K}_{N, h} (U, dV)\). It preserves real values and contracts the supremum norm. Locality here must include every random variable used to choose a gauge-covariant block average or gauge frame. Conditional on the preceding link field, use independent primitive random variables for those choices and for each coarse-link sample. The full ancestor cone of a stencil contains its fine-link reads and all these random variables at every intermediate level. For disjoint cones, conditioning and integrating the independent variables gives

\(\displaystyle \prod_C \mathcal{P}_{N, h} F_C =\mathcal{P}_{N, h} \left( \prod_C F_C \right) . \) (13.20)

This does not assert that coarse links remain conditionally independent after a shared frame choice has been averaged out. The full cones, including that choice, must be disjoint. Here is an explicit uniform radius bound. Let \(j_i\) be the smoothing order in the step \(i + 1 \to i\). In Chapter 4, each endpoint frame reads a box of fine-lattice radius \((j_i + 3) L_i\); the other endpoint is at distance \(L_i\), and the connector paths stay in these boxes and the intervening strip. Thus every input link and every shared frame seed for an output link lies within physical distance \((j_i + 7) a_i\) of its initial endpoint, in the maximum norm. This bound also includes eligibility conditions and one boundary-link margin. If the coarse inspected stencil has radius \(r_d\) in level-\(h\) units, its full ancestor cone therefore has radius at most

\(\displaystyle r_d a_h + \sum_{i = h}^{N - 1} (j_i + 7) a_i .\)

The growing smoothing orders require a schedule condition, not merely finiteness at each step. Impose \((j_i + 7) \leqslant a_i^{- 1 / 2}\) at all sufficiently late levels. This is a permitted finite coupling-threshold requirement: if \(\jmath_q\) is the largest smoothing order used at stage \(q\) or its permitted adjacent pairs, require \(b_q \log 3 \geqslant 2 \log (\jmath_q + 7)\) among the coupling-threshold conditions of Section 10.2. When \(J (i + 1) = q > 1\), that schedule has \(i \geqslant b_q\) and \(a_i \leqslant 3^{- i}\), giving the desired inequality. The finitely many initial levels are irrelevant after increasing \(h\). Since \(a_{i + 1} \leqslant a_i / 3\), the radius is bounded, independently of \(N\), by

\(\displaystyle r_d a_h + \frac{a_h^{1 / 2}}{1 - 3^{- 1 / 2}} \longrightarrow 0.\)

Choose \(h\) so that this is smaller than the fixed interior margin of each reflected level-\(k\) cell. The cones, including their shared seeds, then lie strictly inside disjoint cells; sufficiently large physical periods prevent wrapping identifications.

The bound (13.16) gives \(\|J_h^{- 1} \|_1 \leqslant 2\). Thus the reference insertion with coefficient \(c\) is a linear combination of the trial columns, with sum of absolute coefficients bounded by \(2 \|c\|_1 \exp (\sum_{i < h} \log C_{i + 1})\). Replace these columns by \(\mathcal{P}_{N, h} V_h (e_j)\) and call the resulting observable \(\mathcal{B}'\). Its supremum is finite for fixed \(h\) and independent of \(N, p, y\).

Replicate the discrepancies before applying the kernel. At level \(h\) the coarse factors multiply the complete source density, including its terms involving at least two insertions. Equation (13.20) identifies the resulting residual with the product of the pullback discrepancies. Lemma 13.4 and recombination give

\(\displaystyle \|\mathcal{B}' - O (c)\|_{\mathcal{C}_k} \leqslant C_{k, m, d, c} \exp \left( \sum_{i < h} \log C_{i + 1} \right) E_h e^{- (\tau_h - \tau_m) / 8} .\)

The prefactor is \(e^{o (\tau_h)}\), so choose \(h\) to make this at most \(\delta\), before choosing the cutoff. For real \(c\) take \(\mathcal{B}= \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{B}'\); conjugation invariance and the triangle inequality for the chessboard seminorm preserve the bound. Translations and the finite symmetry orbit use the same stencils and constants. Outside the ordered tails set the approximants to zero if a bound for every index is desired.\(\Box\)

Corollary 13.6. (Real word differences) On these selected histories, the bounded-real approximation used for phase reflection in Section 15.4 holds on a cofinal set of generic grids. Its supremum bound is independent of copy number and copy position; approximation thresholds may depend on each fixed pair.

Proof. Choose the grid level after the degree bound and any prescribed lower level, then fix a generic real word \(w\) of length \(n\). Include all its signed and conjugated labels in a finite family. Choose centered cells with margins fitting between its distinct coordinate projections, and let \(C\) bound the single-insertion seminorms there. Apply Proposition 13.5 with error \(\delta \leqslant 1\). The approximants have seminorm at most \(C + \delta\), regardless of their possibly much larger supremum norms.

For a product of \(q\) such separated factors, telescoping and the chessboard inequality give

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle \prod_{a = 1}^q \mathcal{B}_a - \prod_{a = 1}^q O_a \right\rangle \right| \leqslant q \delta (C + \delta)^{q - 1} .\)

Form the difference \(g_j\) of the bounded products for a translated word and its linear time reflection, using the same single-insertion approximation at every translated copy. If their supremum bounds for single insertions are \(M_i\), then \(|g_j | \leqslant 2 \prod_i M_i = : R_{\varepsilon}\), independent of all copy positions and indices. For two sufficiently widely separated copies, expand their product of differences into four products of \(2 n\) factors. Choose \(\delta\) with \(8 n \delta (C + 1)^{2 n - 1} \leqslant \varepsilon\). The preceding inequality gives the desired pair error. Only finitely many configurations are involved at each fixed pair, so ordered tails can be intersected. The empty word difference is zero. Increasing the initial lower-level request gives the required cofinal grids.\(\Box\)

13.9Scaling dimension and the physical quotient

An estimate on a polynomial coefficient is useful for local product replacement only if it retains the degree of the field that coefficient represents. We derive the required quotient estimate from the triangular marked transfer. This step uses the corrected physical inclusions and the normalized-null statement of Section 13.3; those statements were established using the exact microscopic equation space.

Fix an output scaling dimension \(t\). For \(v \in V_t\), let \(x_l (v) = (x_{l, \alpha} (v))_{\alpha}\) be the vector of polynomial coefficients after transport to level \(l\), in the fixed basis of polynomials of scaling dimension at most \(t\). Write \(|x|_1 = \sum_{\alpha} |x_{\alpha} |\). The leading polynomial map at one step has the form \(M_l \textsf{d}_l\), where

\(\displaystyle (\textsf{d}_l x)_{\alpha} = L_l^{t - | \alpha |} x_{\alpha} .\)

Here \(| \alpha |\) is the scaling dimension of the basis polynomial, not its factor count. The leading map fixes the constant and is triangular: \((M_l)_{\alpha \beta} = 0\) off the diagonal whenever \(| \alpha | \leqslant | \beta |\). Both \(M_l\) and its inverse have norm at most the prescribed stage constant \(K_l\). The full map differs from it by the marked remainder bound of Section 11.4.1. Enlarge the prescribed column bounds \(C_l\) and block floors so that

\(\displaystyle 64 K_l^2 \leqslant C_l, C_{l + 1}, \qquad C_{l + 1} \leqslant L_l, \qquad 2 K_l L_l^{- 3 / 8} (1 + C_{l + 1}) \leqslant 1. \) (13.21)

These are finite-stage inequalities, with constants fixed before the block ratio, and the schedule gives \(\sum_{l < m} \log C_{l + 1} = o (\tau_m)\).

Proposition 13.7. (Graded quotient estimate) For \(0 \leqslant i \leqslant t\) there are constants \(C_{t, i} (m)\) with \(\log C_{t, i} (m) = o (\tau_m)\) such that the following holds. Suppose \(v_{N, p} \in V_t\) converges on an ordered cutoff and volume refinement to \(v\), and \(|x_m (v_{N, p}) |_1 \leqslant \epsilon\) on that tail. Then

\(\displaystyle \text{dist}_{F_t} ([v], \iota_{i - 1, t} F_{i - 1}) \leqslant C_{t, i} (m) a_m^{- (t - i)} \epsilon, \) (13.22)

where \(F_{- 1} = 0\). Constants are independent of the refinement and the convergent label family. The norm on \(F_t\) is fixed once.

Proof. For \(i > 0\) let \(P\) project onto scaling dimensions below \(i\). Triangularity makes \(\ker P\) invariant under \(M_l\) and its inverse. The degree scaling expands low coordinates by at least \(L_l^{t - i + 1}\), and high coordinates by at most \(L_l^{t - i}\). The one-step marked bound and (13.21) imply

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} |x_{l + 1} |_1 \leqslant C_{l + 1} |Px_{l + 1} |_1 & \Longrightarrow \quad |x_l |_1 \leqslant C_l |Px_l |_1, \quad L_l^{t - i + 1} |Px_{l + 1} |_1 \leqslant C_{l + 1} |Px_l |_1, \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.23)} \\ Px_{l + 1} = 0 & \Longrightarrow \quad |x_l |_1 \leqslant C_{l + 1} L_l^{t - i} |x_{l + 1} |_1 . \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.24)} \end{array}\)

To check the first implication, put \(Y = |P \textsf{d}_l x_{l + 1} |_1\). The premise and \(C_{l + 1} \leqslant L_l\) give \(| \textsf{d}_l x_{l + 1} |_1 \leqslant 2 Y\) and \(|x_{l + 1} |_1 \leqslant Y\). Applying the inverse leading map to the low part and absorbing the marked error gives \(Y \leqslant 2 K_l |Px_l |_1\). The full output is at most \(3 K_l Y\); (13.21) gives both assertions. When the low part is zero, the high-degree scaling and \(K_l + 1 \leqslant C_{l + 1}\) give (13.24).

At the microscopic level take the low plane \(PV_t\) and transport it. Equation (13.23) shows that its projection onto the low coordinates at every later level is injective, and hence onto that fixed-dimensional coordinate space. At level \(m\) subtract its vector with the same low coordinates as \(x_m (v_{N, p})\). The remaining coefficient has zero low part and norm at most \((1 + C_m) \epsilon\). Repeat the subtraction after each step towards the fixed reference level, applying (13.24) between subtractions. The sum of all subtracted vectors still lies in the transported microscopic low plane. Calling its reference label \(y_{N, p}\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle |v_{N, p} - y_{N, p} |_1 \leqslant G_m (1 + C_m) (a_{r (t)} / a_m)^{t - i} \epsilon, \qquad G_m = \prod_{l < m} (1 + C_l) C_{l + 1} .\)

Finite initial transfer and norm-equivalence constants are absorbed in \(G_m\). Its logarithm is \(o (\tau_m)\) by the prescribed column bounds.

It remains to identify this microscopic low plane with physical lower degrees; it is not the naive inclusion of unnormalized polynomials. The labels \(y_{N, p}\) are bounded by the displayed inequality. The expansion in (13.23), iterated from the microscopic level, bounds their microscopic low coefficients by \(a_N^{t - i + 1} e^{o (\tau_N)}\). Therefore their degree-\(t\) physical representatives can be written with the normalization \(a_N^{- (i - 1)}\) and coefficient/derivative growth \(e^{o (\tau_N)}\), on the same fixed stencil. Their global value bounds also have this growth, since the threshold inverses have subpower growth.

The local-trial comparison of Chapter 11 matches these representatives to degree-\((i - 1)\) reference labels with vanishing separated residuals. The matched coefficients need not be bounded. Apply the normalized-null representative argument of Section 13.3: division by one plus the norm of their corrected projection makes the equation remainder uniformly controlled, and its separated responses vanish. Since \(y_{N, p}\) has a bounded reference limit, that argument gives a physical degree-\((i - 1)\) field with exactly its limiting responses. The all-spectator definition and corrected inclusion thus put every such limit \([y]\) in \(\iota_{i - 1, t} F_{i - 1}\). Passing to a convergent subsequence of the bounded \(y_{N, p}\) proves (13.22). Extending a tail-defined bounded family by zero outside its tail changes none of these limits.

For \(i = 0\) no low plane is removed. The reverse coefficient estimate directly bounds \(|v|_1\) by \(e^{o (\tau_m)} (a_{r (t)} / a_m)^t \epsilon\). Passing to the quotient proves the same conclusion. All fixed reference spacings are absorbed in \(C_{t, i} (m)\).\(\Box\)

The power \(t - i\) in (13.22) is what recovers a coefficient of degree \(i\) from an auxiliary extraction at a much higher degree \(t\). An ungraded bound on \([v]\) would lose precisely that power. The proof also explains why identifying the lower field requires all spectator responses, even though its eventual coefficient is measured by a fixed finite family of probes.

13.10Local replacement of a cluster on the lattice

We prove a local replacement estimate before using any phase decomposition. It will also apply to mixed correlations with a fixed exterior word. Observable source variables and local polynomial extraction are standard renormalization-group tools; see, for example, [BBS15]. The quantitative estimates below concern the actual block kernels, activity norms and quotient fields constructed here; that reference does not supply these particular estimates.

For \(T \to \infty\), write \(f \preccurlyeq e^{cT}\) if, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), eventually \(|f| \leqslant e^{(c + \varepsilon) T}\) on an ordered cutoff and volume tail, uniformly on the specified bounded label sets and geometries. A factor \(e^{o (T)}\) is called subpower in this paragraph. Choose levels \(l = l (T)\) with \(\tau_l / T \to \theta\), where \(\tau_l = \log (1 / a_l)\). The schedule has \(\log L_l = o (\tau_l)\), so first-crossing levels supply any positive \(\theta\). For \(\theta = 0\) one may first cross \(\sqrt{T}\).

13.10.1Signed local transport

Extracting the polynomial part of a local degree-\(t\) marked density gives a coefficient vector \(x_l\). Write \(r_l\) for the norm of the remaining density, whose extracted polynomial part vanishes. The dimensionless density obeys \(H_l = L_l^t \mathcal{B}^{(L_l)}_{\beta} H_{l + 1}\), with \(\beta\) the inverse coupling used for the step \(l + 1 \to l\) in (4.9). The physical source density has no additional factor. These conventions are those of Section 11.4.1. Keep the full prescribed large-field set and its normalization by the square root of the large-field bound in this remainder norm.

The local-trial argument of Section 11.8 applies on any sufficiently late interval \([k_{\ast}, m]\), not only from the original microscopic level. In detail a remainder with vanishing extracted coefficient of size \(q\) has output coefficient and remainder at most \(L_l^{- 2 / 5} q\). The regular term uses its zero radial Taylor polynomial through \(t\); the large-field term retains half the suppression of its original prescribed set of large-field sites. Subtracting its output column restores the vanishing extracted coefficient and costs at most \((1 + 2 C_l) L_l^{- 2 / 5} q\). The block floor makes this at most \(L_l^{- 3 / 8} q\). This recentering is an equality of marked densities, including terms with prescribed large-field sites. It follows by linearity, so it applies also when an activity vanishes.

Add a free column parameter at the fine end and choose it so that the transported residual coefficient vanishes at \(k_{\ast}\). Invertibility of the column map permits this on the same history. The cone calculation in Section 11.8 then gives

\(\displaystyle r_l + |x_l |_1 \leqslant 4 (1 + C_m) q_{\text{fin}} (a_m / a_l)^{1 / 8}, \qquad r_m \leqslant 2 (1 + C_m) q_{\text{fin}} . \) (13.25)

Here \(q_{\text{fin}}\) is the combined original coefficient and remainder cost, before the affine column subtraction. To recall the numerical point, with \(Y = | \textsf{d}_l x_{l + 1} |_1\), \(U = |x_l |_1\) and \(\epsilon_l = L_l^{- 3 / 8}\) the two inequalities are \(Y \leqslant K_l [U + \epsilon_l (Y + r_{l + 1})]\) and \(r_l \leqslant K_l Y + \epsilon_l r_{l + 1}\). The cone \(32 (1 + C_l)^2 U \leqslant r_l\) gives \(Y \leqslant 4 K_l \epsilon_l r_{l + 1}\) and \(L_l^{1 / 8} r_l \leqslant r_{l + 1}\). It starts with \(x_{k_{\ast}} = 0\). At the fine end absorb the subtracted column cost to obtain the second inequality, and iterate for the first. The sign of the local difference has been retained throughout.

13.10.2Grouping marks and retaining their supports

Use the square-zero source algebra of Chapter 11. The total number of copies may be arbitrary, but a reflected cell contains a fixed number \(n\) of insertion marks. A group is a set of these marks with a common representative. Grouping is bookkeeping for supports and source weights: original variables and every joint mode remain in the expansion. In particular partial modes of a group are not discarded, and grouping is not asserted to be an algebra homomorphism replacing their product by a new variable.

Recall that \(c_B (X)\) counts the radius-\(B\) balls in a smallest connected cover of \(X\), and \(\sigma\) is its exponential support weight. If a marked support \(X\) has to contain new representatives joined by at most \(h\) cover balls per mark, then

\(\displaystyle c_B (X \cup \{y_i : i \in I\}) \leqslant c_B (X) + h |I| .\)

Shrinking each affected weight by \(\eta e^{- \sigma h}\) absorbs this support enlargement. The incidence norm has a second cost: a new incidence can be assigned to an old mark on its joining path. If at most \(\mathfrak{N}\) representatives occupy a cover ball, there are at most \((1 +\mathfrak{N}) n\) assignments per representative. Take \(\eta \leqslant [(1 +\mathfrak{N}) n]^{- 1}\). Unmarked supports are unchanged. This is a termwise estimate on the complete marked series, hence is uniform in its number of replica cells.

At each level retain a maximal \(B_l^2\)-separated set of representatives, keeping the representative of the center, and join each discarded representative to a retained neighbor. A merger decreases the number of groups; there are at most \(n - 1\) mergers within a cell. A deliberately generous joining bound is

\(\displaystyle h_l = 16 [1 + L_l (B_l + B_{l + 1} + 1)^2]^4 .\)

Impose \((\sigma_{l + 1} + 1) h_l = o (\tau_l)\) by choosing the stage coupling thresholds. At fixed stage \(h_l\) is a polynomial in \(\log \hat{\beta}_l\) with fixed ratio, so this is a finite admissible request. The occupancy factors have the same property. The minimum \(\alpha_j \leqslant 1\) of all weight-reduction factors for support enlargement on \(m \leqslant l < j\) therefore has \(- \log \alpha_j = o (\tau_j)\): split the maximum cost into a fixed initial segment and a tail bounded by \(\varepsilon \tau_j\), and then let \(\varepsilon \downarrow 0\).

Between mergers a single live group costs at most \(6 L_l\) in its weight. Here the incoming regular contribution is carried without Taylor subtraction. Its full support conversion cost is \(T_l \leqslant L_l\), as in (11.5); it must not be assigned the contraction factor for a vanishing extracted coefficient. If its incoming mass is \(\mathfrak{m}\), the other local terms with vanishing extracted coefficient have total mass \(p_l\) with \(2 p_l \leqslant \mathfrak{m}\) after increasing the coupling threshold, and the large-field terms have normalized mass \(f_l \leqslant \mathfrak{m}\). These are the termwise local bounds used above, retaining the original events. Resolving real and imaginary parts costs at most two, so

\(\displaystyle 2 (p_l + T_l \mathfrak{m}) + f_l \leqslant (2 L_l + 2) \mathfrak{m} \leqslant 6 L_l \mathfrak{m} \leqslant L_l^4 \mathfrak{m}.\)

The last inequality holds for \(L_l \geqslant 3\). It applies in the submultiplicative marked norm to a whole incoming joint factor; it does not multiply separate bounds for correlated marks. With \(g\) groups left, their available product weight is consequently at least

\(\displaystyle (a_j / a_l)^{4 n} (\alpha_j / 8)^{n (n - g)} \prod_i w_i . \) (13.26)

Each step loses at most \(L_l^{- 4 n}\), and a merger uses at most \(n\) of the weight-reduction factors for support enlargement. This proves the formula by induction, including all support and prescribed set of large-field sites data.

13.10.3Extraction with a freely chosen residual weight

Put

\(\displaystyle \delta_l^{\text{mark}} = \hat{\beta}_{l + 1}^{- 2}, \quad \phi_l = \hat{\beta}_{l + 1}^{- 1}, \quad \Xi_l = e^{\sqrt{\hat{\beta}_{l + 1}}}, \quad y_{t, l} = \hat{\beta}_l^{- \nu_t} .\)

Choose the fixed integer \(\nu_t\) to absorb the finite polynomial and source-attachment losses at degree \(t\). The stage coupling thresholds ensure \(y_{t, l} C_l \leqslant 1\) and \(\phi_l L_l^{t + 1} y_{t, l} \leqslant y_{t, l + 1}\). These are finite threshold requests after the ratio is fixed. All their logarithms, as well as \(\log \Xi_l\), are \(o (\tau_l)\).

For a fixed exterior reflection cell at level \(k\), let

\(\displaystyle k_{\ast} = \min \{l \geqslant k : B_l^2 \leqslant a_k / a_l \}, \qquad N_k = \exp ((a_k / a_{k_{\ast}})^4 q_{k_{\ast}}^{\text{site}} + 5) .\)

The sparse-stopping argument of (13.11) gives \(\tau_{k_{\ast}} / \tau_k \to 1\) and \(\log N_k = o (\tau_{k_{\ast}})\). Indeed if \(k_{\ast} = l + 1 > k\), minimality gives \(a_k / a_{k_{\ast}} \leqslant L_l B_l^2\); the last condition of that equation bounds both the normalization cost and \(\tau_{k_{\ast}} - \tau_k\) by \(o (\tau_{k_{\ast}})\). When \(\tau_k = o (T)\) the same proof gives \(\log N_k = o (T)\).

Suppose the cluster has become one group at level \(m + 1\), with available weight \(\omega\). Set

\(\displaystyle q_m^{\text{ext}} = 4 \Xi_m a_{m + 1}^t / \omega, \qquad E_m = 8 (1 + C_m) q_m^{\text{ext}} .\)

Taylor extraction through radial degree \(t\) gives coefficient and complement each bounded by \(q_m^{\text{ext}}\). To verify this, divide by the available weight, apply the integral Taylor formula in the local derivative norm, and sum the finite coefficient conversions and support moments with their positive exponent reserve. Their costs are fixed stage constants and powers of logarithmic radii and inverse thresholds, hence eventually at most \(\Xi_m\). The factor \(a_{m + 1}^t\) converts the physical source to the normalized degree-\(t\) density. Large-field terms keep their original normalized majorants. Extraction of the coefficient of all marks in a group is a linear operation on the exact density; products of partial modes are retained before taking it. Each term is assigned the union of its supports and original prescribed set of large-field sites, so this operation never divides by the unmarked gas.

Choose a residual weight \(\varpi > 0\) at \(k_{\ast}\) and thereafter \(\varpi_{l + 1} = \phi_l \varpi_l\). The two sufficient inequalities are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \varpi (a_m / a_{k_{\ast}})^{t + 1} y_{t, m} / y_{t, k_{\ast}} & \leqslant \delta_m^{\text{mark}} \omega / (16 \Xi_m),\\ 32 (1 + C_m) \varpi a_{k_{\ast}}^{- t} E_m (a_m / a_{k_{\ast}})^{1 / 8} & \leqslant y_{t, k_{\ast}} / 2. \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.27)} \end{array}\)

The first absorbs the local polynomial coefficient bound and the second the bound for its signed transport. Indeed the recursion for these bounds gives \(\varpi_l \leqslant \varpi (a_l / a_{k_{\ast}})^{t + 1} y_{t, l} / y_{t, k_{\ast}}\). Combining this with (13.25) and the physical factor \(a_l^{- t}\), the ratio of required to allowed weight at level \(l\) is at most its value at \(k_{\ast}\) times \((a_l / a_{k_{\ast}})^{7 / 8} \leqslant 1\). Thus no intermediate level has been left unchecked.

The extracted matched field has coefficient norm at level \(m\) at most \(2 E_m\). The interior residual has chessboard seminorm at most \(2 N_k / \varpi\). To see the latter on arbitrarily many copies, put one residual in every reflected cell before integrating. Taking the coefficient of the required source monomial in the marked sum costs \(\varpi^{- 1}\) per cell; the normalized integral bound (11.17) costs \(N_k\) per cell. Apply this separately to the original local difference and its compensating column, and use the chessboard triangle inequality. The rooted component estimates and bounds used in this calculation depend on marks per cell, not on the number of cells. All the terms involving at least two insertions generated during transport are included in these sums. The estimate is therefore stronger than a bound on one tested mean.

Proposition 13.8. (Lattice cluster replacement) Let a cluster contain \(n\) fields of total degree \(W\). Choose \(0 \leqslant \theta_- < \theta < \theta_+\) and levels \(k, m, j\) with \(\tau_k / T \to \theta_-\), \(\tau_m / T \to \theta\), and \(\tau_j / T \to \theta_+\). The cluster lies inside the centered level-\((m + 1)\) block, its points lie on the level-\(j\) grid with separation at least \(B_j^2 a_j\), and its full dependence supports fit inside the level-\(k\) cell. Put \(Z = W \theta_+ + 4 n (\theta_+ - \theta)\). For each fixed auxiliary degree \(t\) there are output labels \(v_{N, p}^{\text{out}} \in V_t\), on the actual histories, satisfying

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} |x_m (v_{N, p}^{\text{out}}) |_1 & \preccurlyeq e^{(Z - t \theta) T},\\ \left| \prod_i O_{N, p} (v_i, x_i) - O_{N, p} (t, v_{N, p}^{\text{out}} ; 0) \right|_{\mathcal{C}_k} & \preccurlyeq e^{[Z - (t + 1 / 16) (\theta - \theta_-)] T} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.28)} \end{array}\)

The difference is interior. Constants and thresholds are uniform in cutoff and period on the ordered tails and in the number of copies of this fixed local cluster.

Proof. The weights of normalized local insertions in (13.13) give \(\prod_i w_i = e^{- W \theta_+ T + o (T)}\). Keep all incoming modes above \(j\); separation permits their marked bounds but does not assert statistical independence. The grouping estimate gives \(\omega = e^{- ZT + o (T)}\) at \(m + 1\), and therefore \(E_m = e^{(Z - t \theta) T + o (T)}\). Set

\(\displaystyle \varpi = \exp ([(t + 1 / 16) (\theta - \theta_-) - Z] T) .\)

In the first inequality of (13.27), the left exponent is \(- Z - 15 (\theta - \theta_-) / 16\), strictly smaller than the right exponent \(- Z\). In the second, the full left exponent is \(- (\theta - \theta_-) / 16 < 0\) while the right side has exponent zero. Both strict margins absorb every subpower factor. The extraction estimate and \(\log N_k = o (T)\) give the claim. All support shifts take place within the specified cell; reflections and conjugations retain the same bounds.\(\Box\)

13.10.4A mixed-correlation consequence without phases

Fix finite correlation reconstruction maps onto the filtered spaces \(F_D\). More explicitly, choose a basis in each successive quotient and extend it to \(F_D\), with dual coordinates realized by finite linear combinations of separated probe correlations. Arrange that a degree-\(i\) coordinate vanishes on \(F_{i - 1}\), and that reconstruction fixes every field in \(F_D\). Keep each old coordinate functional as its original correlation formula on every larger field space. When lifting a new quotient basis, subtract its values under all the old coordinates, so those coordinates annihilate the new basis vectors. This is possible by finite-dimensional separation by all spectator correlations and successive subtraction of lower degrees. Consequently the degree-\(D\) reconstruction of a field in \(F_t\), \(t > D\), is exactly its low-degree component in this fixed biorthogonal system. Denote the resulting reconstruction of a word by \(\mathcal{P}_D w\). It depends on the cluster, not on its later spectator. No rotation average or phase measure is used here.

Proposition 13.9. (Mixed local replacement) Let \(x_i = o + rz_i\), with the \(z_i\) in a fixed compact separated configuration set, and let \(W\) be the total cluster degree. For any fixed separated exterior word \(v\), any \(D\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} |b_{\alpha} (w ; o) | & \leqslant Cr^{| \alpha | - W - \varepsilon},\\ |S (wv) - S ((\mathcal{P}_D (\tau_{- o} w)) (o) v) | & \leqslant Cr^{D + 1 - W - \varepsilon} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.29)} \end{array}\)

Here \(b_{\alpha}\), \(| \alpha | \leqslant D\), are the fixed probe coordinates. The same field \(\mathcal{P}_D (\tau_{- o} w)\) works for every exterior word. The estimates are uniform on fixed bounded label sets and separated geometries, and hold with any fixed finite positional derivatives, with their increased scaling dimensions.

Proof. First use generic level-\(j\) grid points. Put \(T = \log (1 / r)\) and choose \(0 < \theta_- < \theta < 1 < \theta_+\). The cluster then fits the block and separation conditions for large \(T\). For each fixed geometry, the output labels in (13.28) are bounded at fixed \(m\) and hence at the reference level by the inverse-column bound. Take a coefficient-convergent refinement and call its field \(A_t\). The chessboard inequality bounds its residual tested against any fixed exterior word by exponent \(Z - (t + 1 / 16) (\theta - \theta_-)\). The same refinement works for all spectators, since the coefficient limit was taken before testing.

The graded quotient estimate (13.22) shows that a degree-\(i\) coordinate of \(A_t\) has exponent \(Z - i \theta\). Its coordinate functional vanishes on \(F_{i - 1}\); this is where an ungraded coefficient estimate would lose the desired power. Choose \(t\) so large that \((t + 1 / 16) (\theta - \theta_-) \geqslant (D + 1) \theta\). This also bounds every raw coordinate being evaluated, whose degree is at most \(D\). We do not require the residual to bound all coordinates through the auxiliary degree \(t\). Testing the residual against the fixed probes bounds the coordinate errors. Reconstruct \(A_t\) and omit degrees \(i > D\). Each omitted coordinate has exponent at most \(Z - (D + 1) \theta\), and its fixed exterior correlations are bounded. This gives that exponent for the raw replacement error, and \(Z - i \theta\) for each raw coordinate.

For completeness the grid restriction can be removed without assuming differentiability of the selected coefficients of \(A_t\). Use the fixed correlation reconstruction instead. Its coefficients and its tested residual are smooth functions of the cluster positions. Their derivatives replace an input by a field of degree one higher. The sharp separated bounds, using fixed cells for exterior probes and shrinking cells only for cluster factors, give a crude exponent \(W + 1\): choose total excess one. Thus one can take \(c_0 = 1\). The grid exponent is \(\theta_+ W + c_1\), where \(c_1 = 4 n (\theta_+ - \theta) - (D + 1) \theta\) for the residual, and \(c_1 = 4 n (\theta_+ - \theta) - i \theta\) for a degree-\(i\) coordinate. These numbers are independent of \(W\) with \(n, D, i\) fixed. Multiplicative constants may depend on each of the finitely many higher derivative orders used in the following induction. For all sufficiently large total degrees the crude estimate implies the grid target, because \(\theta_+ > 1\). Descend in total degree. Moving to a generic grid configuration costs \(O (a_j) = e^{- \theta_+ T + o (T)}\); the derivative bound at degree \(W + 1\) gives precisely the target exponent at \(W\). The connecting paths remain separated. Only finitely many higher derivatives are needed for each starting degree, so their constants and thresholds can be chosen together. Finally let \(\theta \uparrow 1\) and \(\theta_+ \downarrow 1\) and absorb the remaining loss into the prescribed \(\varepsilon\). This proves the mixed estimate using neither phase symmetry nor an operator-product theorem.\(\Box\)
