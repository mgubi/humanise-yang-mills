Chapter 9
The nonlinear renormalization step

A block transformation acts on the density, not on its chosen list of local terms. Our task is to express its exact output in the coordinates of Chapter 4, while improving the bounds enough to repeat the operation. The organization by local interactions and large-field regions follows the constructive renormalization method of Bałaban [Bał88]. We use the cluster theorem of Chapter 8; the work here is the identification, normalization and estimation of its concrete activities.

Fix one adjacent pair of scales, with fine scale \(m + 1\) and coarse scale \(m\). Write \(L = L_m\), \(B = B_m\), \(t = t_m\), and \(\mathfrak{b}= \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}\). The actual incoming inverse coupling is \(\beta' \in [\mathfrak{b}/ 2, 2\mathfrak{b}]\). All event indicators use the fixed reference thresholds of (4.15). They do not move when \(\beta'\) varies. Write the prescribed coarse path action as

\(\displaystyle P^{\text{path}}_{N, m} (\beta, U) = \beta \mathcal{K} (U) +\mathcal{L} (U),\)

where \(\mathcal{K}\) includes its cubic and quartic terms and \(\mathcal{L}\) is the quadratic loop correction. This identity defines \(\mathcal{K}, \mathcal{L}\) from (4.20). Neither is the unknown effective action.

For this induction the derivative orders and spatial exponents are stronger than the minimum allowed in the density definition: take \(q_{m + 1} \geqslant q_m \geqslant 6\) and \(\varpi_{m + 1} \geqslant \varpi_m \geqslant 2\), with the prescribed derivative reserves for the local integral estimates. In particular the normalized affine directions used below belong to the activity seminorm. At the finest scale choose \(0 < c_{\text{dir}} \leqslant 1\).

The estimates below distinguish proved algebraic implications from the analytic inputs of Chapters 6 and 7. In particular a product estimate under one Gaussian law, a local low-order matching estimate, and a one-loop drift estimate are different inputs.

9.1The exact local integral and its first expansion

Let \(\mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}} =\mathcal{B}_m^{\beta'} \text{dens} (\rho')\), with the exact kernel (4.9). In each sector one first performs the local coordinate change on its active links and leaves the other links in probability Haar measure. Chapter 6 gives the exact Gaussian identity, including the difference between the chosen finite-range covariance and the inverse precision.

Here is the scalar bookkeeping in a form useful for checking reassembly. Suppose there are \(N_{\text{free}}\) free tree-gauge bonds, of which \(n_{\text{act}}\) use Lie algebra coordinates, and let \(D\) be the dimension after dummy Gaussian coordinates have been added. Set \(g = (\beta')^{- 1 / 2}\), \(q = \dim \mathfrak{g}\), and \(c_{\text{H}}\) equal to the density of probability Haar measure in the chosen exponential coordinates at zero. With \(a = \mu + gz\), the measure multiplier before the Haar logarithm is

\(\displaystyle (2 \pi)^{- (D - n_{\text{act}} q) / 2} g^{n_{\text{act}} q} c_{\text{H}}^{n_{\text{act}}} = (2 \pi)^{- D / 2} \mathfrak{m}^{N_{\text{free}}} e^{- (N_{\text{free}} - n_{\text{act}}) \log \mathfrak{m}}, \quad \mathfrak{m}= c_{\text{H}} (2 \pi g^2)^{q / 2} .\)

The dummy Gaussian integral equals one after the inactive Haar variables have been frozen; Fubini then permits their integration. The flat reference determinant uses the same forest as the sector. It is extracted once, not separately for every eventual connected component. The local precision correction, Haar logarithm, missing-coordinate factor and stationary residual remain among the local exponential factors. Keeping them is what recovers the original Haar integral.

Expand exponentials as \(e^V = 1 + (e^V - 1)\) and retain all sector allocation indicators. A complete dependence region contains every Haar coordinate, tag variable and Gaussian covariance halo read by its factor. If two such supports are disjoint, the Haar and tag coordinates are independent; the Gaussian cross covariance is zero because the chosen covariance has finite range. Their integrals therefore factor. Connected components of intersecting dependence regions define the first polymer activities.

This factorization concerns dependence regions. It does not assert that large-field activities with disjoint exclusion supports \(Y_i\) have disjoint full dependence regions. The large-field indicators in their eligibility criteria can read outside \(Y_i\). Those conditions remain in every exact-cover expression.

The required simultaneous estimate for a connected product is

\(\displaystyle \int G_M \hspace{0.17em} d \nu \leqslant P_j (\mathfrak{b}) (1 + s_M)^{d_j} e^{- r_j \beta' t_{m + 1}^2 |A_M |} \prod_{a \in M} u_a . \) (9.1)

Here \(\nu\) is the one sector Gaussian/Haar law, \(G_M\) majorizes the product and its prescribed ordered derivatives, \(s_M\) counts the full dependence region in radius-\(B\) blocks, and \(A_M\) is its selected large-field condition set. The function \(P_j\) has subpower growth: \(\log P_j (\mathfrak{b}) / \log \mathfrak{b} \to 0\) at fixed stage and ratio. Distinct cutoff-failure labels each contribute their own factor to the product. The bounded-jet estimate (6.26), applied to the primitive factors constructed in Chapter 6, proves this joint bound on their common law. The alternative quadratic-tilt criterion in Proposition 6.6 is not required here. Separate bounds on \(\int |F_a | \hspace{0.17em} d \nu\) cannot replace this estimate.

The elementary power counting is as follows. The entries include the prescribed finite number of derivatives.

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{l|l} \text{factor after deterministic subtraction} & \text{majorant}\\ \hline \text{stationary cubic remainder} & P_j \beta' g^3\\ \text{centered determinant variation} & P_j (t_{m + 1} + g)\\ \text{Haar remainder} & P_j (t_{m + 1} + g)^2\\ \text{inherited quadratic-loop variation} & P_j \Lambda_{m + 1}^{\text{loop}} (t_{m + 1} + g) g\\ \text{covariance and stationary residual} & P_j \mathfrak{b}^{c_j} e^{- c'_j B}\\ \text{incoming regular activity} & P_j \epsilon_{m + 1}^{\text{reg}}\\ \text{cutoff failure at a distinct label} & P_j \mathfrak{b}^{- 1 / 2} \end{array}\)

For example \(\beta' g^3 = g\), and the stationary mean is \(O (gM_{m + 1}^{\text{cut}})\), so a cubic Taylor term is bounded by \(g\) times a polynomial in \(M_{m + 1}^{\text{cut}}\) and Gaussian coordinates. The latter is estimated inside the common integral. The other rows follow from the determinant inverse identity, the second-order vanishing of the Haar correction, and the window errors of Chapter 6. The loop coefficient bound must grow subpolynomially at fixed ratio.

After increasing the inverse coupling threshold these rows are bounded by \(\mathfrak{b}^{- 7 / 16}\), since a subpower factor can be bounded by \(\mathfrak{b}^{1 / 16}\). Read-count polynomials are absorbed by additional exponential support decay. The first component expansion and then Theorem 8.1 give an absolutely convergent logarithm of the ordinary gas without large-field sites. Components attached to a prescribed large-field site set remain in the exact-cover sum. The deletion identity (8.11) accounts for ordinary polymers excluded by a selected large-field region. Repeated polymer labels in the logarithm are counted at every occurrence, with the factor \(1 / n\)! of (8.7).

All rearrangements can first be made for finite selections. Under (9.1) and the strict cluster criterion, the absolute envelopes allow passage to the countable sums. Thus this procedure gives an identity for \(\mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}}\), rather than an asymptotic expansion for its logarithm.

9.2Improving the regular remainder

The first bound \(\mathfrak{b}^{- 7 / 16}\) is too large for the target \(\epsilon_m^{\text{reg}} = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 11 / 10}\). There are three different sources of improvement.

9.2.1Terms containing several elementary factors

Give every elementary ordinary factor a degree one, and give a first-expansion polymer the sum of its factors' degrees. A cluster degree is the sum over its polymer occurrences. Multiply each elementary majorant by \(h =\mathfrak{b}^{3 / 8}\) before performing either expansion. The resulting local smallness parameter is \(h\mathfrak{b}^{- 7 / 16} =\mathfrak{b}^{- 1 / 16}\). The weighted rooted bound of Section 8.3, applied with these inflated activities, bounds the whole sum by \(P_j h\mathfrak{b}^{- 7 / 16}\). Restoring the original weights for terms of total degree at least three gives

\(\displaystyle P_j h^{- 3} (h\mathfrak{b}^{- 7 / 16}) = P_j \mathfrak{b}^{- 19 / 16} \leqslant \mathfrak{b}^{- 23 / 20} . \) (9.2)

The gap \(19 / 16 - 23 / 20 = 3 / 80\) absorbs the remaining subpower factor. This counts elementary factors, not just vertices in the second cluster expansion; a single polymer may already have degree three. The same calculation applies after placing a fixed number of derivatives.

An incoming regular activity accompanied by a further factor is bounded by the rooted estimate with that incoming activity distinguished: \(P_j \epsilon_{m + 1}^{\text{reg}} \mathfrak{b}^{- 1 / 2}\). Long path terms have an extra factor \(e^{- c_j B}\). Both fit the right side of (9.2) after increasing the threshold.

9.2.2The linear inherited regular term

The component linear in an incoming regular activity must use its affine-Hessian cancellation. In local radial gauge, the lifted potential splits into parts with inverse-ratio sizes \(L^{- 1}, L^{- 2}, L^{- 3}\): a constant part, its affine variation, and its remainder. The constant part is removed by a local gauge transformation. Curvature therefore begins at weight two. Invariance under constant gauge transformations remove linear curvature terms, and the incoming affine-Hessian condition removes the quadratic curvature term of weight four. Every surviving selection in the ordered Taylor expansion has weight at least five.

Here is the support estimate needed to sum that local calculation. Let \(d\) be the incoming covering number. For \(d^3 < L\) the inspected support admits a nonwrapping radial gauge and the per-label gain is \(L^{- 5}\). For the other supports use the original bound. Both cases are bounded by

\(\displaystyle \delta_L (d) \leqslant L^{- 5} (1 + d)^{15}, \qquad \delta_L (d) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} L^{- 5}, & d^3 < L,\\ 1, & d^3 \geqslant L. \end{array} \right.\)

The full chain rule adds a fixed polynomial in \(d\). Choose the scale ratio and incoming support exponent so that, after support compression, the outgoing weight uses at most half of the incoming exponential decay. The remaining decay absorbs the polynomial in \(d\). There are \(L^4\) fine anchors above each coarse anchor. Consequently the inherited principal norm is at most \(V_j L^{- 1} \epsilon_{m + 1}^{\text{reg}}\), with \(V_j\) fixed before \(L\).

For the comparison with the actual integral use the same forest and physical local coordinates on both sides. The estimates of Section 6.5.4 give a covariance difference \(O (P (B) t_f)\) and a mean difference, in Gaussian units, of order \(P (B) g (M_f^{\text{cut}})^2 + P (B) M_f^{\text{cut}} e^{- cB}\), where \(t_f = t_{m + 1}\) and \(g = (\beta')^{- 1 / 2}\). Each is \(\mathfrak{b}^{- 1 / 2 + o (1)}\). To see where the gain occurs, for a covariance interpolation \(C_s\) with fixed positive lower bound, the logarithmic density derivative is

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{2} \{ z^{\ast} C_s^{- 1} \dot{C}_s C_s^{- 1} z - \text{tr} (C_s^{- 1} \dot{C}_s) \} .\)

A varying mean adds its linear Gaussian score. Every coefficient contains a covariance or normalized-mean difference. Its fixed moments therefore contain the small factor just stated, times a polynomial in the local dimension.

Hold the physical links in the incoming activity fixed by the protected coordinate change of Chapter 6. The nonlinear coordinate correction is then differentiated through its Jacobian and density, where the same gain is available. This avoids requiring one extra derivative of the incoming activity when adjacent levels have equal derivative orders. Telescope all remaining factor products, marking one changed factor. The nonquadratic local weights contribute a gain of order \(g\) with subpower losses; a second incoming activity contributes \(O ((\epsilon_{m + 1}^{\text{reg}})^2)\) instead. Common Gaussian moments and the unused support exponent sum the resulting polynomial costs. Together with the principal estimate this proves

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{E}_{\sigma + 3} (\text{inherited term}) \leqslant (V_j L^{- 1} + \omega_{j, L} (\hat{\beta}_m)) \epsilon_{m + 1}^{\text{reg}}, \qquad \omega_{j, L} (\hat{\beta}_m) = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 2 + o (1)} .\)

For this comparison the local windows and their dependence halos must be clean. A window meeting a selected large-field component is assigned to that component; a failed remote cutoff has the joint Gaussian-tail bound of Chapter 6. Large remaining supports gain \(e^{- cB}\) from the unused incoming support weight. Thus no comparison between unrelated event indicators or a seeded and an unseeded law has been made. The actual interpolation estimates are essential; the scaling-dimension count alone would not prove the comparison remainder.

9.2.3Low-order terms and localization

One- and two-factor terms must be combined before estimating them. The degree-four classical matching and quadratic determinant matching must apply to the same integral and stationary branch. Rather than use a formal cumulant remainder, we retain a finite stationary region and estimate its complement. The resulting centered auxiliary bound, with \(h =\mathfrak{b}^{- 7 / 16}\), is

\(\displaystyle P_j (\mathfrak{b}) \{ \mathfrak{b}^{- 1} h +\mathfrak{b}h^5 + h^3 \} +\mathfrak{b}^{- K}, \) (9.3)

where \(K\) can be any fixed positive number. Its largest power is \(\mathfrak{b}^{- 19 / 16 + o (1)}\), which is smaller than \(\mathfrak{b}^{- 23 / 20}\). The argument below specifies the actual sector and stationary estimates needed to obtain this bound.

9.2.4The stationary integral with its cutoff retained

We now give the local analytic implication behind the auxiliary bound. It is an application of Laplace's method, for which we refer to [Won01, Chapter II], with three features that must be checked here: the integration dimension grows with the auxiliary period, the stationary Hessian depends on the coarse field, and the integration cutoff cannot be discarded before estimating its complement. All actions and matrices in the following argument belong to the same pure small-field block integral: incoming activities are omitted, the prescribed path and block kernel are unchanged, and the fine-field indicator \(\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\chi (U_p) \leqslant t_f \text{for every fine } p\}}\) is retained, where \(t_f = t_{m + 1}\). Thus its integrand is zero on the fine large-field complement. The other fine sectors have already been assigned to the large-field expansion.

Put \(z = \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}\), \({g = \beta'}^{- 1 / 2}\) and \(h = z^{- 7 / 16}\). The letter \(z\) is a scalar inverse coupling parameter in this subsection; the Gaussian integration variable is \(\xi\). Auxiliary periods and coordinate counts are bounded by fixed powers of \(1 + \log z\). Write \(M = M_{m + 1}^{\text{cut}}\) for the cutoff amplitude in (4.15), and take \(D = d_0 M\) with a fixed small \(d_0 > 0\). We shall use the following two distinct facts:

\(\displaystyle M = z^{o (1)}, \qquad (1 + \log z)^k = o (M^{\epsilon}) \quad \text{for every fixed } k, \epsilon > 0.\)

Both follow directly by taking logarithms of \(M = \exp ((\log \log z)^2)\).

Let \(x\) be the coarse logarithmic coordinates, with their supremum norm, and \(y \in \mathbb{R}^n\) the free fine coordinates after tree gauge fixing. The actual classical action including the block penalty is \(\phi (x, y)\); write \(\rho (x)\) for its stationary branch and \(A (x) = D_y^2 \phi (x, \rho (x))\) for its positive Hessian. The extra exponent \(\psi (x, y)\) includes the fine loop term and minus the logarithm of the product Haar-chart Jacobian. Suppose the branch, action, extra exponent and relative logarithmic determinant have the fixed derivative orders required below on a common neighborhood, with bounds \(z^{o (1)}\). The Hessian gap is uniform in the auxiliary volume at the fixed block ratio. These are the stationary and path estimates of Chapters 5–7; no different flat covariance is substituted here.

Set \(s (x) = \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det A (x)\) and define the smooth divided difference

\(\displaystyle V (g, x, \xi) = \int_0^1 (1 - t) D_y^2 \phi (x, \rho (x) + tg \xi) [\xi, \xi] \hspace{0.17em} dt.\)

Stationarity gives \(\phi (x, \rho (x) + g \xi) - \phi (x, \rho (x)) = g^2 V (g, x, \xi)\), including the limiting value \(V (0, x, \xi) = \tfrac{1}{2} \xi^{\ast} A (x) \xi\). With \(\gamma_0 = N (0, A (0)^{- 1})\), put

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} a (g, x, \xi) & = s (x) - s (0) + V (0, 0, \xi) - V (g, x, \xi)\\ & \quad + \psi (x, \rho (x)) - \psi (x, \rho (x) + g \xi),\\ R (g, x) & = \int_{| \xi | \leqslant D} e^{a (g, x, \xi)} \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_0 (\xi), \qquad L (g, x) = \log R (g, x) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(9.4)} \end{array}\)

The determinant factor is essential: at \(g = 0\) the exact identity is

\(\displaystyle R (0, x) = N (0, A (x)^{- 1}) \{ \hspace{0.17em} | \xi | \leqslant D \hspace{0.17em} \},\)

which is a truncated mass, not identically one. Changing \(\xi\) to \(- \xi\) also gives the exact parity \(R (- g, x) = R (g, x)\).

Here are the uniform bounds needed for this elementary application of Laplace's method. Differentiating the divided difference on \(| \xi | \leqslant D\) costs a fixed polynomial in \(D\) and the stationary derivative bounds. It is therefore \(z^{o (1)}\) at every fixed order. The exponent vanishes at \((g, x) = (0, 0)\), so on \(|g|, \|x\|_{\infty} \leqslant h\) it is at most one in absolute value for large \(z\). If the reference truncated mass is at least \(1 / 2\), then \(e^{- 1} / 2 \leqslant R \leqslant e\), and the same subpower derivative bounds hold for \(L\). To obtain derivatives through order \(r\) and the two derivatives in \(g\) used for parity, it suffices to retain derivatives of \(\phi, \rho\) through order \(r + 4\) and of the other terms through order \(r + 2\). These are finite-stage requirements.

If \(A (x)^{- 1} \leqslant cI\) and \(n\) is at most a fixed polynomial in \(\log z\), a coordinate Gaussian tail bound gives

\(\displaystyle 1 - R (0, x) \leqslant 2 n \exp \{- D^2 / (2 cn)\} .\)

This is smaller than every fixed inverse power of \(z\). The same conclusion for its fixed derivatives follows either by the Gaussian score formula or by finite-order interpolation on a ball of radius \(z^{- 1}\) inside the parameter domain. We recall the needed interpolation fact: a superpolynomially small value and polynomially bounded derivatives through order \(r + 1\) imply superpolynomially small derivatives through order \(r\) on a smaller ball. It follows successively from Taylor's estimate \(\|DF\| \leqslant 2 \sup |F| / u + u \sup \|D^2 F\|\) on a radius-\(u\) ball, applied to successive derivatives with shrinking radii. No coordinate-dimension factor is needed when operator norms are used. Parity and Taylor's theorem in \(g\) now yield, for every fixed \(K > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \|D_x^r L (g, x)\| \leqslant C_r (z) {\beta'}^{- 1} + z^{- K}, \qquad C_r (z) = z^{o (1)} . \) (9.5)

This includes \(r = 0\); the cutoff error at \(g = 0\) has been retained throughout the argument.

9.2.5Identifying the retained region in the actual integral

Write \(\mathcal{D}_x (y)\) for the actual tree-gauge configuration map. Consider the region \(\mathcal{S}_x =\mathcal{D}_x (\rho (x) + g \bar{B} (0, D))\). Let \(\mathscr{Z} (x)\) be the pure block integral, and set

\(\displaystyle \Phi (x) = \beta' \phi (x, \rho (x)) + \psi (x, \rho (x)) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det A (x) .\)

If \(N\) is the number of free fine links and \(c_H\) the Haar density constant, its scalar normalization is \(\Omega = \pi_{\beta'} (\sqrt{2 \pi} g)^n c_H^N\); \(\pi_{\beta'}\) is the block-kernel normalizing prefactor. When \(\mathcal{S}_x\) lies in the chart, all its fine plaquettes pass the cutoff, and all live tag actions coincide with \(\phi\), the change of variables gives exactly

\(\displaystyle \int_{\mathcal{S}_x} \text{(pure integrand)} = \Omega e^{- \Phi (x)} R (g, x) . \) (9.6)

Tag probabilities sum to one, so there is no additional tag-count factor. The hypotheses about this region must be established before using the identity.

Here is a verification using the actual geometry and Hessian gap. The actual tree comparison recognizes a fine configuration as lying in a fixed safe chart whenever its fine plaquettes and gauge-covariant block average mismatches are below a fixed \(\delta > 0\). On that chart assume \(D_y^2 \phi \geqslant \gamma I / 2\). Stationarity gives

\(\displaystyle \phi (x, y) - \phi (x, \rho (x)) \geqslant \gamma |y - \rho (x) |^2 / 4.\)

Thus a chart point outside \(\mathcal{S}_x\) costs at least \(\Delta = \gamma D^2 / 4\) in the exponent. A point outside the chart with all fine plaquettes small must have a mismatch exceeding \(\delta\), and contributes a fixed positive multiple of \(\beta' \delta^2\). The possibly negative cubic, quartic and loop terms are bounded by the path coefficient bounds. If \(W\) bounds the auxiliary coordinate and site counts, their total cost is at most

\(\displaystyle WB_{\text{loop}}, \qquad B_{\text{loop}} = \beta' C (t_f^3 + t_f^4) + \Lambda t_f^2, \qquad 0 \leqslant \Lambda \leqslant 2 M.\)

All such terms and the logarithmic Haar/determinant normalization cost are \(o (M^2)\). In particular, the normalization loss \(- N \log [c_H (\sqrt{2 \pi} g)^{\dim \mathfrak{g}}]\) is only a polynomial in \(\log z\). The bound on \(\phi (x, \rho (x))\) on \(\|x\| < h\) also leaves the fixed large-mismatch penalty available, because that value tends to zero and \(\Delta / \beta' \to 0\). Consequently the exterior integrand, in stationary units, is bounded by \(e^{- \Delta + o (M^2)}\).

It remains to verify the fine cutoff on the retained ball. For the folded auxiliary configurations used here, this can be done directly from the curvature transport, without a lower bound from a sector expansion. A full coordinate cycle of a folded field traverses an original path and then its inverse. Its holonomy is therefore exactly one at every transverse base point. Equivalently, choose a coordinate axial gauge on the original cube and pull that gauge back under the folding map. Plaquette filling bounds give logarithmic coordinates with

\(\displaystyle \|x\|_{\infty} \leqslant P (B) t_c, \qquad \|d_{1, c} x\|_{\infty} \leqslant Ct_c + P (B) t_c^2, \qquad t_c = t_m .\)

The second estimate follows by expanding each plaquette product in these small link coordinates. Crucially, its leading constant is independent of \(B\); the polynomial loss occurs only in the quadratic error. This argument would fail for an arbitrary small-curvature periodic field with nontrivial winding holonomies.

Let \(\mathcal{U} (x) =\mathcal{D}_x (\rho (x))\) be the physical stationary fine field. Its tangent is the forest-coordinate lift \(W_F\) of Chapter 5. Its curl agrees with that of the smooth actual lift \(W_a\). The curvature identity and the fine-row bound of the two-form transport give

\(\displaystyle d_{1, f} W_a = H_f^{- 1} TH_c d_{1, c}, \qquad \|H_f^{- 1} TH_c \|_{\infty \to \infty} \leqslant C_j L^{- 2} .\)

The operator norm here sums each fine-output row over coarse inputs; the opposite column sum has a different scaling. The quadratic remainder of the stationary map and the plaquette product is bounded using their common second derivatives. Thus

\(\displaystyle \sup_p \chi (\mathcal{U}(x)_p) \leqslant C_j L^{- 2} t_c + P_{j, L} (B) t_c^2 . \) (9.7)

Choose \(L\) so that \(C_j L^{- 2} t_c \leqslant t_f / 8\), using the uniform bound \(t_c / t_f \leqslant 2\). Then increase the inverse coupling threshold so that the second term is at most \(t_f / 8\). These choices are in the required order: the constant in the first term is fixed before \(L\), while the polynomial in the second term may depend on \(L\).

Plaquette functions have a fixed Lipschitz constant \(L_0\) in the physical fine-link supremum coordinates. Local chart maps have fixed-stage derivative bounds. Consequently variation of \(x\) by \(z^{- 1}\) and of the stationary fine coordinates by \(g \xi\), \(| \xi | \leqslant D\), changes every plaquette distance from the identity by at most \(C_{j, L} z^{- 1} + L_0 gD\), after incorporating the fixed chart norm conversion into \(L_0\). Choose \(d_0\) sufficiently small, so that \(L_0 gD \leqslant t_f / 8\), and then raise the threshold for the microscopic term. Every point of each retained region is now fine small-field, with a strict margin. The common chart and tag-coincidence radius contain these regions because \(h + gD \to 0\). We have therefore obtained, on a full microscopic neighborhood of each tested small-field coarse configuration,

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \mathscr{Z} (y) - \Omega e^{- \Phi (y)} R (g, y) \leqslant \Omega e^{- \Phi (y)} e^{- c_0 M^2}, \qquad c_0 > 0. \) (9.8)

This deduction uses the actual stationary tangent, its second derivatives, the compatible path bounds and the geometric chart-recognition estimate. The direct cutoff proof applies to the folded auxiliary fields and their microscopic neighborhoods, which are precisely the configurations used for localization.

9.2.6Centering the full logarithm

Put \(E = \log \mathscr{Z} - \log \Omega + \Phi - L (g, \cdot)\). Since \(R \geqslant e^{- 1} / 2\), (9.8) gives \(0 \leqslant E \leqslant 2 e \hspace{0.17em} e^{- c_0 M^2}\). To pass to derivatives, write the positive pure integrand at a fixed fine field and tag as \(a (\omega) e^{p (x, \omega)}\), with \(a \geqslant 0\) independent of \(x\). Only the coarse-link kernel is differentiated. Its exponent has fixed-order derivatives bounded by \(C_r \beta' n_b\), where \(n_b\) is the number of coarse links. The exponential chain rule and integration give relative bounds

\(\displaystyle \|D^r \mathscr{Z} (x)\| \leqslant P_r (\beta' n_b) \mathscr{Z} (x) .\)

The logarithm chain rule thus gives polynomial bounds without dividing an absolute error by a possibly tiny partition function. The other terms in \(E\) have polynomial derivative bounds from the stationary estimates. Interpolation on the microscopic neighborhoods in (9.8) proves that every fixed jet of \(E\) is smaller than every inverse power of \(z\).

Let \(a_c\) be the prescribed coarse classical action and \(d_c\) its quantum contribution. Define

\(\displaystyle C = a_c - \phi (\cdot, \rho), \qquad W = d_c - \psi (\cdot, \rho) - \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det A.\)

The corrected logarithm has the exact decomposition

\(\displaystyle \log \mathscr{Z} + \beta' a_c + d_c = \log \Omega + L + E + \beta' C + W. \) (9.9)

Classical matching gives \(D^r C (0) = 0\) for \(0 \leqslant r \leqslant 4\). The quadratic quantum matching bounds \(D^2 W (0)\) by an exponentially small finite-period error, with polynomial coordinate norm costs. Its higher fixed derivatives are \(z^{o (1)}\). The full corrected logarithm has zero first derivative at zero by constant gauge invariance and simplicity of \(\mathfrak{g}\). Thus \(DW (0) = - DL (0) - DE (0)\); no separate parity claim about each determinant contribution is needed.

For a function on the radius-\(h\) ball write its scaled derivative bound through order \(r\) as \(\max_{0 \leqslant k \leqslant r} h^k \sup \|D^k f\|\). Taylor's theorem, (9.5), and the two matching cancellations show that the centered expression in (9.9) has this bound at most

\(\displaystyle C_r (z) {\{\beta'}^{- 1} h + \beta' h^5 + h^3 \} + z^{- K}, \qquad C_r (z) = z^{o (1)}, \) (9.10)

where \(K\) is arbitrary and the exponentially small quadratic finite-period error has been included in the last term. For \(h = z^{- 7 / 16}\) the three powers are respectively \(z^{- 23 / 16}\), \(z^{- 19 / 16}\) and \(z^{- 21 / 16}\). Their largest exponent is \(- 19 / 16 < - 23 / 20\). Actual small-field logarithmic coordinates and the reflected normalized directions are bounded by a polynomial in \(B\) times \(t_m\), which is \(o (h)\). They therefore obey the same estimate. This proves the auxiliary remainder needed for localization from the explicit stationary, matching and actual-sector inputs above. It does not require a formal expansion of an untruncated exponential of a cubic Gaussian polynomial.

9.2.7Recovering a local density

The passage from auxiliary sums to local activities has an exact finite-dimensional explanation. Truncate the selected low-order formula, retaining a weighted tail \(\varepsilon_{\text{tr}}\). Give an ordinary selected term \(f_a^0\) the shares \(p_{xa} =\boldsymbol{1}_{X_a} (x) / |X_a |\), and a path term the share \(\boldsymbol{1}_{\{o_a \}} (x)\). These shares sum to one. Set

\(\displaystyle F_x = \sum_{a \text{selected}} p_{xa} f_a^0, \qquad M_x = \sum_{a \text{selected}} p_{xa} h_{X_a} f_a^0 .\)

Choose a common dependence radius \(R\) about every site receiving a nonzero share. Set \(w = 4 R + 100 (1 + B)\), \(\bar{w} = w + 5\), and use auxiliary tori \(\Lambda_i\) of sides \(2 (w + i)\), \(0 \leqslant i \leqslant 4\). The relevant reflection cubes must embed in the original torus. For a cyclic coordinate \(q\) of period \(2 v\), define \(\pi_v (q) = \min (\bar{q}, 2 v - \bar{q})\), where \(0 \leqslant \bar{q} < 2 v\). Apply this map coordinatewise. Write \(U_{i, z}\) for the pullback of the original field under \(q \mapsto z + \pi_{w + i} (q)\) on \(\Lambda_i\). Reversed links are inverted, so small plaquette distances from the identity stay small. Let \(F_y^{(i)}\) denote the same local formula on \(\Lambda_i\) with its flat value subtracted, and set

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} \lambda_i & = \frac{(- 1)^i \binom{4}{i}}{2^4 \hspace{0.17em} 4!}, & G_x & = \sum_{i = 0}^4 \lambda_i \sum_{y \in \Lambda_i} F_y^{(i)} (U_{i, x}),\\ L_x & = M_x - F_x (1) - \sum_{i = 0}^4 \lambda_i \sum_{y \in \Lambda_i} h_{N_{\bar{w}} (x - \pi_{w + i} (y))} F_y^{(i)} (U_{i, x - \pi_{w + i} (y)}) . & & \hspace{3cm} \text{(9.11)} \end{array}\)

In the second line the original cube is based at \(x - \pi_{w + i} (y)\), so its site \(y\) corresponds to the original site \(x\). This dependence on \(y\) is essential.

When \(N_{2 \bar{w}} (x)\) is small-field, all these event indicators are one. Classify sites of \(\Lambda_i\) by their distances, through \(R\), from the reflection faces. With the recentering just specified, the local expression in each class is independent of \(i\). The multiplicity of each class is polynomial in \(i\) of degree at most four; only the all-interior class has degree four, with leading coefficient \(2^4\). The fourth-difference weights therefore give \(\sum_i \lambda_i \sum_y F_y^{(i)} (U_{i, x - \pi_{w + i} (y)}) = F_x (U) - F_x (1)\), and \(L_x = 0\). This is not a pointwise assertion that \(G_x = F_x - F_x (1)\).

Sum (9.11) over \(x\) and change variables \(x - \pi_{w + i} (y) \mapsto x\) in each finite auxiliary sum. Translation covariance makes \(F_x (1) = F_0 (1)\), giving exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_x M_x = | \Lambda | F_0 (1) + \sum_x h_{N_{\bar{w}} (x)} G_x + \sum_x L_x .\)

Thus \(G_x\) is the small-field local activity and \(L_x\) is a logarithmic correction supported where its enlarged neighborhood contains a large-field site.

Auxiliary volumes and reflected derivative norms grow only polynomially in \(B\). Thus (9.3) implies

\(\displaystyle \|G_x \|_m \leqslant P_j (\mathfrak{b}) (\mathfrak{b}^{- 23 / 20} + \varepsilon_{\text{tr}}), \qquad \sup |L_x | \leqslant P_j (\mathfrak{b}) \mathfrak{b}^{- 7 / 16} .\)

The first fits an arbitrarily small fraction of \(\epsilon_m^{\text{reg}}\), since \(23 / 20 > 11 / 10\). The second tends to zero and is incorporated into large-field activities by the exact operation below.

The selected low-order terms contain no incoming regular or large-field activity. Their coefficients depend only on the prescribed path, actual inverse coupling and fixed labels. Their weighted continuity on the compact inverse coupling band therefore permits one truncation for all inputs with those data. Terms involving incoming activities have already been estimated separately. More generally compactness along a single activity curve would give a truncation only along that curve. All choices here are retained under restriction of the parameter interval.

9.3A precise preparatory output

The preceding estimates, when their concrete local inputs hold, give local functions \(f_i\), preliminary large-field activities \(k_i\), and a scalar \(\tau\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}} (U) = e^{| \Lambda | \tau - \beta' \mathcal{K} (U) -\mathcal{L} (U) - \alpha \mathcal{Q} (U) - \sum_i h_{X_i} f_i (U)} \mathcal{F}^0 (U) . \) (9.12)

Here \(\mathcal{F}^0\) is the exact-cover sum of the \(k_i\), and \(\mathcal{Q}\) is the quadratic part, restricted by its event indicators, of the prescribed path action. The coefficient \(\alpha\) is the matched determinant coefficient, not an adjustable trial inverse coupling. The bounds needed below are

\(\displaystyle \sup_x \sum_{o_i = x} e^{(\sigma + 3) c (X_i)} a_i \leqslant H_{\text{pre}}, \quad a_i \geqslant \|f_i \|_m, \qquad \sup_x \sum_{i : x \in Y_i} e^{(\sigma + 1) c (Y_i)} k_i^{\text{maj}} \leqslant e^{- c_j (M_{m + 1}^{\text{cut}})^2}, \) (9.13)

where \(c (X) = \text{cov}_{B_m} (X)\) and \(H_{\text{pre}} = \theta c_j^{\text{pre}} \epsilon_m^{\text{reg}}\), \(0 < \theta \leqslant 1\). The positive stage constant \(c_j^{\text{pre}}\) is fixed from the subsequent subtraction and path-replacement operator norms before choosing the ratio.

To obtain these bounds choose \(L\) first so that \(V_j L^{- 1} \epsilon_{m + 1}^{\text{reg}}\) fits the desired fraction of \(H_{\text{pre}}\). This is a condition on the fixed stage constants: the ratio \(\epsilon_{m + 1}^{\text{reg}} / \epsilon_m^{\text{reg}}\) is bounded on the permitted neighboring reference-inverse-coupling bands. Then choose the inverse coupling threshold for the inherited comparison, (9.2), (9.3), and the small logarithmic corrections. Choose the truncation accuracy after its polynomial localization costs have been specified. Allocate distinct positive fractions of the exponent in (9.1) to sector multiplicities, local normalization losses, and the final large-field bound. Polynomial support costs use support-weight reserves. An unexplained extensive normalization cannot be absorbed into a single selected large-field condition.

Equation (9.12) and its bounds are the concrete interface for the rest of the chapter. The remaining operations and estimates will now be proved directly from it.

9.4Local quadratic subtraction

For \(a < b\) define the clover curvature at \(o\) by

\(\displaystyle F^{\text{cl}}_{ab} (U, o) = \tfrac{1}{4} \sum_{\varepsilon, \eta = \pm 1} \varepsilon \eta \hspace{0.17em} \text{proj}_{\mathfrak{g}} \frac{U_{o ; \varepsilon a, \eta b} - U_{o ; \varepsilon a, \eta b}^{- 1}}{2} .\)

The loop follows the four signed edges and starts at \(o\). This field transforms by \(\text{Ad}\) at \(o\) and reads a radius-two neighborhood. For an affine potential \(A_{\mu} (y) = u_{\mu} + \sum_{\nu} y^{\nu} v_{\mu \nu}\) its derivative at the identity is \(v_{ba} - v_{ab}\).

For a preliminary \(f\) on \(X\) anchored at \(o\), put \(Z = X \cup N_2 (o)\). On a nonwrapping \(Z\), let \(A^{q, T}_c (y) =\boldsymbol{1}_{\{c = b\}} \xi_o (y)^a T\) for \(q = (a, b)\), and fix a unit \(T_{\ast} \in \mathfrak{g}\). Define

\(\displaystyle q_f (q, r) = D^2 [f (\exp \cdot)] (0) [A^{q, T_{\ast}}, A^{r, T_{\ast}}], \quad Q_f (U) = \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{q, r} q_f (q, r) \langle F^{\text{cl}}_q (U, o), F^{\text{cl}}_r (U, o) \rangle .\)

Set \(q_f = 0\) if \(Z\) wraps. Gauge invariance makes the first derivative at the identity vanish and the Hessian annihilate local gradients. The zero-curl part of an affine potential is a discrete gradient of a quadratic site function. It can be extended periodically using a cutoff outside the dependence region. Therefore the Hessian on affine fields depends only on their constant curls. Invariance under the adjoint action and simplicity of \(\mathfrak{g}\) make its bilinear form a multiple of the invariant metric. Thus \(Q_f\) has the same affine Hessian as \(f\). This is the local Ward-identity argument of Section 7.1 in finite coordinates.

Set

\(\displaystyle R_f = f - f (1) - Q_f, \qquad \zeta_f = (1 - h_Z) \{f (1) + h_{\{o\}} Q_f - h_X f\} .\)

An exact two-case identity is

\(\displaystyle h_X f = f (1) + h_{\{o\}} Q_f - \zeta_f + h_Z R_f . \) (9.14)

On a small-field \(Z\) all event indicators are one; otherwise \(h_Z = 0\) and the definition of \(\zeta_f\) proves the equality. The remainder vanishes at the flat field and has zero affine Hessian on nonwrapping supports. The function \(\zeta_f\) vanishes on small-field \(Z\). Its large-field indicator dependence must still be recorded in its full dependence region; no derivative of an event indicator is taken.

The evaluations in normalized affine directions and polarization give \(t^2 |q_f (q, r) | \leqslant 3 a_f\). Every allowed derivative of \(F^{\text{cl}}\) is \(O_j (t)\) on its small-field domain, so

\(\displaystyle \|R_f \|_m + \sup | \zeta_f | \leqslant C_j a_f . \) (9.15)

In the supremum term, \(f\) and \(Q_f\) occur only under their own small-field conditions. Enlarging \(X\) to \(Z\) costs a bounded number of covering balls.

The conversion of anchored bounds to containing-site bounds is explicit. If \(x \in Z\) and \(c (Z) = n\), its anchor lies within distance \(CBn\) of \(x\). There are at most \(C (1 + B)^4 (1 + n)^4\) choices. An additional exponent in the support weight absorbs this polynomial. Consequently the discrepancy series has containing-site norm at exponent \(\sigma + 1\) bounded by \(C_j (1 + B)^{d_j} H_{\text{pre}}\).

By translation and hypercubic covariance,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{o_i = o} q_{f_i} (q, r) = \Delta \delta_{qr} .\)

An axis reflection kills off-diagonal orientation pairs; permutations identify the diagonal pairs. Absolute convergence follows from the derivative bound, and

\(\displaystyle t^2 | \Delta | \leqslant C_j H_{\text{pre}} . \) (9.16)

Thus, with \(\mathcal{C} (U) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_o h_{\{o\}} \sum_q |F_q^{\text{cl}} (U, o) |^2\), the sum of the extracted quadratic functions is \(\Delta \mathcal{C} (U)\).

9.5Replacing the local quadratic term by the path action

The path action is the tested and clipped density of (4.20). Its path and cutoff data remain fixed during this replacement, as do the frame choices defining the actual probability kernel (4.9).

Expand the quadratic part into plaquette channels and ordered pairs of entries in each row square. For a channel \(d\) let \(a_d P_d\) be its quadratic expression without its event indicator, \(a_d Q_d\) its constant-curvature clover substitute, and \(L_d\) its actual clipped or tested expression. Its support \(Y_d\) contains both paths and every nonautomatic relaxed small-field condition. On the small-field set of \(Y_d\), \(L_d = a_d P_d\). Define

\(\displaystyle F_d = \Delta a_d (Q_d - P_d), \qquad J_d = (1 - h_{Y_d}) \{(\alpha + \Delta) L_d - \Delta a_d h_{\{o_d \}} Q_d \} .\)

Then

\(\displaystyle (\alpha + \Delta) L_d + h_{Y_d} F_d = \alpha h_{Y_d} a_d P_d + \Delta a_d h_{\{o_d \}} Q_d + J_d . \) (9.17)

The equality follows on each of the two health domains. Every \(F_d\) has zero affine Hessian and every \(J_d\) vanishes on its small-field support.

The selected free metric satisfies \(c_{\text{dir}} I + S (0)^{\ast} S (0) = I\), where \(S (0)\) is the row coefficient matrix at zero momentum. Therefore summing the clover substitutes gives exactly \(\mathcal{C}\), while the raw channels restricted by their event indicators sum to \(\mathcal{Q}\). Add the regular terms \(- (\alpha + \Delta) A_d\) for the cubic and quartic atoms. After the common signed-symmetry average, let \(F\) be the resulting sum of regular terms restricted by their event indicators and \(J\) the sum of the \(J_d\). Equation (9.17) becomes

\(\displaystyle (\alpha + \Delta) \mathcal{K}+ F = \alpha \mathcal{Q}+ \Delta \mathcal{C}+ J. \) (9.18)

Combining (9.12), (9.14) and (9.18) gives, at every field,

\(\displaystyle \mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}} = e^{| \Lambda | \tau - \sum_i f_i (1) - (\beta' + \alpha + \Delta) \mathcal{K}-\mathcal{L}- E} \mathcal{F}^0 \hspace{0.17em} e^{\sum_i \zeta_{f_i} + J}, \qquad E = \sum_i h_{Z_i} R_{f_i} + F. \) (9.19)

No division by the exact-cover gas was used, so this equality also holds where that gas vanishes or is signed.

Weighted path sums and additional exponential decay in path length give constants \(C_{\text{reset}}, L_0, L_1, L_h\) such that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \mathcal{E}_m (E) & \leqslant C_{\text{reset}} H_{\text{pre}} + C_j \{H_{\text{pre}} L_0 + (| \alpha |t^3 + H_{\text{pre}}) L_1 \}, \hspace{3cm} \text{(9.20)}\\ \| \zeta + J\|_{\sigma + 1, \text{site}} & \leqslant C_j (1 + B)^{d_j} H_{\text{pre}} + C_j \{t| \alpha | + H_{\text{pre}} / t + H_{\text{pre}} \} L_h . \hspace{3cm} \text{(9.21)} \end{array}\)

Here the site norm sums supremum majorants of logarithmic terms over supports containing a site. The low regular channels contain two curvatures, the high channels at least three; each derivative costs a polynomial in path length, controlled by the exponential path norm. The actual clipped channel can have size \(O (t)\) on a large-field configuration, which explains the term \(t | \alpha |\) in the second line.

Choose \(c_j^{\text{pre}}\) in (9.13) small enough to absorb the terms linear in \(H_{\text{pre}}\) in the first line. At fixed ratio \(| \alpha |\) is bounded, and

\(\displaystyle t^3 / \epsilon_m^{\text{reg}} = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 2 / 5} (M_m^{\text{cut}})^3 \longrightarrow 0.\)

Thus the first line is at most \(\epsilon_m^{\text{reg}}\). The second line tends to zero even after multiplication by \((2 B + 1)^4\): in particular \(H_{\text{pre}} / t = O (\hat{\beta}_m^{- 3 / 5} / M_m^{\text{cut}})\). This is the smallness needed for the exact regrouping.

9.6Absorbing logarithmic corrections into large-field activities

Write the logarithmic corrections in (9.19) as \(\ell_a\) on nonempty exclusion supports \(Z_a\), with \(\ell_a = 0\) when \(Z_a\) is small-field. Their extra large-field indicator dependence regions are retained. For each finite connected selection \(P\) of preliminary large-field labels and logarithmic labels, require a nonempty large-field part and pairwise disjoint preliminary exclusion supports. Connectivity refers to intersections of these exclusion supports and the \(Z_a\). Set \(Y_P\) equal to their union and \(I_P\) equal to the union of the preliminary prescribed large-field site sets. Define

\(\displaystyle K_P (U) =\boldsymbol{1}_{\{Y_P \cap \text{Bad}_m (U) = I_P \}} \prod_{i \in P_{\text{lf}}} k_i (U) \prod_{a \in P_{\log}} (e^{\ell_a (U)} - 1),\)

with the conjunction of all inherited additional conditions.

Proposition 9.1. (Exact regrouping) Suppose \(\sum_a \sup | \ell_a | < \infty\). The exact-cover sum of the \(K_P\) equals \(\mathcal{F}^0 e^{\sum_a \ell_a}\). If nonnegative majorants satisfy

\(\displaystyle |k_i | \leqslant e^{- \kappa} v_i, \qquad |e^{\ell_a} - 1| \leqslant v_a, \qquad \sup_x \sum_{a : x \in Z_a} e^{(\sigma + 1) c (Z_a)} v_a \leqslant \gamma, \quad (2 B + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1,\)

where the unified index also includes the preliminary large-field supports, then

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{K}_m (K) \leqslant e^{- \kappa} (e^{\gamma} - 1) .\)

Proof. Expand \(e^{\sum_a \ell_a} = \prod_a [1 + (e^{\ell_a} - 1)]\). In a nonzero term each selected logarithmic label contains a large-field site. Exact coverage assigns that site to a preliminary prescribed large-field site set, so its connected component contains a large-field label. Taking components gives disjoint union supports and the displayed new prescribed-site equality. Conversely flattening a compatible family of new labels recovers the old exact cover and the logarithmic selection. No constituent label is repeated because its support is nonempty. All additional conditions are retained. This is a bijection of finite terms and does not require independence of their large-field indicator reads.

For the bound retain \(e^{- \kappa}\) from one large-field label per component, and bound the others by \(v_i\). Subadditivity of \(c\) on connected unions and the rooted tree bound underlying Theorem 8.1 sum the connected selections through a fixed site. One additional unit of exponential support decay bounds the number of attachments, since a support has at most \((2 B + 1)^4 c (Z)\) sites. Their sum is bounded by \(\gamma\), hence by \(e^{\gamma} - 1\). The resulting absolute envelope justifies the countable regrouping.\(\Box\)

Take \(\kappa\) to be half of the exponent retained in the second bound of (9.13). The remaining half makes the preliminary large-field majorants small in the attachment norm. For the logarithmic terms use \(|e^{\ell} - 1| \leqslant e^{\sup | \ell |} \sup | \ell |\) and (9.21). Then \(\gamma \to 0\) and a strict margin \((2 B + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1 / 2\) is available. Finally

\(\displaystyle e^{- \kappa} (e^{\gamma} - 1) \leqslant \exp (- (M_m^{\text{cut}})^{1 / 5}) = \epsilon_m^{\text{lf}}\)

for a sufficiently large inverse coupling threshold, provided the neighboring reference scales have the prescribed comparison. The square \((M_{m + 1}^{\text{cut}})^2\) in \(\kappa\) dominates the exponent \(1 / 5\) and all polynomial logarithmic costs.

9.7The normalization of the field-independent term and continuity

The output inverse coupling and scalar are now fixed:

\(\displaystyle \beta = \beta' + \alpha + \Delta, \qquad v_{\text{sc}} = \tau - | \Lambda |^{- 1} \sum_i f_i (1) . \) (9.22)

Equation (9.19) and Proposition 9.1 prove \(\text{dens} (\rho) = \mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}}\) pointwise. Flat normalization also gives \(v_{\text{sc}} = | \Lambda |^{- 1} \log \mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}} (1)\). The exact block integral of a positive input is positive; signed individual activities cause no contradiction.

Projection already provides the useful estimate

\(\displaystyle | \Delta | \leqslant C_j H_{\text{pre}} / t_m^2 \leqslant C_j \theta c_j^{\text{pre}} \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 10} (M_m^{\text{cut}})^{- 2} \leqslant 1.\)

If the matched coefficient has \(\alpha = - \kappa_G \log L + O_j (1)\) as in Proposition 7.6, then

\(\displaystyle \beta = \beta' - \kappa_G \log L + e, \qquad |e| \leqslant d_j + 1. \) (9.23)

No estimate of \(\Delta\) was used to define its local projection or prove the exact algebra.

There is a second, intrinsic way to identify the reset. On an even-sided torus choose direction-one links alternately equal to \(u\) and \(1\) on even and odd second-coordinate slices, with \(\chi (u) = rt\) for sufficiently small fixed \(r > 0\). Every nontrivial plaquette has this distance from the identity. The direct positive plaquette term dominates the cubic and quartic terms when \(r\) is small, so this small-field configuration \(U_{\ast}\) satisfies \(\mathcal{K} (U_{\ast}) \geqslant c_{\ast} | \Lambda | t^2\). Hold the constructed activities fixed and vary only a trial inverse coupling correction \(d\). The scalar discrepancy is

\(\displaystyle f (d) = (\beta' + \alpha + d) \mathcal{K} (U_{\ast}) +\mathcal{L} (U_{\ast}) + E (U_{\ast}) - \log \frac{\mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}} (1)}{\mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}} (U_{\ast})} = (d - \Delta) \mathcal{K} (U_{\ast}) .\)

It has the unique zero \(d = \Delta\). Two evaluations identify the two scalars only because the full-field identity has already been established. Matching two fields alone would not prove that identity.

For a compact continuous input curve choose fixed local supports, cutoff conditions, truncations and reflected tori. Projection, subtraction and channel replacement are bounded linear operations at these fixed labels. Products are continuous by telescoping with one difference factor. For changes of inverse coupling hold physical field coordinates fixed; for a centered Gaussian of precision \(\beta A\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{d \nu_{\beta_2}}{d \nu_{\beta_1}} (a) = (\beta_2 / \beta_1)^{D / 2} e^{- (\beta_2 - \beta_1) \langle a, Aa \rangle / 2} .\)

Its derivative is the same density ratio times \(D / (2 \beta_2) - \langle a, Aa \rangle / 2\). On a small relative interval it is dominated by a polynomial times a Gaussian with a smaller positive precision. Mean and matrix changes give analogous Gaussian scores. Apply the common-law estimate to these scores; do not differentiate a merely measurable incoming large-field activity.

Infinite regrouping requires more than coefficientwise continuity. Given a sequence of parameters approaching \(u_0\), pass to a subsequence whose weighted input discrepancies have sum at most \(\varepsilon\). Their sum added to the majorant at \(u_0\) is a common summable envelope. The strict attachment margin allows this addition for small \(\varepsilon\). The rooted estimate then gives one summable output envelope; dominated convergence proves weighted continuity on this subsequence. Every potentially nonconvergent sequence would have such a convergent further subsequence, a contradiction.

At fixed volume, the exact-cover sums obey the elementary telescoping estimate

\(\displaystyle |\mathcal{F}_u (U) -\mathcal{F}_{u_0} (U) | \leqslant e^{\sum_i M_i} \sum_i \sup |K_{i, u} - K_{i, u_0} |, \quad M_i = \max (\sup |K_{i, u} |, \sup |K_{i, u_0} |) .\)

Thus the densities are continuous uniformly in \(U\). The positive block-kernel normalizers are continuous and bounded away from zero on compact inverse coupling intervals; dominated convergence in probability Haar measure proves continuity of \(\mathscr{Z}_{\text{blk}, u}\). The scalar formula and projection show continuity of \(v_{\text{sc}}\) and \(\beta\). Restriction to a closed subinterval retains the construction and all its labels.

9.8Wilson initialization

The finest-scale representation can be constructed directly from the Wilson action [Wil74], without a limiting argument. Write \(W (u) = 1 - \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (u) / n_G\) for the Wilson plaquette energy and \(F (u) = \text{proj}_{\mathfrak{g}} (u - u^{- 1}) / 2\). The map \(X \mapsto F (e^X)\) has derivative \(I\) at zero and a local inverse \(\psi\). The map and its inverse are odd, and \(W (u^{- 1}) = W (u)\), so \(W (e^{\psi (Y)})\) is even. Let \(T_4\) be the degree-four Taylor term of the Wilson plaquette energy evaluated at \(e^{\psi (Y)}\). Then

\(\displaystyle R (u) = W (u) - \tfrac{1}{2} |F (u) |^2 - T_4 (F (u))\)

vanishes through degree four at the identity. Choose the finest row \(\sqrt{1 - c_{\text{dir}}} F\), no cubic or loop term, and quartic atom \((1 - c_{\text{dir}}) T_4\) with its prescribed event indicator.

For a plaquette \(p\) write \(h_p\) for the health event indicator on its vertices, \(\chi_p^{\text{rel}}\) for its row cutoff condition, and \(k_p = (1 - c_{\text{dir}}) |F (U_p) |^2 / 2\). Define the residual energy

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{W}_p = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} W (U_p) - c_{\text{lin}} t \chi (U_p), & \chi (U_p) > t,\\ h_p k_p + (1 - h_p) (1 - c_{\text{dir}}) W (U_p) - \chi_p^{\text{rel}} k_p, & \chi (U_p) \leqslant t. \end{array} \right.\)

Since \(|F (u) | \leqslant \chi (u)\) and \(W (u) = \chi (u)^2 / 2\), this is nonnegative; when \(\chi (U_p) > t\) it is at least \(t^2 / 4\). The relaxed small-field condition implies that the plaquette itself is small, and the condition can also fail because of a nearby bad plaquette. Direct substitution gives

\(\displaystyle W (U_p) = L_p + (1 - c_{\text{dir}}) h_p T_4 (F (U_p)) + (1 - c_{\text{dir}}) h_p R (U_p) +\mathcal{W}_p,\)

where \(L_p\) is the actual clipped quadratic channel. If the relaxed small-field condition inspects a larger neighborhood, its failure is included in \(\mathcal{W}_p\) by the same formula; the identity remains valid because on a small-field plaquette the residual is \(k_p - \chi_p^{\text{rel}} k_p\).

Use \(\beta (1 - c_{\text{dir}}) R (U_p)\) as a regular activity. Its normalized derivatives are bounded by \(C_j \beta t^5\). For orders at most five, the missing powers after differentiation are supplied by Taylor's formula; for higher fixed orders, the direction factors supply at least \(t^5\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \beta t^5 / \epsilon_N^{\text{reg}} = O (\hat{\beta}_N^{- 2 / 5} (M_N^{\text{cut}})^5) \to 0.\)

Assign the nonnegative residual energies to connected components of large-field sites whose radius-\(2 B\) neighborhoods overlap. A component site set \(I\) has exclusion support \(Y_I = N_{2 B} (I)\) and activity

\(\displaystyle K_I =\boldsymbol{1}_{\{Y_I \cap \text{Bad}_N = I\}} e^{- \beta d_I},\)

where \(d_I\) is the sum of assigned residual energies with the signed average. Each large-field site meets a large-field plaquette, and a plaquette has at most four vertices, so \(d_I \geqslant c \hspace{0.17em} t^2 |I|\) for a fixed \(c > 0\). Every residual term is assigned exactly once; its relaxed small-field condition has a witnessing large-field site within distance \(B\). The only nonzero exact-cover selection is the actual set of these components, so the density identity is exact.

A connected prescribed set of \(r\) large-field sites is bounded in number by a rooted spanning-walk count. The containing-site weighted sum is at most

\(\displaystyle C_j (1 + B)^4 \sum_{r \geqslant 1} [C_j (1 + B)^8 e^{5^4 \sigma - c \beta t^2}]^r .\)

Since \(\beta t^2 \geqslant (M_N^{\text{cut}})^2 / 2\), this fits \(\epsilon_N^{\text{lf}}\) for a sufficiently large threshold. The activity labels and eligibility criteria are independent of \(\beta\). The resulting full Wilson curve is continuous, and its scalar normalization is zero.

9.9The one-step conclusion and its input data

Theorem 9.2. (One renormalization step) Assume the derivative orders and spatial exponents stated at the start of the chapter. Fix an adjacent quadratic pair with the positive metrics, exact transport identities and actual gauge-covariant block average of Chapter 5. Let the incoming path have the normalized quadratic coefficient and exponential coefficient and derivative bounds of Chapters 4 and 7. The incoming activities obey the small-field cancellations, large-field eligibility rules and weighted bounds of Chapter 4, with the support reserve used above. Assume also that the represented input density is strictly positive; this holds at the Wilson start and is preserved by the exact block kernel. Smallness of signed activities alone is not a substitute for this condition. Use the same forest, path realization, clipped frozen source and cutoff choices throughout the local integral construction of Chapter 6. Construct the outgoing path by the classical and quantum matching in Chapter 7. Choose the stage constants, ratio floors, minimum auxiliary periods and inverse coupling thresholds in the order specified above. Require also that the actual coarse period exceeds the largest local read diameter and twice the largest auxiliary reflection half-period used in the construction. This includes the minimum period \(100 (B_m + 1)\) in Chapter 6; enlarging the stage-dependent period floor ensures that every localization cube and its dependence neighborhood embeds in the actual torus.

Then every admissible continuous input curve has a continuous output representation with fixed labels, satisfying the regular and large-field bounds (4.24) and the pointwise identity

\(\displaystyle \text{dens} (\rho_u) =\mathcal{B}_m^{\beta'_u} \text{dens} (\rho'_u) .\)

Its inverse coupling is (9.22) and obeys (9.23), uniformly in volume, cutoff and curve parameter. The construction retains restriction compatibility and all exact-cover cutoff conditions. Output inverse coupling-band membership additionally requires the numerical band margin in the choice of scales.

Proof. The proof consists of the convergent local expansion, the three remainder estimates, the exact identities (9.14) and (9.18), and Proposition 9.1. Positivity and normalization refer to the actual block integral.\(\Box\)

This is a theorem for one compatible adjacent pair. Its premises include the actual quadratic coefficient and fixed path coefficient bounds; they cannot be replaced by unrelated bounds of the same numerical size. The common Gaussian estimate is supplied by the complete factor construction in Chapter 6, and Sections 9.2.4–9.2.6 give the auxiliary estimate for the same pure integral. The classical bound contraction and inherited-loop contraction of Chapter 7 allow the path bounds to be retained with the stated order of choices.

What remains for a whole trajectory is to make those choices simultaneously, tune the Wilson inverse coupling to the prescribed coarse endpoint, and prove stability as the ultraviolet cutoff changes. These are additional assertions, not consequences of applying an arbitrary finite step repeatedly. In particular, no higher parameter analyticity is inferred from the continuity proved here.
