Chapter 11
Marked insertions and finite-scale comparison

A block transformation acts linearly on an inserted density even though its effective-action coordinates evolve nonlinearly. Keeping the density itself in view resolves two difficulties: several insertions generate joint terms, and an auxiliary sequence of block kernels can preserve all original correlation integrals without preserving their intermediate densities. This chapter establishes that finite-step calculus and the local comparison estimates it needs. Statements about a limiting quantum field require further uniform estimates; they are not consequences of the source algebra alone.

Throughout, the polynomial label space and its local section are \(V_d\) and \(P_d^{\text{loc}}\) from Chapter 2. All block kernels are the normalized kernels of Chapter 4. A history means one fixed choice of these kernels, inverse couplings, adjacent path pairs, and local representations. The estimates of Chapters 6 and 9 are used only on that same history.

The argument has four parts. First the squarefree source algebra and support estimates retain every joint insertion term. Next the single-insertion estimates and the common Gaussian comparison construct the restarted history and the normalized product-moment bounds. Backward comparison then relates local trials to the actual renormalized fields and microscopic equations. Finally a common localization onto the polynomial label space and a larger source weight give the bound from a finite separating family, from which uniform finite-difference bounds follow.

11.1An algebra for finitely many insertions

Let \(\mathcal{I}\) be a finite set of insertion labels and define

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}} =\mathbb{C} [z_i : i \in \mathcal{I}] / (z_i^2 : i \in \mathcal{I}), \qquad z_S = \prod_{i \in S} z_i .\)

The variables commute; a monomial with a repeated label is zero. Every element has the unique form \(\sum_{S \subseteq \mathcal{I}} a_S z_S\). Its positive-degree ideal is nilpotent of order \(|\mathcal{I}| + 1\), so exponential and logarithm series on this ideal are finite. For positive weights \(\omega_i\), put

\(\displaystyle \|a\|_{\omega} = \sum_{S \subseteq \mathcal{I}} |a_S | \prod_{i \in S} \omega_i .\)

This norm is submultiplicative by the disjoint-subset convolution formula. The weights rescale source variables; they are not probabilities.

For an element \(M\) with \(M_{\varnothing} = 1\),

\(\displaystyle [z_S] \log M = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{P} (S)} (- 1)^{| \pi | - 1} (| \pi | - 1) ! \prod_{I \in \pi} M_I . \) (11.1)

Here \(\mathcal{P} (S)\) denotes the set of partitions into nonempty blocks. This is the standard moment–cumulant identity; we use the classical cumulant calculus of Leonov and Shiryaev [LS59], rather than develop a second connected-expansion formalism. Nilpotence makes the formula an algebraic identity even when there is no positive probability law behind the coefficients. On bounded coefficient sets, \(\log\) is Lipschitz with a constant depending only on \(|\mathcal{I}|\) and the coefficient bound: expand the finite sum and telescope each product.

11.1.1The exact density and its integral

At cutoff \(N\), let \(\rho_N \geqslant 0\) be the unmarked density with \(Z_N = \int \rho_N \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda_N > 0\), where \(\lambda_N\) is product Haar probability measure. For physical insertions \(F_i\), assume every \(\rho_N \prod_{i \in S} F_i\) is integrable and set

\(\displaystyle q_N (z, U) = \rho_N (U) \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} (1 + z_i F_i (U)), \qquad q_m =\mathcal{B}_m q_{m + 1} . \) (11.2)

The block map acts coefficientwise. Consequently \(Z_N^{- 1} \int [z_S] q_m \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda_m\) is the original cutoff moment of the insertions indexed by \(S\).

If one instead uses the dimensionless one-insertion density of degree \(d\), its recursion is \(H_m = L_m^d \mathcal{B}_m H_{m + 1}\). The scalar rescaling belongs to this convention; it is absent from (11.2), where the physical normalization is already in \(F_i\). Mixing these two conventions changes the unit insertion. In particular the reference-level coefficient of the physical unit in a degree-\(d\) convention is \(a_{r (d)}^d e_0\), not the coefficient obtained from the individual scaling dimension of \(e_0\).

Proposition 11.1. (Changing the block history) Replace any kernels below a fixed level \(k\) by other normalized positive block kernels. Push the complete polynomial \(q_k\) through them. Every coefficient integral is unchanged. A field-independent polynomial \(J \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{I}}\) commutes with every push; if \(J_{\varnothing} = 1\), the unmarked density and its partition integral are unchanged.

Proof. For any integrable coefficient \(h\), normalization and Fubini give \(\int \mathcal{B}h \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda = \int h \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda\) and \(\|\mathcal{B}h\|_1 \leqslant \|h\|_1\). Iteration proves the first assertion. Multiplication by \(J\) is a fixed linear map of the finite coefficient space, so it commutes with the integral. Its constant coefficient proves the last assertion.\(\Box\)

There is also a conditional-expectation interpretation. Draw the terminal field with law \(\rho_N \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda_N / Z_N\) and then follow the selected block kernels. The conditional expectation of \(\prod_{i \in S} F_i\) given the level-\(m\) field is \([z_S] q_m / [z_{\varnothing}] q_m\) almost everywhere where the denominator is positive. This follows by testing against a bounded function of the level-\(m\) field. Its value on the zero-density set is immaterial. Two histories generally have different conditional laws; the proposition asserts equality of their integrated moments.

11.2Joint local terms and large-field components

At an intermediate level a local representation has the form

\(\displaystyle e^{\Phi (U) + \sum_{S \ne \varnothing} z_S h_S (U)} \sum_{\mathcal{F} \text{eligible}} \prod_{Y \in \mathcal{F}} \left( K_Y (U) + \sum_{S \ne \varnothing} z_S k_{Y, S} (U) \right) . \) (11.3)

Each regular term carries its required small-field restriction; each large-field term retains its original eligibility and large-field conditions. An index \(S\) may contain several insertion labels. Such terms are generated by integration even if the terminal regular source has only terms linear in the sources. The full dependence region includes the insertion stencils, event indicators, paths, and Gaussian windows. It need not coincide with the exclusion support of a large-field activity.

One must never divide a marked coefficient by \(K_Y\): that activity may vanish. For example, two complementary insertion factors on a selected large-field support contribute

\(\displaystyle K_Y (1 + z_1 b_1) (1 + z_2 b_2) = K_Y + z_1 K_Y b_1 + z_2 K_Y b_2 + z_1 z_2 K_Y b_1 b_2 .\)

The joint coefficient contains the original activity once.

More generally expand the terminal product into its small-field parts and complementary parts. Join each complementary stencil to every selected large-field support it meets, and join intersecting complementary stencils. Exact large-field indicator coverage ensures that a complementary stencil containing a failed cutoff condition meets at least one selected support. Merge each resulting component, retaining the constituent indices, original eligibility criteria, and union of their prescribed sets of large-field sites. Its value is the product of those constituents, with each selected activity included once. Expanding the constituent indices recovers the original term uniquely; hence this is an exact coefficient identity. This algebraic construction gives all joint terminal large-field terms, including at zeros of the original activities.

For a bound uniform in the number of inserted points, assume at most one insertion anchor per site and a fixed finite menu of stencil radii and degrees. Then a site belongs to only a bounded number of insertion stencils. To apply the component tree estimate of Chapter 8 to the union of the stencils, use a larger support exponent in the bounds on the individual factors. At a Wilson terminal scale every new component contains an original large-field support, whose suppression is \(\exp (- c \beta_N t_N^2 n)\). Reserve half this exponent before summing attachments. A polynomial in \(B_N, \hat{\beta}_N\) times the remaining suppression tends to zero faster than every inverse power. This proves small joint terminal bounds when the weighted insertion complements themselves have a summable site bound. That latter bound is a quantitative local-section input; it is not implied by the finite polynomial identity.

11.3Support bounds for several marked components

Fix \(n\) insertion labels in each reflected replica cell. Suppose every elementary principal term has at least one marked point, contains its marked points in its full dependence region, and that region is within distance \(\rho\) of each such point. Suppose marked points in different cells are separated by more than \(2 \rho\). A component formed by intersecting these full regions then lies in one cell. Indeed marks on adjacent terms have distance at most \(2 \rho\), so they belong to the same cell. Connect the distinct labels used by the component. Erasing cycles gives a path of at most \(n - 1\) edges. All its marked points are therefore within \(2 n \rho\) of one another, and every point of its full region is within \((2 n + 1) \rho\) of each marked point. This bound is independent of the number of elementary terms in the component.

A second estimate controls increasing the source weights. Let each component carry a nonempty label set \(S\), full region \(X\), and let \(t (S)\) be the number of replica cells represented in \(S\). Suppose at most \(n\) labels occur per cell. Increase the common source weight from \(\omega_-\) to \(\omega = H^U \omega_-\). For components within one cell the cost is at most \(H^{Un}\). For components meeting several cells, retain an additional exponent in the support weight \(\sigma > 0\). If

\(\displaystyle \sigma c_B (X) \geqslant Un \hspace{0.17em} t (S) \log H, \) (11.4)

then the increased label weight and decreased support weight give

\(\displaystyle H^{U |S|} e^{- \sigma c_B (X)} \leqslant 1.\)

Consequently a marked-point incidence bound \(p_1\) for single-cell terms at exponent \(e\), and \(p_2\) for other terms at exponent \(e + \sigma\), give the bound \(H^{Un} p_1 + p_2\) at exponent \(e\).

The geometric lower bound (11.4) and the stronger incoming norm are both required. An estimate proved only at exponent \(e\) cannot be reused at \(e + \sigma\). Large-field components require their own joint attachment estimate because one such component may carry marks from many cells. The suppression reserved above, together with the stronger support norm, absorbs this additional weight. The support exponent is therefore chosen before the marked expansion and before the history thresholds are selected.

11.4One marked step with the actual local factors

We give quantitative bounds before applying the Gaussian comparison. This also specifies the support reserve needed when the number of inserted points grows with the volume. Fix an output exponent \(e \geqslant 0\), put \(v = e + 5\), and let \(B_f \leqslant 2 B_c\) be the adjacent support radii. For the prescribed enlargement \(X^{\uparrow}\) of a fine support, the covering argument of Section 6.5 gives

\(\displaystyle Lc_{B_c} (X^{\uparrow}) \leqslant 15^4 \{L + 10 c_{B_f} (X)\} .\)

Consequently, if \(10 \cdot 15^4 (e + 7) \leqslant L \sigma_f\),

\(\displaystyle e^{(v + 2) c_{B_c} (X^{\uparrow})} \leqslant T_e e^{\sigma_f c_{B_f} (X)}, \qquad T_e = \exp (15^4 (e + 7)) . \) (11.5)

The numerical constant describes this choice of support enlargement; the essential feature is its fixed additive cost and \(L^{- 1}\) fine cover cost.

In one sector and tag, distinguish three kinds of factors: unmarked local corrections, incoming large-field factors, and insertion factors with nonempty sets of labels. Their full dependence regions determine the intersection graph. The exact prescribed set of large-field sites condition says that every selected incoming prescribed large-field set is supplied by the unmarked local correction factors in its component. In particular a component with no unmarked correction has no incoming large-field factor. This is a condition on the exact cover decomposition, retained when marked factors are attached, rather than a consequence of spatial intersection alone.

Let \(\delta\) be the containing-site bound for unmarked correction amplitudes. Suppose the incoming large-field amplitudes, already weighted by \(e^{\sigma_f c_{B_f}}\), have site bound \(\eta\). Let \(a\) bound the insertion incidence sum at a fixed mark after (11.5), at exponent \(v + 2\). If at most \(g\) marks project to a coarse site, set

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{W} (B) = (33 B)^4 \sum_{j \geqslant 0} (j + 1)^4 e^{- j}, \qquad \Sigma = \delta +\mathcal{W} (B_c) L^4 T_e \eta +\mathcal{W} (B_c) ga.\)

The insertion and background incidence bound at a specified mark is \(T_e \eta + a\); no \(L^4\) occurs there. The site bound is \(\Sigma\) at exponent \(v + 1\). To see the latter conversion, root a support at one of its actual fine sites or marks. Its covering number is at least \(\lfloor d (o, x) / (8 B_c) \rfloor\). The number of possible roots in shell \(j\) is at most \((33 B_c)^4 (j + 1)^4\), while losing one support exponent provides \(e^{- j}\). Background roots have \(L^4\) possible fine residues; mark roots have at most \(g\) choices.

Choose inflation factors \(m, l \geqslant 1\). The common local integral bound of Chapter 6 may be applied with the unmarked amplitudes inflated by \(m\) for factors with a nonempty prescribed set of large-field sites, and by \(l\) for other corrections. Smallness remains available by increasing the inverse coupling threshold at fixed \(m, l\). A nonleading insertion factor is either pointwise at most its bounded amplitude divided by \(l\), or supported on an event of actual conditional probability at most \(l^{- 1}\) and bounded by that amplitude. For a compatible connected collection \(C\), therefore,

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} t_C \left| \int \prod_{j \in C} F_j \hspace{0.17em} d \nu \right| & \leqslant \prod_{j \in C} b_j,\\ t_C & = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} m, & \text{some factor prescribes large-field sites},\\ l, & \text{no sites are prescribed and some factor is nonprincipal},\\ 1, & \text{purely principal} . \end{array} \right. \hspace{3cm} \text{(11.6)} \end{array}\)

Here \(\nu\) is the single actual Gaussian/Haar law of the sector. If an unmarked correction occurs, its inflated common product bound absorbs \(t_C\), while the other factors are bounded pointwise. If none occurs, the prescribed set of large-field sites condition removes incoming large-field factors; use either the pointwise or event bound for one nonleading insertion. No independence is used in either case.

Assume \((2 B_c + 1)^4 \Sigma \leqslant 1\). The rooted-tree bound gives connected component site sum at most \(\Sigma\) at exponent \(v\), and marked-point incidence at most \(T_e \eta + a\). Indeed let \(c_i = c_{B_c} (X_i)\), \(a_i = b_i e^{vc_i}\) and \(\hat{b}_i = b_i e^{(v + 1) c_i}\). The sum of \(\hat{b}_j\) over regions meeting \(X_i\) is at most \(|X_i | \Sigma \leqslant c_i\). Iterating \(R_i^{(0)} = a_i\) and \(R_i^{(q + 1)} = a_i \exp (\sum_{j : X_j \cap X_i \ne \varnothing} R_j^{(q)})\) gives \(R_i^{(q)} \leqslant \hat{b}_i\). Every finite connected collection has a spanning tree and is included in this upper bound. This is the rooted-tree majorization underlying the polymer criterion of Chapter 8; it is applied here with the full marked regions, rather than proved anew for each subset of marks.

The split of an incoming principal factor \(Wz_S\) by a local cutoff is exact:

\(\displaystyle (1 + \gamma Wz_S) (1 + (1 - \gamma) Wz_S) = 1 + Wz_S .\)

The second term is nonleading and the actual warning estimate gives its supporting event probability at most \(x^{- 1}\). Thus \(l \leqslant x\) absorbs that term. If principal and arbitrary remainder incidence bounds before enlargement are \(h\) and \(z\), respectively, one may take

\(\displaystyle a = 2 T_e h + lT_e z. \) (11.7)

Both pieces retain the same incoming event indicator and dependence region. Purely principal connected components have the one-cell support bound proved earlier. Their actual integral is the joint moment in Proposition 11.2, since it contains neither an unmarked correction nor an incoming large-field factor.

Finally normalize the unmarked local gas by the same exact connected expansion as in Chapter 9. If its local normalization corrections have total site bound \(k\) at exponent \(v\) after the \(l\) inflation, choose an output bound \(\psi\) with \(e^{\Sigma} - 1 + k \leqslant \psi\). The regular nonleading amplitudes are then bounded by \(l\), the selected components by \(m\), and the marked-point incidence by \(T_e \eta + a\). The empty-label corrections contribute zero to that incidence. In particular output incidence at most one follows from \(T_e \eta + a \leqslant 1\); it is not an extra independence or finite-arity assumption. The estimates hold for every finite label set with the stated occupancy \(g\), uniformly in the number of replica cells.

11.4.1The one-insertion graph estimates

Fix a degree \(d\) with coarse derivative order at least \(\max (2, d + 1)\), fine spatial weight at least \(d\), fine kernel order at least \(d + 2\), and coarse kernel level at least \(d + 3\) for the simultaneous calculation for normalized local insertions and the regular and large-field remainders. Assume the period is at least \(100 (1 + B_c)\). The fixed normalized local insertion is tested with one additional derivative beyond each requested derivative used in a coefficient evaluation; this requirement does not impose an additional derivative on an arbitrary incoming activity.

Write \(\kappa_i = (\epsilon_i^{\text{for}})^{1 / 2}\) for the square root of the actual large-field bound at level \(i\). The remainder norm used here is the sum of the anchored, support-weighted regular majorants and \(\kappa_i^{- 1}\) times the anchored, support-weighted large-field majorants, with their original support–prescribed set of large-field sites indices. Thus the fine and coarse remainder norms use \(\kappa_f\) and \(\kappa_c\), respectively, not a common value.

For this degree write \(w_i\) and \(f_i\) for the scaling dimension and curvature-factor count of a homogeneous basis vector. 11.5A common Gaussian comparison

The comparison of several local polynomials must preserve their cross covariances. Separate Gaussian approximations for individual factors are insufficient. The following finite-degree statement is the useful form, permitting a different cutoff for every subset of marks.

Proposition 11.2. (Joint polynomial comparison with cutoffs) Fix \(n, D\) and \(A, B \geqslant 1\). For each nonempty \(I \subseteq \{1, \ldots, n\}\), let \(p_I\) be a gauge-invariant polynomial of degree at most \(D\) and coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm at most \(A\). For every member \(t\) of a finite probability mixture, let one probability space carry a centered joint Gaussian array \(\eta_t\), common to all subsets, with covariance \(C_t\). Suppose

\(\displaystyle |a_j | \leqslant B, \quad | (C_t)_{ij} |, | (C_{\ast})_{ij} | \leqslant B, \quad | (C_t - C_{\ast})_{ij} | \leqslant \varepsilon, \qquad 0 \leqslant \varepsilon \leqslant 1.\)

For each \(I\) let \(0 \leqslant \gamma_{t, I} \leqslant 1\) satisfy \(\mathbb{E}_t (1 - \gamma_{t, I}) \leqslant \varepsilon^2\). On its positive set suppose the actual normalized local curvature coordinates, after an allowed gauge rotation, obey \(| \xi_{t, I, j} - a_j - \eta_{t, j} | \leqslant \varepsilon\) on all used coordinates. The polynomial's invariance removes the rotation.

With the same weights \(\lambda_t\) for all subsets and the empty product, define

\(\displaystyle M_S = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{P} (S)} \sum_t \lambda_t \mathbb{E}_t \prod_{I \in \pi} \gamma_{t, I} p_I (\xi_{t, I}), \qquad M_S^{\ast} = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{P} (S)} \mathbb{E}_{C_{\ast}} \prod_{I \in \pi} p_I (a + \eta),\)

and set \(M_{\varnothing} = M_{\varnothing}^{\ast} = 1\). The symbol \(\mathbb{E}_{C_{\ast}}\) may denote the Gaussian pairing functional for a symmetric array, without assuming positivity. There is a polynomial \(P_{n, D}\) such that

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S \ne \varnothing} |M_S - M_S^{\ast} | + \| \log M - \log M^{\ast} \|_1 \leqslant \varepsilon P_{n, D} (A, B) .\)

Its constants are independent of the number of unused coordinates and of the number of mixture members.

Proof. Fix a partition with \(\ell\) blocks and put \(\gamma = \prod_{I \in \pi} \gamma_{t, I}\). Then \(1 - \gamma \leqslant \sum_I (1 - \gamma_{t, I})\), so \(\mathbb{E}_t (1 - \gamma) \leqslant \ell \varepsilon^2\) without independence. Telescope each product monomial to replace the physical coordinates by \(a + \eta_t\). Every difference contains one factor of size \(\varepsilon\) and at most \(\ell D - 1\) Gaussian factors. Fixed marginal moments and Hölder's inequality bound the result by \(\varepsilon A^{\ell} C_{n, D} (1 + B)^{nD}\). Only coordinates in that monomial occur; no ambient-dimension count is introduced.

Cauchy–Schwarz removes \(\gamma\) at cost \(\sqrt{\ell} \hspace{0.17em} \varepsilon\) times the second moment of the polynomial. Finally the Gaussian pairing formula [Iss18] expresses each reference moment as a finite sum of products of means and covariances. Telescope these products to replace \(C_t\) by \(C_{\ast}\). This costs another \(\varepsilon\) times a fixed polynomial in \(A, B\). Average with the common probability weights and sum over finitely many partitions. The finite polynomial formula (11.1) gives the logarithm bound. The empty coefficient is exactly one because the empty product has no cutoff.\(\Box\)

The proposition applies to the purely principal components of a marked expansion, after the scalar normalization has been extracted once. It does not identify the whole interacting density with a Gaussian. Deleting an additive remainder for an estimate may leave the precision, affine mean and prescribed path data unchanged, but it changes the interacting density. Exact reconstruction always uses the genuine density. This distinction is indispensable when matching the Gaussian comparison to the block transformation.

11.5.1The exact cross-covariance on the selected forest

\(\displaystyle K = D^{\ast} M_f D, \quad K' = (D')^{\ast} M_c D', \quad A = K + bQ^{\ast} Q,\)

where \(M_f, M_c\) are the actual positive curvature metrics and \(Q\) is the rooted gauge-covariant block average derivative. \(\displaystyle KW = Q^{\ast} K', \quad AW = bQ^{\ast}, \quad DW = JD',\)

where \(J = H_{0, f}^{- 1} TH_{0, c}\) is the curvature response. This proves every cross-entry simultaneously, including different plaquettes and coordinates in the basis of Section 1.1. Harmonic and gauge modes were handled through the range equations, not discarded.

\(\displaystyle s = \hat{\beta}_k, \quad H = e^{(\log \log s)^2}, \quad M = \lceil (\log s)^2 \rceil, \quad B = M^2, \quad b = 8 B, \quad \gamma = \frac{1}{16 (D + 2)} .\)

A bound by one at the original source weight is not enough for the comparison of separating correlations. \(\displaystyle \omega_- = H^{1 / 16 - U}, \qquad \omega = H^{1 / 16} .\)

Each prescribed support is contained in the fixed enlargement of a radius-\(AB\) ball around its cell's finitely many insertion points, with \(A\) chosen before the inverse coupling. This uniform delay, rather than treating a changing stage constant as an absolute constant, justifies the exponent comparisons below. This bounded-cover assertion is used at restoration levels. It is not an assertion that the separated opening sites at distance \(H^b\) fit in one radius-\(AB\) ball. The supports of their terms involving at least two insertions are instead estimated by (11.31). At an earlier leading step a nonzero connected principal component has radius at most \((6 n + 3) B\) around each of its own marked insertion points; a term involving two insertions with more widely separated insertion points is absent. Only these nonzero components enter the principal support bound.

Let \(Y_c\) contain all prescribed supports in cell \(c\). Suppose these sets have mutual distance at least \(D'\), and every activity support contains the sites of all its marks. \(\displaystyle c_B (X) \geqslant \lambda t (X), \qquad \lambda = \frac{D'}{3 (2 B + 1)},\) (11.27)

once \(D' / 3 > 2 B + 1\). To see this, choose one point of \(X \cap Y_c\) per represented cell, and follow a path in its connected cover until distance \(\lfloor D' / 3 \rfloor\) from that point. A cube covers at most \(2 B + 1\) of the successive distance values. The resulting initial path segments belong to disjoint distance balls, and a cube cannot meet two of them. Summing proves the inequality. Here connected cover means the connected cover used in the activity norm; one cannot replace it by an arbitrary disconnected cover of the marked sites.

at \((\omega, e)\), for all sufficiently large \(H\).

\(\displaystyle w_o = e^g e^{2^n Ft (X_o)} p_o,\)

\(\displaystyle w_o \leqslant e^g H^{(Un + 2^n) t (X_o)} e^{- c_B (X_o)} p_o^- .\)

Each source monomial gains at most \(\omega^{|I|} \leqslant H^{1 / 8}\) at the restored weight. Regular weight restoration therefore gives \(H^{- 1}\); the corrected leading contribution is at most \(2^n R_e H^{- 27 / 32}\); and the regrouping of activities constrained to cover prescribed sites has bound \(4 H^{Un + 2^n} \eta_-\). The upper and lower recurrences then give incidence and site bounds at most one at the output. Their scalar envelope has expense at most \(1 + n\) per site. The actual partition estimate absorbs this finite expense by imposing \(C_{\text{stage}} + 1 \leqslant I_k\). Thus the corrected source coefficient has chessboard seminorm at most \(e^{I_k} \omega^{- |I|} \leqslant e^{- I_k}\), proving (11.32).

For the geometric bound, the leading scale ratio is at most \(LH^{b_j + 2}\) and the lower ratio at most \(LC^{10}\). The stage delays give \(L^2 C^{10} \leqslant H^3\), so the total physical cell side is at most \(a_k H^{b_j + 5}\). All covariance estimates depend only on the full displacement between distinct points. On the compact window its minimum length is positive, also after rounding at sufficiently large \(H\). The opening-support estimate uses the same positive maximum-coordinate distance. Neither argument divides by a single coordinate difference. This proves the asserted uniformity, including configurations arbitrarily close to coordinate coincidences.\(\Box\)

Different scales in this proposition use their own normalized lifts, but the coefficient map is identical. Consequently, if \(b_0 > b_j\), the lift normalized at \(b_0\) is exactly the linear combination of the degree-\(d\) lifts at \(b_j\) obtained by applying \(W_{H^{b_0 - b_j}}\) to its free label. On a fixed finite-dimensional degree space that costs at most \(CH^{(b_0 - b_j) d}\) in coefficient norm. The chessboard estimate for a single insertion and its free expectation therefore give the same factor for its chessboard seminorm. This is the comparison needed for the size and displacement bounds at three scales; it does not assert that one fixed unnormalized label has a small corrected error at all three.

Proposition 11.11. (Comparison of the separating correlations at finite cutoff) On the selected trajectory with the stage orders, block floors and choices of sufficiently large starting scales specified above, for each \(e\) there is an injective free measurement matrix \(G : \mathbb{C}^r \to \mathbb{C}^s\) and the following finite-cutoff approximations. For arbitrarily small \(\eta > 0\) one can choose a fixed comparison depth \(k\), bounded real local polynomial coefficient families \(u_{1, i}, \ldots, u_{r, i}\) with \(\|u_{j, i} \|_1 \leqslant B_k\), and finitely many bounded families of normalized local spectator insertions, all on the selected history, such that their point-correlation matrix \(Q_i^0\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \|Q_i^0 - G\|_{\max} \leqslant \eta\)

eventually for every period. The correlation configurations and small neighborhoods of them have a positive common coordinate separation and lie inside the smallest physical period. All these bounded normalized local insertion families have uniform chessboard bounds at every sufficiently fine fixed cell depth.

Proof. Choose the free basis and separating configurations from Chapter 3. Construct their actual common polynomial localization and simultaneous lifts by Section 11.12.1, and use the restarted family of Proposition 11.4. The opening estimate, stronger support bounds, restored source weight and output normalization in Section 11.12.4 give corrected subset chessboard-seminorm error \(e^{- I_k}\), uniformly in cutoff and period. Undoing the connected subtractions gives point-matrix error at most \(C_k e^{- I_k}\), where \(C_k\) is a polynomial in the finitely many free subset moments. These moments remain bounded as the rounded, rescaled configurations approach their fixed separated targets. Thus choose one sufficiently deep \(k\) to obtain the requested \(\eta\). The exact lift equations give a fixed bound \(B_k\) for all cutoffs and periods at that depth. The product-moment estimate supplies the chessboard bounds for normalized local insertions. No convergence or choice of a limiting equation subspace is used.\(\Box\)

This is a comparison before any cutoff limit. Its common coefficient extraction, auxiliary density and normalized lifts are the ones constructed above.

Recall the exact moving equation kernel \(K_i = \textsf{Z}_{N, p, e} P_{N, p, e} S_{t_N} R_e \mathcal{I}_e\). Its bounded projection \(\Pi_i\) is distinct from the near-identity section comparison \(P_{N, p, e}\). The latter is invertible; the former has kernel \(K_i\).

Proposition 11.12. (A separating correlation family on the entire moving complement) Using Proposition 11.11, for every fixed finite difference word \(l\) there are averaged separating correlation functionals \(L_i\) and a constant \(Z\), independent of \(N, p\), such that

\(\displaystyle \| \Pi_i v\|_1 \leqslant Z \|L_i \Pi_i v\|_{\infty} \quad (v \in V_e)\)

eventually for every period. Their tested correlation of a finite difference \(D_l F\) of a normalized local insertion with bounded chessboard seminorm is uniformly bounded. Their tested correlation of any trial whose chessboard seminorm tends to zero tends to zero uniformly in period.

Proof. Choose \(K\) with \(\|c\|_{\infty} \leqslant K \|Gc\|_{\infty}\) and put \(\delta = (2 Kr)^{- 1}\). The case \(r = 0\) is immediate. Use the assumed comparison with entrywise error at most \(\delta / 4\). The positional modulus for normalized local insertions from Section 11.7.1 holds on the separated neighborhoods with one constant and threshold for this finite list.

Choose \(b > 0\) so small that moving the distinguished point by at most \(2 |l| b\) stays in those neighborhoods and changes every normalized local insertion measurement by at most \(\delta / 4\). Put \(h_N = \lceil b / a_N \rceil\). Average over the shifts \(0, \ldots, h_N - 1\) once in each coordinate listed in \(l\), always in the distinguished slot. These normalized averages define \(L_i\). For the empty word take no averages.

The rows \((I - \Pi_i) u_{j, i}\) are bounded, hence admissible. Lemma 11.7 and the chessboard inequality with one distinguished insertion show that their averaged measurements tend to zero. Thus the matrix

\(\displaystyle Q_i = (L_i \Pi_i u_{j, i})_{j = 1}^r\)

is within \(\delta\) of \(G\) eventually. For any \(c \in \mathbb{C}^r\),

\(\displaystyle \|c\|_{\infty} \leqslant K\|Q_i c\|_{\infty} + Kr \delta \|c\|_{\infty}, \qquad \|c\|_{\infty} \leqslant 2 K \|Q_i c\|_{\infty} .\)

The \(r\) vectors \(\Pi_i u_{j, i}\) are therefore independent. They span \(\text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} \Pi_i\), since its dimension is exactly \(r\). Writing \(\Pi_i v = \sum_j c_j \Pi_i u_{j, i}\) gives

\(\displaystyle \| \Pi_i v\|_1 \leqslant ArB_k \|c\|_{\infty} \leqslant 2 ArB_k K \|L_i \Pi_i v\|_{\infty} .\)

Take \(Z \geqslant \max (1, 2 ArB_k K)\).

For the tested correlation assertions, inserting a distinguished trial of chessboard seminorm \(u_i\) with the bounded spectator fields gives a bound \(Cu_i\) uniformly throughout the neighborhoods. Averaging preserves it. For a difference word, differences commute with these averages, and each coordinate telescopes exactly:

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{h_N} \sum_{j = 0}^{h_N - 1} \frac{H (x + (j + 1) e_{\alpha}) - H (x + je_{\alpha})}{a_N} = \frac{H (x + h_N e_{\alpha}) - H (x)}{h_N a_N} .\)

Iteration bounds the averaged difference by \((2 / b)^{|l|}\) times the undifferentiated correlation bound. All endpoints stay in the chosen neighborhoods. This proves the tested correlation bound without assuming any pointwise bound on the differentiated field.\(\Box\)

Theorem 11.13. (Uniform bounds for finite differences) For the selected trajectory with the trial estimates of Section 11.8 and the stage requirements already specified for their uniformity, every fixed finite list of fields, every finite difference word applied separately to its slots, and every \(r_0 > 0\), their finite-volume correlation is bounded by one constant for all sufficiently large \(N\), every period \(p\), and every tuple with pairwise periodic maximum-coordinate distance at least \(r_0\) in physical units.

Proof. Start with a bounded local polynomial coefficient and a difference word \(l\). The exact physical difference identity and the translated local section bound make this a protected trial at degree \(e = d + |l|\). The factor \(a_N^{|l|}\) in its normalization index cancels all inverse mesh powers. Its stencil uses at most \(2^{|l|}\) fixed translates; its derivative cost grows by at most \((1 + 2^{\varpi_N q_N})^{|l|}\), a subpower quantity under the stage requirement on the starting scale. Trial descent gives an admissible coefficient \(x_i\) and an error \(E_i = O (x_i) - D_l F\) with chessboard seminorm tending to zero.

The correlation pairings just proved give \(\|L_i x_i \| \leqslant C\). Apply Lemma 11.5 with \(y_i = \Pi_i x_i\) and \(z_i = (x_i - y_i) / (1 +\|y_i \|_1)\). The latter family is admissible: its first term is a bounded scalar multiple of \(x_i\), and its second is norm bounded. It lies in the exact kernel of \(\Pi_i\), so its tested correlation tends to zero by Lemma 11.7. Consequently \(\|y_i \|_1 \leqslant 2 ZC + 1\). The chessboard bound for normalized local insertions applies to \(O (y_i)\), and the unnormalized admissible kernel family \(x_i - y_i\) has vanishing chessboard seminorm. The identity

\(\displaystyle D_l F = O (y_i) + O (x_i - y_i) - E_i\)

gives a uniform chessboard bound for the difference trial. Repeat for the finite list of orientations and slots.

For clarity, two cell scales are needed to plant a separated tuple. Choose a fixed coarse cell side \(a_{\ell} < r_0\). Choose a finer fixed depth \(k \geqslant \ell\), beyond all the finitely many scale thresholds for the chessboard bounds, with \(6 na_k < a_{\ell}\). At mesh \(N\) the corresponding integer spans \(t, T\) satisfy \(a_N t = a_k\) and \(a_N T = a_{\ell}\). In each coordinate one point forbids at most \(6 t\) translations of the coarse tiling if a margin \(3 t\) is required. The \(n\) points therefore leave a permissible translation, since \(6 nt < T\). Distinct points occupy distinct coarse cells: sharing a cell would make their periodic maximum-coordinate distance at most \(a_{\ell} < r_0\). The chessboard-seminorm comparison between aligned fine and coarse tilings transfers the depth-\(k\) bounds to these interior cells; it follows from the repeated chessboard inequality (maximizing the position of a repeated copy in its containing cell). Translation invariance alone is not this comparison. Planting the finitely many trials now bounds the correlation by the product of their fixed chessboard bounds. All spans, margins and mesh thresholds were chosen independently of \(p\). The periodic difference identity also holds at wrapped sites, completing the proof.\(\Box\)
