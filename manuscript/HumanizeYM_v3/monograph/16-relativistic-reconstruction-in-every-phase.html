Chapter 16
Relativistic reconstruction in every phase

We now pass from separated Euclidean correlations to relativistic fields. The order of the argument matters. Bounded slab insertions give analytic functions of complex time and their joint tempered boundary values. Lorentz covariance and spectral support then supply a full spacetime tube, in which Euclidean permutation symmetry becomes locality. Only after these steps do we form the Hilbert space of field words.

Throughout this chapter we use the conclusions of Chapters 14.1 and 15: the coordinate reflection spaces, exact Euclidean symmetries and common phase field calculus. Write \(\Xi\) for the compact phase space, \(\nu\) for its full-support probability measure, and \(S^{\xi}\) for its correlations. The finite-dimensional spaces \(F_d\) have compatible inclusions; their algebraic direct limit is denoted by \(F\). All estimates below concern a fixed finite list of labels. Uniformity means uniformity in \(\xi\), not in the number or degree of those labels.

The underlying reconstruction principles are those of Wightman and Osterwalder–Schrader [Wig56, OS73, OS75]. Here the available Euclidean data are smooth functions off the diagonals, with bounded slab operators. We therefore verify the boundary construction directly; an unproved extension of these functions across Euclidean collisions is not an input.

16.1Complex time and a uniform boundary distribution

Use coordinate four as time. In the phase reflection space let \(E_{\xi} (s) = e^{- sH_{\xi}^{\text{OS}}}\) and \(U_{\xi} (y)\) be the heat and spatial translation operators. The spectral theorem extends \(E_{\xi}\) to \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} s > 0\) as a holomorphic contraction. Let \(B_{\varepsilon}^{\xi} (A)\) be the slab operator of width \(2 \varepsilon\) with the single label \(A\) placed at its center. The phase version of the slab bound gives

\(\displaystyle \|B_{\varepsilon}^{\xi} (A)\| \leqslant C_A \varepsilon^{- q_A}, \qquad B_{\varepsilon + u}^{\xi} (A) = E_{\xi} (u) B_{\varepsilon}^{\xi} (A) E_{\xi} (u), \quad 0 < \varepsilon \leqslant 1, \) (16.1)

where \(u \geqslant 0\) in the padding identity. Linearity and compatibility under \(F_d \hookrightarrow F_{d + 1}\) follow by testing against all exterior words.

For \(z_j = t_j + iy_j\) with \(y_1 < \cdots < y_n\), set

\(\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}^{\xi} (z, x) = E_{\xi} (- iz - 2 \varepsilon) U_{\xi} (x) .\)

For \(4 \varepsilon < \min_j (y_{j + 1} - y_j)\) define

\(\displaystyle \textsf{F}_n^{\xi} (\boldsymbol{A}; z, \boldsymbol{x}) = \left\langle \Omega_{\xi}^{\text{OS}}, B_{\varepsilon}^{\xi} (A_1) \prod_{j = 2}^n [G_{\varepsilon}^{\xi} (z_j - z_{j - 1}, x_j - x_{j - 1}) B_{\varepsilon}^{\xi} (A_j)] \Omega_{\xi}^{\text{OS}} \right\rangle . \) (16.2)

The product is ordered from left to right with increasing \(j\). Padding and the semigroup law show that this expression is independent of \(\varepsilon\): replacing it by \(\varepsilon + u\) moves two factors \(E_{\xi} (u)\) onto each intervening gap, while the exterior factors fix the vacuum. At purely imaginary times it is \(S^{\xi}\) at the corresponding Euclidean points. It is holomorphic in the time variables and continuous in the real spatial variables. No norm continuity of the spatial unitaries is required.

Contraction and (16.1), with \(\varepsilon = \delta / 8\), give

\(\displaystyle | \textsf{F}_n^{\xi} (t + iy, x) | \leqslant C \delta^{- m}, \qquad 0 < \delta \leqslant 1, \quad y_{j + 1} - y_j \geqslant \delta, \) (16.3)

uniformly in all real positions and in \(\xi\). This is the quantitative regularity needed for reconstruction.

Proposition 16.1. (Joint tempered boundary) For every label tuple there is a tempered distribution \(W_n^{\xi} (\boldsymbol{A})\) in all \(4 n\) real coordinates, obtained as the boundary of (16.2) along arbitrary strictly ordered heights tending to zero. The distributions obey one common Schwartz seminorm bound, depend continuously on \(\xi\) when tested, and have the mixed correlation distribution as their \(\nu\)-average. The same assertions hold at fixed weakly ordered imaginary heights, including tied heights.

Proof. For a joint Schwartz test function \(f\) put \(\psi_y^{\xi} (f) = \int \textsf{F}_n^{\xi} (t + iy, x) f (t, x) \hspace{0.17em} dt \hspace{0.17em} dx\). Let \(r = (0, 1, \ldots, n - 1)\) and \(D = - i \sum_j r_j \partial_{t_j}\) acting on test functions. Cauchy's formula, (16.3), and integration by parts give \(\frac{d}{ds} \psi_{y + sr}^{\xi} (f) = \psi_{y + sr}^{\xi} (Df)\). Taylor's formula, evaluated backwards from \(s = 1\), is the exact identity

\(\displaystyle \psi_y^{\xi} (f) = \sum_{k = 0}^m \frac{(- 1)^k}{k!} \psi_{y + r}^{\xi} (D^k f) - \int_0^1 \frac{(- s)^m}{m!} \psi_{y + sr}^{\xi} (D^{m + 1} f) \hspace{0.17em} ds. \) (16.4)

The integrand is bounded by \(C \|D^{m + 1} f\|_1\) for every weakly ordered \(y\): the factor \(s^m\) cancels the worst gap singularity. The right-hand side therefore defines the extension to all such \(y\), continuously by dominated convergence. Its bound is a fixed finite sum of \(L^1\) norms of derivatives of \(f\), hence a Schwartz seminorm. In particular there is no restriction on ratios of the heights approaching zero.

For strictly ordered heights, phase continuity follows from the normal family theorem in the time variables: every convergent phase sequence has locally convergent analytic subsequences, and agreement at imaginary times identifies each limit with the limiting phase by the identity theorem. Local boundedness is uniform by (16.3). Dominated convergence after testing gives continuity of \(\psi_y^{\xi}\). The same uniqueness argument continues the barycenter identity from imaginary times. Formula (16.4) transfers both claims to weakly ordered heights and to the boundary. Arity zero and one require no gap argument and are covered by their constant bounds.\(\Box\)

16.2Lorentz covariance and the spectral cone

Let \(s_4\) be reflection of Euclidean coordinate four. The field adjoint on \(F\) is the antilinear involution

\(\displaystyle A^{\ast} = R (s_4) \bar{A} .\)

If \(L_{ab}\) is the infinitesimal Euclidean rotation action on labels, the spatial rotation generators are \(L_{ij}\) and the boost generators are \(- iL_{4 i}\). Their commutation relations give the proper orthochronous Lorentz action \(\rho\). It descends from the covering group because a full spatial turn already acts trivially under \(R\). The above involution commutes with \(\rho\): the sign from conjugating \(i\) cancels the reflection sign on \(L_{4 i}\). We use spacetime coordinates \((x^1, x^2, x^3, t)\), metric \(\eta = \text{diag} (- 1, - 1, - 1, 1)\), and

\(\displaystyle \bar{V}_+ = \{(p, E) : E \geqslant |p|\} .\)

Fourier duality uses the ordinary coordinate pairing \(p \cdot x + Et\). The dual Lorentz action is the inverse transpose, which also preserves \(\bar{V}_+\).

Differentiate Euclidean rotational covariance at imaginary times and continue the identity using the Cauchy–Riemann equations. The result is the infinitesimal Lorentz covariance identity for \(\textsf{F}_n^{\xi}\). Testing and taking its joint boundary proves the distributional identity: terms containing a height tend to zero because the tested boundary families have the common seminorm bound of Proposition 16.1. Integrating the finite-dimensional label action and the corresponding flow on Schwartz test functions proves Lorentz covariance of \(W_n^{\xi}\). Translations and spatial rotations continue directly.

Use the Fourier convention

\(\displaystyle \hat{f} (X) = \int e^{- iX \cdot p} f (p) \hspace{0.17em} dp, \qquad \hat{W} (f) = W (\hat{f}) .\)

For a compactly supported test function \(b\) in momentum space, differentiating simultaneously the imaginary height and the factor multiplying \(b\) gives

\(\displaystyle \hat{W}_n^{\xi} (b) = \psi_y^{\xi} \hspace{-0.17em} \left( \widehat{e^{\sum_j y_j p_j^4} b} \right) . \) (16.5)

Indeed the two differentiated terms cancel, and their common value at height zero is the left side. Compact frequency support makes every intermediate test function a Schwartz function.

Translation invariance restricts support to \(\sum_j p_j = 0\). Put \(q_j = \sum_{k = j}^n p_k\). If the support of \(b\) lies in \(q_j^4 < 0\), let the corresponding imaginary gap tend to infinity in (16.5), keeping the other gaps positive. Exponential decay of the weighted test function and the uniform bound at gaps at least one show that its pairing vanishes. A partition of unity and Lorentz covariance now give

\(\displaystyle \text{supp} \hat{W}_n^{\xi} \subset K_n, \qquad K_n = \left\{ p : \sum_j p_j = 0, \quad q_j \in \bar{V}_+ (2 \leqslant j \leqslant n) \right\} . \) (16.6)

To justify the last step, a vector outside \(\bar{V}_+\) can be made to have negative time component by a proper orthochronous Lorentz transformation. Compact subsets of the complement admit finitely many such neighborhoods.

16.3The spacetime tube and locality

The boundary distribution just constructed is denoted by \(W_n^{\xi}\); \(K_n\) in (16.6) is its momentum support cone. Define

\(\displaystyle C_n = \{Y : Y_{j + 1} - Y_j \in \bar{V}_+ \}, \qquad \mathcal{T}_n = \{X + iY : Y_{j + 1} - Y_j \in V_+ \} .\)

For \(p \in K_n\), telescoping gives \(p \cdot Y = \sum_{j = 2}^n q_j \cdot (Y_j - Y_{j - 1}) \geqslant 0\). For strictly interior \(Y\) this is bounded below by \(c_Y |p|\), locally uniformly in \(Y\). One way to see the strict margin is to choose a ball of radius \(2 c_Y\) around \(Y\) inside the strict-gap cone and apply positivity to \(Y - c_Y p / |p|\).

Choose a smooth cutoff \(\chi\) equal to one on the distance-one neighborhood of \(K_n\), supported in its distance-three neighborhood, with all derivatives bounded. Convolution of the distance-two neighborhood's indicator with a bump of radius \(1 / 2\) is one such choice. Then

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{W}_n^{\xi} (\boldsymbol{A}; Z) = (2 \pi)^{- 4 n} \hat{W}_n^{\xi} (\chi (p) e^{ip \cdot Z}), \qquad Z \in \mathcal{T}_n, \) (16.7)

is well defined: the tested function and all its derivatives decay exponentially on the support of \(\chi\). It is holomorphic into Schwartz space before the distribution is applied. It agrees with (16.2) at strictly ordered scalar heights, by (16.5) and compact-frequency approximation.

For \(Y \in C_n\), the multiplier \(\chi e^{- p \cdot Y}\) and its derivatives are uniformly bounded on compact sets of \(Y\). In fact distance at most three from \(K_n\) gives \(- p \cdot Y \leqslant 3 |Y|\). Multiplication by this function is therefore continuous on Schwartz space, including as \(Y\) approaches a tied gap. This proves the partial boundary formula

\(\displaystyle W_{n, Y}^{\xi} (f) = \hat{W}_n^{\xi} \left( \chi e^{- p \cdot Y} (2 \pi)^{- 4 n} \int e^{ip \cdot X} f (X) \hspace{0.17em} dX \right) . \) (16.8)

It has boundary \(W_n^{\xi}\) at \(Y = 0\). The cutoff is essential here: the exponential alone need not be a Schwartz multiplier off the spectral cone. Tube Lorentz covariance follows from boundary covariance; changed cutoffs agree near \(K_n\) and hence give the same pairing.

Proposition 16.2. (Adjacent spacelike exchange) Let \(\sigma\) exchange slots \(j\) and \(j + 1\). On the open set where \(X_{j + 1} - X_j\) is spacelike,

\(\displaystyle W_n^{\xi} (\boldsymbol{A}; X) = W_n^{\xi} (\sigma \boldsymbol{A}; \sigma X)\)

as distributions. This statement permits arbitrary exterior insertions.

Proof. Keep positive scalar imaginary gaps except for one tied pair \(y_j = y_{j + 1}\). Write its spacelike real difference as \((v, t)\), and set \(e = v / |v|\). The imaginary part of its image under a boost of parameter \(i \alpha\) in direction \(e\) is \(\sin \alpha \hspace{0.17em} (te, |v|)\), a strictly forward vector for small positive \(\alpha\). All other gaps stay strictly forward by continuity. Thus a small positive imaginary boost enters the original tube, and a negative one followed by exchange enters the exchanged tube.

Fix one sufficiently small nonzero boost parameter first. The transformed tied-gap point is strictly inside the relevant tube, so a whole complex neighborhood of the original point is mapped into that tube. Pulling back the holomorphic function, with its entire finite-dimensional label action, therefore defines a genuine germ on that neighborhood. Use the opposite sign and the exchanged tube for the second germ. Real Lorentz covariance and the identity theorem show that each germ extends its original tube function; the whole short imaginary-boost path stays in that tube at neighboring interior points.

At vanishing real times these germs coincide. Their restrictions to the Euclidean slice \((it_k, x_k)\) equal the same smooth, permutation-symmetric function on its two ordered sides. At the tied pair the spatial points are distinct, so this Euclidean function is smooth there. Consequently all derivatives of the two restrictions agree at the tie. The slice is totally real and its tangent space spans the ambient complex vector space. The holomorphic Taylor coefficients therefore agree, proving identity of the germs.

For general real times multiply every real time by \(s \in [0, 1]\). The distinguished difference remains spacelike along this path and the other imaginary gaps remain strict. A finite chain of overlapping germ neighborhoods propagates the equality from \(s = 0\) to \(s = 1\) by uniqueness. Take the continuous partial boundaries (16.8), and then remove the other imaginary gaps. This proves the identity against test functions compactly supported in the spacelike open set. Smooth compact cutoffs converging in Schwartz space give the stated distributional version. No permutation of coincident Lorentzian boundary values has been assumed.\(\Box\)

16.4Field words and the reflection space

A letter is a pair \((A, f)\) with \(A \in F\) and \(f \in \mathscr{S} (\mathbb{R}^4)\). The star of a word reverses its order and replaces every letter by \((A^{\ast}, \bar{f})\). Let \(\mathcal{P}\) be their algebraic span, including the empty word \(1\). The distributions \(W_n^{\xi}\) define a functional \(w_{\xi}\) on \(\mathcal{P}\) by evaluation on product test functions.

The essential positivity statement follows inside the phase reflection space. Give a word strictly increasing positive imaginary heights, smear its real variables, and use the slab product to obtain a vector \(h_y^{\xi} (b)\). Its Gram pairing with another such vector is a tested continued correlation, with the reflected first word on the left. Proposition 16.1 permits the two height lists to tend to zero independently, so

\(\displaystyle \langle h_y^{\xi} (b), h_z^{\xi} (c) \rangle \longrightarrow w_{\xi} (b^{\ast} c) .\)

Expanding \(\|h_y^{\xi} (b) - h_z^{\xi} (b)\|^2\) proves that these vectors are Cauchy. Their limits \(v_{\xi} (b)\) have Gram form \(w_{\xi} (b^{\ast} c)\), which is therefore positive. The same common seminorm bounds extend positivity to finite sequences of joint Schwartz test functions by tensor-product density.

The standard word reconstruction [Wig56] now gives

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{N}_{\xi} = \{b : w_{\xi} (b^{\ast} b) = 0\}, \quad \mathcal{D}_{\xi} =\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{N}_{\xi}, \quad \mathcal{H}_{\xi} = \overline{\mathcal{D}_{\xi}}, \quad \Omega_{\xi} = [1], \qquad \Phi_{\xi} (A, f) [b] = [(A, f) b] .\)

For clarity, its null-space check is worth recording. If \(b\) is null, Cauchy–Schwarz says it pairs to zero with every word. The concatenation identity moves a letter to the other side of the pairing, so its product with \(b\) also pairs to zero with every word, including itself. Thus all fields are well defined on the same invariant dense domain. The same identity gives

\(\displaystyle \Phi_{\xi} (A^{\ast}, \bar{f}) \subset \Phi_{\xi} (A, f)^{\ast} .\)

They are closable, and the joint distribution bound gives continuity of \(f \mapsto \Phi_{\xi} (A, f) v\) in Hilbert norm for every \(v \in \mathcal{D}_{\xi}\). We do not assert that these generally unbounded fields are bounded or that their self-adjoint closures exist without further input.

Theorem 16.3. (Fields and unitary equivalence with the reflection-positive space) In every phase the fields above are local, covariant under a strongly continuous unitary representation of the proper orthochronous Poincaré group, and have joint translation spectrum in \(\bar{V}_+\). The map

\(\displaystyle J_{\xi}^{\text{OS}} [b] = v_{\xi} (b)\)

extends to a unitary map from \(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\) onto the phase reflection space. It sends vacuum to vacuum, intertwines spatial translations and heat semigroups, and the field labeling is faithful.

Proof. Covariance preserves the word Gram form, hence defines unitaries on its quotient. Schwartz continuity proves strong continuity first on words and then on their completion. Locality follows by applying Proposition 16.2 inside arbitrary word matrix elements. The joint spectral theorem for the commuting translation unitaries, together with (16.6) applied to such matrix elements, places their spectrum in \(\bar{V}_+\).

The Gram identity makes \(J_{\xi}^{\text{OS}}\) an isometry. Its range is closed, but density must still be checked. For a smooth compactly supported joint frequency test function \(b\), multiplication by \(e^{- \sum_j y_j p_j^4}\) is harmless. Equation (16.5) identifies the norms and mixed pairing of the image of the resulting boundary word with the smeared positive-height vector at heights \(y\). They are the same vector. Test functions with compact Fourier support are dense in Schwartz space; therefore the closed range contains every smeared positive-height vector. Approximate identities, followed by a perturbation of tied times and permutation of separated Euclidean points, give all Euclidean word vectors. These span the reflection space. The same positive-height identities prove heat and spatial intertwining.

Finally a label acting as zero has every boundary correlation zero. The Laplace identity, Euclidean symmetry and continuity imply that all its separated Euclidean correlations vanish. The common null-space identity of Chapter 15, and the injectivity of the matrix of correlation pairings used there, force this label to be zero in \(F\).\(\Box\)

16.5The low-energy estimate and uniqueness of the vacuum in each phase

The phase measure has full support, and the zero-energy space is a line for almost every phase by 15.10. To remove the exceptional set requires a bound uniform in \(\xi\). We prove it from locality and the spectral cone. This argument controls energies near zero, not a positive mass gap.

For a finite sum \(A\) of field words with compactly supported smooth test functions, let \(\mu_{\xi}\) be the joint spectral measure of \(A \Omega_{\xi}\), and let \(\mu_{\xi}^{\ast}\) be that of \(A^{\ast} \Omega_{\xi}\). Their total masses have a common bound \(M\) by Proposition 16.1. Enclose all supports in a fixed spacetime box. Locality gives some \(R < \infty\), independent of phase, such that

\(\displaystyle a_{\xi} (y, t) = \langle \Omega_{\xi}, [A^{\ast}, \alpha_{(y, t)} (A)] \Omega_{\xi} \rangle = 0 \quad (|y| > R, |t| \leqslant 1), \) (16.9)

where \(\alpha_z (A) = U_{\xi} (z) AU_{\xi} (z)^{\ast}\) on the word domain. The spectral expression is the difference of the Fourier transforms of \(\mu_{\xi}\) and the reflected measure \(\mu_{\xi}^{\ast}\).

Take the odd time average

\(\displaystyle \bar{a}_{\xi} (y) = \int_0^1 \frac{a_{\xi} (y, t) - a_{\xi} (y, - t)}{2 i} \hspace{0.17em} dt.\)

It is bounded by \(M\) and supported in \(\bar{B}_R\). Its spatial Fourier measure is the sum of the pushforwards of \(\varpi (E) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\xi} (p, E)\) and \(\varpi (E) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\xi}^{\ast} (p, E)\) under \(p\) and \(- p\), respectively, where

\(\displaystyle \varpi (E) = \frac{1 - \cos E}{E}, \qquad \varpi (0) = 0.\)

Both measures are positive; on \(0 \leqslant E \leqslant 1\), \(\varpi (E) \geqslant E / \pi\). Choose a smooth momentum bump \(b_u\), equal to one on \(B_u\), supported in \(B_{2 u}\), and between zero and one. Its inverse Fourier transform is bounded by \(Cu^3\). On the shell \(u / 2 < E \leqslant u\), cone support implies \(|p| \leqslant u\), so positivity and Fourier inversion yield

\(\displaystyle cu \hspace{0.17em} \mu_{\xi} \{u / 2 < E \leqslant u\} \leqslant \left| \int \bar{a}_{\xi} (y) \hat{b}_u (y) \hspace{0.17em} dy \right| \leqslant CMR^3 u^3 .\)

Dyadic summation proves the first assertion below.

Theorem 16.4. (Uniform low-energy bound) For every such \(A\) there is \(C_A\), independent of phase, such that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} \mu_{\xi} \{0 < E \leqslant u\} & \leqslant C_A u^2 & & (0 < u \leqslant 1), \hspace{3cm} \text{(16.10)} \\ 0 \leqslant \langle A \Omega_{\xi}, (e^{- sH_{\xi}} - P_{\Omega, \xi}) A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle & \leqslant 2 C_A' s^{- 2} & & (s \geqslant 1), \hspace{3cm} \text{(16.11)} \end{array}\)

where \(P_{\Omega, \xi}\) is the projection onto \(\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\xi}\). In every phase, \(\ker H_{\xi} =\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\xi}\); the same holds in its reflection space.

Proof. Initially use the full zero-energy projection \(Q_{\xi}\), without assuming that its range is a line. Increase the constant in (16.10) so the estimate holds for all \(u > 0\) by the total mass bound. Tonelli's theorem gives

\(\displaystyle \langle A \Omega_{\xi}, (e^{- sH_{\xi}} - Q_{\xi}) A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle = \int_{E > 0} e^{- sE} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\xi} \leqslant C_A' s \int_0^{\infty} e^{- su} u^2 \hspace{0.17em} du = 2 C_A' s^{- 2} .\)

Theorem 16.3 transfers almost-everywhere vacuum uniqueness from the reflection spaces, so \(Q_{\xi} = P_{\Omega, \xi}\) almost everywhere. For fixed \(s > 0\) the vacuum-subtracted heat pairing is continuous in \(\xi\): it is the joint distribution at a fixed weakly ordered height with gap \(s\) between its two word halves. This follows either by the independent height limits or by (16.8). Thus its bound by \(2 C_A' s^{- 2}\) holds everywhere, since any nonempty open violation set would have positive \(\nu\)-measure.

Let \(s \to \infty\). Strong heat convergence gives \(\|Q_{\xi} A \Omega_{\xi} \|^2 = | \langle \Omega_{\xi}, A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle |^2\). Since \(Q_{\xi}\) fixes the unit vacuum, its value on each such word vector lies in the vacuum line. Compactly smeared words are dense: multiply Schwartz test functions by expanding smooth cutoffs and use joint Gram continuity. It follows that \(Q_{\xi} = P_{\Omega, \xi}\) on the full space. The onto heat intertwiner gives the last assertion.\(\Box\)

16.6The weak commutant and local stress charges

Irreducibility should be stated in a form appropriate to unbounded fields. Their common domain is \(\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\), the span of field words applied to the vacuum. A bounded operator \(B\) belongs to their weak commutant if

\(\displaystyle \langle \Phi^{\ast} u, Bv \rangle = \langle u, B \Phi v \rangle, \qquad u, v \in \mathcal{D}_{\xi}, \) (16.12)

for every smeared field \(\Phi\). The adjoint inclusion proved above ensures that these \(u\) are in its adjoint domain; no preservation of \(\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\) by \(B\) is assumed.

Proposition 16.5. The weak commutant in every phase consists of scalar operators.

Proof. Iterate (16.12) to move words through matrix elements. For a word \(A\) put \(u = A \Omega_{\xi}\), \(v = A^{\ast} \Omega_{\xi}\). The functions

\(\displaystyle p (z) = \langle u, e^{izH_{\xi}} B \Omega_{\xi} \rangle, \qquad q (z) = \langle B^{\ast} \Omega_{\xi}, e^{izH_{\xi}} v \rangle\)

are bounded and holomorphic in the upper half-plane, continuous to its boundary, and satisfy \(p (t) = q (- t)\). Pasting across the real line and using Morera's theorem gives a bounded entire function, hence a constant. The heat limit and Theorem 16.4 imply \(B \Omega_{\xi} = b \Omega_{\xi}\). Moving words again gives \(\langle A' \Omega_{\xi}, BA \Omega_{\xi} \rangle = b \langle A' \Omega_{\xi}, A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle\). Density proves \(B = bI\). Scalars plainly satisfy the condition.\(\Box\)

Let \(T_{ab}\) be the normalized Euclidean stress tensor of Proposition 14.6. Define

\(\displaystyle \zeta_i = 1 (i \leqslant 3), \qquad \zeta_4 = - i, \qquad T^{\text{M}}_{ab} = - \zeta_a \zeta_b T_{ab} .\)

We use the same notation for its smeared relativistic field. Thus \(T^{\text{M}}_{44} = T_{44}\), \(T^{\text{M}}_{4 i} = iT_{4 i}\), and \(T^{\text{M}}_{ij} = - T_{ij}\). The adjoint involution, covariance and the continued derivative identity give a symmetric Hermitian Lorentz tensor with

\(\displaystyle \sum_a \eta_{aa} T^{\text{M}}_{ab} (\partial_a f) = 0 \quad \text{on } \mathcal{D}_{\xi} .\)

Indeed continuation of a Euclidean derivative label is \(\Phi_{\xi} (\partial_a A, f) = - \zeta_a^{- 1} \Phi_{\xi} (A, \partial_a f)\).

For an affine Killing vector \(K (x) = k + Vx\), where \(V^{\textsf{T}} \eta + \eta V = 0\), let \(\Sigma_K\) be its infinitesimal Lorentz label action. Choose real \(f \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R})\) of integral one and \(c \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R}^3)\), and define the local charge

\(\displaystyle Q_K (f, c) = \sum_b T^{\text{M}}_{4 b} (K^b (c \otimes f)) .\)

Suppose \(g \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R}^4)\) and \(c = 1\) on a ball such that every point \((y, t)\) outside that ball with \(t \in \text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} f\) is spacelike to \(\text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} g\). Then

\(\displaystyle i [Q_K (f, c), \Phi_{\xi} (A, g)] = \Phi_{\xi} (\Sigma_K A, g) - \sum_a \Phi_{\xi} (A, \partial_a (K^a g)) \quad \text{on } \mathcal{D}_{\xi} . \) (16.13)

This is a local charge identity, not a claim that the unsmeared spatial integral of stress exists as an operator.

To justify its continuation, first integrate the Euclidean shell identity with a spatial cutoff \(c\) on the two sides of the distinguished insertion. Conservation moves the derivative to \(c\); that term stays separated from the insertion. The two current integrals have one-sided limits whose jump is \(\Sigma_K A + K \cdot \partial A\). Retain strictly ordered positive heights for every spectator and put their complex times on a path \(z_j (\lambda) = iy_j + \lambda t_j\). Place the current immediately above or below the distinguished time, at distance \(\varepsilon\).

On \(\text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} \nabla c\) the spacelike holomorphic germs from Proposition 16.2 bound the current integrals uniformly as \(\varepsilon \downarrow 0\), locally along this path. Conservation bounds the difference between two such integrals by \(C | \varepsilon - \varepsilon' |\). Their limits are therefore holomorphic. At small imaginary \(\lambda\) the jump is the Euclidean one; analytic continuation gives the same jump at \(\lambda = 1\). Smearing with \(f\) and \(g\) and taking the joint partial boundaries proves (16.13); integration by parts produces \(\partial_a (K^a g)\). General word spectators follow from the common Schwartz bounds. Locality removes any change in \(c\) outside the stated ball. This retains precisely the normalization established by the Euclidean shell flux.
