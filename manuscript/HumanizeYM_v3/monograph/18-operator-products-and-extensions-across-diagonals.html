Chapter 18
Operator products and extensions across diagonals

There are two distinct questions about colliding insertions. An operator product expansion replaces a small cluster by finitely many local fields when tested against separated spectators. A distributional extension assigns values to test functions whose supports meet collision diagonals. The first requires quantitative information about the marked renormalization group. The second already follows, with a conservative finite order, from the polynomial separated bounds. Neither construction defines a product of point fields by simply setting their positions equal.

We use the common filtration \(F_d\), its inclusions \(\iota_{d, e}\), and the mixed correlations \(S\) from Chapter 13. The orthogonal actions on these spaces and the phase correlations \(S^{\xi}\) are those of Chapters 14 and 15. Write \(\mathfrak{A}\) for the commutative compression algebra of Chapter 15 and \(M (w)\) for the compression of a whole separated word \(w\). We first convert the lattice replacement estimate into the norm bound needed for the operator product expansion. The contact extension constructed later does not use it.

The OPE originates with Wilson [Wil69]. Fröb and Holland establish the Yang–Mills OPE to every fixed order of perturbation theory [FH16]. That result explains the relevant structure, but does not supply the nonperturbative estimate required below. The standard distribution-extension argument is the normal Taylor subtraction of Brunetti and Fredenhagen [BF00, Section 5.2, Theorems 5.2–5.3]. We use that procedure with an explicit choice adapted to the union of all pair diagonals; no reconstruction theorem requires this particular extension.

18.1A fixed prescription for all finite degrees

A probe is a fixed word \(v\) at distinct nonzero positions. It defines \(\lambda (A) = S (A (0) v)\) on each field space on which \(A\) is defined. These functionals separate \(F_d\), by the definition of correlation-null fields. Since \(F_d\) is finite dimensional, finitely many probes span its dual. A probe chosen at one degree continues to act at every higher degree by the same correlation, using the physical inclusions.

One can choose fields \(u_{\alpha}\) and finite linear combinations \(b_{\alpha}\) of probes, with a degree \(| \alpha |\) attached to each, such that

\(\displaystyle b_{\alpha} (u_{\beta}) = \delta_{\alpha \beta}, \qquad A = \sum_{| \alpha | \leqslant d} b_{\alpha} (A) u_{\alpha} \quad (A \in F_d), \) (18.1)

where fields in lower degrees are included in \(F_d\) when necessary. All these identities hold simultaneously at every finite degree. Here is the useful linear-algebra construction. Suppose the choices through \(d\) have been made and let \(R : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\) be inclusion. The probes already chosen give the map \(B : F_{d + 1} \to F_d\), \(B (A) = \sum_{| \alpha | \leqslant d} b_{\alpha} (A) u_{\alpha}\). By (18.1), \(BR\) is the identity on \(F_d\). For any section \(S_0\) of the quotient map \(q : F_{d + 1} \to F_{d + 1} / RF_d\), replace it by \(S_0 - RBS_0\). The corrected section is killed by every old probe. Lift a quotient basis through it, and represent its dual quotient coordinates by finite probe combinations. This extends (18.1) without changing an earlier field or probe. In particular \(b_{\alpha}\) vanishes on all degrees below \(| \alpha |\). This is only finite-dimensional linear algebra; no infinite expansion of a field is being asserted.

For a cluster word \(w\), define the unsymmetrized field reconstruction

\(\displaystyle H_d (w) = \sum_{| \alpha | \leqslant d} b_{\alpha} (w) u_{\alpha},\)

where \(b_{\alpha} (w)\) means replacing the distinguished field in its probe correlations by the entire cluster. Each probe correlation is evaluated in the mixed hierarchy \(S\). Thus the coefficients have no dependence on a phase or on spectators later placed outside the cluster. At a center \(o\), apply this definition to \(\tau_{- o} w\). For each finite collection of probes there is a positive clearance: a cluster sufficiently close to \(o\) cannot meet a probe point, even after rotating the cluster. All subsequent formulas are used on this clear region.

Write \(R_d (K)\) for the orthogonal action on \(F_d\), with normalized Haar measure on \(O (4)\). Symmetrize the reconstruction by

\(\displaystyle A_d w = \int_{O (4)} R_d (K) H_d (K^{- 1} w) \hspace{0.17em} dK, \qquad B_d w = \tfrac{1}{2} (A_d w + \overline{A_d \bar{w}}) .\)

Both maps fix every single field of degree at most \(d\) inserted at zero. They are smooth on the clear configuration region, since finitely many smooth correlation functions are integrated over a compact group. They preserve reality and intertwine rotations. Translation covariance is implemented by translating the center as above.

Averaging must be followed by a compatible removal of lower degrees. Let \(I_q\) send \(A \in F_q\) to the single insertion \(A (0)\) and put \(L_{i, q} = B_i I_q : F_q \to F_i\). Define on \(F_j\)

\(\displaystyle Q_0 = 1, \qquad Q_j = 1 - \sum_{i < j} \iota_{i, j} Q_i L_{i, j}, \qquad E_j = \bigcap_{i < j} \ker (Q_i L_{i, j}) . \) (18.2)

The inclusion identities give, by induction,

\(\displaystyle Q_j L_{j, q} = 0 \quad (q < j), \qquad \sum_{i \leqslant j} \iota_{i, j} Q_i L_{i, q} = \iota_{q, j} \quad (q \leqslant j) .\)

Consequently \(Q_j\) is the projection onto \(E_j\) along \(\iota_{j - 1, j} F_{j - 1}\). These spaces are invariant under rotations and conjugation. Fix real bases \((O_{j, a})_a\) of \(E_j\), taking the unit itself in degree zero, and denote their coordinate functionals by \(\omega_{j, a}\). The coefficients used in this chapter are

\(\displaystyle c_{j, a} (w ; o) = \omega_{j, a} Q_j B_j (\tau_{- o} w) . \) (18.3)

The fields, probes and projections are fixed once, before any truncation, phase, spectator list or small-distance limit.

18.2The marked replacement estimate

The expansion of Chapter 11 keeps every joint nilpotent marker coefficient. To replace a cluster by a single field, one needs more than the one-insertion graph contraction. Markers may merge as their separation becomes smaller than a block. Their source weights multiply; an incoming term involving at least two insertions is kept as one factor, with its support, prescribed set of large-field sites and eligibility condition. Its stronger support norm absorbs the loss when several marks are assigned to the same coarse region. After local Taylor subtraction, the signed residual must be transported on the same history and compared in the physical quotient. Taking absolute values before that subtraction loses the cancellation.

Here is the quantitative replacement estimate. It is formulated so that its two uses, determination of local polynomial coefficients and phase-uniform error control, are visible. Put \(w_0 = \sum_i d_i\) for a fixed cluster degree list, and let

\(\displaystyle x_i = o + rz_i, \qquad |z_i | \leqslant M, \qquad |z_i - z_j | \geqslant \eta > 0.\)

Cluster labels range over fixed bounded subsets of their finite-dimensional spaces. Spectator labels have bounded degrees and coefficients; their positions stay at distance at least \(c > 0\) from \(o\) and from one another. The constants below may depend on these finite lists, \(M, \eta, c\) and a requested error exponent, but not on positions, phases or \(r\).

Proposition 18.1. (Same-history marked replacement) For every finite degree \(D\) and every \(\epsilon > 0\), on the geometry just specified and for all sufficiently small \(r\), the unsymmetrized reconstruction coefficients \(b_{\alpha} (w ; o) = b_{\alpha} (\tau_{- o} w)\), \(| \alpha | \leqslant D\), obey

\(\displaystyle |b_{\alpha} (w ; o) | \leqslant Cr^{| \alpha | - w_0 - \epsilon} .\)

Let \(A = H_D (\tau_{- o} w) \in F_D\) be the reconstructed field. For each spectator word \(v\), define the difference of compressed operators

\(\displaystyle Z_D = M (wv) - M (A (o) v) .\)

The actual lattice marked replacement, with its signed residual and all original terms involving at least two insertions retained, gives the bound uniform in the number of reflected copies

\(\displaystyle \langle \Omega, (Z_D^{\ast} Z_D)^k \Omega \rangle \leqslant (Cr^{D + 1 - w_0 - \epsilon})^{2 k}, \qquad k \geqslant 1. \) (18.4)

The constant and the small-\(r\) threshold precede \(k\); cutoff thresholds may depend on each fixed \(k\). The same estimates hold for every fixed finite list of differentiated insertions, with the corresponding scaling dimensions, and for the finitely many probe families above, uniformly under their rotations and conjugations.

This is an analytic estimate on the selected family, not an assertion that any bounded marked expansion has an OPE. Its construction requires the signed local transport, the stronger anchored marked support bound, and the graded quotient comparison. In particular, if a moving field has coefficient size \(\varepsilon_m\) at scale \(m\), the needed quotient estimate is of the form

\(\displaystyle \text{dist}_{F_t} (A, \iota_{i - 1, t} F_{i - 1}) \leqslant a_m^{- (t - i)} e^{o (\log (1 / a_m))} \varepsilon_m .\)

The lower field must be identified by all spectator responses, using the normalized-null lemma of Chapter 13. A bound on a chosen coordinate representative alone does not supply this statement. The estimate uniform in the number of reflected copies also cannot be replaced by a bound on the mixed mean of \(Z_D\). These are supplied by the lattice estimate of Section 13.10 and its passage to the compression algebra below.

Proof of Proposition 18.1. The lattice estimate in Proposition 13.8 was proved before phase decomposition. We use its uniformity in replica number to obtain the algebra-valued statement for the reconstructions \(H_D\) fixed above.

Let \(F\) be an interior lattice difference with chessboard seminorm at most \(B\), and let its exterior factors have interior seminorms at most \(B_1, \ldots, B_q\). Put copies of the complete difference and exterior factors in separated cells before taking limits. The repeated-word identity and chessboard inequality give, for its compressed limit \(Z\) and each integer \(n \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle \int_{\Xi} | \xi (Z) |^{2 n} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\xi) \leqslant (B \prod_{l = 1}^q B_l)^{2 n} .\)

The seminorm bounds \(B, B_1, \ldots, B_q\) are independent of \(n\). For each fixed \(n\), the cutoff and the separation of the copies may need to be increased. This uses the complete signed density, with every term involving at least two insertions, from the proof of (13.28). Equation (15.6) then gives \(\|Z\| \leqslant B \prod_l B_l\). Positivity here belongs to the limiting commutative algebra and the original Wilson law, not to an individual signed effective activity.

Apply this observation to the auxiliary field \(A_t\) in the proof of Proposition 13.9. On grid configurations its replacement error has norm with exponent \(Z_0 - (t + 1 / 16) (\theta - \theta_-)\), where \(Z_0 = W \theta_+ + 4 n_{\text{cl}} (\theta_+ - \theta)\). Fixed exterior factors cost a bounded factor. The graded quotient bound gives exponent \(Z_0 - i \theta\) for a degree-\(i\) component of \(A_t\); all the finitely many fields used in its reconstruction have uniformly bounded compressed exterior words. Testing the same error against the fixed probes gives the probe coordinate errors. With \(t\) chosen as in that proof, the omitted degrees and the low-coordinate errors therefore give

\(\displaystyle \|M (wv) - M ((H_D (\tau_{- o} w)) (o) v)\| \preccurlyeq e^{[Z_0 - (D + 1) \theta] T} .\)

There is no spectator-dependent choice of \(H_D\): the only geometry-dependent auxiliary field has disappeared from this inequality.

The derivative descent of Proposition 13.9 works in this norm. Equivalently, apply it to every character, using the same sharp chessboard seminorm bounds, and take their supremum. The crude and grid constants are uniform in the character. A positional derivative changes its input degree by one; a finite descending induction removes the grid restriction, also for any fixed list of differentiated fields. Letting the two resolution heights tend to one yields

\(\displaystyle \|M (wv) - M ((H_D (\tau_{- o} w)) (o) v)\| \leqslant Cr^{D + 1 - W - \varepsilon} .\)

The coefficient assertion is exactly the corresponding fixed-probe estimate in Proposition 13.9. Compact label sets, conjugation, and the finite probe families do not change its constants. For their rotation orbits the same uniform estimates apply on the compact set of rotated labels and positions; choose \(r\) so all these probes remain separated from the cluster.

Finally apply the vacuum state to \((Z_D^{\ast} Z_D)^n\). Its norm is at most \(\|Z_D \|^{2 n}\), giving (18.4) with the same \(C\) and the same small-\(r\) threshold for every \(n\). Thus the order of quantifiers is obtained from the norm estimate, rather than from an uncontrolled sequence of separate moment constants.\(\Box\)

18.3Finite-order operator product expansion

Theorem 18.2. (Phase-uniform finite-order expansion) The fixed coefficients (18.3) satisfy, for every finite \(D\) and \(\epsilon > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} |c_{j, a} (w ; o) | & \leqslant Cr^{j - w_0 - \epsilon}, \qquad j \leqslant D,\\ \left| M (wv) - \sum_{j \leqslant D, a} c_{j, a} (w ; o) M (O_{j, a} (o) v) \right| & \leqslant Cr^{D + 1 - w_0 - \epsilon} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(18.5)} \end{array}\)

The constants and threshold are uniform on the bounded label sets and separated geometries of Section 18.2. Evaluating a character, or the mixed vacuum functional, gives the same bound for its correlations.

Proof. Full support of the phase measure converts (18.4) into the operator-norm estimate by (15.6). To compare the unsymmetrized and symmetric reconstructions, write \(h = H_H (\tau_{- o} w)\) at an auxiliary finite degree \(H\). Every component of \(B_j w - L_{j, H} h\) is an average of finitely many replacement errors tested against rotated probe words. Their clearance is uniform by compactness of \(O (4)\). Hence

\(\displaystyle |B_j (\tau_{- o} w) - L_{j, H} h| \leqslant Cr^{H + 1 - w_0 - \epsilon} .\)

This estimate uses covariance of the mixed correlations to rotate the probes, as well as the cluster; rotating labels alone would not suffice.

Apply \(Q_j\). In the expansion of \(h\), every field of degree below \(j\) is killed. The remaining finitely many terms have powers at least \(r^{j - w_0 - \epsilon}\), by the raw coefficient estimate. Taking \(H \geqslant j\) proves the first bound. For the second, take \(H = D\) and sum the reconstruction comparison through \(j = D\). The reconstruction identity following (18.2) returns \(h\) exactly. Multiplying the coefficient errors by \(M (O_{j, a} (o) v)\) costs a uniform constant on the separated spectator geometry. Add the raw operator error. Finitely many sums and averages are controlled by first using a smaller error exponent.\(\Box\)

For two fields of degrees \(d_A, d_B\), the degree-\(j\) coefficient thus has size at most \(Cr^{j - d_A - d_B - \epsilon}\). The remainder may still grow when \(D + 1 < d_A + d_B\); its order nevertheless improves with \(D\). No assertion concerns the convergence of the infinite sum in \(D\).

The derivative version uses the differentiated marked replacement estimate. One first fixes the finite derivative order and the degree margin, then carries out the same finite probe functionals. The derivative identities of the field filtration identify the resulting derivatives. Uniform differentiated bounds permit passage from a dense grid to all separated positions by the mean-value theorem. Mere pointwise grid estimates without those bounds would not justify that passage.

For two nested clusters at radii \(\rho < R\), replacements can be composed by telescoping the signed difference. The first error is tested against the unchanged second cluster; the next is multiplied by the already produced coefficients. Accordingly a uniform nested estimate requires the replacement bound when exterior separation also tends to zero, with its dependence on \(R\) retained. Fixed-positive-clearance Proposition 18.1 by itself does not give that stronger result. For any prescribed finite tree, the required additional statement is a signed bound at every vertex, with degree-\(q\) coefficient cost \(\rho^{q - d}\) and residual gain \((\rho / R)^{D + 1}\), up to arbitrary small power losses, including the coefficients and the operators that its descendants apply to the test function. With those bounds, finite telescoping proves the corresponding finite-tree expansion. This stronger multi-scale assertion is not needed for the distributional extension below.

18.4An explicit finite-order extension across every diagonal

We now use only a uniform polynomial separated bound. Fix an arity \(n \geqslant 2\) and a field list \(\boldsymbol{A}\). Write

\(\displaystyle \Delta = \bigcup_{i < j} \{x_i = x_j \}, \qquad \delta (x) = \min_{i < j} |x_i - x_j | .\)

Suppose, as supplied by the separated bounds and the uniform phase slab bounds, that for some finite \(P \geqslant 0\),

\(\displaystyle |S_n^{\xi} (\boldsymbol{A}; x) | \leqslant C_K \min (1, \delta (x))^{- P} \quad (x \in K \setminus \Delta), \) (18.6)

uniformly in \(\xi\) on every compact \(K\). The mixed family has the same bound. No optimal value of \(P\) is required. Choose once an integer \(k \geqslant 0\) with \(k + 1 > P\), and a length \(s > 0\).

We specify which normal jet is subtracted near simultaneous collisions. Let \(m = n (n - 1) / 2\) be the number of pairs and put, on \(\text{Conf}_n\),

\(\displaystyle \rho (x) = \left( \sum_{i < j} |x_i - x_j |^{- 2} \right)^{- 1 / 2} .\)

Then \(\delta / \sqrt{m} \leqslant \rho \leqslant \delta\). Choose a fixed nonnegative smooth function \(\varphi\) on \([0, \infty)\), equal to one on \([0, 2 \sqrt{m}]\) and zero on \([4 \sqrt{m}, \infty)\). For a pair \(h = \{i, j\}\) define

\(\displaystyle a_h (x) = \frac{\varphi (|x_i - x_j | / \rho (x))}{\sum_{u < v} \varphi (|x_u - x_v | / \rho (x))} . \) (18.7)

The denominator is at least one, because a closest pair has ratio at most \(\sqrt{m}\). Thus \(a_h \geqslant 0\) and \(\sum_h a_h = 1\). Only pairs whose separation is at most \(4 \sqrt{m} \rho\) are selected. These weights are smooth off \(\Delta\), invariant under simultaneous translations and rotations, and permuted with the slots.

Let \(\pi_h x\) replace \(x_i, x_j\) by their centroid, leaving the other positions unchanged, and put \(z_h = x - \pi_h x\). This is the orthogonal normal displacement from the pair diagonal; its norm is \(|x_i - x_j | / \sqrt{2}\). Define the normal Taylor polynomial of a test function by

\(\displaystyle T_{h, k} f (x) = \sum_{\ell = 0}^k \frac{1}{\ell !} D^{\ell} f (\pi_h x) [z_h, \ldots, z_h] . \) (18.8)

Only normal directions occur; the centroid and all external positions remain parameters. Finally fix \(\chi \in C^{\infty} ([0, \infty))\) with \(0 \leqslant \chi \leqslant 1\), \(\chi = 1\) on \([0, 1]\) and \(\chi = 0\) on \([2, \infty)\), and set

\(\displaystyle (\mathcal{W}f) (x) = f (x) - \chi (\rho (x) / s) \sum_h a_h (x) T_{h, k} f (x) . \) (18.9)

This formula, including \(k, s, \varphi, \chi\), is the subtraction convention. It is the same in every phase and is fixed before the test function. It deliberately subtracts more jets than an optimal scaling-degree prescription may require. Figure 18.1 shows how several pair projections can contribute at the same configuration.

Theorem 18.3. (A common contact extension) Under (18.6), the formula

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\xi} (f) = \int_{\text{Conf}_n} S_n^{\xi} (\boldsymbol{A}; x) (\mathcal{W}f) (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx \) (18.10)

defines a distribution of local order at most \(k + 1\). It extends the separated correlation, is uniform in the phase on compact supports of test functions, and is continuous in \(\xi\) on each test function. Averaging over phases gives the same formula for the mixed correlations. Translations, orthogonal covariance and simultaneous permutation symmetry are preserved by this prescription.

Proof. This is the normal Taylor extension argument of [BF00, Theorem 5.3]; the following verification explains why one subtraction treats every partial diagonal here. When \(\rho \leqslant s\), the weights sum to one, so

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{W}f = \sum_h a_h (f - T_{h, k} f) .\)

For each selected pair, \(|z_h | \leqslant 2 \sqrt{2 m} \rho\). Taylor's integral remainder therefore bounds this expression by \(C \rho^{k + 1} \|f\|_{C^{k + 1}}\). Since \(\delta \geqslant \rho\), (18.6) bounds its product with the kernel by \(C \rho^{k + 1 - P} \|f\|_{C^{k + 1}}\), which is bounded and hence locally integrable. This remains true when arbitrarily many other separations vanish at independent rates: the selected pair always has displacement comparable to the smallest separation. No fixed collision pattern or single radial approach is assumed.

On the transition region \(s \leqslant \rho \leqslant 2 s\), all separations are bounded below by \(s\), and the finite Taylor polynomials are bounded by the same seminorm of the test function. For \(\rho \geqslant 2 s\) the integrand uses \(f\) itself. If \(f\) is supported in a compact \(K\), a nonzero subtracted jet requires \(\pi_h x \in K\) and \(|z_h | \leqslant C_n s\). Thus the integrand is supported in one fixed compact enlargement \(K'\) of \(K\). We obtain

\(\displaystyle |\mathfrak{D}_{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\xi} (f) | \leqslant C_{K'} \max_{| \alpha | \leqslant k + 1} \| \partial^{\alpha} f\|_{\infty} .\)

This proves continuity and the local order assertion. If \(f\) is supported away from \(\Delta\), all its jets at every \(\pi_h x \in \Delta\) vanish, so (18.10) is the original separated integral. Continuity in the phase and the mixture formula follow by dominated convergence with the same bound. The normal projections, pair weights and radial cutoff commute with the stated geometric symmetries.\(\Box\)

For \(n = 1\) use the original smooth one-point function, and for \(n = 0\) use the normalized unit. A consistent multilinear family on the whole filtration is obtained using the fixed invariant summands \(E_j\) in (18.2). For each finite tuple \((j_1, \ldots, j_n)\) choose \(P\) uniformly on the finite basis lists of \(E_{j_1} \times \cdots \times E_{j_n}\), and choose the same \(P, k\) for every permutation of that tuple. Use this one subtraction on the tuple and extend multilinearly there. Every field has a unique finite sum of components in the \(E_j\), so extension by finite multilinearity gives a prescription on all of \(F\). It is compatible with the inclusions and preserves rotations because each \(E_j\) is invariant. Choosing an order independently for each vector, or redefining it on each larger degree space, would not have these properties.

18.5Sharper subtraction and dependence on the prescription

The explicit extension above is conservative. A degree-sensitive alternative assigns to every collision subset \(h\) the normal dimension \(q_h = 4 (|h| - 1)\), the total input degree \(d_h = \sum_{i \in h} d_i\), and

\(\displaystyle L_h = \max (0, d_h + 1 - q_h) .\)

If a contact projection extracts a degree-\(q\) field from that cluster, one subtracts the normal Taylor jet of the test function through order \(L_h - 1 - q\) when \(q < L_h\). A single shell then has the combined power

\(\displaystyle \rho^{q - d_h} \hspace{0.17em} \rho^{L_h - q} \hspace{0.17em} \rho^{q_h} = \rho^{q_h + L_h - d_h},\)

with exponent at least one. This explains the source prescription and its proposed sharper local-order bound. For several collisions the shell estimate must be established for the complete signed expression, including nested subtractions and all child operators acting on test functions. The parent Taylor operator acts first on the test function; the child operators act after it. They cannot be commuted for nested subsets. A partition of unity cannot be moved through these normal Taylor operators without its resulting diagonal jets. A one-shell estimate alone does not verify that stronger forest prescription. We have therefore used the fully specified pair-weighted subtraction (18.9), for which absolute convergence at all collision patterns was proved directly.

Two extensions agreeing on \(\text{Conf}_n\) differ by a distribution supported on \(\Delta\). Along a smooth stratum, a finite-order difference is a sum of normal derivatives of delta distributions with distributional coefficients. This is the usual local renormalization freedom [BF00, Section 5.2]. Changing the cutoff or subtraction order can change contact terms while preserving separated correlations and the OPE estimates. Contact reflection positivity, contact Ward identities, uniqueness, unit removal and identification with unsubtracted coincident-point lattice limits require additional normalization arguments.
