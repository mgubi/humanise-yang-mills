Chapter 17
The trace law and its consequences

The leading short-distance limit of the stress tensor is the traceless Maxwell tensor. It therefore says nothing by itself about the leading nonzero term in the trace. That term is smaller by one inverse running coupling, and its two-point function by two. The perturbative origin of this phenomenon is the trace anomaly of Collins, Duncan and Joglekar [CDJ77]. The all-radius assertion below requires additional estimates for the selected lattice construction; it does not follow by substituting a perturbative beta function into a formal identity.

Retain the Ward-normalized tensor \(T\) of Proposition 14.6, the phase \(\xi_{\ast}\) chosen in Section 15.6, and its relativistic reconstruction. The latter supplies a positive-cone translation spectrum and tempered boundary correlations. We prove the stronger quantitative current transfer needed for the trace asymptotic, including its low-moment cancellations. The construction and reconstruction in the preceding chapters do not depend on this refinement.

17.1Centering and the positive spectral measure

Put \(\tau = \sum_{a = 1}^4 T_{aa}\), \(e_{\text{tr}} = S^{\xi_{\ast}} (\tau)\) and

\(\displaystyle \Theta = \tau - e_{\text{tr}} 1, \qquad G_{\Theta} (r) = S_2^{\xi_{\ast}} (\Theta, \Theta ; re_4, 0) .\)

The real number \(e_{\text{tr}}\) is the expectation in the selected phase, which need not equal the mixed expectation. The unit field is retained through the common filtration. The fact that inserting the identity leaves correlations unchanged extends to the boundary correlations, so, on the common invariant word domain,

\(\displaystyle \Phi_{\Theta} (f) = \Phi_{\tau} (f) - e_{\text{tr}} \left( \int f \right) I, \qquad \langle \Omega, \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega \rangle = 0.\)

For completeness, the Euclidean correlation with a unit insertion is constant in that insertion position. Holomorphic uniqueness on ordered time regions identifies its continuation with the correlation after deletion. Boundary values and smearing give the displayed operator identity on dense word matrix elements. This establishes centering on the domain, as well as for separated expectations.

Write \(p = (\boldsymbol{p}, E)\), \(p^2 = E^2 - |\boldsymbol{p}|^2\), and \(\bar{V}_+ = \{E \geqslant |\boldsymbol{p}|\}\). Use \(\hat{f}_+ (p) = \int e^{ip \cdot x} f (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx\) in the translation spectral convention, and \(\mathcal{W} \psi (p) = \int e^{ip \cdot (y - x)} \psi (x, y) \hspace{0.17em} dx \hspace{0.17em} dy\). Then \(\mathcal{W} (\bar{f} \otimes h) = \overline{\hat{f}_+} \hat{h}_+\). Positivity, translation covariance and temperedness give a unique positive measure \(\rho_{\Theta}\) of polynomial growth, supported in \(\bar{V}_+\), with

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} W_2 (\Theta, \Theta ; \psi) & = \int \mathcal{W} \psi \hspace{0.17em} d \rho_{\Theta}, \hspace{3cm} \text{(17.1)} \\ G_{\Theta} (r) & = \int e^{- rE} \hspace{0.17em} d \rho_{\Theta} (p) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(17.2)} \end{array}\)

Here is the direct spectral identification needed for these formulas. For \(v_f = \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega\), covariance gives \(\hat{h}_+ (P) v_f = \hat{f}_+ (P) v_h\) by convolution. On a compact momentum set choose \(\hat{f}_+\) nonzero and define \(d \rho_{\Theta} = | \hat{f}_+ |^{- 2} d \mu_{v_f}\) there. The convolution identity makes the definitions agree on overlaps. Polarization gives (17.1) first on product test functions and then on joint Schwartz test functions. Tempered continuity bounds compact masses polynomially. The positive cone permits exponential damping, and uniqueness of the holomorphic boundary value identifies its Laplace transform with the Euclidean function. The scalar transformation law of \(\Theta\) makes \(\rho_{\Theta}\) Lorentz invariant. In particular \(G_{\Theta}\) is real, nonnegative and nonincreasing. The expression \(r^8 \log^2 (1 / r) G_{\Theta} (r)\) need not be monotone.

17.2Comparison at prescribed radii

Let \(U_{i, d} : \mathcal{M}_d^{\text{Max}} \to F_d\) be real comparison isomorphisms, preserving the unit, inclusions and signed coordinate symmetries. They are evaluated at a radius \(r_i \downarrow 0\): \(U_{i, d} A\) is inserted at \(r_i x\). The actual inverse coupling at a finer insertion level \(k_i\) is denoted by \(\beta_i\), and \(b_i = \hat{\beta}_{k_i}\) is its reference value. The radius level \(n_i\) precedes \(k_i\).

The strengthened comparison, proved below, is as follows. Every prescribed positive null sequence has a subsequence with these data such that

\(\displaystyle \beta_i / b_i \to 1, \qquad \frac{\beta_i}{\log (1 / r_i)} \to \kappa_G, \) (17.3)

and all fixed separated correlations and their fixed derivatives, with convergent moving free labels, converge to their Maxwell values. For pairs without derivatives this comparison is uniform over the phases, including at nearest lattice-rounded points. Shell integrals are included. It must use the same field quotient and lattice family as before. A comparison along one freely chosen scale sequence is weaker.

We explain the radius coverage and the relevant quantitative demands. Write \(M_n = M_n^{\text{cut}} = \exp ((\log \log \hat{\beta}_n)^2)\) and allow

\(\displaystyle r (n, u) = a_n M_n^u, \quad \alpha_0 \leqslant u \leqslant \alpha_0 + 1, \qquad k (n) = \min \{m > n : \log (a_n / a_m) \geqslant (\log \hat{\beta}_n)^{48} \} .\)

At fixed degree bound \(D\) and arity bound \(N_{\ast}\), choose comparison exponents with

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} c < 0, \quad \alpha_0 - \alpha_1 > 6, \quad \alpha_0 - \alpha_2 > 5 - c,\\ (\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) DN_{\ast} + c < 0, \qquad (\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) DN_{\ast} - \alpha_0 - c < 0. \end{array}\)

The common size of the \(\alpha_j\) can be increased after their differences and \(c\) have been fixed. The marked comparison required at every sufficiently late level has, uniformly in \(u\), single-insertion seminorm bounds \(CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_j) D}\) and grid errors tending to zero. With strip width \(M_n^c\) and scaled mesh at most \(M_n^{- \alpha_0}\), the two integration errors are bounded by

\(\displaystyle CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) DN_{\ast} + c}, \qquad CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) DN_{\ast} - \alpha_0 - c} .\)

They tend to zero. Thus the strip removal and derivative-transport argument of Section 14.3 applies to these windows. Bounds uniform in the number of copies give phase uniformity by (15.6). The next subsection verifies these window estimates, including their uniformity across changes of stage, from marked propagation.

The coupling thresholds can be chosen to ensure \(L_n \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_n\). Since \(M_n\) increases and \(L_n \leqslant M_{n + 1}\) eventually, \(r (n, \alpha_0) \leqslant r (n + 1, \alpha_0 + 1)\). The intervals therefore cover every sufficiently small radius. The first crossing places a prescribed radius in one of them. Diagonalize finite degree, arity, window and derivative requests, requiring \((\alpha_0 + 1) \log M_{n_i} \leqslant (\log \hat{\beta}_{n_i})^{48}\). The overshoot in \(k (n)\) is negligible compared with \(\hat{\beta}_n\), as is the logarithm of the radius multiplier. The running law in Chapter 10 then gives (17.3). This proves radius coverage. It remains to verify the uniform comparison estimates used in these windows.

17.2.1Comparison windows crossing changes of stage

The finite comparison in Proposition 11.10 extends to the radius windows just described. We give the estimates because requiring every window to lie inside a plateau would leave gaps in radius coverage.

Fix a finite degree, arity and derivative request. In this paragraph \(C_j\) includes the linear marked-column constants, the curvature-response row constants and the finite polynomial-source propagation constants for this request, all fixed before the block ratio of stage \(j\). Write \(\mathscr{L}_j\) for the ratio chosen at stage \(j\), so \(L_i = \mathscr{L}_{J_i}\) with \(J_i = J (i + 1)\) in the scale convention of Chapter 10. The ratio choices may require

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_j := \frac{\log (2 + C_j)}{\log \mathscr{L}_j} \longrightarrow 0.\)

For example one may impose \(\varepsilon_j \leqslant j^{- 2}\). Constants depending on the already selected ratio, such as nonlinear stationary-jet bounds, are not included here. They enter the local small errors and endpoint constants, and are handled by sufficiently large coupling thresholds. These local errors are summed after propagation; their post-ratio constants are never multiplied once per step. Only a bounded number of endpoint factors is multiplied, giving \(H_n^{o (1)}\). This distinction avoids a circular ratio choice.

Put \(H_n = \exp ((\log \log \hat{\beta}_n)^2)\). For \(b\) in a fixed compact interval of sufficiently large positive numbers, stop backwards from \(n\) at the first crossing of \((b + 2) \log H_n\), and then at the first crossing of another \(10 \log B_n\). Here \(B_n\) is the radius of the restarted representation; \(\log B_n = O (\log \log \hat{\beta}_n)\). The schedule bound \(L_i \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_i\) makes each overshoot \(O (\log \log \hat{\beta}_n) = o (\log H_n)\). The entire logarithmic window has length \(O (\log H_n)\), and the number of steps is \(O (\log H_n)\) since every \(L_i \geqslant 3\). It is therefore shorter than \((\log \log \hat{\beta}_n)^4\), as required by Proposition 11.4. All its levels tend to infinity. The actual increment estimate is a constant times the logarithmic block length plus the coupling-threshold-controlled local drift error. Summing it over this window gives

\(\displaystyle \max_i | \beta_i / \beta_n - 1| \leqslant \frac{H_n^{o (1)} (1 + \log H_n)}{\hat{\beta}_n} .\)

In particular coupling changes are smaller than every fixed inverse power of \(H_n\), not just qualitatively \(o (1)\). A window may cross any number of stages; no bound by two stages is needed.

For the linear propagation constants the exact bound is

\(\displaystyle \log \prod_{i \text{ in window}} (2 + C_{J_i}) \leqslant \sup_{i \text{ in window}} \varepsilon_{J_i} \sum_{i \text{ in window}} \log L_i = o (\log H_n) .\) (17.4)

Thus their total cost is \(H_n^{o (1)}\). In particular the differentiated response estimate in Chapter 11 becomes

\(\displaystyle S^{r + 2} \sum_y | \Delta^r (\Psi_{n - 1} \cdots \Psi_{\ell}) (x, y) | \leqslant 2^d \prod_{i = \ell}^{n - 1} R_{J_i} = H_n^{o (1)}, \qquad S = a_{\ell} / a_n .\)

The actual covariance decomposition telescopes for these unequal responses just as it does for equal ratios: each conditional covariance is transported by the product of the subsequent actual responses. It uses no replacement by a common stage's quadratic form. The finite-cutoff covariance estimate (11.30) is uniform in the actual free history. Its stage-dependent constants, protected-path errors, fixed-ratio jet errors and finite incidence costs are made smaller than the requested powers of \(H_n\) by choosing sufficiently large coupling thresholds. Sums over \(O (\log H_n)\) steps lose only \(H_n^{o (1)}\). At each step nonlinear propagation is the stated pre-ratio linear map plus an coupling-threshold-controlled small error. Thus the post-ratio constants in the nonlinear comparison occur additively in those local errors, not in the repeated linear amplification. The regular and large-field source recurrences therefore retain their strict negative powers after the amplification (17.4).

One can now repeat the proof of Proposition 11.10 with unequal ratios. The initial Taylor order and support reserves are chosen for the largest \(b\) and the fixed finite request. The entire incoming squarefree polynomial is retained. The actual quadratic covariance and corrected free-subset polynomial are common to all subsets; the empty coefficient, and hence the denominator, is unchanged. Weight restoration still uses \(\omega = H_n^{1 / (8 N_{\ast})}\) and \(I = \log H_n / (16 N_{\ast})\). Every additional propagation loss is \(H_n^{o (1)}\); the margins in the \(H_n^{- 3 / 2}\) moment error, \(H_n^{- 31 / 32}\) connected error and \(H_n^{- 1}\) regular-restoration bounds absorb it. The root estimate is therefore at most \(H_n^{- c_{\ast}}\) for a fixed \(c_{\ast} > 0\) depending only on the finite arity, for example \(c_{\ast} = 1 / (32 N_{\ast})\) after increasing the coupling threshold. Its cell side is at most \(a_n H_n^{b + 5}\). The positive partition estimate is imposed at the actual bottom stage, with its constant at most \(I\); it is not multiplied once per step.

The top polynomial extraction \(T\) depends only on the actual insertion and its original self covariance. It is independent of the middle and output stopping levels. Restart on the longest window and restrict that history for the shorter ones. This supplies the same \(T\) for all \(b\) in the compact interval. The finite number of coefficient bounds above is uniform in \(b\): changing \(b\) only changes stopping integers and the scaling dilation \(W_{H_n^b}\), whose exponents lie in a fixed compact interval. All upper bounds used the longest window and the largest dilation cost. No compactness over arbitrary incoming activity arrays is invoked.

Apply this construction to \(b = \alpha_j + u - \alpha_0\), \(j = 0, 1, 2\), and \(u \in [\alpha_0, \alpha_0 + 1]\). At its own normalization a single insertion has bounded chessboard seminorm. Changing from \(\alpha_j\) to \(\alpha_0\) costs at most \(CH_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_j) D}\) by the exact scaling dilation on the common top extraction. These are precisely the size bounds used in the two strip and mesh estimates above. Their exponents are strictly negative, so the harmless \(H_n^{o (1)}\) losses do not change the conclusion. The full-copy estimates give the same pair bounds in every phase. The existing argument using separated correlation pairings then gives fixed derivatives and shell fluxes with these same frames. Thus the prescribed-radius comparison used in this chapter follows from the actual marked construction, including windows that cross changes of stage.

17.3Tensor coordinates and the regulated current

Use the free scalar and tensor

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{O}= \tfrac{1}{4} \sum_{a, b, \alpha} : (F_{ab}^{\alpha})^2 :, \quad L = T^{\text{Max}}, \quad D_{ab} = \delta_{ab} \mathcal{O}, \quad X_{ab} = (1 - \delta_{ab}) \sum_{c, \alpha} : F_{ac}^{\alpha} F_{bc}^{\alpha} : .\)

The coordinate sum in \(\mathcal{O}\) is over ordered pairs. The symbol \(X\) here denotes the off-diagonal tensor, not a polymer support. These three tensors span the centered degree-four symmetric hypercubic tensors. Before imposing the Maxwell equation, their vector divergences have coordinates in \(E =\mathbb{R}^3\), represented by

\(\displaystyle \sum_c F_{bc} \partial_b F_{bc}, \qquad \sum_{a, c} F_{ac} \partial_b F_{ac}, \qquad \sum_{a, c} F_{bc} \partial_a F_{ac},\)

with colour contraction and Wick ordering understood. The free quotient \(s : E \to V\) has kernel spanned by \(v = (0, 0, 1)\), and \(V\) is the two-dimensional vector space of Section 14.4. For a tensor with diagonal entries \(z_0 \sum_c F_{ac}^2 + z_1 \sum_{b, c} F_{bc}^2\) and off-diagonal entries \(z_2 \sum_c F_{ac} F_{bc}\), Bianchi gives divergence coordinates \((2 (z_0 - z_2), 2 z_1 + z_2 / 2, z_2)\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \rho (x, y, z) = - 2 x - 8 y, \qquad \rho (\text{div}_{\text{raw}} \hspace{0.17em} B) \mathcal{O}= - \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} B. \) (17.5)

In particular \(\rho (v) = 0\) and the values for \(L, X, D\) are \(0, 0, - 4\).

At a lattice site set \(f_{ab} = \text{proj}_{\mathfrak{g}} (U_{\square (a, b)} - U_{\square (a, b)}^{- 1}) / 2\) and start with

\(\displaystyle J_{ab} = \tfrac{1}{4} \delta_{ab} \sum_{c, d} \langle f_{cd}, f_{cd} \rangle - \sum_c \langle f_{ac}, f_{bc} \rangle, \qquad (Y_b^x)_e =\boldsymbol{1}_{e^- = x} f_{b, e^{\text{dir}}} (x) .\)

Here \(Y\) is a left link displacement. If \(\textsf{S}\) is the leading path action without its coupling, define its current defect by \(\mathcal{E}_b = \sum_a \nabla_a J_{ab} - D \textsf{S} [Y_b]\), using

\(\displaystyle \nabla_a = \sum_{j = 1}^4 c_j (\textsf{s}_a^j - \textsf{s}_a^{- j}), \qquad (c_1, c_2, c_3, c_4) = (4 / 5, - 1 / 5, 4 / 105, - 1 / 280) .\)

The operator \(\textsf{s}_a\) shifts by one site in direction \(a\). Initially the trace and antisymmetric part of \(J\) vanish. During transfer they must remain exact forward differences; smallness of their values alone would not suffice for the trace rate below.

We fix the coefficient functionals in that calculation. For a local scalar \(F\), its Hessian Fourier kernel is

\(\displaystyle \hat{F}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (p, q) = \sum_{x, y} e^{i (p \cdot x + q \cdot y)} D^2 F (0) [e_{x, c, \alpha}, e_{y, c, \alpha}],\)

where \(e_{x, c, \alpha}\) is the link-coordinate basis vector. Fix one colour \(\alpha\). For a vector \(F_b\) and a scalar action \(F\) put

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \textsf{l} (F) & = \text{Re} \sum_{a \ne c, b} i [z^2 t] \widehat{F_b}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (ze_a, - ze_a + te_b),\\ \textsf{q} (F) & = | \Lambda |^{- 1} \text{Re} \sum_{a \ne c} [z^2] \hat{F}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (ze_a, - ze_a),\\ \textsf{h} (F) & = \textsf{l} (F) - \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Re} \sum_{a \ne c} [z^2] \widehat{F_c}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (ze_a, - ze_a) . \end{array}\)

Brackets denote Taylor coefficients including reciprocal factorials. The volume normalization in \(\textsf{q}\) is for the translation-invariant action; \(\textsf{l}\) evaluates the anchored vector response. Define \(\mathfrak{h} (Y) = \textsf{h} (\text{div}_{\text{Haar}} \hspace{0.17em} Y)\). The second term in \(\textsf{h}\) corrects the lattice derivative. The regulated current calculation uses

\(\displaystyle \Gamma_{\mu} = \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det (H + dd^{\ast} + \mu^2 I) - \log \det (d^{\ast} d + \mu^2 I), \quad R (\mu) = \textsf{q} (\Gamma_{\mu}), \quad \mathcal{D} (\mu) = - \mu R' (\mu) .\)

Here \(H\) is the left Hessian of \(\textsf{S}\), \(d\) its infinitesimal gauge insertion, and \(\textsf{q}\) is the coefficient functional just defined. Its normalization counts twelve ordered transverse planes. The actual Ward calculation requires its Haar coefficient \(\mathfrak{h} (Y)\) to obey

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{h} (Y) =\mathcal{D} (\mu) + O (\mu) + o (1), \qquad R (\mu) = 12 \kappa_G \log \mu + O (1) . \) (17.6)

The \(o (1)\) is uniform on each fixed interval \([\delta, 1]\) as the current defect tends to zero and the period tends to infinity; the constant in \(O (\mu)\) is independent of \(\mu \leqslant 1\). This identity concerns the actual gauge-fixed Hessian and transferred current. The perturbative anomaly citation does not supply it.

The next section derives this regulated identity from explicit weighted current bounds and convergence of the matched current jets. It keeps the gauge-coordinate corrections and the lattice coefficient correction. The following current-contraction section then verifies these hypotheses for repeated stationary transfer.

17.4Exact current transport and the scalar regulator

Several ingredients of the trace transfer can be established before its quantitative current estimates. We give them separately, since they use the actual difference operator and the actual path action. They do not identify a lattice coefficient by comparison with a separately chosen continuum action.

17.4.1Difference-preserving block averages

Let \(L\) be a block ratio and \(s_a\) a unit fine translation. Write \(\delta_a = s_a - 1\), \(\delta_a^{\text{c}} = s_a^L - 1\), and let \(\nabla_a = \sum_{j = 1}^4 c_j (s_a^j - s_a^{- j})\) be the eighth-order centered difference used above. Its coefficients satisfy \(2 \sum_j jc_j = 1\). Define finite Laurent polynomials

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} M_a & = L^{- 1} \sum_{v = 0}^{L - 1} s_a^v, & G_a & = \sum_{j = 1}^4 c_j \sum_{v = - j}^{j - 1} s_a^v,\\ N_a & = G_a (s_a^L) M_a, & K_0 & = \prod_a M_a N_a,\\ K_a & = G_a M_a \prod_{c \ne a} M_c N_c, & K_a^{\text{e}} & = N_a \prod_{c \ne a} M_c N_c . \end{array}\)

All these operators act on the fine anchor of a local function before stationary substitution. If \(\mathcal{I}F = F (U_{\text{stat}} (V))\), coarse translation of \(\mathcal{I}F\) is \(\mathcal{I} (s_a^L F)\); fine unit shifts are not silently identified with coarse shifts.

Lemma 17.1. (Exact block identities) There is a finite Laurent polynomial \(K_a^{\text{t}}\) such that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlllll} K_0 \nabla_a & = L^{- 1} \nabla_a^{\text{c}} K_a, & K_0 \delta_a & = L^{- 1} \delta_a^{\text{c}} K_a^{\text{e}}, & K_a - K_0 & = \delta_a^{\text{c}} K_a^{\text{t}} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(17.7)} \end{array}\)

The kernels have support of radius \(CL\) and absolute coefficient sums bounded by a numerical constant independent of \(L\); their masses are one except that no mass is prescribed for \(K_a^{\text{t}}\).

Proof. The identities \(G_a \delta_a = \nabla_a\) and \(M_a \delta_a = L^{- 1} \delta_a^{\text{c}}\) prove the first two assertions by commutation of Laurent polynomials. For the third, work in the quotient ring with \(s_a^L = 1\). There \(M_a^2 = M_a\), \(M_a G_a = G_a (1) M_a = M_a\) and \(G_a (s_a^L) = G_a (1) = 1\). Thus \(M_a (G_a - G_a (s_a^L) M_a)\) is zero in that quotient, which is precisely divisibility of \(K_a - K_0\) by \(s_a^L - 1\). The original kernels have finite products of a fixed number of bounded coefficient-sum factors, each of range \(CL\). For the quotient, group coefficients in each residue class modulo \(L\). Its numerator has zero sum in each class, and only a bounded number of nonzero entries in that class. Division by \(s_a^L - 1\) is a partial sum along this finite sequence, so increases its absolute sum by only a bounded factor. This also proves the support bound for \(K_a^{\text{t}}\).\(\Box\)

For a fine current with \(\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} J = \sum_a \delta_a q_a\), set

\(\displaystyle J'_{ab} = L^4 \mathcal{I} (K_a J_{ab}), \qquad q'_a = L^3 \mathcal{I} (K_a^{\text{e}} q_a) + L^4 \mathcal{I} (K_a^{\text{t}} J_{aa}) .\)

Equation (17.7) proves exactly \(\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} J' = \sum_a \delta_a^{\text{c}} q'_a\). Indeed split \(K_a = K_0 + \delta_a^{\text{c}} K_a^{\text{t}}\) and then apply the second identity to \(K_0 \delta_a q_a\). Similarly, if \(J_{ab} - J_{ba} = \sum_c \delta_c t_{abc}\), the transferred antisymmetric part is the sum of the transferred old differences and

\(\displaystyle \delta_a^{\text{c}} L^4 \mathcal{I} (K_a^{\text{t}} J_{ab}) - \delta_b^{\text{c}} L^4 \mathcal{I} (K_b^{\text{t}} J_{ba}) .\)

In particular symmetry may fail pointwise after blocking, but its failure and the trace remain exact differences. This conclusion is independent of estimates on the current's Taylor coefficients.

17.4.2What scalar mass regularization changes

Consider the same admissible path action used in Chapter 7, with its left Hessian \(H\) and infinitesimal gauge insertion \(d\). At the zero field the completed vector operator is \(H + dd^{\ast} = |k|^2 I + O (|k|^3)\), and the ghost operator is \(d^{\ast} d = |k|^2 I + O (|k|^4)\), with the global lattice ellipticity stated there. Define \(\Gamma_{\mu}\) with the scalar mass \(\mu^2 I\) as above. The normalized quadratic response of \(\Gamma_{\mu}\) has the same logarithmic coefficient as the regulated response in Chapter 7. This fact concerns its coefficient integral, before any relation to the Haar divergence of a current.

To see this directly, retain the actual vector and ghost first and second background insertions \(b, c\) and replace only their denominators by the scalar-mass denominators. In each channel the logarithmic Hessian is

\(\displaystyle \text{tr} \{a_{\mu} (k)^{- 1} c (- p, p ; k) - a_{\mu} (k)^{- 1} b (- p ; k + p) a_{\mu} (k + p)^{- 1} b (p ; k)\} .\)

The scalar mass has no background insertion. The actual local Taylor expansion and vertex estimates of Chapter 7 therefore apply without the regulator-vertex terms. With \(T = |k| + \mu\), the inverse and its \(r\) momentum derivatives cost \(CT^{- 2 - r}\), for every fixed required \(r\). Differentiate \(Ra = I\) repeatedly to obtain this bound; the denominator floor is now simply \(a_{\mu} \geqslant cT^2 I\). Two external derivatives of the displayed expression cost at most \(CT^{- 4}\). For \(\mu \leqslant |k| \leqslant 1\), comparison with the massless continuum vertices by the resolvent identity costs

\(\displaystyle C (|k|^{- 3} + \mu^2 |k|^{- 6}) .\)

The first term is the actual lattice Taylor remainder, and the second is the scalar-mass denominator difference. Both have bounded integrals in four dimensions, uniformly in \(\mu\). The region \(|k| < \mu\) also has bounded integral, since its volume is \(O (\mu^4)\) and its integrand is \(O (\mu^{- 4})\). Away from zero the integral is uniformly bounded. Thus the continuum homogeneous term alone determines the logarithm. With the twelve-plane coefficient convention of this chapter this gives

\(\displaystyle R (\mu) = 12 \kappa_G \log \mu + O (1), \qquad 0 < \mu \leqslant 1. \) (17.8)

This is the same one-loop normalization already calculated using the actual action in Chapter 7. At fixed \(\mu > 0\) the integrand is periodic analytic on a common smaller strip, so its torus sums converge to this integral; no uniform rate as \(\mu \downarrow 0\) is required. This proof does not differentiate the bounded remainder.

17.4.3Exact integration by parts and the remaining current estimate

For a smooth vector field \(Y\) on a finite product of compact link groups, Haar integration gives the exact identity

\(\displaystyle \int \left[ \text{div}_{\text{Haar}} \hspace{0.17em} Y - D\mathcal{A}[Y] \right] e^{-\mathcal{A}} \hspace{0.17em} dU = 0. \) (17.9)

It follows by integrating the divergence of \(e^{-\mathcal{A}} Y\). With a spectator \(F\) the right side becomes \(- \int DF [Y] e^{-\mathcal{A}} \hspace{0.17em} dU\). In particular it is zero when \(Y\) acts only on links disjoint from those of \(F\). Smooth truncations can be inserted before this identity is used; their differentiated terms must be retained as marked remainders.

There is also an exact cancellation available for the inherited quadratic loop term. Chapter 7 represents its Hessian as a sum with two lattice differences on each leg. Consequently it annihilates constant and affine polynomial link fields. The degree-three external momentum coefficient in its variation by the leading current pairs a constant leg with a quadratic leg, or two affine legs; both vanish. This proves the zero trace-functional coefficient of that quadratic loop contribution. It does not bound the nonlinear current remainder.

The missing quantitative step is now quite specific. One needs the actual stationary pullback of the current defect, with the gauge-covariant block average frame correction, in the exponentially weighted order-two, -three and -four coefficient norms. In particular the order-two pullback must annihilate its affine–affine and affine–quadratic polynomial moment conditions before estimating the remainder. The usual smooth transport bound \(\nabla_f^r W_a = O (L^{- 1 - r})\) alone does not assert these cancellations. After gauge removal the relevant affine field costs \(L^{- 2}\) and its quadratic term \(L^{- 3}\); multiplication by the current rescaling \(L^5\) would leave the unacceptable factors \(L\) and \(1\) from their two products. Only removal of these exact terms leaves the \(L^{- 1}\) remainder used in the proposed contraction. Fourth-order action matching must therefore be combined with an explicit identity for the current's matched jets, not merely with a bound on the action's matching error.

The group-coordinate identity needed for this comparison is proved next. Its proof is independent of the contraction of the actual current sequence, established in Section 17.5.

17.4.4The group-coordinate contact identity

Fix a finite lattice and a background \(U\) in the product of link groups. Use the invariant inner product on its link Lie algebra and the chart \(z \mapsto e^z U\) (edge by edge). Let \(S\) be a smooth gauge-invariant action, \(H = D_z^2 S (e^z U) |_{z = 0}\) its symmetric left-coordinate Hessian, and \(d\) the infinitesimal gauge map at \(U\). The displacement \(Y\) is assumed gauge covariant, so its local flow \(\Psi_s (U) = e^{sY (U)} U\) commutes with gauge transformations. It need not be the exact time-\(s\) flow; all following identities use only its first derivative at zero.

Define the chart velocity and its linear part by

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{Y}_U (z) = \partial_s |_0 \log (\Psi_s (e^z U) \Psi_s (U)^{- 1}), \qquad V = D_z \mathcal{Y}_U (0) .\)

In particular \(\mathcal{Y}_U (0) = 0\). This definition includes the change of the left frame as the base point moves. Replacing \(V\) by an ordinary derivative of the coefficient function \(Y\) would omit that change. Let

\(\displaystyle J_U (v, z) = \partial_s \partial_t |_0 \log (\text{gauge}_{e^{sv}} (e^{tz} U) U^{- 1}), \qquad N_{i \alpha} = DS (U) [J_U (e_{\alpha}, e_i)] .\)

Indices \(i\) run through link coordinates and \(\alpha\) through gauge coordinates. Differentiating gauge invariance first in \(v\), then in \(z\), gives \(Hd = - N\).

For \(\mu > 0\) at a background where the following inverses exist, write

\(\displaystyle R = (H + dd^{\ast} + \mu^2 I)^{- 1}, \qquad G = (d^{\ast} d + \mu^2 I)^{- 1},\)
\(\displaystyle \Gamma = \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det (H + dd^{\ast} + \mu^2 I) - \log \det (d^{\ast} d + \mu^2 I) .\)

Near the zero background the positive denominators of the preceding subsection make these ordinary real analytic functions. Define a link vector \(Z\) by its action against an arbitrary covector \(\ell\):

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \ell (Z) = & \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{i, j} R_{ij} \hspace{0.17em} \ell (D_z^2 \mathcal{Y}_U (0) [e_i, e_j])\\ & - \sum_{i, \alpha} [Gd^{\ast} (V + V^{\ast}) R]_{\alpha i} \hspace{0.17em} \ell (J_U (e_{\alpha}, e_i)) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(17.10)} \end{array}\)

All matrices and derivatives here belong to the same background and coordinate chart.

Proposition 17.2. (Exact contact identity) With these definitions,

\(\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (RD^2 (DS [Y])) - \text{div}_{\text{Haar}} \hspace{0.17em} Y = D \Gamma [Y] - \mu^2 \text{tr} (RV) + DS [Z] . \) (17.11)

No stationarity of \(U\) is assumed. In particular the \(DS [Z]\) term cannot be discarded before applying the relevant coefficient functional.

Proof. Let a dot denote differentiation along \(\Psi_s (U)\), using the left chart anew at that base point. The chain rule for \(S (\Psi_s (e^z U))\) gives

\(\displaystyle D^2 (DS [Y]) = \dot{H} + V^{\ast} H + HV + C, \qquad C_{ij} = DS [D_z^2 \mathcal{Y}_U (0) [e_i, e_j]] .\)

This is an exact chart calculation, not a replacement of the group by its tangent space. The differential of \(\Psi_s\) in these moving charts is \(I + sV + o (s)\). Exponential charts have Haar density one at their origins, so its Jacobian at the base point has derivative \(\text{div}_{\text{Haar}} \hspace{0.17em} Y = \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} V\).

Gauge equivariance of \(\Psi_s\) gives \(\dot{d} = Vd\): differentiate \(\Psi_s (\text{gauge}_{e^v} \hspace{0.17em} U) = \text{gauge}_{e^v} \hspace{0.17em} \Psi_s (U)\) first in \(v\) and then in \(s\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle D \Gamma [Y] = \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (R \dot{H}) + \text{tr} (dd^{\ast} RV) - \text{tr} (dGd^{\ast} (V + V^{\ast})) .\)

On the other hand the Hessian chain rule and \(RH = I - Rdd^{\ast} - \mu^2 R\) yield

\(\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (RD^2 (DS [Y])) - \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} V = \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (R \dot{H}) - \text{tr} (Rdd^{\ast} V) - \mu^2 \text{tr} (RV) + \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (RC) .\)

Finally \(Hd = - N\) implies the exact resolvent identity

\(\displaystyle Rd = dG + RNG.\)

Subtract the two preceding displays. The leftover gauge terms are

\(\displaystyle - \text{tr} ((Rdd^{\ast} + dd^{\ast} R - 2 dGd^{\ast}) V) = - \text{tr} (Gd^{\ast} (V + V^{\ast}) RN) .\)

Inserting the definition of \(N\) and the term \(\frac{1}{2} \text{tr} (RC)\) identifies their sum with \(DS [Z]\) from (17.10). This proves (17.11).\(\Box\)

17.4.5Cancellation of the action-gradient terms

The functional \(\textsf{l}\) above extracts the coefficient of \(z^2 t\) from the diagonal polarization pair at momenta \(p = ze_a\), \(q = - ze_a + te_b\), and sums over \(a \ne c\) and \(b\). We check why it kills the action variations in (17.11). Work at fixed positive \(\mu\) so all kernels are analytic in momentum near zero. A gauge-invariant, translation- and hypercubic-invariant scalar action \(F\) has quadratic kernel \(c_F (|p|^2 I - p \otimes p) + O (|p|^3)\) by the Ward calculation in Chapter 7. This applies to both \(S\) and \(\Gamma\). Let \(Z_b\) be any anchored gauge-covariant displacement, including \(Y_b\) or the displacement (17.10). Simplicity of the gauge algebra implies \(Z_b (0) = 0\). The linearized covariance identity says \(DZ_b (0) d_0 = 0\).

Use \(i\) for its output link direction and \(c\) for its input polarization. Its joint momentum symbol, with output dual variable \(p\) and input variable \(q\), therefore has expansion

\(\displaystyle (DZ_b)_{ic} (p, q) = \sum_j q_j A_{b, ic}^{\hspace{0.17em} j} + O ((|p| + |q|)^2), \qquad A_{b, ic}^{\hspace{0.17em} j} = - A_{b, ij}^{\hspace{0.17em} c} .\)

The factors \(i\) from Fourier differentiation may be absorbed into \(A\). There is no term depending only on \(p\): the identity obtained by multiplying by the gradient symbol in \(q\) forces it to vanish. For \(a \ne c\), the coefficient of \(z^2 t\) in the quadratic variation \(DF [Z_b]\) is, apart from the common factor \(c_F\),

\(\displaystyle A_{b, cc}^{\hspace{0.17em} b} - 2 \delta_{ab} A_{b, cc}^{\hspace{0.17em} a} + \delta_{bc} A_{b, ac}^{\hspace{0.17em} a} .\)

This follows by inserting the two kinetic kernels on its two legs. The first sum over \(a \ne c\) gives \(3 \sum_{b, c} A_{b, cc}^{\hspace{0.17em} b}\). The second gives minus twice that sum, since \(A_{c, cc}^{\hspace{0.17em} c} = 0\). The last gives minus the same sum after using \(A_{c, ac}^{\hspace{0.17em} a} = - A_{c, aa}^{\hspace{0.17em} c}\) and exchanging \(a, c\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \textsf{l} (DF [Z]) = 0. \) (17.12)

Higher momentum orders cannot contribute to the coefficient of degree three. Gauge covariance of (17.10) follows by transforming its chart tensors, inverse operators and contracted indices together. The mass \(\mu^2 I\) is unchanged by these orthogonal gauge frame changes. The calculation therefore applies to both action-gradient terms of the exact contact identity. It is a finite-momentum cancellation, not a statement that the corresponding functions vanish pointwise.

17.4.6Power counting the regulator remainder

The remaining estimate has a transparent sufficient numerator condition. Let \(W^{(1)}, W^{(0)}\) be the vector and ghost remainder operators obtained by subtracting the leading curvature displacement from \(V\); in the notation of the current calculation these are

\(\displaystyle W_b^{(1)} = \text{sym} (V_b - dM_b - D_b) + \tfrac{1}{2} T_b^{(1)}, \qquad W_b^{(0)} = \text{sym} (M_b d) - \tfrac{1}{2} T_b^{(0)} .\)

Here \(\chi\) is the indicator of the anchored lattice site, \((M_b h) (x) = \chi (x) h_b (x)\), \((D_b h)_a (x) = \delta_a \chi (x) h_b (x)\), and \((T_b^{(r)} h) (x) = \delta_b \chi (x) h (x)\) on \(r\)-forms. The full lattice shifts and chart tensors must be retained when extracting their coefficient; their continuum symbols only identify the leading terms that cancel.

For that leading calculation the gauge derivative is \(d = - \partial\) and the curvature displacement is \((V_b h)_a = \chi (\partial_b h_a - \partial_a h_b)\). Then \(dM_b + D_b = - \chi \partial_a h_b\) and \(\text{sym} (\chi \partial_b) = - \frac{1}{2} \partial_b \chi\). Thus both \(W^{(r)}\) have zero first-order principal symbol. Covariantizing these expressions gives the same cancellation at constant background to first field order: the skew-adjoint multiplication by \(\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} A_b\) has zero symmetric part, and the remaining \(\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} A_a h_b\) term cancels the corresponding term of \(dM_b\). Accordingly their zero-, one- and two-field symbols have orders \(2, 1, 0\), respectively. A lattice/current sequence having the stated matched free jets inherits precisely these vanishings in its limit. Weighted coefficient bounds control the Taylor remainders.

Here is the resulting estimate without suppressed differentiation orders. Set \(z = |q| + \mu\). For \(n \leqslant 2\) background derivatives and \(r \leqslant 3\) external momentum derivatives the inverse estimates are \(Cz^{- 2 - n - r}\). The remainder symbols have bounds \(Cz^{2 - n - r}\). The quadratic-field coefficient of \(\text{tr} (RW^{(1)}) + \text{tr} (GW^{(0)})\), after the three momentum derivatives defining \(\textsf{l}\), is therefore bounded by a sum of terms

\(\displaystyle Cz^{- 2 - n - r} \hspace{0.17em} z^{2 - (2 - n) - (3 - r)} = Cz^{- 5} .\)

In four dimensions \(\int_{|q| \leqslant 1} (|q| + \mu)^{- 5} \hspace{0.17em} dq \leqslant C / \mu\). The region away from zero is bounded. Hence the regulator factor \(\mu^2\) gives a remainder at most \(C \mu\). For approximate matched jets, first fix \(\mu \geqslant \delta > 0\); their errors then tend to zero uniformly on that regulator interval. Only afterwards send \(\delta\) to zero. This is the order required by the logarithmic mean-value argument.

17.4.7The corrected coefficient and order of limits

We can retain the lattice correction in \(\textsf{h}\) throughout, avoiding an unsupported claim that it vanishes separately. Write \(\textsf{c} (F) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Re} \sum_{a \ne c} [z^2] \widehat{F_c}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (ze_a, - ze_a)\), so \(\textsf{h} = \textsf{l} - \textsf{c}\). The quadratic variation \(DF [Z]\) in the preceding calculation starts at momentum degree three. Thus \(\textsf{c} (DF [Z]) = 0\), in addition to (17.12). Both functionals therefore annihilate the action-gradient terms of (17.11).

Suppose now that the actual current sequence has the following properties at the fixed output level: uniform exponentially weighted order-two through order-four coefficient bounds; convergence of the needed low polynomial jets to the curvature current; the exact trace and antisymmetry difference identities; and defects tending to zero through order four in those weighted norms in

\(\displaystyle DS [Y_b] \equiv_4 \sum_a \nabla_a J_{ab} -\mathcal{E}_b .\)

The notation \(\equiv_4\) refers to equality of field derivatives through order four at the zero background. It suffices because the contact expression uses two internal and two external field derivatives. At fixed \(\mu \geqslant \delta > 0\), contracted defect errors tend to zero. Translation covariance makes contraction by the inverse \(R\) commute with translating the anchor. The \(\textsf{l}\) coefficient of the contracted current divergence selects \(a = b\) at its sole linear total-momentum derivative. The sum is the contracted trace of \(J\), which has a further total-momentum zero because it is a forward difference. Its coefficient is zero. For \(\textsf{c}\), the total momentum is zero from the outset and the divergence vanishes directly. The exact contact identity consequently yields

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{h} (Y) = \mu^2 \textsf{h} (\text{tr} (RV_b)) + o (1),\)

uniformly on every fixed positive regulator interval. All operators here are functions of the background before the two field derivatives are extracted.

The definitions of \(W^{(1)}, W^{(0)}\) give an exact trace decomposition. Use \(Rd = dG + RNG\) to obtain

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \text{tr} (RV_b) = & \text{tr} \left( R (D_b - \tfrac{1}{2} T_b^{(1)}) \right) + \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (GT_b^{(0)}) + DS [Z_b'] + \text{Rem}_b,\\ \text{Rem}_b = & \text{tr} (RW_b^{(1)}) + \text{tr} (GW_b^{(0)}),\\ \ell (Z_b') = & \sum_{i, \alpha} (GM_b R)_{\alpha i} \ell (J_U (e_{\alpha}, e_i)) . \end{array}\)

For example, the part containing \(dM_b\) becomes \(\text{tr} (GM_b d) + \text{tr} (RNGM_b)\); its symmetric ghost part is precisely \(\frac{1}{2} \text{tr} (GT_b^{(0)}) + \text{tr} (GW_b^{(0)})\), and its remaining term is \(DS [Z_b']\). This also fixes the sign of the gauge correction.

For the three displayed principal terms the Fourier transform of \(\delta_b \chi\) supplies \(e^{- it} - 1 = - it + O (t^2)\) at total momentum \(te_b\). With the factor \(i\) in \(\textsf{l}\), the \(D_b\) terms sum to \(\textsf{q} (\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} R)\): the selected derivative has \(a = b\) and the component trace then sums its diagonal entries. Each of the four \(T_b^{(1)}\) terms gives \(\textsf{q} (\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} R)\), and each \(T_b^{(0)}\) term gives \(\textsf{q} (\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} G)\). The volume normalization is exactly the translation average of the single anchor. Their total is therefore

\(\displaystyle - \textsf{q} (\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} R) + 2 \textsf{q} (\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} G) .\)

Their \(\textsf{c}\) coefficients are zero, since \(\delta_b \chi\) has zero symbol at total momentum zero. Multiplication by \(\mu^2\) gives \(- \mu \partial_{\mu} \textsf{q} (\Gamma)\) by differentiation of the two determinants. Thus, with the lattice correction fully retained,

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{h} (Y) =\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda} (\mu) + \mu^2 \textsf{h} (\text{Rem}) + o (1) . \) (17.13)

The action-gradient term \(DS [Z_b']\) is annihilated by both parts of \(\textsf{h}\) as proved above.

The \(\textsf{l}\) remainder was bounded by \(C \mu\) after multiplication by \(\mu^2\). The correction \(\textsf{c}\) uses only two external momentum derivatives, so the same product rule costs \(Cz^{- 4}\). Its integral is at most \(C (1 + | \log \mu |)\) in four dimensions, and \(\mu^2 (1 + | \log \mu |) \leqslant C \mu\) for \(0 < \mu \leqslant 1\). Consequently (17.13) proves the regulator estimate, including the anchored correction, from the stated current hypotheses.

Finally combine it with (17.8). On a fixed interval \([u - A, u]\) in the variable \(\log \mu\), the bounded error in \(R (\mu) - 12 \kappa_G \log \mu\) implies that some regulator has \(|\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda} (\mu) + 12 \kappa_G | \leqslant (2 C + o (1)) / A\) after the period is large. The remainder is at most \(Ce^u + o (1)\) on that interval. First take the current and period limits there, next \(u \to - \infty\), and finally \(A \to \infty\). Since \(\mathfrak{h} (Y)\) is independent of \(\mu\), this proves

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{h} (Y) \longrightarrow - 12 \kappa_G .\)

The group-coordinate contact identity, coefficient cancellation and regulator estimate are now established. Their application still requires the actual transferred current sequence to satisfy the weighted bounds and matched-jet convergence stated at the start of this subsection; those conditions are not consequences of the contact algebra.

17.5Current conservation and uniform transfer bounds

The potentially dangerous quadratic defect has an exact cancellation that should be imposed before estimating its norm. We prove preservation of that cancellation for the actual stationary tangent. This removes an additive interpretation of the order-two matching error. We then control the nonlinear stationary jets and the current itself.

Fix a stage and an admissible action. Let \(\mathcal{E}_b\) be a gauge-invariant anchored current defect, with zero constant and linear field coefficients. Its Hessian at zero has an exponentially local joint Fourier symbol \(E_b (p, q)\), with two independent field momenta. Gauge invariance gives a Ward identity on each leg. We say that its quadratic defect has order at least four when

\(\displaystyle E_b (p, q) = O ((|p| + |q|)^4) \quad \text{near } (0, 0) . \) (17.14)

This refers to spatial momentum degree, not the field degree of the Hessian. It includes the affine–affine and affine–quadratic polynomial cancellations needed in the current estimate.

For the initial Wilson current, (17.14) follows from the leading continuum identity. Its linearized curvature is \(dA\); the leading quadratic action is the curvature square; and the centered difference \(\nabla\) has linear symbol \(ip\). The quadratic Taylor coefficient of \(\sum_a \nabla_a J_{ab} - DS [Y_b]\) therefore has leading spatial momentum degree three. At that degree it is exactly

\(\displaystyle \partial_a \left( \tfrac{1}{4} \delta_{ab} \sum_{c, d} F_{cd}^2 - \sum_c F_{ac} F_{bc} \right) + \sum_c F_{bc} \partial_a F_{ac} = 0.\)

The identity uses the linear Bianchi equation; colour inner products are implicit. Lower spatial degrees vanish because each curvature contains a difference and the action variation contains one more. All remaining lattice Taylor terms have degree at least four. The same reasoning applies to any initial path action with the normalized weight-four Taylor polynomial already established in Chapter 7.

17.5.1Preservation of the low moments

Let \(W\) be the actual stationary tangent from coarse link variables to fine link variables, with block ratio \(L\). The first derivative of the nonlinear stationary substitution is this \(W\); its higher derivatives cannot enter the quadratic pullback because the defect has zero first field derivative. Fine gauge changes of \(W\) have no effect on this quadratic calculation by the Ward identities of \(E_b\).

The exact stationary identity of Chapter 5 implies, for the alias \(k_l (p) = (p + 2 \pi l) / L\),

\(\displaystyle K_f (k_l (p)) N_l (p) = A_l (p) K_c (p), \) (17.15)

where \(N_l\) is the alias amplitude of \(W\), \(A_l\) is the corresponding amplitude of the adjoint of the gauge-covariant block average, and \(K_c (p) = O (|p|^2)\). At a nonprincipal alias \(l \ne 0\), project \(N_l\) off the fine gradient direction. The completed fine kinetic matrix is invertible near \(k_l (0) \ne 0\); hence that projected amplitude is analytic and \(O (|p|^2)\). The removed gradient is killed by the defect Ward identity. The principal amplitude is analytic by the exponential locality of \(W\). No bound uniform in \(L\) on an individual nonprincipal inverse is needed for this exact Taylor-order statement.

Consider the actual anchor average \(K_0\) from Lemma 17.1, performed before stationary substitution. Its symbol has a double zero at every nonprincipal alias: at least one coordinate of \(l\) is nonzero, and the factor \(M_a^2 G_a (s_a^L)\) has two factors of the block average \(M_a\) there. In a two-leg alias term the averaging multiplier is evaluated at \(k_l (p) + k_{l'} (q)\). The quadratic pullback therefore has the following three kinds of terms.

If both aliases are principal, (17.14) and analytic amplitudes retain order four. If exactly one is nonprincipal, its transverse amplitude has order two and the anchor average has another order-two zero, giving order at least four. If both are nonprincipal, the two projected amplitudes already give order four, including when their alias indices cancel in the anchor multiplier. Every term thus satisfies (17.14) on the coarse lattice. This is a finite alias identity and holds on the same actual history.

In particular the quadratic jet is not merely close to having the required affine cancellations: it has them exactly, provided the current update uses the exact pulled-back defect \(\mathcal{E}'_b = L^5 \mathcal{I} (K_0 \mathcal{E}_b)\) through the relevant field order. If an implementation redefines that defect by action matching, equality of these quadratic jets must be retained; a bounded additive matching error would not preserve this argument.

17.5.2A homogeneous bound for the quadratic channel

The analytic division argument of Section 7.2 can be applied to each of the two independent momentum legs of \(E_b\). The Ward identities produce one curvature difference on each leg. Their homotopy divisions lower Taylor order by one each, so (17.14) leaves a coefficient of total order at least two. Coordinatewise Taylor division in the remaining eight variables factors two additional differences, distributed between the legs. On a fixed smaller coefficient width these operations are bounded by constants depending only on the stage and the width loss. Thus \(E_b\) is a sum of terms with either one and three differences, or two and two differences, on its two field legs. The coefficient sum is bounded by \(C_j \|E_b \|\) in the original weighted norm.

The actual tangent may be replaced by the smooth representative \(W_a\) because the quadratic Ward identities kill its gradient correction. Its fine-difference bounds are

\(\displaystyle \| \nabla_f^r W_a \| \leqslant C_{j, r} L^{- 1 - r}, \qquad r \leqslant 3.\)

Consequently either allocation of four differences costs at most \(C_j L^{- 6}\) for the two legs. The anchor average \(K_0\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum, not a factor \(L^4\); it is an average rather than an unnormalized sum. Translated coefficient weights are absorbed by a fixed narrower coarse width, exactly as in the inherited quadratic action estimate of Chapter 7. Multiplication by the current normalization \(L^5\) proves

\(\displaystyle \|D^2 \mathcal{E}'_b (0)\|_{\text{out}} \leqslant \frac{C_j}{L} \hspace{0.17em} \|D^2 \mathcal{E}_b (0)\|_{\text{in}} . \) (17.16)

This is homogeneous in the actual defect. No constant source term has been introduced. Dividing the coefficients by \((\log \hat{\beta})^2\) only adds the adjacent logarithmic ratio, which is bounded and tends to one on a fixed-stage tail. The selected block ratio makes this quadratic channel a strict contraction.

17.5.3The nonlinear terms need fixed-ratio bounds

The nonlinear stationary jets enter only below the diagonal in field order. Their constants may depend on \(L\), because the coupling threshold is chosen after the ratio. What must remain uniform before \(L\) is the coarse coefficient width on which these constants are measured. We obtain that width from the completed inverse of Chapter 7.

Write \(z (v)\) for the stationary internal coordinates, and \(\mathscr{S} (v, z)\) for the same joint fine-plus-frame action as there. Let \(Tu = (u, Dz (0) u)\), and let \(\mathscr{H}\) be its internal Hessian. On the forest slice implicit differentiation gives

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \mathscr{H} z_2 [u, v] & = - D^3 \mathscr{S} [Tu, Tv, \hspace{0.17em} \cdot \hspace{0.17em}],\\ \mathscr{H} z_3 [u, v, w] & = - D^4 \mathscr{S} [Tu, Tv, Tw, \hspace{0.17em} \cdot \hspace{0.17em}] - \sum_{\{ij\} \{k\}} D^3 \mathscr{S} [(0, z_2 [u_i, u_j]), Tu_k, \hspace{0.17em} \cdot \hspace{0.17em}] . \hspace{3cm} \text{(17.17)} \end{array}\)

There are three terms in the sum. The right sides are internal covectors, with all mixed gauge-covariant block average and frame terms included.

These formulas have period-independent bounds \(C_{j, L}\) on one fixed smaller coarse Fourier strip. To check the width statement, let \(\Pi_F\) be projection onto the forest slice along the joint gauge directions. At zero it is computed by finite sums along the block tree and the root frame; its coefficients and those of \(\Pi_F^{\ast}\) have norm bounded by a finite \(C_{j, L}\), but bounded range in coarse blocks. The dual \(\Pi_F^{\ast}\) extends a slice covector by zero on gauge directions. For such a covector the completed inverse \(\mathscr{C}^{- 1}\) of Chapter 7 solves the Hessian equation modulo gauge. Thus the slice inverse is

\(\displaystyle \Pi_F \mathscr{C}^{- 1} \Pi_F^{\ast} .\)

The completed inverse has a common positive coarse width chosen before \(L\); multiplication by the finite-range projections does not destroy it. The path vertices and derivatives of the gauge-covariant block average also have a common coarse width, with finite \(C_{j, L}\) norms. Restrict the external strips by a fixed factor so all intermediate momentum sums lie in this inverse strip. Formula (17.17) then proves the asserted bounds for \(z_2, z_3\). It does not assert bounds uniform in \(L\) for their norms.

For every anchored gauge-invariant function, evaluate this stationary branch in an anchored radial gauge. At first order, the gauge-corrected link at fine displacement \(x\) is a sum of the tangent curvature over coordinate rectangles of total area at most \(C (1 + |x|)\). The smooth curvature bound for \(W_a\) gives

\(\displaystyle |W^{\text{rad}} (x, y) | \leqslant C_j L^{- 2} (1 + |x|) e^{C |x| / L} e^{- c_j d (0, y)} .\)

The polynomial factor and fine exponential are absorbed by the weighted local coefficient norm. A common strip reduction handles the coarse weight. Higher derivatives of this gauge change are finite products along the same paths; the fixed-order factors are polynomial in path length. Their period-independent norms are therefore still finite \(C_{j, L}\) on the same smaller coarse width.

The raw higher coefficients \(D^3 \mathcal{E}, D^4 \mathcal{E}\) need not separately satisfy linear Ward identities. We use gauge invariance of the complete function to make this radial substitution first; its nonlinear derivatives multiply only lower field derivatives of the defect.

Now differentiate the exact pullback \(\mathcal{E}' = L^5 \mathcal{I} (K_0 \mathcal{E})\). In field order \(r\) the term involving \(D^r \mathcal{E}\) has only \(r\) first stationary tangents. For \(r = 3, 4\), radial gauge bounds it by \(C_j L^{5 - 2 r} \|D^r \mathcal{E}\|\). The quadratic diagonal has the stronger estimate (17.16). Every other chain-rule term involves \(D^s \mathcal{E}\) with \(s < r\) and higher stationary or gauge jets. Hence, for \(r = 2, 3, 4\),

\(\displaystyle e'_r \leqslant a_{j, r} (L) e_r + \sum_{2 \leqslant s < r} C_{j, L, r, s} e_s, \qquad a_{j, 2} (L), a_{j, 3} (L) \leqslant C_j / L, \quad a_{j, 4} (L) \leqslant C_j / L^3 . \) (17.18)

Here \(e_r\) is the weighted norm of the field derivative of order \(r\). The estimates are homogeneous in the defect, because every term in the composition contains one of its derivatives. There is no action-independent source on the right.

Put \(\bar{e}_{m, r} = e_{m, r} / (\log \hat{\beta}_m)^r\). For a fine-to-coarse step from \(m + 1\) to \(m\), the coefficient below the diagonal becomes

\(\displaystyle C_{j, L, r, s} \frac{(\log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1})^s}{(\log \hat{\beta}_m)^r}, \qquad s < r.\)

At fixed \(j, L\) this tends to zero as the coupling threshold increases. The diagonal logarithmic ratio tends to one. Choose \(L\) first so the three diagonal coefficients are at most \(1 / 8\), then choose the coupling threshold so each row sum, including the below-diagonal terms, is at most \(1 / 2\). The finitely many adjacent-stage cases are included before each choice. This proves a homogeneous contraction of the normalized defect vector. Starting with the Wilson defect, whose normalized norm is bounded, its value after \(n\) transfers is at most \(C 2^{- n}\). At a fixed output level the logarithmic normalization is fixed, so the actual unnormalized defect derivatives tend to zero as well.

17.5.4Why action matching introduces no source

We specify the displacement map, including its sign. At the stationary fine field and coarse frame, let \(\tilde{Q} (U, h)\) be the actual gauge-covariant block average with its chosen frame. Its left-trivialized differential is

\(\displaystyle (R_f \eta)_e = \left. \frac{d}{ds} \right|_0 \log (\tilde{Q} (e^{s \eta} U, h)_e \tilde{Q} (U, h)_e^{- 1}) .\)

Let \(b_{y, f}\) be the fixed frame-route coefficients defined by \(\sum_f b_{y, f} \eta_f = \tau_y (0 ; \eta) - \sum_r w_r^{\text{av}} \tau_y (r ; \eta)\), where \(\tau\) is the signed path average in (5.23). With the fixed route \(\gamma_{y, f}\) from coarse root \(Ly\) to the tail of the fine link \(f\), set

\(\displaystyle (R_0 \eta) (y) = \sum_f b_{y, f} \text{Ad} (h (y) U (\gamma_{y, f})) \eta_f, \qquad (d_V \phi)_e = \phi (e^-) - \text{Ad} (V_e) \phi (e^+) .\)

At coarse anchor zero the displaced coarse current is

\(\displaystyle Y'_b = L^5 \sum_x K_0 (x) (R_f + d_V R_0) Y_b^x (U_{\text{stat}} (V)) . \) (17.19)

Translations give the other anchors. The sum is over fine anchors; the output is a vector on coarse links. At the identity \(R_0 = R\) of (5.24), \(R_f = Q = M + d_{0, c} R\), and \(d_V = - d_{0, c}\). In this convention the plus sign in (17.19) cancels the root correction exactly:

\(\displaystyle (R_f + d_V R_0) |_0 = M. \) (17.20)

The smooth straight average, rather than a rough root-path row, therefore controls its equal-order Taylor terms. All derivatives of \(R_f + d_V R_0\) have finite \(C_{j, L}\) bounds on the common coarse strip. Their fixed paths have length \(O_j (L)\) and remain within bounded coarse distance; differentiation adds only finite path-length factors.

For the exact stationary action the current identity is preserved before any estimates. The envelope theorem differentiates the stationary value only through the explicit coarse field. In the block penalty, simultaneous left multiplication of the gauge-covariant block average and the coarse link leaves the penalty unchanged. Thus a fine displacement \(Y\) induces the coarse displacement \(R_f Y\); its root correction \(d_V R_0 Y\) has zero action variation by coarse gauge invariance. Together with (17.7) this gives

\(\displaystyle DS_{\text{stat}} [Y'_b] = L^5 \mathcal{I}K_0 DS [Y_b], \qquad \sum_a \nabla_a^{\text{c}} J'_{ab} = L^5 \mathcal{I}K_0 \sum_a \nabla_a J_{ab} .\)

Joint fine and frame stationarity is used here, as in the completed quotient calculation of Chapter 7. Matching replaces \(S_{\text{stat}}\) by an action with identical Taylor coefficients through field order four. Since \(Y' (0) = 0\), its action variation still agrees through order four. Therefore the displayed defect pullback is exact at every field order used in (17.18). A separate additive \(C / (\log \hat{\beta})^2\) matching error is neither needed nor permitted in this argument.

17.5.5Uniform current and difference-primitive bounds

Recall that \(J_{ab}\) is the lattice stress current and \(Y_b\) its associated gauge-covariant displacement. The functions \(q_a\) and \(t_{abc}\) are the forward-difference primitives of its trace and antisymmetric part, respectively, as defined in Section 17.4. They are distinct from the small-field threshold \(t\) used in marked norms.

For completeness we describe the accompanying bounds needed in the regulator criterion. Subtract fixed local reference stress and curvature displacements, whose leading Taylor coefficients by scaling dimension are those used initially. The reference functions have finite stencils and fixed coefficient bounds. The lowest jets of the transported functions agree with them exactly. For the stress this follows from the principal tangent amplitude \(N_0 (0) = L^{- 1} I\), the mass-one anchor average, and the fact that nonprincipal transverse amplitudes have order at least two. If exactly one alias is nonprincipal, the linear Ward identity on the other leg supplies one further principal momentum, so this contribution has order at least three. Two nonprincipal aliases have order at least four. Consequently only the principal pair contributes to the quadratic weight-four coefficient, which is unchanged.

For the displacement one additionally uses the column sum of the gauge-covariant block average. By (17.20), the corrected differential is exactly \(M\) at zero. The block-average alias formula for \(M\) kills every nonprincipal alias at zero coarse momentum, so its fine column sum in a fixed direction is constant. Its row mass is \(L\); there are \(L^4\) fine residues. The column sum is therefore \(L^{- 3}\). This is an identity for the corrected differential, with no residual root-gradient term. Since the leading fine curvature of \(W_a\) is \(L^{- 2}\) times the coarse curvature, the factors in the current update multiply to \(L^5 L^{- 3} L^{- 2} = 1\). This preserves the linear curvature jet of \(Y\). Gauge covariance then fixes its accompanying quadratic commutator term: its leading covariant weight-two polynomial is that same curvature.

The stress remainder's quadratic symbol now has order at least three. Two-leg curl division leaves one extra difference, so its diagonal transfer is \(C_j L^4 L^{- 5} \leqslant C_j / L\). Higher stress orders have radial-tangent bounds \(C_j L^{4 - 2 r}\). For \(Y\), its zero-order corrected differential \(M\) has row sum at most \(C_j L\), while \(\|K_0 \|_{\infty} \leqslant C_j L^{- 4}\). To see the resulting local gain, write a translated current coefficient as \(y (f - x ; z_1 - x, \ldots, z_r - x)\), with output link \(f\) and anchor \(x\). For each fixed coarse output edge \(e\) its absolute sum is bounded by

\(\displaystyle \|K_0 \|_{\infty} \sum_f |M_{ef} | \sum_x |y (f - x ; \hspace{0.17em} \cdot \hspace{0.17em}) | \leqslant C_j L^{- 3} \|y\| .\)

Only a bounded number of coarse output edges are within the gauge-covariant block average and averaging patches; exponential coefficient tails control the rest. This proves the same gain for the full local output-link norm. The linear displacement remainder has a curvature difference and one additional difference. If the extra difference acts on its field leg, it costs \(L^{- 1}\) through \(W_a\). If it acts on the fine output index, summation by parts moves it onto \(M_{ef}\); the box-filter identity gives \(\sum_f | \nabla_f M_{ef} | \leqslant C_j\), again gaining \(L^{- 1}\) relative to its row mass \(C_j L\). Thus its diagonal factor is \(C_j L^5 L^{- 3} L^{- 3} = C_j / L\). For field order \(r \geqslant 2\) its diagonal factor is \(C_j L^{2 - 2 r}\). Derivatives of the gauge-covariant block average or its frame correction multiply lower field orders of \(Y\), so their fixed-\(L\) constants are absorbed by the same logarithmic normalization as above. Evaluating in a local radial gauge is legitimate for this covariant displacement: transform its output frame and the differential of the gauge-covariant block average together. The additional frame derivatives again occur only below the field-order diagonal.

The trace and antisymmetry primitives start at field order two. Their old terms carry \(L^3\) and hence gain \(C_j L^{3 - 2 r} \leqslant C_j / L\). Their new terms are the explicit \(L^4 K_a^{\text{t}} J\) terms proved in Lemma 17.1; the equal-order forcing from \(J\) is bounded independently of \(L\). Choose a fixed smaller weight for these primitive norms so this one-way forcing has norm at most \(1 / 8\). The other diagonal terms are then made small by \(L\), and all lower-order terms by coupling threshold. Reference functions contribute finite \(C_{j, L}\) forcing, divided by positive powers of \(\log \hat{\beta}\); a sufficiently large coupling threshold can make that forcing at most \(1 / 4\).

Thus a weighted sum of the normalized current and primitive norms obeys \(X_m \leqslant \frac{1}{2} X_{m + 1} + \frac{1}{4}\). The initial Wilson current and zero primitives have bounded normalized norms. Uniform bounds follow throughout the history, and in particular at each fixed output level. At that level these are the unnormalized finite constants required by the regulator criterion, since \(\hat{\beta}_m\) is fixed. The needed free scaling-dimension jets are unchanged, and the defect tends to zero by the homogeneous contraction above. All estimates use the same gauge-covariant block average, stationary branch, action matching and periodized kernels as the marked trajectory.

17.6Quantitative stability of the trace functional

The finite-dimensional argument is elementary, but its rate is essential. Suppose \(s : E \to V\) has right inverse \(t\) and one-dimensional kernel \(\mathbb{R}v\). Let \(s_i : E \to V\), \(t_i : V \to E\) satisfy \(s_i t_i = I\), \(t_i \to t\), and let \(v_i \to v\). If \(e_i \geqslant 1\) and

\(\displaystyle e_i \|s_i v_i \| \to 0, \qquad e_i | \rho (v_i) | \to 0,\)

then, for \(\lambda_i = \rho t_i\),

\(\displaystyle s_i \to s, \quad \sup_i \| \lambda_i \| < \infty, \quad e_i \| \lambda_i s_i - \rho \| \to 0. \) (17.21)

Indeed \(k_i = v_i - t_i s_i v_i \to v\) lies in \(\ker s_i\). The maps \((h, c) \mapsto t_i h + ck_i\) converge to the invertible map \((h, c) \mapsto th + cv\), so their inverses are uniformly bounded. Writing \(y = t_i s_i y + c_i (y) k_i\) gives \(|c_i (y) | \leqslant C \|y\|\) and \((\lambda_i s_i - \rho) y = - c_i (y) \rho (k_i)\), which proves the claim.

Here are the complete rate estimates needed for the trace conclusion; the next section proves them for the common family. Write

\(\displaystyle e_i = \exp ((\log \log b_i)^2 / 5), \qquad q_i = \exp ((\log \log b_i)^2 / 6),\)

so \(q_i / e_i \to 0\) and both are smaller than every positive power of \(b_i\). The letter \(q_i\) in this section is a scalar bound, unrelated to the inserted densities of Chapter 11. Let \(B_i^L, B_i^X, B_i^D\) be the centered transferred tensors and set

\(\displaystyle A_i = \beta_i r_i^4 U_{i, 4}^{- 1}, \qquad K_i = \beta_i r_i^5 U_{i, 5}^{- 1} \text{div} .\)

The required same-family current transfer estimates are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} & \| \beta_i K_i B_i^L \| \leqslant Cq_i, \quad \lambda_i (\beta_i K_i B_i^L) \to - \kappa_G, \hspace{3cm} \text{(17.22)} \\ & K_i B_i^Z \to \text{div}^{\text{Max}} \hspace{0.17em} Z, \quad e_i [\lambda_i (K_i B_i^Z) - \rho (\text{div}_{\text{raw}} \hspace{0.17em} Z)] \to 0 \quad (Z = X, D),\\ & A_i B_i^Z \to Z \quad (Z = L, X, D), \quad b_i^p \text{tr} (A_i B_i^Z) \to 0 \quad (Z = L, X, p \in \mathbb{N}) . \end{array}\)

These bounds are stronger than smooth free comparison. The next section proves them using the kernel identities and a separate low-moment estimate for spatial remainders.

17.7From the current identity to marked coefficient estimates

The integration by parts needed here is performed before the large-field expansion. This order matters: a large-field activity is only measurable, so its derivative cannot be inserted into the expansion as an ordinary bounded function.

17.7.1A differentiated block kernel

Use the exact block kernel of (4.8). Conditional on its fine field and frame variables, its dependence on a coarse link is \(q_{b \beta} (V_e Q_e^{- 1})\), where \(q_{\gamma} (u) = Z_{\gamma}^{- 1} \exp [- \gamma w (u)]\). For every fixed order \(r\), compactness and smoothness of \(w\) give

\(\displaystyle |D^r q_{\gamma} (u) | \leqslant C_r (1 + \gamma)^r q_{\gamma} (u) .\) (17.23)

Indeed successive left derivatives give a polynomial in derivatives of \(w\), with at most \(r\) factors of \(\gamma\). The normalization is independent of \(u\). For a product of link kernels the corresponding bound has an additional polynomial in the number of differentiated links. No lower bound on \(q_{\gamma}\) is used.

First truncate the exponentially local current and its stationary kernels on a finite neighborhood. For the displacement use a finite covariant completion of its controlled jets through field order four, not the uncontrolled higher derivatives of a deep transferred current. Use the coefficientwise covariant path completion: replace each fixed-order curvature or covariant-difference monomial by its plaquette and transported-path representative, and correct its lower jets triangularly through order four. Each fixed number of link derivatives adds only a fixed power of the path length. The completion therefore has a bounded weighted norm at a strictly narrower exponential width; it is not an arbitrary jet section on a space of growing dimension. Complete in that weighted norm first and then truncate. All additional fixed-order derivative bounds depend only on the controlled jets and the fixed stage. Extend this local small-field formula smoothly to the compact product group. One can use a smooth cutoff supported strictly inside the logarithmic chart, equal to one on the smaller chart used for Taylor extraction, and then average over the compact local gauge group. Equivalently extend its finite covariant holonomy jets. This preserves the required jets and covariance. The finite derivative costs are inverse powers of the chart width and polynomials in the support size. They are allowed in the bounds below. Only after this extension do we integrate by parts. For an exponentially local current do this first for finite support truncations. Fixed powers of the support length are absorbed by a strict reserve in the exponential coefficient width. The derivative bounds and tail errors are consequently summable, so the finite-period identity passes to the weighted tail limit.

Let \(k (U, V)\) be that exact kernel, let \(f (U)\) be the entire incoming density, and put \(F (V) = \int k (U, V) f (U) \hspace{0.17em} dU\). All original fine large-field indicators and compatibility conditions are part of \(f\) or of the frame integral; none is differentiated. For a smooth coarse displacement \(Y\), define

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{D}_Y k = (\text{div}_{\text{Haar}} \hspace{0.17em} Y) k + D_V k [Y] .\)

Dominated differentiation, using (17.23), gives the density identity

\(\displaystyle \text{div}_{\text{Haar}} (FY) = \int \mathcal{D}_Y k (U, V) f (U) \hspace{0.17em} dU.\) (17.24)

Consequently its integral against every spectator independent of the varied coarse links is zero. This is an identity for the full source, including its nonpolynomial remainder, not an assertion that a finite Taylor polynomial alone has zero exterior moments.

The right hand side is an admissible marked conditional integral. Its extra factor is a finite sum of smooth local kernel scores times \(Y\) or its derivative. On a protected dependence region the incoming measurable product remains fixed under the change of variables in Chapter 6. Additional coarse derivatives act on the score and the common density ratio only. The bounded pointwise-jet estimate (6.26) therefore applies with a fixed polynomial loss in \(\beta\), support size and current derivative bounds. Rooting the connected expansion at this extra factor gives the same marked expansion as in Chapter 11. In particular large-field terms retain their original prescribed sets of sites. This argument does not differentiate the event indicators introduced when that marked integral is subsequently decomposed.

The ordinary contribution needs a sharper identity than the absolute score bound. Fix the full dependence region of a rooted connected component and use one additional coarse derivative beyond those requested for the extracted coefficient. On its smaller small-field chart, make exactly the conditional Gaussian change of variables used to construct the ordinary expansion. Write its normalized conditional integrand as \(p (V, z)\) and its integral as \(Z_{\text{ord}} (V)\). For finite ordinary selections, differentiation under this integral gives

\(\displaystyle D_V Z_{\text{ord}} [Y] = \int D_V p [Y] \hspace{0.17em} dz, \qquad \int D_V p [Y] \hspace{0.17em} dz = Z_{\text{ord}} D_V \log Z_{\text{ord}} [Y] .\)

Every Jacobian, relative determinant and conditional normalization is included in \(p\); thus this is not the derivative of an unnormalized Gaussian moment. The absolutely convergent ordinary logarithm, with one derivative reserve, passes this identity to the countable expansion. By its definition in Chapter 9,

\(\displaystyle Z_{\text{ord}} =\mathcal{C} \exp [- \beta S -\mathcal{L}- E_{\text{reg}}], \qquad D_V \log \mathcal{C}= 0.\)

If an intermediate local normalization depends on \(V\), it is included in \(\mathcal{L}+ E_{\text{reg}}\), not in \(\mathcal{C}\). Consequently the ordinary score is exactly \(- \beta DS [Y] - D\mathcal{L} [Y] - DE_{\text{reg}} [Y]\). The regular term is estimated using its already proved differentiated regular norm. We do not estimate it by the polynomial-in-\(\beta\) absolute score bound.

This argument uses the full rooted component, not just the score stencil. In the rooted connected expansion, ordinary factors in components disjoint from the marked component cancel between the differentiated numerator and its normalization. Every term with a failure anywhere in the marked connected component, including a distant failure joined to the score, is kept in the complementary marked sum. The smooth cutoffs used above are constant on the smaller small-field chart; their derivatives are supported where at least one enlarged chart condition fails. Attach this condition to its actual dependence support. The same Gaussian or energy estimate for the nested cutoffs bounds this new failure, while all incoming prescribed large-field sets are retained. The absolute score bound is used only for these complementary terms, where the exponential failure probability absorbs its polynomial \(\beta\) cost. No derivative of a hard indicator is taken. Proposition 17.3. \(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_l =\mathcal{B}_l + \text{diag} (g_{l, a}) x_l \sigma, \qquad \mathcal{B}_l = D_l F_{1, l}^{\ast} D^{- 1} .\)

Here \(D_l\) is regular at a nonprincipal alias, and \(s \alpha\) and \(B_a\) are analytic and nonzero at zero. Thus no uncancelled factor \(s^{- 1}\) remains. The principal column has value \(I\). This is stronger than the order-two transverse estimate sufficient for the current contraction. Stars and conjugates in these symbols mean analytic transpose at the opposite momentum, as in Chapter 5.

Let \(A_x^{ab} (z)\) be the physical row from coarse direction \(b\) at \(\lfloor x / L \rfloor + z\) to fine direction \(a\) at \(x\). Differentiating the Fourier reconstruction through order less than \(j\) therefore gives, for every vector polynomial \(p\) of degree less than \(j\),

\(\displaystyle (W_s p) (x)_a = \sum_{b, z} A_0^{ab} (z) p_b (x / L + z), \qquad \sum_z A_0^{ab} (z) = L^{- 1} \delta_{ab} .\) (17.26)

The exponential row bound justifies the derivatives and the sum. Polarization gives mixed polynomial moments. The polynomial-moment cancellation of Section 11.4.1 makes its deterministic coefficient evaluation exactly zero. Cropping the coarse field at radius \(C_j (\log \hat{\beta}_k)^2\) changes this coefficient evaluation only by the exponential row tail, times fixed polynomial directional and support costs. The actual tangent is \(W_a = W_s - d_f ERW_s\). Its difference from \(W_s\) is a fine gradient. with an appropriate enlargement of the crop radius in the last term. The factor-count extraction has already been performed; no further \(t^{- 2}\) is applied to this bound. Use the joint polynomial moments and differentiated density scores of that one conditional law to estimate the evaluation difference; no small total-variation distance between the nonlinear law and a Gaussian is asserted. Proposition 17.4. (The trace coefficient) For the prescribed-radius comparison maps constructed above,

\(\displaystyle \beta_i r_i^4 U_{i, 4}^{- 1} \Theta \longrightarrow - \kappa_G \mathcal{O}.\)

Proof. The free divergences of \(X, D\) form a basis of \(V\). Thus the maps \(f_i (c) = c_0 K_i B_i^X + c_1 K_i B_i^D\) have bounded inverses eventually. Theorem 17.5. (The all-radius trace law) The centered trace in the selected phase satisfies

\(\displaystyle \lim_{r \downarrow 0} r^8 \log^2 (1 / r) G_{\Theta} (r) =\mathcal{A}_G := \frac{3 \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}}{\pi^4} > 0. \) (17.29)

Start with any prescribed null sequence of radii, and take its comparison subsequence. Proposition 17.4 gives convergent moving labels, while (17.3) cancels the factor \(\kappa_G^2\) in their pair correlation. For a generic pair at distance \(t\), lattice rounding therefore yields

\(\displaystyle r_i^8 \log^2 (1 / r_i) G_{\Theta} (r_i t_i) \to \mathcal{A}_G t^{- 8}, \qquad t_i \to t.\)

17.9What the trace law excludes

Specify the comparison class before using this asymptotic. Consider finite linear combinations of finite products of the mass-shell Laplace kernels

\(\displaystyle K_{m, k, h} (r) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \frac{\omega_m (\boldsymbol{p})^k h (\boldsymbol{p})}{2 \omega_m (\boldsymbol{p})} e^{- r \omega_m (\boldsymbol{p})} \hspace{0.17em} d^3 \boldsymbol{p}, \qquad \omega_m = (m^2 + |\boldsymbol{p}|^2)^{1 / 2},\)

where \(m \geqslant 0\), \(k\) is a nonnegative integer and \(h\) is a polynomial; empty products are constants. The measure has no implicit Fourier normalization. All these integrals are locally integrable at zero and converge for \(r > 0\).

Each such profile has, near zero, a finite sum of terms \(r^a (\log r)^b\), with integer \(a\) and nonnegative integer \(b\), plus a bounded remainder. This is the standard Laplace asymptotic expansion [Won01], applied as follows: decompose \(h\) into homogeneous terms, use polar coordinates, and expand \((\omega^2 - m^2)^{(j + 1) / 2}\) for \(\omega \geqslant 2 m\). Choose enough terms that the remainder has as many energy moments as needed for the other factors in a product. The retained integer powers integrate to powers and logarithms; the bounded energy interval contributes an analytic function. Thus only nonnegative powers of the logarithm occur.

After multiplication by \(r^8 \log^2 (1 / r)\), a nonzero leading term with \(a \leqslant - 8\) diverges in magnitude, while every term with \(a > - 8\) and the bounded remainder tend to zero. Therefore a finite limit, if it exists, is zero. The positive limit in (17.29) proves that \(G_{\Theta}\) cannot agree with any such profile on any interval \((0, \delta)\).

This excludes that specified finite mass-shell polynomial comparison. It does not exclude a generalized free field chosen to have the same positive measure \(\rho_{\Theta}\), nor an infinite Wick expansion. No mass gap follows from a short-distance trace law.
