Chapter 19
Assembly and the scope of the construction

The preceding chapters distinguish finite algebraic identities, analytic estimates for the lattice transformation, and consequences for the continuum theory. This chapter states how they fit together. The construction and finite-order operator products use the estimates proved in the preceding chapters. The sharper trace asymptotic is separated below because it requires the additional current estimates of Chapter 17.

19.1One family and the order of its limits

Choose the compact matrix group \(G\) with simple nonabelian real Lie algebra, its trace inner product as in Section 1.1, and a positive soft-block penalty \(b\). Fix a sufficiently large coarse inverse coupling \(\bar{\beta}\). The common schedule of Chapter 10 selects all block lengths, derivative orders, support weights, coupling thresholds and admissible physical periods \(R_p\). At cutoff \(N\) and period index \(p\), shooting from the Wilson measure gives its bare coupling \(\beta_{N, p, N}\) with prescribed coarse endpoint \(\bar{\beta}\).

The same finite histories must define the coefficient normalizations of every local insertion. One cannot separately choose convenient histories for convergence, for free separating families and for the trace law. The microscopic sections are fixed before \(N, p\), and their comparison maps act simultaneously on all labels and spectator correlation pairings. Any additional finite stage requirements, such as the equation calibration floor, belong to the selection of this one schedule.

For each fixed physical volume the first limit is the full limit \(N \to \infty\). Only afterwards is a subsequence of physical volumes extracted. Given any increasing sequence of period indices, a single refinement supplies all arities, degree bounds, label basis elements and derivatives of the separated kernels. Auxiliary coefficient limit points may be selected on ordered tails; they do not change this full inner limit of the correlations. Scaling comparison sequences and the choice of a phase are later operations on the same continuum family.

19.2Assembly of the construction

The hypotheses used along the construction have now been supplied for one common family. Chapters 9 and 10 give the actual block estimates and fixed-input convergence. Chapter 11 supplies the marked comparisons, including restarted histories and arbitrary fixed numbers of insertions. Chapter 13 constructs the bounded approximations and local product estimates. The free comparison and annular estimates in Chapter 14, and the repeated estimate for a separating family in Chapter 15, use these same objects. Thus they are not additional assumptions in the following assembly statement.

Theorem 19.1. (Assembly of the common Wilson family) The chosen Wilson family has the following properties. For every prescribed increasing volume sequence there is one refinement with a hierarchy \(S\) of smooth separated Euclidean correlations and compatible finite-dimensional field spaces \(F_d\). Their dimensions and orthogonal representations agree with those of the invariant free Maxwell comparison spaces. The fields have units, injective inclusions and commuting positional derivatives.

There is a compact metrizable phase space \(\Xi\), a full-support probability measure \(\nu\), and phase hierarchies \(S^{\xi}\) with \(S = \int S^{\xi} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu\). All phases have the same field-null spaces, Euclidean covariance and reflection positivity. Each phase reconstructs a local relativistic quantum field theory with tempered Wightman correlations, a faithful common field filtration, a positive-cone translation spectrum and a unique invariant vacuum. Its weak field commutant is scalar. These statements are uniform over the phases in the finite-list senses specified in the preceding chapters.

The common real stress tensor is symmetric and conserved, with its normalization fixed by local translation and rotation Ward identities. Its relativistic continuation gives the local charge law (16.13). The fixed OPE prescription of Section 18.1 gives the phase-uniform finite-order coefficient and remainder bounds (18.5). Independently, the fixed subtraction prescription of Section 18.4 extends all separated correlations to finite-order distributions across collision diagonals, compatibly with the field inclusions, translations, rotations and permutations.

Proof. The dependency chain uses the estimates established above. Chapter 12 takes the full cutoff limit at each fixed period. The common diagonal extraction and two rank bounds in Chapter 13 give the fields and their differential calculus. That chapter also proves the repeated-word bound, so bounded slab insertions in Chapter 14 do not depend on later reconstruction or operator products.

Coordinate reflection positivity, the free comparison and the annular estimates give the conserved tensor, its local charges, Euclidean rotations and Ward normalization in Chapter 14. Chapter 15 then compresses whole words to the mixed ground space. The real approximation and moments for separating families prove symmetries and the equality of field spaces in every character, beyond what abstract commutative algebra alone gives.

The slab bounds supply joint boundary distributions in Chapter 16. Covariance gives the spectral cone, analytic continuation gives locality, and word reconstruction gives the relativistic fields. The unitary equivalence of the reconstructed and reflection-positive Hilbert spaces and the phase-uniform low-energy estimate remove exceptional vacua. The same chapter proves weak irreducibility and continues the stress charge law.

The finite OPE and contact extension follow from their separate arguments in Chapter 18. Each step preserves the originally chosen family and the already selected correlation limits.\(\Box\)

Corollary 19.2. (The trace law) In the retained phase \(\xi_{\ast}\),

\(\displaystyle \lim_{r \downarrow 0} r^8 \log^2 (1 / r) G_{\Theta} (r) = \frac{3 \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}}{\pi^4} .\)

Consequently the centered trace cannot agree near zero with any finite linear combination of products of the mass-shell Laplace kernels specified in Section 17.9.

Proof. Apply the rate-sensitive tensor cancellation and prescribed-radius squeeze of Chapter 17. The positive spectral measure required there already follows from reconstruction.\(\Box\)

19.3Conclusions that are not supplied

The logarithmic ultraviolet law and the low-energy estimate describe different regimes. The latter bounds spectral mass near zero by a power; it does not show that the spectrum avoids an interval above the vacuum. A positive mass gap is not proved.

Vacuum uniqueness within each phase does not imply uniqueness of the phase, a point-mass phase measure, or uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit. The construction uses a refinement of a prescribed volume sequence. It does not prove a simultaneous continuum and infinite-volume limit, independence of the block penalty or all normalization choices, or a separate lattice realization of each character phase.

The trace comparison excludes the finite class explicitly defined in Chapter 17. It does not rule out every generalized free field or every infinite Wick expansion on the basis of one two-point function. The OPE is an expansion to each fixed finite degree; no infinite series convergence has been asserted. The contact extension has a specified subtraction convention and may have larger local order than an optimized prescription. Positivity at contacts, contact Ward identities, the fact that inserting the identity leaves correlations unchanged, and uniqueness of contact terms require additional normalization conditions.

The trace argument uses more than convergence to the free tensor. Its low-moment estimate distinguishes field degree from scaling dimension: a quadratic expression with extra spatial differences is still quadratic in the field. Exact polynomial preservation by the linear response, together with the normalized Gaussian fluctuation estimate, supplies the required decay in Proposition 17.3. This is why that additional calculation is needed after reconstruction.
