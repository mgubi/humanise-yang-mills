1The result

How can a lattice model give a quantum field theory in continuous space-time? We study this question for four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory with a compact gauge group whose Lie algebra is simple and nonabelian, starting from Wilson's lattice gauge theory [35]. On a finite periodic lattice the variables are group elements attached to the links, and their law is an ordinary probability measure. Let \(G \subset U (n_G)\) be a closed matrix group, \(s \geqslant 1\) an integer and \(\Lambda_s = (\mathbb{Z}/ s\mathbb{Z})^4\). A configuration assigns \(U (y, a) \in G\) to the positive link from the site \(y\) in the coordinate direction \(a\), and \(U (y, a)^{- 1}\) to the reversed link. For \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\),

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} U_{y ; ab} & = U (y, a) U (y + e_a, b) U (y + e_b, a)^{- 1} U (y, b)^{- 1},\\ E_s^{\text{W}} (U) & = \sum_{y \in \Lambda_s} \sum_{a < b} \left( 1 - n_G^{- 1} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} U_{y ; ab} \right), \qquad d \mu_{s, \beta} (U) = Z_{s, \beta}^{- 1} e^{- \beta E_s^{\text{W}} (U)} \prod_{y, a} dh (U (y, a)) \hspace{0.17em}, \end{aligned}\) (1.1)

where \(dh\) is probability Haar measure and \(Z_{s, \beta}\) normalizes the measure. The plaquette holonomy \(U_{y ; ab}\) is the transport around an elementary square, and \(\beta\) penalizes its distance from the identity. The problem is to choose the inverse coupling and the local observables as functions of the lattice spacing so that their correlations converge as the spacing tends to zero and the volume grows, and to identify the limit. The proposed construction does not give a measure on continuum connections. Its claimed limits are correlations of gauge-invariant local observables at distinct points; the resulting correlation functions are intended to reconstruct Hilbert spaces and relativistic quantum fields.

Main result.
The following claims are conditional on the inputs listed in the proof-status notice. Let the real Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}\) of \(G\) be simple and nonabelian; \(G\) need not be connected. Fix a block penalty \(\textsf{b} > 0\): the coefficient of the soft constraint that ties each coarse link to a predictor computed from the fine transports in the block-spin kernel (Definition 5.1). The proposed construction gives one family of Wilson measures on tori of physical side \(L_p \uparrow \infty\) with spacings \(a_N = D_N^{- 1} \downarrow 0\), together with continuous gauge-invariant local insertions on fixed finite stencils, all chosen before any sequence of volumes is prescribed. Its bare inverse couplings satisfy the one-loop running law
\(\displaystyle \frac{\beta_{N, p}}{\log (1 / a_N)} \longrightarrow \kappa_G = \frac{11 C_{\mathfrak{g}}}{24 \pi^2} \qquad (N \to \infty), \quad \text{uniformly in } p \hspace{0.17em}, \) (1.2)

where \(C_{\mathfrak{g}}\) is the adjoint Casimir in the trace normalization of Definition 2.1 (for \(SU (n)\), \(\kappa_G = 11 n^2 / (12 \pi^2)\); Remark 2.2). The family has the following properties; Theorem B in Section 3 states them precisely.

    (i)

    At each fixed volume, correlations of the insertions, smeared against smooth tests supported away from coincident points and inside one period cell, converge along the full sequence of spacings.

    (ii)

    Every prescribed strictly increasing sequence \(\textsf{p}\) of volume indices has a strictly increasing refinement \(\textsf{q}\) such that, with \(P = \textsf{p} \circ \textsf{q}\) and the lattice correlations \(C^{\text{lat}}_{N, p}\) of Definition 2.4,

    \(\displaystyle \lim_{k \to \infty} (\lim_{N \to \infty} C^{\text{lat}}_{N, P (k)} (v ; f)) = \int f (x) S_n (v ; x) \hspace{0.17em} dx\) (1.3)

    for one table \(S\) of Euclidean correlation functions, symmetric under permutations, translations and signed permutations of the axes, reflection positive, smooth away from collisions and polynomially bounded near them (properties E1–E7 of Section 2.4). One refinement serves all insertions and all tests.

    (iii)

    \(S = \int_{\textsf{X}} S^{\xi} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\xi)\) is an average of phases over a compact metric space with a fully supported probability measure, and each \(S^{\xi}\) is the Euclidean continuation of a faithful Wightman theory with unique vacuum.

    (iv)

    The fields carry a conserved symmetric stress tensor, normalized by its action on the other fields. In a selected phase, the two-point function \(G_{\Theta}\) of the centered trace of this tensor satisfies

    \(\displaystyle \lim_{r \downarrow 0} r^8 \log^2 (1 / r) \hspace{0.17em} G_{\Theta} (r) = \frac{3 \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}}{\pi^4} > 0 \hspace{0.17em},\) (1.4)

    and on no interval \((0, \delta)\) does \(G_{\Theta}\) agree with a finite Wick profile, a finite sum of finite products of free mass-shell Laplace kernels (Definition 2.18).

    (v)

    Nearby fields have an operator product expansion whose coefficients depend neither on the other fields nor on the phase, with remainders bounded uniformly in the phase. The correlations of each phase extend across coincident points by an explicit subtraction of Taylor jets.

The claimed conclusion of Theorem B applies to every \(\textsf{b} > 0\), subject to the proof inputs above. Section 8 states a weaker existence theorem (Theorem A), which asks only for some positive logarithmic slope, and deduces it from Theorem B.

What is not claimed.
The Clay problem [18, §4] asks for existence and a positive mass gap. The intended construction gives Wightman theories with unique vacuum, one in each phase; no mass gap is claimed or used. Uniqueness of the vacuum means that zero is a simple eigenvalue of the energy operator. It gives no lower bound on the rest of the spectrum: a positive operator with one-dimensional kernel may have spectrum accumulating at zero, as the energy of a free massless field does, and nothing stated in this paper excludes such a spectrum for the theories constructed here. The infinite-volume limit, the phase space and the selected phase are not shown to be unique, and independence from \(\textsf{b}\) is not proved. Section 3 lists the limitations next to Theorem B.

1.1Proof status

Unresolved proof inputs.
The arguments for the main results require the following inputs, which remain open: support-weighted determinant locality and the physical boundary estimate in Lemma 11.14, including its comparison with the coefficient calculation of Lemma 12.8, and the common shifted-strip estimate used in Lemmas 11.9 and 11.8; the fine-health, localization and cutoff-continuity inputs of Lemmas 13.6, 13.7 and 13.22; the higher-mode support reserve and stopping-level restart for mixed marked attachments in Lemma 14.14; and the same-sample guarded Gaussian comparison, its scalar and forced estimates, and finite-cutoff continuity of the marked graph operator (Lemma 14.34). The joint forced initialization of Lemma 14.12 gives a late-cutoff, site-root bound but does not supply the higher-mode propagation. The trace deduction additionally requires the normalized raw right-inverse convergence identified in the proof of Lemma 18.8. The local signed forest bound of Lemma 19.21 uses the fixed contact choices of Definitions 19.16–19.18; its application to the selected family depends on the marked and quotient inputs above. The claims depending on these inputs are conditional until the missing estimates and convergence statements are established.

The renormalization group is organized as in the method of Bauerschmidt, Brydges and Slade [6]: an explicitly defined map, a finite-dimensional flow of couplings, norms in which the remaining coordinate contracts, and a global trajectory with a tuned initial parameter. What requires proof here is the compatibility of the one-loop slope with the exact gauge block map, the coupling reset and the tuning to a common endpoint, followed by continuum identifications on one family: control of composite fields, restoration of rotations through stress-tensor fluxes, the trace law through all radii and product bounds uniform over phases.

1.4Notation and conventions

Throughout, \(\mathbb{N}= \{0, 1, \ldots\}\), coordinate indices run from \(1\) to \(4\), \(e_a\) is the \(a\)th coordinate vector, and time is the fourth coordinate. For the relativistic statements write

\(\displaystyle \textsf{m} (x) = (x^4)^2 - | \vec{x} |^2, \qquad \bar{V}_+ = \{\mathbf{p}: | \vec{\mathbf{p}} | \leqslant \mathbf{p}^4 \} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Inner products are antilinear in the first variable, and spacetime integrals use Lebesgue measure. Correlations of arity zero are included; empty sums are zero and empty products are one. The group \(H_4\) consists of the signed permutation matrices, and \(\textsf{s}_a^{\text{refl}}\) denotes reflection in the \(a\)th coordinate.
