16Free fields, Ward identities and rotations

The only rotations that preserve the lattice, the Wilson action and the block-spin transformation of Sections 11–15 are those of the hypercubic group \(H_4\). Continuous rotational covariance of the limit is therefore an output of the construction. This section obtains it from a conserved stress tensor and proves Proposition 6.7, Theorem 6.10 and Proposition 6.11. The argument has three tasks.

    (1)

    Find the conserved line. The Maxwell comparison identifies the fields of degrees four and five with their free counterparts. A three-dimensional space of candidate tensors maps by divergence onto a two-dimensional space of vectors, so conserved tensors form a line (Lemma 16.10).

    (2)

    Make the translation shell act as differentiation. Shell fluxes of a member of that line define charge maps on the field spaces (Lemma 16.11). Finite free Ward tests identify the translation charge with a multiple of differentiation (Lemma 16.17).

    (3)

    Identify the angular action and recover rotations. Coordinate reflection positivity controls the angular flux at infinity for configurations on coordinate axes through the origin, with no lower bound on the energy spectrum (Lemma 16.15). Analyticity extends the angular identity to all configurations; finite internal tests remove the defect in the bracket relations, and the angular charges integrate to an \(O (4)\) action (Lemma 16.17).

Section 16.1 treats the free field, Sections 16.2–16.3 the comparison and the conserved line, Section 16.4 the coordinate reflection spaces, and Section 16.5 the Ward identities. Section 16.4 uses no continuous rotation; continuous rotations are first available in Lemma 16.17. The free calculation of Lemma 16.1 also supplies the positivity used in Lemma 14.23; its proof uses no interacting input.

Two estimates go beyond convergence at fixed separated points. The lattice comparison must control tests uniformly in their supremum norm, including near configurations with a shared coordinate; narrow strips around those configurations are removed, with different scales for the kernel and for its variation. Later, conservation removes the inner boundaries in the angular identity but not the boundary at infinity, which an anisotropic cutoff and the coordinate spectral estimates remove.

Except in the free calculation, fix the table, volume refinement, quotient fields and calculus of Definitions 2.6, 2.12 and 6.3 and Lemmas 15.1–15.6. Their application to the selected family retains the full fixed-period ultraviolet limit and all-period bounds, and the actual equation comparison, projection, inclusion and difference remainders and finite detector ranks of Lemmas 14.19–14.28, with their stated ordered or all-period thresholds. In particular, the conclusions below are conditional on those inputs wherever they are used; the quotient construction alone does not supply them. Subsequences below change only comparison radii. Write \(\jmath_{d, e}\) for the inclusions and \(\partial_a\) for field differentiation.

16.1Free fields

Put \(z = x - y\) and \(\textsf{h}_{ab} (z) = \partial_a \partial_b \Gamma (z)\). The curvature covariance in Definition 6.3 is

\(\displaystyle C_{ab, ij} (z) = - \delta_{bj} \textsf{h}_{ai} + \delta_{bi} \textsf{h}_{aj} + \delta_{aj} \textsf{h}_{bi} - \delta_{ai} \textsf{h}_{bj}, \qquad \textsf{h}_{ab} = \frac{8 z_a z_b - 2 \delta_{ab} |z|^2}{4 \pi^2 |z|^6} .\) (16.1)

Different colours have zero covariance. Wick correlations sum products of covariances over the pairings that join factors of different insertions; for instance \(S^{\text{Max}}_2 (: F_1 F_2 :, : F_3 F_4 :) = C_{13} C_{24} + C_{14} C_{23}\), and no covariance at zero separation enters. Set

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} Q_{ab, ij} & = \sum_{\alpha} : F_{ab}^{\alpha} F_{ij}^{\alpha} :, \qquad Q_{ab} = \sum_c Q_{ac, bc}, \qquad Q_{\text{tot}} = \sum_a Q_{aa},\\ T^{\text{Max}}_{ab} & = - Q_{ab} + \tfrac{1}{4} \delta_{ab} Q_{\text{tot}}, \qquad \mathcal{O}_{F^2} = \tfrac{1}{4} Q_{\text{tot}} . \end{aligned}\) (16.2)

The minus sign is fixed by the shell convention in Definition 6.8.

For the coordinate reflection \(s_a\), strict reflected positivity of \(u \in M_d^{\text{Max}}\) means

\(\displaystyle S^{\text{Max}}_2 (R^{\text{Max}} (s_a) \bar{u}, u ; s_a x, x) > 0 \qquad (x = te_a, t > 0) .\)

Write \(\textsf{L}^{\text{Max}} (K)\) for the derivative at zero of \(R^{\text{Max}} (e^{tK})\). The two free shell Ward identities, with the fluxes of Definition 6.8, are

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{P}_b^{T^{\text{Max}}} = S^{\text{Max}} ((\partial_b^{\text{Max}} u) (x_0), \boldsymbol{v}(x)), \qquad \mathcal{Y}_K^{T^{\text{Max}}} = S^{\text{Max}} ((\textsf{L}^{\text{Max}} (K) u) (x_0), \boldsymbol{v}(x)) .\) (16.3)

The next lemma gives the nondegeneracy of free correlation tests and the normalization used in the interacting comparison. The proof of Lemma 16.2 uses its strict positivity and harmonic-pole extraction to obtain detection by bounded spectators; the vector Ward test uses that detection, while the internal Ward test uses strict positivity directly, and finite-dimensionality then selects the tests in Lemmas 16.3 and 16.5.

Lemma 16.1. (Free positivity and stress)

    (i)

    The Maxwell table satisfies E1–E7, the field calculus and \(O (4)\) covariance.

    (ii)

    Every \(u \in M_d^{\text{Max}} \setminus \{0\}\) has strict reflected positivity.

    (iii)

    The centered real symmetric \(H_4\)-tensorial conserved degree-four tensors form the line \(\mathbb{R}T^{\text{Max}}\), and \(T^{\text{Max}}\) satisfies (16.3).

    (iv)

    For \(x \ne y\),

    \(\displaystyle S^{\text{Max}}_2 (\mathcal{O}_{F^2}, \mathcal{O}_{F^2} ; x, y) = \frac{3 d_{\mathfrak{g}}}{\pi^4 |x - y|^8} .\)

Proof. (i) Formula (16.1) satisfies Bianchi and \(\sum_a \partial_a C_{ab, ij} = 0\) off zero, so Wick correlations descend to the on-shell quotient. It also gives permutation symmetry, reality, units, translations and orthogonal covariance. Each differentiated contraction is homogeneous of a fixed negative degree and bounded on the unit sphere; a finite product of these bounds proves E6–E7. Differentiating the pairings proves the derivative law; inclusions retain the same polynomial. Reflection positivity E4–E5 is proved after (ii).

(ii) Kelvin inversion \(z \mapsto z^{\vee} = z / |z|^2\) makes the reflected two-point pairing of curvature jets triangular with positive pivots (16.5); strict positivity then follows from Wick's theorem. Let \(\text{Harm}_j\) be the real space of \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued homogeneous polynomial two-forms of degree \(j\) satisfying \(df = \delta f = 0\). Its Frobenius inner product identifies it with the dual of the order-\(j\) on-shell curvature jets. Use Taylor coefficients \(D^j / j\)! and the inner product summing both ordered form indices and ordered derivative indices; \(p (\partial)\) denotes contraction with this normalized derivative. For \(z \ne 0\) put \(\mathcal{R}_z =\boldsymbol{1}_4 - 2 zz^{\textsf{t}} / |z|^2\). Let \(R_p (y)\) be the covariance of \(F (y)\) with the jet probe \(p\) at zero. Then

\(\displaystyle R_p (y) = \varsigma_j |y|^{- 4} (\mathcal{R}_y \otimes \mathcal{R}_y) p (y^{\vee}), \qquad \varsigma_j = \frac{(j + 1) (j + 2) (- 2)^j}{\pi^2} .\) (16.4)

Indeed \(p (\partial) \Gamma = (- 2)^j |y|^{- 2 j} p (y) \Gamma\); induction proves this, since traces of the coefficient tensor vanish by harmonicity. In the two curls, terms containing \(\sum_a \partial_a p_{ab}\) and \(\partial_a p_{bc} + \partial_b p_{ca} + \partial_c p_{ab}\) vanish. The remaining radial derivatives use \(\sum_a y_a \partial_a p = jp\) and \(\partial_a |y|^{- 2 j - 2} = - (2 j + 2) y_a |y|^{- 2 j - 4}\), and give \(\varsigma_0 = 2 / \pi^2\), \((j + 1) \varsigma_{j + 1} = - 2 (j + 3) \varsigma_j\).

For towers \(p = (p_j)\) and \(q = (q_j)\), let \(B_z (p, q)\) be their two-site covariance at separation \(z\), with \(\mathcal{R}_z\) acting on all tensor indices of the second probe; this is the reflected jet pairing at \(\pm z / 2\). Define the triangular map \(\sigma_z\) by replacing a single order-\(j\) probe \(p\) with the tower whose order-\(k\) component is \((\varsigma_j / \varsigma_k) T_k [p (\cdot - z^{\vee})]\), \(k \leqslant j\), where \(T_k\) is the homogeneous Taylor term at zero. Its generated field is the right side of (16.4) with \(p (y^{\vee} - z^{\vee})\). Since \(D (z^{\vee}) = |z|^{- 2} \mathcal{R}_z\), its Taylor terms at \(z\) below \(j\) vanish, and its top term is \(\varsigma_j |z|^{- 2 j - 4} \mathcal{R}_z p\). Symmetry removes the entries above the diagonal. Thus

\(\displaystyle B_z (\sigma_z p, \sigma_z q) = \sum_j \frac{(j + 1) (j + 2) 2^j}{\pi^2 |z|^{2 j + 4}} \langle p_j, q_j \rangle .\) (16.5)

Evaluating the second order-\(j\) probe supplies \((- 1)^j\), since its positional derivatives are opposite to derivatives of the separation; this cancels the sign in \(\varsigma_j\). In coordinates diagonalizing this form, Wick pairing between two sites gives \(\langle X^{\mu}, X^{\nu} \rangle =\boldsymbol{1}_{\mu = \nu} \mu ! \prod_i b_i^{\mu_i}\) with \(b_i > 0\). Every polynomial uses a finite tower, proving strict positivity.

For several positive-side sites the reflected curvature covariance is positive semidefinite: spatial Fourier transformation of \(\Gamma\) gives \(e^{- |p| (t + s)} / (2| p|)\), and its two curls, with reflection of the form indices, give the sum of squares of the two transverse polarizations. Jet derivatives differentiate the same factors. Include the contractions within each positive-side word in its Fock vector; its reflected pairing is then the symmetric Fock pairing of this positive semidefinite form, so finite sums of words have nonnegative squared norm. On compact separated supports every pairing is continuous and bounded; integration gives E5 and point evaluation gives E4.

(iii) Simplicity makes every invariant linear colour form zero and every invariant bilinear form a multiple of the fixed metric. Through weight four only the unit and the quadratics \(Q\) remain, and sign changes and permutations give the basis

\(\displaystyle Z^{(1)}_{ab} = \delta_{ab} Q_{aa}, \qquad Z^{(2)}_{ab} = \delta_{ab} (Q_{\text{tot}} - 2 Q_{aa}), \qquad Z^{(3)}_{ab} = (1 - \delta_{ab}) Q_{ab}\)

of centered real symmetric tensors. Sign parity reduces their divergences to contractions of \(F \partial F\) with the free index \(b\). The equations remove \(\sum_d \partial_d F_{dc}\), and Bianchi gives \(2 \sum_{c, d} F_{cd} \partial_c F_{bd} = \sum_{c, d} F_{cd} \partial_b F_{cd}\). So the divergences lie in the plane spanned by

\(\displaystyle \sum_c : F_{bc} \partial_b F_{bc} := \tfrac{1}{2} \partial_b Q_{bb}, \qquad \sum_{c < d, c, d \ne b} : F_{cd} \partial_b F_{cd} := \tfrac{1}{4} \partial_b (Q_{\text{tot}} - 2 Q_{bb}),\)

with colour sums understood. Test the first components of the divergences of \(Z^{(1)}, Z^{(2)}, Z^{(3)}\) (columns) at \(e_1\) against \(Q_{12, 12} (0)\) and \(Q_{23, 23} (0)\) (rows), dividing by \(d_{\mathfrak{g}} / (4 \pi^2)^2\). For example \(S^{\text{Max}}_2 (Q_{11} (te_1), Q_{12, 12} (0)) = 2 d_{\mathfrak{g}} / (\pi^4 t^8)\), whose derivative gives the first entry; differentiating (16.1) gives the others:

\(\displaystyle \left( \begin{array}{ccc} - 256 & 0 & 128\\ 0 & - 512 & - 128 \end{array} \right) .\) (16.6)

Its first two columns are independent, so divergence maps the span of the \(Z^{(i)}\) onto this plane. The test of \(Z^{(3)}_{12}\) against \(Q_{13, 23}\) gives \(- 16\), and zero for \(Z^{(1)}, Z^{(2)}\), so the three tensors are independent. The kernel is the line of \(\tfrac{1}{2} Z^{(1)} - \tfrac{1}{4} Z^{(2)} + Z^{(3)} = - T^{\text{Max}}\).

To compute its charge, polarize \(T^{\text{Max}}\) between a smooth Maxwell wave \(f\) and the field \(g\) generated by a probe at \(x_0\). Replace \(\Gamma\) by \(\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\) with \(- \Delta \Gamma_{\varepsilon} = \rho_{\varepsilon}\), a smooth approximate identity. For a probe \(F_{ij}\) the divergence of \(g\) is \(\delta_{bj} \partial_i \rho_{\varepsilon} - \delta_{bi} \partial_j \rho_{\varepsilon}\). The polarized tensor is \(t_{ab} = - \sum_c (f_{ac} g_{bc} + g_{ac} f_{bc}) + \tfrac{1}{2} \delta_{ab} \sum_{i, j} f_{ij} g_{ij}\), and Bianchi gives \(\sum_a \partial_a t_{ab} = - \sum_c f_{bc} \sum_a \partial_a g_{ac}\). With the minus sign of the shell integral, \(\int \chi v_b \partial_a t_{ab} = - \int \chi v_b f_{bc} (\delta_{cj} \partial_i \rho_{\varepsilon} - \delta_{ci} \partial_j \rho_{\varepsilon})\), and a second integration by parts gives

\(\displaystyle \partial_i (v_b f_{bj}) (x_0) - \partial_j (v_b f_{bi}) (x_0) = (\text{Lie}_v \hspace{0.17em} f)_{ij} (x_0) .\)

No derivative of \(\chi\) appears at \(x_0\), and the contraction with \(Dv\) vanishes by symmetry. For \(v = K (\cdot - x_0)\) this is \(\sum_c (K_{ci} f_{cj} + K_{cj} f_{ic}) (x_0)\), the generator of the free action on curvature; for constant \(v\) it is \(v \cdot \nabla f\). Higher probes follow by differentiating in \(x_0\). In a Wick contraction, the terms in which both stress legs end at \(x_0\) contribute zero to the shell flux: dilation by \(2\) multiplies their shell integral by \(2^{3 + p - 8 - j - l} \ne 1\) (probe orders \(j, l\), weight degree \(p = 0, 1\)), while the flux is independent of the cutoff. Terms with both legs outside vanish by conservation. The remaining contractions give the derivation \(\text{Lie}_v\), proving (16.3).

(iv) At \(z = re_1\) the six unordered curvature components have diagonal covariances \(\pm (\pi^2 r^4)^{- 1}\). Since \(\mathcal{O}_{F^2} = \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{a < b, \alpha} : (F_{ab}^{\alpha})^2\):, its two-point Wick sum is \(\tfrac{1}{4} \cdot 2 \cdot 6 d_{\mathfrak{g}} / (\pi^4 r^8)\). Orthogonal covariance gives the formula for every \(z \ne 0\).\(\Box\)

By Lemma 16.1(iv), \(S^{\text{Max}}_2 (\mathcal{O}_{F^2} (x), \mathcal{O}_{F^2} (y)) = c |x - y|^{- 8}\) with \(c = 3 d_{\mathfrak{g}} / \pi^4\). The translation shell Ward identity (16.3) and the free derivative law give, for a shell around \(x\),

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{P}_b^{T^{\text{Max}}} = \partial_{x_b} (c|x - y|^{- 8}) = - 8 c \hspace{0.17em} (x_b - y_b) |x - y|^{- 10} .\)

Replacing \(T^{\text{Max}}\) by \(\lambda T^{\text{Max}}\) multiplies this flux by \(\lambda\), so the requirement that the flux be the derivative fixes both the normalization and the sign of the stress tensor. The next lemmas concern only the invariant polynomial algebra of Definition 6.3, realized by the Taylor sections of Lemma 10.2. A curvature jet of order \(j\) has weight \(j + 2\). Let \(\mathfrak{P}\) be the complex polynomial algebra on all on-shell jets, \(\mathfrak{P}_{\leqslant j}\) its subalgebra using orders at most \(j\), and \(\mathfrak{P}^G\) the invariant subalgebra. The invariant polynomials of weight at most \(d\) identify with \(M_d^{\text{Max}}\). For a spectator degree \(d\) put

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{N}_d = \{P \in \mathfrak{P}: S^{\text{Max}} (P (0), u_1 (x_1), \ldots, u_n (x_n)) = 0 \text{ for all } n, u_i \in M_d^{\text{Max}}, (0, x) \in \text{Conf}_{n + 1} \},\)

with every polynomial Wick ordered at its own site. The degree of \(P\) is unrestricted. The next result says that spectators of one bounded degree detect every invariant polynomial, however high its jet order.

Lemma 16.2. (Detection by bounded spectators) There is \(d_0 \in \mathbb{N}\) such that

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{N}_{d_0} \cap \mathfrak{P}^G = \{0\} .\) (16.7)

The proof has two stages. At a fixed jet order, finite generation and reflected positivity give a bound on the spectator degree. To make the bound independent of the jet order, a quadratic spectator isolates a highest-order jet derivative of \(P\) by its harmonic pole, and induction on weight removes that jet order.

The extraction rests on the difference between a single harmonic pole and a product of poles. In four dimensions \(K (z) = \partial_1 \partial_2 |z|^{- 2} = 8 z_1 z_2 |z|^{- 6}\) is harmonic and homogeneous of degree \(- 4\); multiplication by \(|z|^4\) leaves \(8 z_1 z_2 |z|^{- 2}\), which is not analytic at zero, and only \(|z|^6\) clears it. The product of two poles \(|z|^{- 2} \cdot |z|^{- 2} = |z|^{- 4}\) has the same degree but is already cleared by \(|z|^4\). In general a product of poles of orders \(r_1, r_2 \geqslant 2\) is cleared by \(|z|^{2 (r_1 + r_2 - 2)}\), while a harmonic pole of order \(r_1 + r_2\) needs \(|z|^{2 (r_1 + r_2 - 1)}\).

Proof of Lemma 16.2. Step 1: bounded jet order. Fix \(j\). The algebra \(\mathfrak{P}_{\leqslant j}\) is a polynomial algebra on finitely many variables, hence Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem [32, Lemma 10.31.1]. Its ideal generated by invariant polynomials of positive ordinary degree therefore has finitely many invariant, ordinary-homogeneous generators \(f_{\alpha}\). Every invariant ordinary-homogeneous polynomial is a sum of products of invariant homogeneous polynomials of weight at most some \(b_j\): write \(P = \sum a_{\alpha} f_{\alpha}\), keep only terms of total degree \(\deg P\), and replace \(a_{\alpha}\) by its gauge average, whose degree is smaller; induction on the degree concludes.

Spectators of degree \(b_j\) detect: \(\mathfrak{N}_{b_j} \cap \mathfrak{P}^G \cap \mathfrak{P}_{\leqslant j} = \{0\}\). Otherwise take its lowest nonzero ordinary-homogeneous part \(P_r\) and split a product of homogeneous factors of total ordinary degree \(r\) into distinct nearby spectator sites. Every leg of \(P\) must pair with a spectator leg, so terms with internal spectator contractions, or with higher-degree parts of \(P\), vanish. As the spectator sites coalesce, Wick's rule gives the two-site pairing of \(P_r\) with their ordinary product. Each factor has weight at most \(b_j\), so for \(P \in \mathfrak{N}_{b_j}\) this limit is zero. Choose the product sum to be the reflection of \(\overline{P_r}\); Lemma 16.1(ii) makes this pairing strictly positive, a contradiction.

Step 2: pole extraction. Put \(g = d_{\mathfrak{g}}\) and choose \(d_0 \geqslant \max \{b_g, 2 g + 5\}\). In a real colour basis write \(X_{j, \alpha} (p)\) for the jet coordinate tested by a scalar order-\(j\) probe \(p\). For scalar probes \(p, q\) of orders \(k, l\), the invariant quadratic \(\sum_{\alpha} : X_{k, \alpha} (p) X_{l, \alpha} (q)\): has weight \(k + l + 4\). Its collision with a polynomial using jets through order \(j\) has, in the coefficient where just the order-\(k\) leg contracts at order \(j\), the polynomial

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\alpha} X_{l, \alpha} (q) \sum_{\mu} C_{k, j} (p, e_{\mu} ; z) \frac{\partial P}{\partial X_{j, \alpha} (e_{\mu})},\) (16.8)

where \((e_{\mu})\) is a basis of order-\(j\) probes and \(C_{k, j}\) their differentiated curvature covariance.

Claim. If \(P \in \mathfrak{N}_{d_0}\), \(l < k\) and \(k + l + 4 \leqslant d_0\), then (16.8) lies in \(\mathfrak{N}_{d_0}\). Put \(r = 4 + k + j\). Tested against fixed exterior spectators, the term (16.8) is harmonic and homogeneous of degree \(- r\) in \(z\), so \(|z|^{2 (r - 1)}\) times it is analytic at zero. The other terms, expanded in Taylor series at each singular order, give at order \(r\) homogeneous functions that become analytic after multiplication by \(|z|^{2 (r - 2)}\): a double contraction is a product of poles of orders \(r_1, r_2 \geqslant 2\), cleared by \(|z|^{2 (r_1 + r_2 - 2)}\), and Taylor terms keep this improvement. The contraction of the order-\(l\) leg alone has order at most \(4 + l + j < r\). The claim now follows from:

\(\displaystyle \text{a harmonic $K$, homogeneous of degree $- r \leqslant - 2$, with $|z|^{2 (r - 2)} K$ analytic at $0$, vanishes.}\)

Indeed \(\Delta (|z|^{2 s} K) = 4 s (s + 1 - r) |z|^{2 s - 2} K\) in four dimensions; successive Laplacians show that \(K\) extends analytically, and negative homogeneity makes it zero. Expand the collision through an order exceeding \(8 + k + l + 2 j\). Since its correlation with the quadratic test and further bounded-degree spectators vanishes identically, comparing successive radial homogeneities makes the order-\(r\) harmonic pole zero, so (16.8) belongs to \(\mathfrak{N}_{d_0}\).

Step 3: induction on weight. Take \(k = g + 1\) and \(l = 0, \ldots, g - 1\). For each \(l\) choose a probe \(q_l\) and a jet value \(v_l\) with \(q_l (v_l) = 1\). The matrix \((X_{l, \alpha} (q_l))_{l, \alpha}\) has nonzero polynomial determinant: assigning \(v_l\) to colour \(l\) and zero to the other colours in order \(l\) makes it the identity. For \(j > g\) the polynomial (16.8) replaces an order-\(j\) jet by an order-\(l\) jet, so its weight is smaller, and the induction hypothesis makes it zero. Multiplication by the transposed cofactor matrix shows that every one-colour contraction in (16.8) vanishes. As \(z, p\) vary, the forms \(C_{k, j} (p, \cdot ; z)\) span the dual of the order-\(j\) jets: their common annihilator generates a Maxwell wave whose order-\(k\) jets vanish everywhere, a polynomial of degree below \(k\) with negative homogeneity, hence zero, and (16.4) makes the probe zero. Hence every order-\(j\) partial derivative of \(P\) vanishes. Descending from the top order removes all orders above \(g\); Step 1 with spectator bound \(b_g\) treats the rest.\(\Box\)

Let \(\mathcal{C}_d\) be the real space of complex linear tuples \(\textsf{X}_a : M_d^{\text{Max}} \to M_{d + 1}^{\text{Max}}\) that commute with conjugation, kill the unit, are vector-covariant under \(H_4\), and send \(M_e^{\text{Max}}\) into \(M_{e + 1}^{\text{Max}}\) for \(e \leqslant d\). For \(\textsf{X} \in \mathcal{C}_d\) the Ward sum is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{E}_b (\textsf{X} ; \boldsymbol{u}, x) = \sum_i S_n^{\text{Max}} (u_1, \ldots, \textsf{X}_b u_i, \ldots, u_n ; x) .\)

Finitely many tests on lines determine its kernel.

Lemma 16.3. (Finite vector Ward tests) For all sufficiently large \(d\), finitely many positional derivatives of \(\mathcal{E}_b (\textsf{X} ; \boldsymbol{u}, x)\), with \(u_i \in M_d^{\text{Max}}\) and \(x\) on a coordinate axis through the origin perpendicular to \(e_b\), define a real linear map \(\mathcal{T}_d\) with

\(\displaystyle \ker \mathcal{T}_d =\mathbb{R} \partial^{\text{Max}} \quad \text{in } \mathcal{C}_d .\) (16.9)

Proof. Take \(d \geqslant d_0\) from Lemma 16.2. Suppose all the stated jets of the Ward sums of \(\textsf{X}\) vanish. The free moments are translation invariant, including after a label is changed, so their Ward sums and positional jets have the same values on any parallel affine line as on the corresponding axis through zero.

Step 1: continuation off the lines. Free correlations are real analytic off the diagonals by (16.1), so every Ward sum vanishes near each testing line. For a separated configuration choose distinct ordered line coordinates respecting the strict order of its coordinates in that axis. The straight segment to the line configuration stays separated: strict inequalities persist, and coordinate ties are separated by the line coordinates. Analytic continuation along it gives \(\mathcal{E}_b = 0\) on \(\text{Conf}_n\).

Step 2: weight four. Let \(\textsf{J}_j\) be the scalar on-shell order-\(j\) jet space. For \(p \in \textsf{J}_0^{\ast}\) put \(U (p) = \sum_{\alpha} : X_{0, \alpha} (p)^2\):. Simplicity, the filtration and inversion parity force \(\textsf{X}_b U (p)\) to be a colour contraction of one order-zero and one order-one jet, represented by a linear map \(A_p : \textsf{J}_0 \to \textsf{J}_1^{\ast}\). In the Ward identity of \(U (p), U (q), U (w)\) let the first two points coalesce along \(tu\) and multiply by \(t^5\). The term with \(\textsf{X}_b\) at the distant point tends to zero, and the two remaining triangular Wick contractions give, for every \(X \in \textsf{J}_0\) and with \(C_{1, 0}\) taken in one colour,

\(\displaystyle C_{1, 0} (A_p X, q ; u) \hspace{0.17em} q (X) = C_{1, 0} (A_q X, p ; u) \hspace{0.17em} p (X) .\)

Arbitrary \(X\) are reached by choosing the distant probe \(w\) through the invertible order-zero covariance (16.4). If \(p (X) = 0\), choose \(q (X) \ne 0\); varying \(q, u\) gives \(A_p X = 0\). Thus \(A_p X = p (X) f_b p\), and the same identity shows that \(f_b : \textsf{J}_0^{\ast} \to \textsf{J}_1^{\ast}\) is real linear with \(C_{1, 0} (f_b p, q ; u) = C_{1, 0} (f_b q, p ; u)\).

Calculation of \(f_b\). Write \(e^{ij}\) for the curvature coordinate and \(\partial_h e^{ij}\) for its first jet. For \(f_1 e^{12}\), sign parity permits only \(\partial_1 e^{12}, \partial_3 e^{23}, \partial_4 e^{24}\); interchange of axes \(3, 4\) and \(\sum_i \partial_i e^{i 2} = 0\) reduce their span to \(\mathbb{R} \partial_1 e^{12}\). For \(f_1 e^{23}\) the candidates \(\partial_1 e^{23}, \partial_2 e^{13}, \partial_3 e^{12}\) reduce, by antisymmetry in axes \(2, 3\) and Bianchi, to \(\mathbb{R} \partial_1 e^{23}\). Permutations leave two constants, which agree because \(C_{1, 0} (\partial_1 e^{12}, e^{23} ; e_1 + e_3) = - 1 / (2 \pi^2) \ne 0\) by (16.1). Therefore \(f_b = c_{\text{jet}} \partial_b\), and since \(\partial_b U (p)\) has two identical terms, \(\textsf{X}_b = c \hspace{0.17em} \partial^{\text{Max}}_b\) on weight four with \(c = c_{\text{jet}} / 2\).

Step 3: higher weights. Set \(\textsf{X}^{\circ}_b = \textsf{X}_b - c \hspace{0.17em} \partial^{\text{Max}}_b\). Translation invariance makes its Ward sums zero, and it kills every free stress component. Insert into such a sum a free stress shell for the dilation field \(v (y) = y - x_0\), surrounding only the distinguished point. The term where \(\textsf{X}^{\circ}_b\) acts on the stress is zero. The other shell terms are given by the polarized calculation in Lemma 16.1, now with this \(v\); the extra derivative of \(v\) contracts with the trace of the free stress, which is zero by (16.2). The local Lie derivative of a two-form under dilation is \(y \cdot \nabla + 2\), and on jets it gives the weight operator \(\textsf{D}^{\text{wt}} X_{j, \alpha} = (j + 2) X_{j, \alpha}\). Subtracting the Ward sum with distinguished label \(\textsf{D}^{\text{wt}} u\) shows \([\textsf{D}^{\text{wt}}, \textsf{X}^{\circ}_b] u \in \mathfrak{N}_d\), and Lemma 16.2 makes it zero. So \(\textsf{X}^{\circ}_b\) preserves each weight. Inversion acts there by \((- 1)^{\text{weight}}\), whereas vector covariance makes \(\textsf{X}^{\circ}_b\) odd under inversion; hence \(\textsf{X}^{\circ}_b = 0\).

Step 4: finite selection. Conversely translation invariance annihilates every test on \(\partial^{\text{Max}}\). The real space \(\mathcal{C}_d\) is finite-dimensional; successively choose a test nonzero on the current kernel modulo this line. Each choice lowers its dimension, so finitely many tests have precisely the asserted kernel.\(\Box\)

The internal tests rest on one elementary fact about the plane generators \(K_{ab} = E_{ba} - E_{ab}\) of \(\mathfrak{so} (4)\). Call a coordinate quarter turn the rotation \(e_a \mapsto e_b\), \(e_b \mapsto - e_a\) in one coordinate plane.

Lemma 16.4. (Alternating planes) Let \(\beta : \mathfrak{so} (4) \times \mathfrak{so} (4) \to V\) be real bilinear and alternating, with \(\beta (hKh^{- 1}, hLh^{- 1}) = \beta (K, L)\) for every coordinate quarter turn \(h\). Then \(\beta = 0\).

Proof. The quarter turns generate the determinant-one signed permutations: they project onto the transpositions of \(S_4\), and squares of quarter turns give the diagonal sign changes with two minus signs. Conjugation by a diagonal sign matrix \(s\) multiplies \(K_{ab}\) by \(s_a s_b\); conjugation by a permutation matrix permutes the planes. Overlapping planes: \(s = \text{diag} (1, 1, - 1, - 1)\) fixes \(K_{12}\) and reverses the sign of \(K_{13}\), so \(\beta (K_{12}, K_{13}) = - \beta (K_{12}, K_{13}) = 0\). Disjoint planes: the even permutation \((13) (24)\) interchanges \(K_{12}\) and \(K_{34}\), so \(\beta (K_{12}, K_{34}) = \beta (K_{34}, K_{12}) = - \beta (K_{12}, K_{34}) = 0\). Every pair of distinct planes is carried to one of these two pairs by a signed permutation of determinant one, and \(\beta (K, K) = 0\); bilinearity gives \(\beta = 0\).\(\Box\)

Let \(\mathcal{B}_d\) consist of alternating real-bilinear families \(\Delta : \mathfrak{so} (4)^2 \to \text{End}_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}\) which commute with conjugation, kill the unit, preserve every lower-degree subspace, and satisfy

\(\displaystyle R^{\text{Max}} (h) \Delta (K, L) = \Delta (hKh^{- 1}, hLh^{- 1}) R^{\text{Max}} (h) \qquad (h \in H_4, \det h = 1) .\)

The next result removes the defect in the rotation commutator.

Lemma 16.5. (Finite internal Ward tests) For every \(d \geqslant 4\), finitely many sums

\(\displaystyle \sum_i S_n^{\text{Max}} (u_1, \ldots, \Delta (K, L) u_i, \ldots, u_n ; x), \qquad u_i \in M_d^{\text{Max}}, \quad x \in \text{Conf}_n,\) (16.10)

separate \(\mathcal{B}_d\).

Proof. Suppose all these sums vanish and fix \(\textsf{Z} = \Delta (K, L)\). The arity-one Ward condition gives \(S_1^{\text{Max}} (\textsf{Z} u) = 0\). Since \(\textsf{Z}\) preserves degree and kills the unit, this excludes a unit component in the image of a centered weight-four field; the centered weight-four space is invariant. On that space let \(b_x (u, v) = S_2^{\text{Max}} (u, v ; x, 0)\). The order-zero Kelvin formula and Wick's rule give \(b_x (u, v) = b_y (R^{\text{Max}} (\mathcal{R}_y \mathcal{R}_x) u, v)\) for \(|x| = |y| \ne 0\). Both \(\textsf{Z}\) and the orthogonal action preserve this space. Applying \(b_x (\textsf{Z} u, v) + b_x (u, \textsf{Z} v) = 0\) at \(x\) and \(y\), and nondegeneracy of \(b_y\), shows that \(\textsf{Z}\) commutes with \(R^{\text{Max}} (\mathcal{R}_y \mathcal{R}_x)\), in particular with every coordinate quarter turn. With the signed covariance of \(\Delta\), the function \((K, L) \mapsto \Delta (K, L) u\) satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma 16.4 for each weight-four \(u\), so \(\textsf{Z}\) vanishes on weight four, including the stress.

Insert a free angular stress shell around the distinguished point only, as in Step 3 of the proof of Lemma 16.3. Subtracting the Ward sum with the angularly differentiated label gives \([\textsf{L}^{\text{Max}} (M), \textsf{Z}] u \in \mathfrak{N}_d\). This polynomial has weight at most \(d\), so Lemma 16.1(ii) makes it zero. Hence \(\textsf{Z}\) commutes with all infinitesimal rotations, and differentiating \(e^{t \textsf{L} (M)} \textsf{Z} - \textsf{Z} e^{t \textsf{L} (M)}\) gives commutation with coordinate quarter turns in every degree. Lemma 16.4 again gives \(\Delta = 0\). Finite-dimensionality selects a finite separating subfamily by successively reducing the common kernel.\(\Box\)

\(\displaystyle d_j = D^{- 2 j} \|T^j v\|, \qquad M_v = \sup_{j \geqslant 1} d_j < \infty .\)

If \(\vartheta_j U = \varepsilon_j U\) with \(\varepsilon_j \in \{1, - 1\}\), then, for \(x^{\mu} = 0\) and \(t\) separating the words,

\(\displaystyle S \left( U^{\ast} \hspace{0.17em} \tau_{te_{\mu} + x} U \right) = \varepsilon_{\mu} \int e^{- tE + ip \cdot x} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^U (E, p) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Opposite reflection signs compare coordinate spectra. Fix a set of axes \(\mathcal{A} \subset \{1, 2, 3, 4\}\) and a finite word combination \(U\) whose offsets have zero coordinates in \(\mathcal{A}\), with \(\vartheta_j U = \varepsilon_j U\) for \(j \in \mathcal{A}\), and assume that both signs occur. For even nonnegative \(f_j \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R})\) set \(f_{j, v} (p) = v^{- 1} f_j (p / v)\). Throughout, \(\lambda\) ranges over \(e^{\mathbb{N}}\).

Lemma 16.13. (Opposite reflection signs) For every \(i \in \mathcal{A}\), \(q < 0\), \(\beta_j \leqslant 0\) and \(\delta > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \int_{E \leqslant 1} \frac{\prod_{j \in \mathcal{A} \setminus \{i\}} f_{j, \lambda^{\beta_j}} (p_j)}{E + \lambda^q} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_i^U = O_{\delta} (\lambda^{\delta}) .\)

Constants may depend on \(U, \mathcal{A}, q, \beta, f, \delta\).

Tested against a product of even profiles, the reflected correlation of \(U\) has a positive spectral representation in each time axis \(i \in \mathcal{A}\), with sign \(\varepsilon_i\). For two axes of opposite parity, these representations of the same number differ by a sign; after the annular corrections below, this gives the transfer inequality (16.33). A narrow momentum profile is replaced by an annular profile with the same low moments; the residual has a nonnegative Laplace response, so the low-energy response in axis \(i\) is bounded by two responses, one with each of the profiles in \(i\) and in the next axis \(j\) replaced by its annular correction, which the moment bounds control. Each transfer replaces one narrow profile by one whose width is bounded below; once every axis has been treated, the moment bound applies directly.

Proof. Moments. For a placement of margin \(r > 0\), E7 bounds the \(2 m\)th transverse derivative of the reflected-pair correlation by \(Cr^{- D}\). Its centered difference has spectral integrand \([2 \sin (tp_j / 2) / t]^{2 m} e^{- 2 uE}\), and the integral remainder formula and Fatou give

\(\displaystyle \int e^{- 2 uE} |p_j |^{2 m} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^U \leqslant Cr^{- D} .\) (16.29)

Extra heat supplies energy powers, so for every damping \(u\) as in (16.27) and \(m \in \mathbb{N}\),

\(\displaystyle \int e^{- 2 uE} (1 + E + |p|)^m \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^U < \infty \hspace{0.17em} .\) (16.30)

For offsets perpendicular to \(\mu\) take \(u = t / 2\), \(r \asymp t\); integrating \(Ct^{- D}\) against \(t^D \hspace{0.17em} dt\) on \((0, 1)\) gives, after enlarging \(D\), \(\int (1 + E)^{- D} |p_j |^{2 m} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^U < \infty\).

Annular correction. Choose even \(\sigma \geqslant 0\) of integral one supported in \(1 \leqslant |v| \leqslant 2\). Let \(L_j\), \(0 \leqslant j < m\), be the Lagrange polynomials of degree \(m - 1\) with \(L_j ((i + 2)^2) = \delta_{ij}\). For radius \(\varrho > 0\) define

\(\displaystyle \int \mathcal{C}_{m, \varrho} f (q) g (q) \hspace{0.17em} dq = \int \sigma (v) \int f (p) \sum_{j < m} L_j ((p / (\varrho v))^2) g (\varrho v (j + 2)) \hspace{0.17em} dp \hspace{0.17em} dv.\)

Since \(\sum_j L_j (x) (j + 2)^{2 i} = x^i\) for \(i < m\), the residual \(f -\mathcal{C}_{m, \varrho} f\) has vanishing moments \(\int p^{2 i} (\cdot) \hspace{0.17em} dp\) for \(i < m\), hence \(2 m\) vanishing moments when \(f\) is even; the correction is an annular profile whose coefficients contain \(\varrho^{- 2 r}\). Put \(\text{Lap} \hspace{0.17em} f (E) = \int e^{- E |t|} \hat{f}_+ (t) \hspace{0.17em} dt\). Interpolating \((p^2 + E^2)^{- 1}\) gives the residual response

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{R}_{m, \varrho} f (E) = 2 E \int \frac{f (p)}{p^2 + E^2} \int \sigma (v) \prod_{j < m} \frac{(\varrho v (j + 2))^2 - p^2}{(\varrho v (j + 2))^2 + E^2} \hspace{0.17em} dv \hspace{0.17em} dp = \text{Lap} (f -\mathcal{C}_{m, \varrho} f) (E) .\) (16.31)

Its value at \(E = 0\) is \(2 \pi f (0)\), and for \(f \geqslant 0\) supported in \([- \varrho, \varrho]\) all factors lie in \([0, 1]\). Let \(f \geqslant 0\) be even, supported in \([- 1, 1]\) and equal to one on \([- \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}]\), and let \(0 < v \leqslant 1 \leqslant \varrho\). Then

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} \frac{c_m}{E + v} & \leqslant \mathcal{R}_{m, \varrho} f_v (E) \leqslant 2 \pi \|f_v \|_{\infty} & & (0 \leqslant E \leqslant 1),\\ \mathcal{R}_{m, \varrho} f_v (E) & \leqslant C (\varrho / E)^D & & (E \geqslant 1, m \geqslant D / 2), \end{array}\)

with \(C = C (m, D, \|f\|_{L^1})\): the upper bounds follow from \(2 E \int (p^2 + E^2)^{- 1} dp = 2 \pi\) and from bounding the product by \(\prod_j (2 \varrho (j + 2) / E)^2\); for the lower bound the product is at least \(2^{- m} (1 + E)^{- 2 m}\) for \(|p| \leqslant v\), and one integrates over \(0 < p < vE / (2 (E + v))\). With the moment bound, for \(\varrho \geqslant 1\), \(\text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} f_v \subset [- \varrho, \varrho]\) and \(m \geqslant D / 2\) chosen after the moment exponent \(D\),

\(\displaystyle \int \mathcal{R}_{m, \varrho} f_v (E) |p_j |^{2 r} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^U \leqslant C (v^{- 1} + \varrho^D) .\) (16.32)

Transfer between two axes. For \(i, j \in \mathcal{A}\) write \(\text{Resp}_i (g) = \int \text{Lap} \hspace{0.17em} g_i (E) \prod_{k \in \mathcal{A} \setminus \{i\}} g_k (p_k) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_i^U\). For \(\mathcal{A}\) containing all axes, the same product test gives

\(\displaystyle (2 \pi)^{- 3} \int S (U^{\ast} \tau_x U) \prod_k \widehat{g_k}_+ (x_k) \hspace{0.17em} dx = \varepsilon_i \text{Resp}_i (g) = \varepsilon_j \text{Resp}_j (g),\)

since \(\int e^{ipx} \hat{g}_+ (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx = 2 \pi g (p)\) for even profiles, while the time integral is \(\text{Lap} \hspace{0.17em} g (E)\); both temporal profiles have the zero moments required at collisions. For smaller \(\mathcal{A}\), put \(g_k (p) = h (p / T)\) in omitted axes, with \(h \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R})\) even and equal to one near zero, and let \(T \to \infty\) by (16.32). Hence \(\text{Resp}_i (g) = - \text{Resp}_j (g)\) when \(\varepsilon_i = - \varepsilon_j\). Subtract annular corrections \(a_i, a_j\) from the two profiles in the current axes; positivity of the residual responses gives

\(\displaystyle \text{Resp}_i (g_i - a_i ; g_{\ne i}) \leqslant \text{Resp}_i (g_i - a_i ; g_j \mapsto a_j) + \text{Resp}_j (g_j - a_j ; g_i \mapsto a_i),\) (16.33)

after expanding the signed identity and discarding \(\text{Resp}_j (g_j - a_j ; g_{\ne j}) \geqslant 0\).

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \int \mathcal{R}_{m, R_0 \lambda^{\eta}} f_{i_s, \lambda^{\omega_{i_s}}} (E) \prod_{j \in \mathcal{A} \setminus \{i_s \}} f_{j, \lambda^{\omega_j}} (p_j) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{i_s}^U \leqslant C \lambda^{\delta},\) (Hs)

Proof. Sign estimates. Diagonal tensor: moment-subtracted extension. This is a functional on products of one-dimensional tests, not a positive extension of a coincident correlation. \(\displaystyle R^0_{ab, cd} (f) = \int C_{ab, cd} (x) \left[ \prod_j f_j (x_j) - \prod_j \Pi_{D_{\text{col}}} f_j (x_j) \right] dx.\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j, k \ne \mu} p_j p_k \hat{R}_{k \mu, j \mu} = p_{\mu}^2 \hat{R}_{\mu \mu, \mu \mu} + \sum_{j \ne \mu} p_j F_{\mu, j \mu} - p_{\mu} F_{\mu, \mu \mu},\)

16.5Ward identities and rotations

Write \(\boldsymbol{q}= (A_i (x_i))_i\) and define the angular sum

\(\displaystyle \text{Ang}_K [\textsf{Y}, \textsf{p}] (\boldsymbol{q}; x) = \sum_i \left[ S (\ldots, \textsf{Y}_K A_i, \ldots ; x) + \sum_b (Kx_i)_b S (\ldots, \textsf{p}_b A_i, \ldots ; x) \right] .\) (16.38)

It is the total angular flux of the raw charges of Lemma 16.11. Fix distinct axes \(a, b\), a line axis \(l\), and the exponents \(\omega_j\), profile \(\psi\) and weights \(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\) of Section 16.4. For a word \(B\) on \(\mathbb{R}e_l\) projected to odd parity in \(a, b\) and even parity in the other axes, Lemma 16.14 gives the sign estimates (16.35), the diagonal bound (16.36), the crude bound \(I_{\mu} (J_{\mu \mu} ; 0) = O (\lambda^{900})\), and \(O_M (\lambda^{- M})\) from the region \(X_{\lambda}\). These estimates use coordinate reflection positivity, signed permutations and conservation only; no continuous rotation enters them, and they precede the phase decomposition of Section 17, which uses the rotations obtained here.

The next lemma removes the angular boundary at infinity before continuous rotations are known. Its mechanism is as follows. The shell identities and conservation write the angular sum as the integral of the conserved tensor against the derivative of a vector field equal to \(Ky\) on a large box; the sum does not depend on the box, so it suffices that this integral tends to zero. No decay of correlations is assumed, so the smallness must come from positivity. The configuration lies on the axis \(\mathbb{R}e_l\). A coordinate reflection in an axis other than \(l\) fixes every point of this line, and the reflection in \(l\) maps it to itself; so reflecting the integration variable replaces the spectator word by its parity projections, which are again combinations of words on the line, odd in \(a, b\) and even in the other axes. Such a word has positive spectral representations of opposite signs in two coordinate time axes, and Lemma 16.13 turns this into a bound on its low-energy spectral mass; the diagonal components of the tensor are controlled by conservation instead (Lemma 16.14). The box has the large anisotropic extents \(\lambda^{- \omega_j}\) of Section 16.4. In the terms that survive two exact cancellations, differentiating the cutoff in the time axis \(\mu\) leaves a profile of bounded \(L^1\) norm supported where \(|y_{\mu} | \geqslant \lambda^{- \omega_{\mu}}\), whose Laplace transform gains the factor \(\lambda^{\omega_{\mu}} / (E + \lambda^{\omega_{\mu}})\). On the momentum side the cutoff is concentrated where \(|p_j | \lesssim \lambda^{\omega_j}\); the windows \(|p_j | \leqslant w_j = \lambda^{1 + \omega_j}\) are wider by the factor \(\lambda\), so outside them every power of \(\lambda\) is gained. The exponents are chosen so that every term is \(O (\lambda^{- 101})\) before the prefactor \(\lambda^{100}\), the size of \(|Ky|\) on the box.

Lemma 16.15. (Collinear angular identity) For every separated configuration \(x\) with \(x_i \in \mathbb{R}e_l\) for one coordinate axis \(l\), every field list \(\boldsymbol{q}\) and every \(K \in \mathfrak{so} (4)\), the raw charges satisfy

\(\displaystyle \text{Ang}_K [\textsf{Y}, \textsf{p}] (\boldsymbol{q}; x) = 0.\) (16.39)

Proof. It suffices to treat \(K = E_{ba} - E_{ab}\), \(a \ne b\), for which \((Ky)_b = y_a\), \((Ky)_a = - y_b\) and the other components vanish. Choose even \(\eta \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R})\) equal to one on \([- 1, 1]\) and put \(\ell (t) = t \eta (t)\). Set

\(\displaystyle f_{ab} (y) = \lambda^{100} \ell (\lambda^{- 100} y_a) \prod_{j \ne a} \ell' (\lambda^{\omega_j} y_j), \qquad V_b = f_{ab}, \quad V_a = - f_{ba}, \quad V_j = 0 (j \ne a, b) .\)

The vector field \(V\) equals \(Ky\) on the box \(|y_j | \leqslant \lambda^{- \omega_j}\) for every \(j\), hence near every insertion for large \(\lambda\). Choose disjoint shell cutoffs \(\chi_i\) equal to one near the respective insertions and put \(U (y) = \sum_i \chi_i (y) Ky\). For large \(\lambda\), \(V - U\) vanishes near every insertion. Its stress contraction has compact support away from the insertion points, so integration by parts and conservation make it zero. In the contraction with \(U\), symmetry of \(J\) cancels the terms \(\chi_i K_{c \mu}\). Writing \(Ky = K (y - x_i) + Kx_i\) in the remaining terms gives the angular and positional shell responses of Lemma 16.11. Thus

\(\displaystyle \text{Ang}_K = - \int \sum_{\mu, c} \partial_{\mu} V_c \hspace{0.17em} S (J_{\mu c} (y), \boldsymbol{q}) \hspace{0.17em} dy.\)

Two exact cancellations. Only the columns \(c = a, b\) occur, since \(V_c = 0\) otherwise. Since \(\omega_a = \omega_b\), both \(\partial_a f_{ab}\) and \(\partial_b f_{ba}\) equal \(\prod_j \ell' (\lambda^{\omega_j} y_j)\), and \(J_{ab} = J_{ba}\); so the term \(\mu = a\) of the column \(b\) cancels the term \(\mu = b\) of the column \(a\):

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\mu, c} \partial_{\mu} V_c \hspace{0.17em} J_{\mu c} = \sum_{\mu \ne a} \partial_{\mu} f_{ab} \hspace{0.17em} J_{\mu b} - \sum_{\mu \ne b} \partial_{\mu} f_{ba} \hspace{0.17em} J_{\mu a} \hspace{0.17em} .\) (16.40)

These are identities for every \(\lambda\); no estimate enters them. The second sum is the first with \(a, b\) exchanged, and \(\omega_a = \omega_b\), so the bounds below for the first sum apply to it; this includes its diagonal component \(J_{aa}\). Since the angular sum is independent of \(\lambda\), it suffices to show that each term \(\mu \ne a\) of the first sum is \(O (\lambda^{- 101})\) before the prefactor \(\lambda^{100}\).

Spectral form of one term. Fix \(\mu \ne a\). The spectral readout uses the reversed conjugate of the spectator word, whose adjoint recovers the original word. Apply the commuting reflection-sign projections to the entire word, positions and labels together. They produce finitely many collinear word combinations, including when reflection reverses the line. Choose one \(u_0 > 0\) placing every word of every component and the stress insertion strictly in the positive \(\mu\)-half-space. For sufficiently large \(\lambda\), the temporal support gap \(\lambda^{- \omega_{\mu}} > 2 u_0\) holds. The positive polarization measures have finite \(e^{- 2 u_0 E}\) moments by (16.30). This damping, the time support gap and the integrability of the time and transverse profiles give an absolutely integrable product and justify Fubini for each sign component.

For a component \(B\) of \(\mu\)-reflection sign \(\varepsilon_{\mu}\), set \(P (y) = \lambda^{- 100} \partial_{\mu} f_{ab} (y) = \prod_j P_j (y_j)\) and \(\phi_j (p) = (2 \pi)^{- 1} \int e^{ipy} P_j (y) \hspace{0.17em} dy\) for \(j \ne \mu\). Put \(\beta = \varepsilon_{\mu} (- 1)^{1 +\boldsymbol{1}_{\mu = b}}\) and \(\sigma_{\beta} (t) = \beta\) for \(t < 0\), \(1\) for \(t \geqslant 0\). The signed cross-slice identity is

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_{\mu} \int P (y) S (B^{\ast} \hspace{0.17em} \tau_y J_{\mu b}) \hspace{0.17em} dy = (2 \pi)^3 \int \hspace{-0.17em} \left( \int \sigma_{\beta} (t) e^{- |t|E} P_{\mu} (t) \hspace{0.17em} dt \right) \prod_{j \ne \mu} \phi_j (p_j) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\mu}^{B, J_{\mu b}} (E, p) .\)

Here the measure is the ordered polarization of the coordinate spectral measures (16.27); it lives on \(\{E \geqslant 0\}\), including \(E = 0\). On the positive-time side the gap leaves a heat interval of length \(|t| - 2 u_0\) between the placed vectors; reflection of both clusters gives the negative-time side with sign \(\beta\). The factor \((2 \pi)^3\) is from the three transverse Fourier inversions. A measure-preserving reflection now cancels every nonmatching component: the combined sign is \(\varepsilon_k (- 1)^{\boldsymbol{1}_{k = a} +\boldsymbol{1}_{k = b}}\). The only surviving projection is odd in \(a, b\) and even in the other axes. The hypotheses of Lemma 16.14 hold: \(B\) is a combination of words on \(\mathbb{R}e_l\), odd in \(a, b\) and even in the other axes, and \(J\) is real, symmetric, \(H_4\)-tensorial and conserved. The differentiated profile is supported where \(|y_{\mu} | \geqslant \lambda^{- \omega_{\mu}}\), so its Laplace transform has the factor \(e^{- E / \lambda^{\omega_{\mu}}} \leqslant \lambda^{\omega_{\mu}} / (E + \lambda^{\omega_{\mu}})\). For each of the three spatial factors \(f_j = \ell\) or \(\ell'\), Fourier scaling gives, on \(|p_j | \leqslant w_j\),

\(\displaystyle | \widehat{f_j (\lambda^{\omega_j} \hspace{0.17em} \cdot \hspace{0.17em})} (p_j) | \leqslant \lambda^{- \omega_j} \|f_j \|_{L^1} \leqslant C \lambda w_j^{- 1} \psi (p_j / w_j) .\)

Their product gives \(\lambda^3 \mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\). For an off-diagonal component, (16.28) bounds the integral over \(E \leqslant 1\) and the windows by \(C \lambda^{3 + \omega_{\mu}} [I_{\mu} (B ; - 3000) I_{\mu} (J_{\mu b} ; \omega_{\mu})]^{1 / 2}\), with \(\delta = 1\). For \(\mu = b\) split at \(E = \lambda^{- 3000}\). Above it use \(e^{- E / \lambda^{- 100}} \leqslant 4 [W / (E + W)]^2\) and (16.36); below it, where \(E = 0\) lies, \(E + \lambda^{- 3000} \leqslant 2 \lambda^{- 3000}\) supplies \(\lambda^{- 1500}\) in Cauchy–Schwarz, and the crude bound \(I_{\mu} (J_{\mu \mu} ; 0) = O (\lambda^{900})\) completes the estimate.

To pass from lines to arbitrary configurations, the rotation argument needs analyticity of a vector-valued function, obtained from its Gram kernel.

Lemma 16.16. (Analyticity from a stationary Gram kernel) Let \(v : \mathbb{R} \to \mathcal{H}\) be a smooth curve in a Hilbert space with \(\langle v (s), v (s') \rangle = A (s' - s)\), and suppose that the derivatives of \(A\) at zero coincide with those of a function analytic at zero. Then \(v\) is real analytic at every point of \(\mathbb{R}\).

Proof. Differentiating the Gram identity gives \(\langle v^{(m)} (s), v^{(k)} (s') \rangle = (- 1)^m A^{(k + m)} (s' - s)\). Let \(\rho > 0\) be smaller than the radius of the analytic function; Cauchy estimates give \(|A^{(2 m)} (0) | \leqslant C \rho^{- 2 m} (2 m)\)!. Hence

\(\displaystyle \|v^{(m)} (s)\|^2 = |A^{(2 m)} (0) | \leqslant C \rho^{- 2 m} (2 m) ! \leqslant C (2 / \rho)^{2 m} (m!)^2\)

for every \(s\), using \((2 m) ! \leqslant 4^m (m!)^2\). Taylor's remainder in the Hilbert space then tends to zero on the interval of radius \(\rho / 2\) around every point.\(\Box\)

For a finite-dimensional rotation action write \(\textsf{L}_d (K) = \left. \frac{d}{dt} R_d (e^{tK}) \right|_{t = 0}\) for its generator. The shell charges are now identified without assuming rotation covariance; the proof fixes their common scale and then constructs an orthogonal-group action.

Lemma 16.17. (Rotation and charge identification) There are \(c_{\star} \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}\) and continuous complex linear \(O (4)\) representations \(R_d\) extending \(H_4\), fixing units and commuting with conjugation and inclusions, with

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} \textsf{p}_b = c_{\star} \partial_b, \qquad \textsf{Y}_K = c_{\star} \textsf{L}_d (K), \qquad R_{d + 1} (h) \partial_a = \sum_i h_{ia} \partial_i R_d (h),\\ S_n (R_{d_i} (h) A_i ; hx) = S_n (\boldsymbol{A}; x) \qquad (h \in O (4)) . \end{array}\)

For every \(d\), an isomorphism \(\mathcal{V}_d : M_d^{\text{Max}} \to F_d\) intertwines the free and interacting \(O (4)\) actions.

Proof. We first identify the translation charge up to one common scalar. After this normalization the angular charge satisfies the derivative commutator needed to extend the collinear identity. We then prove that identity on all separated configurations, remove the bracket defect by finite free tests, and construct an intertwiner with the free rotation action.

Step 1: shell commutators. Fix a shell cutoff and move its distinguished point. Differentiating the two shell identities of Lemma 16.11 under the compact integral gives

\(\displaystyle \partial_b \textsf{p}_a = \textsf{p}_a \partial_b, \qquad \partial_b \textsf{Y}_K + \sum_i K_{ib} \textsf{p}_i = \textsf{Y}_K \partial_b,\) (16.41)

the second term coming from the derivative of \(K (y - x_0)\). Testing the unit, applying conjugation or a signed permutation, and including a field show that the charges kill units, are real, have vector or angular covariance, and respect inclusions; injectivity of responses turns these tested identities into field identities.

Step 2: the common normalization. Translate a configuration on \(\mathbb{R}e_l\) by \(e_l\) in (16.39), with \(K = E_{bl} - E_{lb}\); subtraction gives \(\sum_i S (\ldots, \textsf{p}_b A_i, \ldots ; x) = 0\) for \(b \ne l\), and (16.41) gives every positional jet of these identities. Translation invariance gives the same identities for \(\partial\). Apply the finite free vector tests (16.9) to the transported mixed vector maps; their test matrices converge by (16.24). Adjoining a real functional nonzero on \(\partial^{\text{Max}}\), the free matrix becomes injective and stays so under small perturbations, so the mixed kernel has dimension at most one. It contains \(\textsf{p}\) and \(\partial\), so \(\textsf{p} = c_d \partial\) in every large degree; inclusions and nonzero free differentiation make the constants equal and give the identity in lower degrees. Rescale the two shell identities by \(r_k / c_k\) and \(1 / c_k\); changing variables \(y = r_k z\) and using (16.24) gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{r_k}{c_k} (\textsf{U}_{k, d + 1}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} \textsf{p}_a \textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}} \to \partial_a^{\text{Max}}, \qquad c_k^{- 1} (\textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} \textsf{Y}_K \textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}} \to \textsf{L}_d^{\text{Max}} (K),\) (16.42)

since finite separating tests recover operator limits from the shell integrals. In particular \(c_{\star} = c_d \ne 0\) and \(c_k \to c_{\star}\). Put \(\textsf{D}_K = c_{\star}^{- 1} \textsf{Y}_K\); then (16.41) reads \(\partial_b \textsf{D}_K + \sum_i K_{ib} \partial_i = \textsf{D}_K \partial_b\).

Step 3: analyticity of one-insertion vectors. By this commutator, every positional derivative of an angular sum is again an angular sum, with differentiated labels; so all jets of the collinear identity vanish on a line. In a coordinate plane write \(G_{u, v} (t, s) = S (u (te_a + se_b), v (0))\), \(\varrho = \sqrt{t^2 + s^2}\) and \(\theta = \arctan (s / t)\) for \(t > 0\), and \(G^{\text{rot}}_{u, v} (t, s) = S ((e^{- \theta \textsf{D}_K} u) (\varrho e_a), (e^{- \theta \textsf{D}_K} v) (0))\). For positive separation the axial function is a matrix coefficient of \(e^{- tH_a^{\text{OS}}}\) in \(\mathcal{H}_a^{\text{OS}}\), after fixed positive-time damping of its end vectors, hence analytic by Lemma 16.12 and spectral calculus; so \(G^{\text{rot}}\) is analytic. The angular equation holds for \(G^{\text{rot}}\), and \(\mathcal{F}= G - G^{\text{rot}}\) vanishes on the axis. Its angular jets give the recurrence

\(\displaystyle t \partial_s^{m + 1} \mathcal{F}_{u, v} (t, 0) = - \partial_s^m (\mathcal{F}_{\textsf{D}_K u, v} +\mathcal{F}_{u, \textsf{D}_K v}) (t, 0) + m \partial_t \partial_s^{m - 1} \mathcal{F}_{u, v} (t, 0),\)

the last term omitted for \(m = 0\); induction, for all labels at once, makes every transverse Taylor coefficient of \(\mathcal{F}\) zero. In the reflection Hilbert space of axis \(a\), fix \(x\) with \(x_a > 0\) and let \(v (s)\) be the one-insertion vector for \(u\) at \(x + se_b\), with \(b \ne a\). Write \(v_b (s)\) for the corresponding vector with label \(\partial_b u\). Its Gram entries are continuous at separated configurations, so \(s \mapsto v_b (s)\) is norm-continuous. For every positive-word vector \(\psi\), Proposition 6.4 differentiates the separated correlation and gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{d}{ds} \langle \psi, v (s) \rangle = \langle \psi, v_b (s) \rangle .\)

On each bounded interval the norm-continuous curve \(v_b\) has a Bochner integral. The scalar fundamental theorem of calculus and continuity of the inner product show that \(\int_0^s v_b (r) \hspace{0.17em} dr\) and \(v (s) - v (0)\) have the same pairing with every positive-word vector. Their span is dense, so these vectors are equal. The Hilbert-space fundamental theorem of calculus now gives the strong derivative \(v \hspace{-0.17em} \hspace{0.17em}' (s) = v_b (s)\). Applying the same argument to each iterated derivative label proves that \(v\) is smooth in norm. Its Gram kernel is \(\langle v (s), v (s') \rangle = G_{u, u^{\dagger_a}} (2 x_a, s' - s)\), where \(u^{\dagger_a}\) is the reflected conjugate label for axis \(a\); translation invariance removes the common transverse coordinates. The transverse jets just compared are those of \(G^{\text{rot}}\) for this label pair, which is analytic. Lemma 16.16 makes \(v\) real analytic. Pairing with a fixed exterior word proves that an angular sum is analytic in the position of an insertion that is strictly earliest or latest in some coordinate.

Step 4: from a line to arbitrary configurations. If the angular sums vanish for all labels at one end of a segment along which each of them is analytic, they vanish along it, and so do all their jets at the other end. Fix a configuration with distinct first coordinates; such configurations are dense. Treat its points one at a time, always taking the unmoved point with the smallest first coordinate; it is strictly earliest in axis 1, since every moved point has first coordinate larger than all original ones. Move it along \(e_2\) until its second coordinate exceeds all others; then along \(e_1\) until its first coordinate exceeds all others, remaining strictly latest in axis 2; then set its coordinates \(2, 3, 4\) to zero in turn, remaining strictly latest in axis 1. Each segment is separated and moves an insertion that is strictly extreme in some axis, so Step 3 applies along it. The final configuration lies on the first axis, where all jets vanish. Reversing the path proves the angular identity at the given configuration, and continuity extends it to \(\text{Conf}_n\).

Step 5: the bracket identity. The defect \([\textsf{D}_K, \textsf{D}_L] - \textsf{D}_{[K, L]}\) has vanishing correlation sums by the commutator of two angular identities; it is real, alternating in \(K, L\), covariant under signed permutations of determinant one, kills the unit and preserves the filtration. The finite free tests (16.10) are injective on this defect space, and their injectivity transfers by (16.24); pulling the mixed defect back by the stable frames makes it zero in large degree, and injective inclusions give the lower degrees. Thus \([\textsf{D}_K, \textsf{D}_L] = \textsf{D}_{[K, L]}\).

Step 6: the orthogonal-group representation. Let \(C_i = \textsf{L}_d^{\text{Max}} (K_i)\) and \(\textsf{B}_{k, i} = (\textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} \textsf{D}_{K_i} \textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}}\) for the six plane generators; \(\textsf{B}_k \to C\) by (16.42). On the endomorphism space define the relative generators and their quadratic operator

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_{k, i} (X) = \textsf{B}_{k, i} X - XC_i, \qquad \text{Cas}_k = \sum_i \mathcal{A}_{k, i}^2,\)

and \(\mathcal{A}_i\), \(\text{Cas}\) with \(C\) in place of \(\textsf{B}_k\). By Step 5, \(K_i \mapsto \mathcal{A}_{k, i}\) is a Lie-algebra representation, so \(\text{Cas}_k\) commutes with every \(\mathcal{A}_{k, i}\): in the commutator, the antisymmetric structure constants cancel in pairs. An invariant inner product makes \(\text{Cas}\) self-adjoint, with kernel the commutant of the free action, and a real polynomial \(z\) with \(z (0) = 1\) gives the kernel projection \(P = z (\text{Cas})\). We first construct projections \(P_k \to P\) commuting with the \(\mathcal{A}_{k, i}\), then show that the relative generators vanish on their ranges. Iterate \(\varphi (t) = 3 t^2 - 2 t^3\) on \(z (\text{Cas}_k)\). The identities

\(\displaystyle \varphi (t) - t = (1 - 2 t) (t^2 - t), \qquad \varphi (t)^2 - \varphi (t) = (4 (t^2 - t) - 3) (t^2 - t)^2\)

show that, near \(P\), the errors square and the displacements have a convergent geometric sum; the limits are projections \(P_k \to P\), polynomial limits in \(\text{Cas}_k\), hence commuting with every \(\mathcal{A}_{k, i}\). The restrictions of the \(\mathcal{A}_{k, i}\) to \(\text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} P_k\) satisfy the plane commutator relations and tend to zero, since \(\mathcal{A}_i\) vanishes on \(\text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} P\). Every plane generator is a commutator of two others, so

\(\displaystyle m_k = \max_i \|\mathcal{A}_{k, i} |_{\text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} P_k} \| \longrightarrow 0, \qquad m_k \leqslant 2 m_k^2,\)

and \(m_k = 0\) eventually. Therefore \(f_k = P_k (I) \to I\) is invertible and \(\textsf{B}_{k, i} f_k = f_k C_i\). All operations preserve the real signed commutant. Set \(\mathcal{V}_d = \textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}} f_k\) for one large \(k\), and

\(\displaystyle R_d (h) =\mathcal{V}_d R_d^{\text{Max}} (h) \mathcal{V}_d^{- 1} .\)

This is continuous and real, extends the signed action, and has generator \(\textsf{D}\). The global angular identity makes \(S (R (e^{tK}) A ; e^{tK} x)\) constant in \(t\); plane rotations and coordinate reflections generate \(O (4)\), proving covariance. Covariance, unit deletion, inclusion and differentiation give the unit, inclusion and vector laws by injectivity of responses, with \(\textsf{L} = \textsf{D}\) and the same \(c_{\star}\) in every degree.\(\Box\)

Put \(J_0 = c_{\star}^{- 1} J\) and define, with probability Haar measure on \(O (4)\),

\(\displaystyle T_{ab} = \int_{O (4)} \sum_{i, j} h_{ai} h_{bj} R_4 (h) (J_0)_{ij} \hspace{0.17em} dh.\) (16.43)

This average imposes exact tensor covariance while retaining the normalized shell actions.

Lemma 16.18. (Normalized tensor) The tensor \(T\) of (16.43) satisfies \(R_4 (h) T_{ab} = \sum_{i, j} h_{ia} h_{jb} T_{ij}\) for \(h \in O (4)\). It is centered, nonzero, real, symmetric and conserved, and has both normalized shell Ward identities of Theorem 6.10.

Proof. The integrand is a continuous function on a compact group with values in a finite-dimensional space; Haar invariance gives the tensor law. Reality and symmetry pass through the integral, and the vector law for derivatives gives conservation. Changing variables \(y = hz\) in each transformed shell and using covariance gives the translation and angular actions \(\partial_b\) and \(\textsf{L}_d (K)\); for the latter use \(R (h) \textsf{L} (h^{- 1} Kh) = \textsf{L} (K) R (h)\). Integration preserves both identities, and the positional derivative law of Proposition 6.4 identifies the translation action with \(\partial_{x_0^b} S\). The tensor is centered: covariance gives \(S_1 (R_4 (h) J_{ij}) = S_1 (J_{ij}) = 0\) in every integrand. If \(T = 0\), its translation shell identity would give \(S ((\partial_b A) (x_0), \boldsymbol{B}(x)) = 0\) for every label and separated spectator list; Definition 2.12 would give \(\partial_b = 0\) in degree four, contradicting (16.23) and nonzero free differentiation of a quadratic curvature polynomial.\(\Box\)

Proof of Theorem 6.10. Lemma 16.17 gives the representations and covariance, and Lemma 16.18 gives the tensor with the stated properties.\(\Box\)

Finally the charge normalization removes the factors \(c_k\) from stress insertions; no new choice depending on the arity or derivative order is needed.

Lemma 16.19. (Transported infinitesimal actions) Along the sequence of Lemma 16.10, for every fixed \(d, a, K\),

\(\displaystyle r_k (\textsf{U}_{k, d + 1}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} \partial_a \textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}} \longrightarrow \partial_a^{\text{Max}}, \qquad (\textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} \textsf{L}_d (K) \textsf{U}_{k, d}^{\text{sc}} \longrightarrow \textsf{L}_d^{\text{Max}} (K)\)

in operator norm, and \((\textsf{U}_{k, 4}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} (r_k^4 T_{ab}) \to T^{\text{Max}}_{ab}\).

Proof. The derivative limit is (16.23); the rotation limit follows from (16.42) and \(c_k \to c_{\star}\). By Lemma 16.18 the tensor \(T\) is centered, nonzero, signed-tensorial and conserved; since \(J \ne 0\) spans the line of Lemma 16.10, \(J = cT\) for a real \(c \ne 0\), and comparing translation shells gives \(c = c_{\star}\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle (\textsf{U}_{k, 4}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} (r_k^4 T_{ab}) = \frac{c_k}{c_{\star}} (\textsf{U}_{k, 4}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} (c_k^{- 1} r_k^4 J_{ab}) \longrightarrow T^{\text{Max}}_{ab} .\)

Proof of Proposition 6.11. Use the convergent free preimages of Lemma 16.19 in the chosen stress slots and the fixed free labels in the others. The derivative differentiates \(x \mapsto S (\boldsymbol{A}_k ; r_k x)\), so all chain-rule factors are included.
