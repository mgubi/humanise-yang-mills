9Constants and order of choice

The choices come in four groups. First fix the gauge group, the block penalty, the local sections and, for each stage, the constants that do not involve the block ratio. Next choose the block ratios, and then delay the entry into each stage until the coupling and the period satisfy its remaining inequalities. These choices determine the lattice family, with its couplings and insertions, before the volume subsequence \(\textsf{p}\) is prescribed; only afterwards are the volume refinement and the continuum objects chosen. Table 3 records the individual requirements in this order. At each stage there are finitely many inequalities; a later coupling threshold or entry level may be increased without changing the earlier data.

} } } } }

Choice Dependence, requirements and purpose
1 Gauge normalization; \(\textsf{b} > 0\) Fix \(G\), its realization, probability Haar measure and the metric, then \(d_{\mathfrak{g}}, C_{\mathfrak{g}}, b_0, \kappa_G\) as in Definition 2.1; \(\kappa_G > 0\). All later choices use these data.
2 Frame and predictor cutoffs; axial factor Depend on \(G\). On every patch the unique-minimizer branches of the block kernel are valid whenever their cutoff tests pass (Lemma 11.4). On a radius-\(R\) patch, the total link chord in an eligible axial frame is at most \(a_{\text{ax}} (2 R + 1)\) times the total plaquette chord, with \(a_{\text{ax}} \geqslant 16\).
3 Bases, norms, local sections, stencils Depend on \(G, d\); Taylor normalization, reality and \(H_4\) covariance (Lemma 10.2, Definitions 2.7–2.8).
4 Stage constants Depend on items 1–3 and \(j\). Derivative orders \(\mathfrak{q}_j \geqslant 6\), spatial powers \(\varpi_j \geqslant 2\), support exponents \(\sigma_j = 100\); the path weight \(w_j^{\text{path}} > 0\) and the bound \(A_j^{\text{path}} > 0\) on the weighted row, cubic and quartic sums of Lemma 12.3. Fix the whole nondecreasing sequence \(K_j \geqslant 1\) bounding the leading coefficient maps and their inverses, including the finite symmetry averages (before Lemma 14.2); then nondecreasing column bounds \(C^{\text{col}}_j \geqslant 64 K_{j + 1}^2\), including derivative factorials and basis-recombination constants (item 1 there), which bound the marked coefficient transfer (Lemma 14.7).
With \(\gamma_j > 0\) half a uniform lower bound for the free effective plaquette forms of Lemma 11.5, for the top form and both incoming stages, choose clipping coefficients \(0 < 16 c_{\text{lin}, j} < c_{\text{dir}, j} \leqslant \min (\gamma_j, 1)\), so that the remaining free form has the positive row factor of Definition 12.2; the threshold of the relaxed threshold test grows through \(e^{\text{dist} / R_j^{\text{rel}}}\) with \(R_j^{\text{rel}} w_j^{\text{path}} \geqslant 12\). With \(d_j \geqslant 0\) the ratio-independent one-loop bound of Lemma 12.9, fix the drift bound \(\omega_j^{\text{RG}} \geqslant d_j + 1\) (Lemma 13.14). Orders, powers and support exponents are nondecreasing along levels, and orders and powers tend to infinity. All costs that may not depend on the ratio are now fixed.
5 Odd stage ratios \(\ell_j^{\text{st}}\) Depend on items 1–4; nondecreasing, \(\ell_j^{\text{st}} \geqslant 3\) and above every ratio threshold, including \(u_j\) of Lemma 11.9. At \(j = J_{m + 1}\) and \(\ell_m = \ell_j^{\text{st}}\), \(\displaystyle \frac{\omega_j^{\text{RG}}}{\log \ell_m} \leqslant \min (\kappa_G / 4, j^{- 2}), \frac{\log (1 + A_j^{\text{path}} + (w_j^{\text{path}})^{- 1} + C^{\text{col}}_j + R_j^{\text{rel}} + \sigma_j)}{\log \ell_m} \leqslant j^{- 2},\)which control the drift and the logarithmic stage costs relative to \(\log \ell_m\) (Theorems 5.5 and 5.6). Also the graph and matching requirements (14.8)–(14.9), the local marked error requirement of item 2 before Lemma 14.2, the ratio inequalities (19.2) and the source comparison constants through (14.11); among them \(\ell^2 \geqslant C^{\text{tr}}_{j, k} \zeta_j\) (Lemma 11.5) and \(C_j^{\text{pb}} \ell^{- 2} \leqslant 1 / 256\) (proof of Lemma 13.22).
6 Exponents and coupling entry bounds Depend on items 1–5. The positive antitone exponents \(\theta_j\) control the quadratic loop bound of item 9. The bounds \(\beta_j^{\text{ent}}\) are lower bounds on the reference coupling whenever stage \(j\) is used. They meet the finite maximum of the remaining stage requirements, including those of Sections 16.3, 18.1 and 19, the comparison entry bounds (14.10) for each arity admitted at that stage, and the insertion-initialization bounds of item 6. They are chosen so that, at every level from stage entry on, \(\ell_m \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_m\) (item 7 before Lemma 14.2) and \(\max (\ell^{\text{st}}_{J_m}, \ell^{\text{st}}_{J_m - 1}) \leqslant M^{\text{cut}}_m\), with both adjacent stage ratios at stage transitions and the finite coefficient-error thresholds of Lemma 18.8 (used in Section 18.1 and (14.44)).
7 \(\bar{\beta}\), entry levels \(m_j^{\text{ent}}\), minimum periods \(n_j^{\min}\) Depend on items 1–6. The levels \(m_j^{\text{ent}}\) delay the first use of stage \(j\); \(n_j^{\min}\) are its minimum lattice periods. Choose \(\bar{\beta} > 1\) large enough for the initial coupling requirements, including \(\bar{\beta} \geqslant 2^{224}\), so that \(\chi_m \leqslant 1 / 128\) in (13.26), and \(t_m \leqslant 1\) at every level. Entry levels and periods satisfy the remaining level and geometric requirements, including the scale restrictions (14.3) and the comparison intervals (14.4). For each incoming stage \(j = J_{m + 1}\), fix integers \(v_j^{\text{per}}, h_j^{\text{per}} \geqslant 1\), depending only on the stage data, and require at every step and every \(p\) \(\displaystyle L_p D_m \geqslant v_j^{\text{per}} (1 + B_m + B_{m + 1})^{h_j^{\text{per}}} \hspace{0.17em},\)which postponing stage entry achieves. The marked construction uses Lemma 19.7, with weight requirements (19.6) and entry requirements (19.7); also the support-enlargement and occupancy requirements in the proof of Lemma 19.8 and the regular marked weight inequalities before Lemma 19.7.
8 \(J_m, \ell_m, D_m\), \(a_m, L_p\), \(m_{\text{ref}} (d)\) Depend on items 1–7. \(D_0 = 1\), \(D_{m + 1} = \ell_m D_m\), \(a_m = D_m^{- 1}\), \(s_m = \log D_m\), \(\ell_m = \ell^{\text{st}}_{J_{m + 1}}\), \(4 \leqslant J_m \leqslant J_{m + 1} \leqslant J_m + 1\), \(J_m \to \infty\), \(0 < \theta_{J_m} \leqslant J_m^{- 1}\), \(\log \ell_m \to \infty\), \(\log \ell_m / s_m \to 0\). Strictly increasing \(L_p\) with \(4 \mid L_p\) and every positive integer dividing infinitely many \(L_p\); \(L_p D_m \geqslant 100 (B_m + 1)\) and \(L_p D_{m + 1} \geqslant 100 ((J_{m + 1} + 3) \ell_m + 1)\). \(J_{m_{\text{ref}} (d)} \geqslant d + 3\), with \(\mathfrak{q}_{J_m} \geqslant d + 1\) and \(\varpi_{J_m} \geqslant d\) for \(m \geqslant m_{\text{ref}} (d)\).
9 Derived level scales Determined by earlier choices: equation (5.2), \(\mathfrak{B}_m = [\hat{\beta}_m / 2, 2 \hat{\beta}_m]\) and \(\Lambda_m^{\text{loop}} = 2 (M^{\text{cut}}_m)^{\theta_{J_m}}\), which bounds the weighted quadratic loop sum of Lemma 12.3.
10 Actions, classes, recursion, trajectories, couplings; width \(w_{\text{tun}}\) Depend on items 1–9: the path actions, the admissible classes, the specified recursion and its trajectories for every finite \(N, p\), with the common endpoint (Theorem 5.6). Proposition 5.7 states the fixed-level convergence, conditional on the cutoff-continuity and path-drift inputs discussed there; Remark 7.2 records the analytic dependence on the endpoint, not used later.
11 Marked transfers, terminal maps, insertions Depend on items 1–10; reference normalization (Definition 2.8). Fixed before any volume sequence.
12 \(\textsf{p}\), then \(\textsf{q}, P, S, F_d, \partial_a, \mathcal{L}\) An arbitrary strictly increasing \(\textsf{p}\) is prescribed first. Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.4 give \(P = \textsf{p} \circ \textsf{q}\) and the continuum fields; Theorem 5.9 keeps the full inner limit.
13 Comparison radii, frames, \(J\), charges, \(c_{\star}, R_d, T\) Depend on item 12 and the lattice family: Proposition 6.7, then Theorem 6.10 and Proposition 6.11. Only comparison radii are refined.
14 Slab operators, \(\textsf{X}, \nu, S^{\xi}\), then \(\xi_{\ast}\) Theorem 6.13; \(\xi_{\ast}\) is chosen in the full-measure set with one-dimensional time-axis reflection ground space (Lemma 17.3), before reconstruction.
15 Theories and continuations in every phase; \(\Theta, \mu_{\Theta}\) Theorem 6.16; Definition 6.17 centers the trace in the already selected phase; Theorem 6.18 and Lemma 18.2.
16 Product prescription; contact projections, distributions, bases, scales Depend on items 12–14 and the lattice family. Theorems 6.20 and 6.21 use the same original fields and correlation table; the contact projections, separating functionals and clearance are chosen separately from those of the product coefficients.
17 Estimate constants and thresholds Depend only on the data preceding them in each statement, uniformly in positions, volumes and states wherever asserted.
