18The trace law and non-free comparisons

This section proves Theorem 6.18 and the non-free comparisons. The stress tensor \(T\) of Section 16 is normalized by its comparison with the free Maxwell stress (Proposition 6.11), and the free stress is traceless. So this comparison cannot determine the first nonzero trace term, which is smaller by the inverse running coupling (Table 9). That term corresponds to the one-loop trace anomaly [9]; we compute its coefficient from a lattice Ward identity, with a rate. The proof of Theorem 6.18 has five steps.

    (1)

    \(L = T^{\text{Max}}\), \(D = \delta_{ab} \mathcal{O}_{F^2}\) and \(\Xi\) span the centered real symmetric \(H_4\)-tensorial tensors of degree four; a functional \(\rho_{\text{tr}}\) reads the trace from the divergence (18.3).

    (2)

    A lattice stress current (Definition 18.4) has divergence equal to the action variation up to a defect, which block transfer (18.4) makes small; block transfer also keeps the trace and antisymmetric part of the current lattice differences.

    (3)

    Lemma 18.5 computes the regulated Ward coefficient \(- 12 \kappa_G\). Lemma 18.8 carries it to continuum fields along the radii of Lemma 18.3, with the rate of Lemma 18.6.

    (4)

    A conserved combination of these fields is a multiple of \(T\); its \(D\)-coefficient tends to \(d_2 = - \kappa_G / 4\) (Proposition 18.9).

    (5)

    Positivity makes \(G_{\Theta}\) nonincreasing. This turns comparison along subsequences, which uses frames, into the frame-free limit at every radius (Figure 18).

Section 18.2 proves the non-free comparisons, each with its comparison class: no two-point statement excludes a generalized free field with the same spectral measure. In the selected phase, on no interval \((0, \delta)\) does \(G_{\Theta}\) agree with a finite Wick profile (Proposition 6.19).

Throughout, retain the fields, inclusions and units of Definition 2.12 and Proposition 6.4, the tensor of Theorem 6.10, and the selected reconstruction of Theorem 6.16. The symbols \(\tau, e_{\text{tr}}, \Theta, G_{\Theta}\) and \(A_G\) have the meanings of Definition 6.17. Write \(\Phi_{\Theta} (f) = \Phi (\iota_4 \Theta, f)\), and use the word domain of item W1 of Definition 2.14.

18.1Trace and spectrum

Centering must hold on the common operator domain, as well as in separated correlations. The proof below identifies the subtraction after continuation.

Lemma 18.1. (Centering as an operator identity) For every \(f \in \mathcal{S}\), on the word domain,

\(\displaystyle \Phi_{\Theta} (f) = \Phi (\iota_4 \tau, f) - e_{\text{tr}} \left( \int f \right) \text{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}, \qquad \langle \Omega, \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega \rangle = 0 \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Proof. Unit deletion (Definition 2.9) and the quotient calculus of Lemma 15.5 remove the unit insertion; the continuation of Lemma 17.5 and the field realization of Lemma 17.7 carry this to the operators. Deleting the unit insertion leaves a Euclidean correlation independent of its position. On each ordered-time region its continuation is therefore constant in that variable. The identity theorem on the connected continuation domain \(\mathcal{Z}_n\) of Definition 2.16, applied successively in each time variable, identifies it with the correlation containing the unit. Boundary values and smearing give \(\langle u, \Phi (\iota_4 \boldsymbol{1}_4, f) v \rangle = (\int f) \langle u, v \rangle\) for word vectors \(u, v\). Density and linearity give the identity. The zero Euclidean one-point function continues to zero and has zero boundary distribution, proving the centering assertion.\(\Box\)

We use the Fourier convention of Section 6.4:

\(\displaystyle \hat{f}_+ (\mathbf{p}) = \int e^{i\mathbf{p} \cdot x} f (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx, \qquad (\mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} \psi) (\mathbf{p}) = \int e^{i\mathbf{p} \cdot (y - x)} \psi (x, y) \hspace{0.17em} dx \hspace{0.17em} dy \hspace{0.17em} .\)

In particular, \(\mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} (\bar{f} \otimes h) = \overline{\hat{f}_+} \hat{h}_+\). Write \(W_2^{\xi_{\ast}} (\Theta, \Theta ; \bar{f} \otimes h) = \langle \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega, \Phi_{\Theta} (h) \Omega \rangle\) for the joint tempered boundary distribution of Lemma 17.5. The next lemma constructs its positive measure.

Lemma 18.2. (Unique trace spectral measure) There is a unique positive tempered-growth Borel measure \(\mu_{\Theta}\), supported in \(\bar{V}_+\), such that

\(\displaystyle d \mu_{\Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega} = | \hat{f}_+ |^2 \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\Theta} \qquad (f \in \mathcal{S}) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

It is Lorentz invariant in momentum variables, and

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} W_2^{\xi_{\ast}} (\Theta, \Theta ; \psi) & = \int \mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} \psi \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\Theta} & & (\psi \in \mathcal{S}_2),\\ G_{\Theta} (r) & = \int e^{- r\mathbf{p}^4} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\Theta} & & (r > 0) \hspace{0.17em} . \end{array}\)

Proof. The measure is built from the positive boundary distributions of Lemma 17.5. For \(v_f = \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega\), translation covariance gives \(\hat{h}_+ (P) v_f = \hat{f}_+ (P) v_h\). Here \(\hat{h}_+ (P) = \int \hat{h}_+ (\mathbf{p}) \hspace{0.17em} d \textsf{E} (\mathbf{p})\), with \(\textsf{E}\) the joint translation spectral resolution of Theorem 6.16. Both sides smear the field with \(f \ast h\). Choose tests whose transforms do not vanish on successively larger balls. On such a ball define \(d \mu_{\Theta} = | \hat{f}_+ |^{- 2} d \mu_{v_f}\). The displayed identity makes these definitions agree on overlaps. Polarization gives \(\langle v_f, v_h \rangle = \int \overline{\hat{f}_+} \hat{h}_+ \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\Theta}\). Product tests determine the joint distribution.

The spectrum condition gives cone support. Schwartz continuity, applied to inverse transforms of cutoffs equal to one on \(\{|\mathbf{p}| \leqslant R\}\), gives \(\mu_{\Theta} (\{|\mathbf{p}| \leqslant R\}) \leqslant C (1 + R)^k\). Lemma 17.11 continues \(T\) to a symmetric Hermitian Lorentz tensor. Contraction and invariance of the unit (Lemma 16.17) make \(\iota_4 \Theta\) a Hermitian Lorentz scalar. Covariance of \(W_2\) and uniqueness of the measure give Lorentz invariance.

Tempered growth and \(|\mathbf{p}| \leqslant \sqrt{2} \mathbf{p}^4\) on the cone make the Fourier–Laplace integral holomorphic at positive imaginary time, with boundary \(W_2\). To identify it with the Euclidean continuation of Lemma 17.5, take enough primitives to obtain continuous boundary values, using the polynomial boundary bound (17.8). Their zero difference extends by Schwarz reflection [29, Theorem 11.14]; holomorphic uniqueness and differentiation give equality and the Laplace formula. In particular \(G_{\Theta}\) is real, nonnegative and nonincreasing.\(\Box\)

Comparison at prescribed radii.
Use the scaling frames of Definition 6.6: \(\textsf{U}_{i, d}^{\text{sc}} : M_d^{\text{Max}} \to F_d\). Insert \(\textsf{U}_{i, d}^{\text{sc}} A\) at \(r_i x\), and read tensors by \(r_i^4 (\textsf{U}_{i, 4}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1}\). Write \(x^{(i)}\) for nearest points to \(x\) in \(r_i^{- 1} a_{n_i} \mathbb{Z}^4\). Jets use positional derivatives of smooth interpolants and their normalized lattice differences, including derivatives of the inserted fields. A shell flux is the integral against \(- \partial_a \chi\) in Definition 6.8. Define \(\beta_i = \lim_{(N, p)} \beta_{N, p, k_i}\) along the ordered limits retained in the proof of Lemma 15.5; the radius level \(n_i\) precedes the insertion level \(k_i\).

The next lemma extends Lemma 16.8 to prescribed radii. Its proof also transfers the derivative argument of (16.23) to these frames.

Lemma 18.3. (Prescribed radii) Every positive null sequence has a subsequence \((r_i)\), with frames \(\textsf{U}_{i, d}^{\text{sc}}\) making \((r_i, \textsf{U}_{i, \cdot}^{\text{sc}})\) a stable scaling sequence (Definition 6.6) and levels \(n_i < k_i\) tending to infinity, such that:

    (i)

    the couplings satisfy

    \(\displaystyle \beta_i \to \infty, \qquad \frac{\log (1 / r_i)}{\beta_i} \to \kappa_G^{- 1} \hspace{0.17em} ;\) (18.1)
    (ii)

    for every arity \(n_{\text{ar}} \geqslant 1\), degrees \(d_j \leqslant D\), labels \(A_j^{(i)} \to A_j\) and compact \(\mathcal{K} \subset \text{Gen}_{n_{\text{ar}}}\),

    \(\displaystyle \sup_{x \in \mathcal{K}} | S_{n_{\text{ar}}} (\textsf{U}_{i, \boldsymbol{d}}^{\text{sc}} \boldsymbol{A}^{(i)} ; r_i x^{(i)}) - S^{\text{Max}}_{n_{\text{ar}}} (\boldsymbol{A}; x) | \to 0 \hspace{0.17em},\)

    the same holds for each fixed jet. On compact subsets of \(\text{Conf}_{n_{\text{ar}}}\), the unrounded correlations converge in \(C^{\infty}\), and the corresponding translation-shell fluxes converge;

    (iii)

    for \(n_{\text{ar}} = 2\) without derivatives, the rounded comparison in (ii) holds uniformly over the phases.

Proof. The proof follows the finite-dimensional construction of Lemma 16.8, with radii placed in comparison windows. The source estimates of Lemmas 14.26 and 14.15 control the windows, and Lemma 14.29 moves the insertion points.

Radii and levels. Put \(M_n = M^{\text{cut}}_n\). At radius level \(n\) allow

\(\displaystyle r (n, u) = a_n M_n^u, \qquad u \in [\alpha_0, \alpha_0 + 1], \qquad k (n) = \min \{m > n : \log (a_n / a_m) \geqslant (\log \hat{\beta}_n)^{48} \} \hspace{0.17em} .\) (18.2)

The separation in \(k (n)\) leaves room for all fixed-degree marked transports; its logarithmic size is negligible compared with \(\hat{\beta}_n\). For degree \(D\) and an arity bound \(n_{\text{ar}} \geqslant 2\) choose comparison exponents \(\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \alpha_2\) and \(c\) with

\(\displaystyle c < 0, \quad \alpha_1 + 6 < \alpha_0, \quad \alpha_2 + 5 < \alpha_0 + c, \quad (\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) Dn_{\text{ar}} + c < 0, \quad (\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) Dn_{\text{ar}} - \alpha_0 - c < 0 \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Choose the differences \(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1 > 6\), then \(- c > (\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) Dn_{\text{ar}}\), then \(\alpha_0 - \alpha_2 > 5 - c\), and finally increase all three \(\alpha_j\) together until \(\alpha_2 \geqslant Dn_{\text{ar}} + 10\) and \((\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) Dn_{\text{ar}} - \alpha_0 - c < 0\).

Comparison windows. We first obtain comparison windows at every sufficiently late radius level, rather than only the intervals selected in Lemma 14.2. For each of the three exponents \(b = \alpha_j + u - \alpha_0\), stop backwards from \(n\) when the accumulated logarithmic block length first exceeds \((b + 2) \log M_n\), then stop again after another \(10 \log B_n\). The entry bounds fixed in Section 14.1 give \(\ell_m \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_m\) at every level \(m\). Each overshoot is therefore \(O (\log \log \hat{\beta}_n) = o (\log M_n)\), so the resulting cell side is at most \(a_n M_n^{b + 5}\). All levels in these windows tend to infinity and their couplings have ratio tending to one. The accumulated source loss satisfies

\(\displaystyle \log \prod_{m \text{in the window}} \mathcal{C}_{J_m} \leqslant (\sup_{m \text{in the window}} J_m^{- 2}) \sum_{m \text{in the window}} \log \ell_m = o (\log M_n),\)

by (14.11). Thus these windows satisfy the window hypotheses (Win1)–(Win3) stated before Lemma 14.25: its initial rounding error is \(M_n^{- b}\), its covariance and healthy-chart errors are smaller than any required fixed inverse power of \(M_n\) after entry, and its source recurrence (14.45) has total linear loss \(M_n^{o (1)}\). The entry bounds of Section 14.1 include the comparison requirements (14.10) for the finitely many arities admitted at each stage. At mark weight \(M_n^{1 / (8 n_{\text{ar}})}\) the resulting error is at most \(M_n^{- 3 / 4}\). The normalized integral is bounded by (14.47); its stage constant is at most \(\log M_n / (16 n_{\text{ar}})\) after entry. These estimates are uniform in \(u \in [\alpha_0, \alpha_0 + 1]\) and do not require a window to remain in one stage.

Chessboard seminorms. At its own normalization a singleton therefore has chessboard seminorm at most \(1 + |S^{\text{Max}}_1 (A) |\). Indeed coefficient extraction at amplitude \(e^{2 q}\), \(q = \log M_n / (16 n_{\text{ar}})\), bounds its centered seminorm by \(e^{q - 2 q} \leqslant 1\). Expand a degree-\(D\) label in weight-homogeneous basis vectors. Changing from exponent \(\alpha_1 + u - \alpha_0\) to \(u\) multiplies a weight-\(j\) coordinate by \(M_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) j}\), hence bounds this seminorm by \(CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) D}\). Include all signed and conjugated basis vectors in the finite comparison request. The chessboard inequality (14.19) then bounds their reflected products by the product of these seminorms. The same calculation with \(\alpha_2\) gives seminorms \(CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) D}\). The \(\alpha_2\) cell sides are \(o (r (n, u) \delta_n)\) for \(\delta_n = M_n^c\); the \(\alpha_1\) cell sides are \(o (r (n, u))\). Apply Lemma 14.29 only when every coordinate hybrid of the two physical configurations has periodic coordinate separation at least \(4 r (n, u) \delta_n\). The centered level \(D_{\text{cen}}\) can be chosen with \(a_{D_{\text{cen}}} < r (n, u) \delta_n\); the insertions and their single-axis reflections are interior by (14.41). At small radii these periodic separations equal the ordinary coordinate separations on the fixed compact set. The lemma bounds the positional difference by the product of the seminorms times \(4 n_{\text{ar}} / \delta_n\). With scaled mesh \(h^{\text{mesh}}_n = M_n^{- u}\), the two errors are consequently bounded by

\(\displaystyle CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) Dn_{\text{ar}}} \delta_n, \qquad CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_2) Dn_{\text{ar}}} h^{\text{mesh}}_n / \delta_n \hspace{0.17em} .\)

These are the contributions of coordinate-coincidence strips of width \(4 \delta_n\) and interpolation across one mesh cell; include a strip for each coordinate hybrid, a finite union of volume \(O (\delta_n)\) since \(h^{\text{mesh}}_n = o (\delta_n)\). For the strip bound, the unrounded word has the first bound of (16.13) on the entire compact subset of \(\text{Conf}_{n_{\text{ar}}}\), including coordinate ties. Indeed, use a translated coarser tiling with margins at least three \(\alpha_1\) cell sides and diameter \(o (r (n, u))\); adjacent-ratio growth and its logarithmic upper bound permit such a neighbouring coarser level. Excluding the finitely many bad translation intervals for the anchors puts the pairwise distinct points into different cells. Lemma 14.11 and the chessboard inequality (14.19) then give the displayed \(CM_n^{(\alpha_0 - \alpha_1) Dn_{\text{ar}}}\) bound there. Outside the strips apply Lemma 14.29 on each fine-cutoff grid before taking the ordered limit; the same comparison identifies the unrounded and rounded values. Both errors vanish uniformly in \(u \in [\alpha_0, \alpha_0 + 1]\) by the choice of the comparison exponents. For the remaining error, use the corrected Gaussian source of Lemma 14.26 on every subset of the \(n_{\text{ar}}\) slots, as in (16.16). The weighted mode norm in the proof of Lemma 14.15 is submultiplicative at every arity. Coefficient extraction at the amplitudes just chosen gives \(\varepsilon_n \leqslant CM_n^{- 1 / (16 n_{\text{ar}})} \to 0\) for the complete reflected array.

Phase uniformity. By Lemma 17.2 and the lattice paragraph after it, these seminorm bounds on every repeated reflected product bound the norm of the corresponding compressed zero-energy operator, hence its value at every character of the phase algebra of Theorem 6.13. This gives (iii), including moving coefficients by finite-dimensional multilinearity.

Every small radius lies in a window. By the same entry bound \(\ell_m \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_m\), applied to both adjacent stage ratios, \(\ell_n \leqslant M_{n + 1}\) eventually. Monotonicity of \(M_n\) gives \(r (n, \alpha_0) \leqslant r (n + 1, \alpha_0 + 1)\), while \(r (n, \alpha_0) \to 0\). First crossing of any prescribed small radius, followed by continuity in \(u\), puts that radius in one of these intervals. Diagonal extraction over degrees, arities, compact sets and jet orders gives \(n_i, k_i = k (n_i)\) and frames, using Lemma 14.8 for symmetries and units and the recursive correction (16.22) for inclusions. Fix the exponents before each extraction, and require \((\alpha_0 + 1) \log M_{n_i} \leqslant (\log \hat{\beta}_{n_i})^{48}\).

Jets and couplings. First integrate the rounded comparison against a bounded test supported in a compact subset of \(\text{Conf}_{n_{\text{ar}}}\). The coordinate strips and interpolation errors just estimated, together with \(\varepsilon_n\) and the modulus of continuity of the free correlations, give the tested estimate (16.21) on the present windows. Thus the unrounded correlations converge locally in \(L^1\) on \(\text{Conf}_{n_{\text{ar}}}\), even where coordinate differences vanish. Use the same diagonal sequence and frames for these tests and for every degree needed below. The argument of (16.23) then applies with Proposition 6.4: finitely many tested free responses have convergent matrices and bounded left inverses, and integration by parts identifies the tested derivative \(r_i (\textsf{U}_{i, d + 1}^{\text{sc}})^{- 1} \partial_a \textsf{U}_{i, d}^{\text{sc}} A\) with the response of \(\partial_a^{\text{Max}} A\). Iterating gives local \(L^1\) convergence of every mixed derivative; the Fourier-inversion argument of Corollary 16.9 upgrades it to \(C^{\infty}\) convergence on compact subsets of \(\text{Conf}_{n_{\text{ar}}}\). In particular, integration on a fixed compact shell gives the flux limit on these same frames, including the coordinate-tie sets crossed by the shell. Minimality of \(k (n)\) gives \(\log (a_n / a_{k (n)}) = O ((\log \hat{\beta}_n)^{48}) = o (\hat{\beta}_n)\). The running law of Theorem 5.6, in (5.14), gives \(\beta_i / \hat{\beta}_{k_i} \to 1\). Since \(\hat{\beta}_n = \bar{\beta} + \kappa_G \log (1 / a_n)\), the imposed bound on \(u \log M_n\) proves (18.1).\(\Box\)

Tensor coordinates.
Set \(L = T^{\text{Max}}\), \(D_{ab} = \delta_{ab} \mathcal{O}_{F^2}\), and \(\Xi_{aa} = 0\), \(\Xi_{ab} = \sum_{c, \alpha} : F_{ac}^{\alpha} F_{bc}^{\alpha}\): for \(a \ne b\). In the notation of (16.2), \(L = - Q + \tfrac{1}{4} \delta Q_{\text{tot}}\), \(\Xi_{ab} = (1 - \delta_{ab}) Q_{ab}\) and \(D = \tfrac{1}{4} \delta Q_{\text{tot}}\); in the basis \(Z^{(1)}, Z^{(2)}, Z^{(3)}\) of the proof of Lemma 16.1,

\(\displaystyle L = - \tfrac{1}{2} Z^{(1)} + \tfrac{1}{4} Z^{(2)} - Z^{(3)}, \qquad \Xi = Z^{(3)}, \qquad D = \tfrac{1}{2} Z^{(1)} + \tfrac{1}{4} Z^{(2)} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

By that proof, these three tensors span the centered real symmetric \(H_4\)-tensorial tensors of degree four.

Write \(\langle F_{ac}, F_{bc} \rangle = \sum_{\alpha} : F^{\alpha}_{ac} F^{\alpha}_{bc}\): and similarly with derivatives. Before imposing the Maxwell equation, divergence has three coordinates, represented in component \(b\) by

\(\displaystyle \sum_c \langle F_{bc}, \partial_b F_{bc} \rangle, \quad \sum_{a, c} \langle F_{ac}, \partial_b F_{ac} \rangle, \quad \sum_{a, c} \langle F_{bc}, \partial_a F_{ac} \rangle \hspace{0.17em} .\)

For diagonal entries \(z_0 \sum_c \langle F_{ac}, F_{ac} \rangle + z_1 \sum_{b, c} \langle F_{bc}, F_{bc} \rangle\) and off-diagonal entries \(z_2 \sum_c \langle F_{ac}, F_{bc} \rangle\), the divergence coordinates are \((2 (z_0 - z_2), 2 z_1 + z_2 / 2, z_2)\). Indeed Bianchi gives \(\sum_{a, c} \langle F_{ac}, \partial_a F_{bc} \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{a, c} \langle F_{ac}, \partial_b F_{ac} \rangle\). Thus the functional \(\rho_{\text{tr}} (x, y, z) = - 2 x - 8 y\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \rho_{\text{tr}} (\text{div}_{\text{raw}} \hspace{0.17em} B) \mathcal{O}_{F^2} = - \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} B, \qquad \rho_{\text{tr}} (\text{div} \hspace{0.17em} L) = \rho_{\text{tr}} (\text{div} \hspace{0.17em} \Xi) = 0, \qquad \rho_{\text{tr}} (\text{div} \hspace{0.17em} D) = - 4 \hspace{0.17em} .\) (18.3)

It annihilates the third coordinate, the Maxwell equation direction. Write \(R_5 \cong \mathbb{R}^3\) for this raw space and \(V_5\) for the space of real \(H_4\)-covariant axis vectors in \((M_5^{\text{Max}})^4\). The free Maxwell quotient map \(s : R_5 \to V_5\) is onto, with \(\ker s =\mathbb{R}v_{\text{eom}}\), \(v_{\text{eom}} = (0, 0, 1)\) and \(\rho_{\text{tr}} (v_{\text{eom}}) = 0\); thus \(V_5\) is two dimensional.

Lattice stress current.
We construct the lattice insertions from plaquette curvatures. Let \(\textsf{S} = \textsf{S}_{N, m}\) be the leading analytic path action without its coupling, defined in Section 12.2, and let \(\textsf{s}_a q (x) = q (x + e_a)\) be the lattice shift.

Definition 18.4. (Lattice stress current) At a site \(x\) put \(f_{ab} (x) = \text{proj}_{\mathfrak{g}} (U_{\square (x ; a, b)} - U_{\square (x ; a, b)}^{- 1}) / 2\) and

\(\displaystyle J_{ab} = \frac{\delta_{ab}}{4} \sum_{c, d} \langle f_{cd}, f_{cd} \rangle - \sum_c \langle f_{ac}, f_{bc} \rangle, \qquad (Y_b^x)_e =\boldsymbol{1}_{e^- = x} f_{b, e^{\text{dir}}} (x) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

With \(\nabla_a = \sum_{j = 1}^4 c^{\nabla}_j (\textsf{s}_a^j - \textsf{s}_a^{- j})\) and \((c^{\nabla}_1, c^{\nabla}_2, c^{\nabla}_3, c^{\nabla}_4) = (4 / 5, - 1 / 5, 4 / 105, - 1 / 280)\), the Ward defect is \(\mathcal{E}_b = \sum_a \nabla_a J_{ab} - D \textsf{S} [Y_b]\).

The current \(J\) is the lattice form of \(T^{\text{Max}}\), and \(Y_b\) is the initial left displacement. Initially \(\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} J = 0\) and \(J_{ab} - J_{ba} = 0\), so these data satisfy the identities (a)–(b) of Lemma 18.5 below with \(q = 0\) and \(\textsf{t} = 0\).

Block transfer.
The averaging kernels must respect the particular difference \(\nabla_a\). For odd block ratio \(\ell\), define commuting finite sums

\(\displaystyle \textsf{M}_a = \ell^{- 1} \sum_{j = 0}^{\ell - 1} \textsf{s}_a^j, \qquad \textsf{G}_a = \sum_{j = 1}^4 c^{\nabla}_j \sum_{v = - j}^{j - 1} \textsf{s}_a^v, \qquad \textsf{N}_a = \textsf{G}_a^{(\ell)} \textsf{M}_a, \qquad \textsf{K}_0 = \prod_a \textsf{M}_a \textsf{N}_a \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Here \(\textsf{G}_a^{(\ell)}\) is \(\textsf{G}_a\) with every shift replaced by its \(\ell\)th power, and \(\textsf{G}_a \hspace{0.17em} (\textsf{s}_a - 1) = \nabla_a\). \(\displaystyle \textsf{K}_0 \nabla_a = \ell^{- 1} \nabla_a^{\text{coarse}} \textsf{K}_a, \qquad \textsf{K}_0 (\textsf{s}_a - 1) = \ell^{- 1} (\textsf{s}_a^{\ell} - 1) \textsf{K}_a^{\text{edge}}, \qquad \textsf{K}_a - \textsf{K}_0 = (\textsf{s}_a^{\ell} - 1) \textsf{K}_a^{\text{tr}} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

We first calculate the scalar coefficient before marked transport. For a chart function \(F\) define its Hessian Fourier kernel by

\(\displaystyle \hat{F}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (p, q) = \sum_{x, y} e^{i (p \cdot x + q \cdot y)} D^2 F (0) [e_{x, c, \alpha}, e_{y, c, \alpha}] \hspace{0.17em} .\)

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \textsf{l} (F) & = \text{Re} \sum_{a \ne c, b} i [z^2 t] \widehat{F_b}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (ze_a, - ze_a + te_b),\\ \textsf{q} (F) & = | \Lambda |^{- 1} \text{Re} \sum_{a \ne c} [z^2] \hat{F}_{c \alpha, c \alpha} (ze_a, - ze_a) \hspace{0.17em} . \end{aligned}\)

\(\displaystyle \Gamma_{\mu} = \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det (H + dd^{\ast} + \mu^2 I) - \log \det (d^{\ast} d + \mu^2 I), \quad R_{\Lambda} (\mu) = \textsf{q} (\Gamma_{\mu}), \quad \mathcal{D}_{\Lambda} (\mu) = - \mu R_{\Lambda}' (\mu),\)

where \(H\) is the left Hessian of \(\textsf{S}\) and \(d\) is the infinitesimal gauge insertion. For \(a > 0\) and \(r \in \mathbb{N}\), with \(\|x\| = |x|_{\infty}\) in the kernel's lattice units, put

\(\displaystyle \vartheta_{a, r} (x) = e^{a \|x\|} (1 +\|x\|)^r \hspace{0.17em} .\)

For a chart function anchored at \(o\), its weighted order-\(r\) seminorm is the sum of absolute link coefficients of \(D^r F (0)\) against \(\prod_{v = 1}^r \vartheta_{a, r} (e_v^- - o)\); for a displacement use its link components. Lemma 18.5. (Regulated Ward coefficient) Fix a level \(k\), a weight \(a > 0\) and \(C < \infty\). For \(\nu \in \mathbb{N}\) let \(N_{\nu} > k\) and let \((J, Y, \mathcal{E}, q, \textsf{t})^{(\nu)}\) be translation-invariant local data on the level-\(k\) torus of period \(n_{\nu} \to \infty\), with \(\textsf{S} = \textsf{S}_{N_{\nu}, k}\), such that

    (a)

    \(D \textsf{S} [Y_b] \equiv_4 \sum_a \nabla_a J_{ab} -\mathcal{E}_b\);

    (b)

    \(\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} J = \sum_a \delta_a q_a\) and \(J_{ab} - J_{ba} = \sum_c \delta_c \textsf{t}_{abc}\);

    (c)

    the \(a\)-weighted seminorms of orders \(2\)–\(4\) of \(J, Y, q, \textsf{t}\) are at most \(C\);

    (d)

    those of \(\mathcal{E}\), and the deviations of the zero-field Hessians of \(J\) and \(Y\) from their free values on tests of weight at most \(4\) and \(5\), are at most \(\varepsilon_{\nu} \to 0\).

Then, for every colour, \(\mathfrak{h} (Y^{(\nu)}) \to - 12 \kappa_G\).

Proof. The inputs are the normalization of \(\kappa_G\) (Definition 2.1), the kinetic and quadratic cancellations and the classical matching of the path action (Lemmas 12.4 and 12.6), and the one-loop coefficient of Lemma 12.9. Write \(\mathcal{R}_{\mu} = (H + dd^{\ast} + \mu^2 I)^{- 1}\), \(\mathcal{G}_{\mu} = (d^{\ast} d + \mu^2 I)^{- 1}\). For \(\Psi_s (U) = e^{sY (U)} U\) let \(\mathcal{Y}_U (z) = \partial_s |_0 \log (\Psi_s (e^z U) \Psi_s (U)^{- 1})\) and \(\mathcal{V}= D\mathcal{Y}_U (0)\). If \(\mathcal{J}_U\) is the mixed derivative of \(\log (\text{gauge}_{e^v} (e^z U) U^{- 1})\) in \(v, z\) at zero, gauge invariance gives \(Hd = -\mathcal{N}\), \(\mathcal{N}= D \textsf{S} [\mathcal{J}_U]\), hence

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{R}_{\mu} d = d\mathcal{G}_{\mu} +\mathcal{R}_{\mu} \mathcal{N}\mathcal{G}_{\mu} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The product rule for \(D^2 (D \textsf{S} [Y])\) and cyclicity of trace now give

\(\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (\mathcal{R}_{\mu} D^2 (D \textsf{S} [Y])) - \text{div}_{\text{Haar}} \hspace{0.17em} Y = D \Gamma_{\mu} [Y] - \mu^2 \text{tr} (\mathcal{R}_{\mu} \mathcal{V}) + D \textsf{S} [Z],\) (18.5)

with \(Z = \frac{1}{2} \text{contr} (\mathcal{R}_{\mu}, D^2 \mathcal{Y}_U (0)) - \text{contr} (\mathcal{G}_{\mu} d^{\ast} (\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{V}^{\ast})\mathcal{R}_{\mu}, \mathcal{J}_U)\). Contractions sum the indicated matrix indices.

Cancellation. The leading quadratic kernel of an invariant scalar action at the required momentum order is a colour multiple of \(|p|^2 I - p \otimes p\). Write \(X_{acb}\) for the coefficient of momentum \(e_a\) in the diagonal polarization-\(c\) entry of \(DY_b\), with its colour indices contracted against the action kernel. Gauge covariance makes the mixed polarization term \(- X_{cac}\) and gives \(X_{aaa} = 0\). In the tested variation the three terms, after summing the intermediate polarization, are

\(\displaystyle - 2 X_{aca} - X_{cac} + \sum_b X_{bcb} \qquad (a \ne c) .\)

Summing over \(a \ne c\) makes the first and second sums \(- 3 \sum_{b, c} X_{bcb}\), cancelling the third sum. Higher momentum orders do not contribute. This applies to \(D \Gamma_{\mu} [Y]\), \(D \textsf{S} [Z]\) and the further action variation below, uniformly on each fixed \(\mu \in [\delta, 1]\). For the first term on the left of (18.5), substitute \(D \textsf{S} [Y_b] \equiv_4 \sum_a \nabla_a J_{ab} -\mathcal{E}_b\). \(\displaystyle \mathfrak{h} (Y) =\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda} (\mu) + \mu^2 \textsf{l} (\text{Rem}) + o (1),\) (18.6)

A stability lemma.
The next lemma gives a criterion for raw coefficient maps to converge to the free quotient map \(s\). It also turns a nearly null vector of these maps into functionals that read \(\rho_{\text{tr}}\) with a rate.

Lemma 18.6. (Stability of the trace functional) Let \(E, H\) be finite-dimensional real normed spaces, \(s : E \to H\) linear with a right inverse \(t\) and \(\ker s =\mathbb{R}v\), \(v \ne 0\), and \(\rho : E \to \mathbb{R}\) linear. Let \(s_i : E \to H\) and \(t_i : H \to E\) be linear with \(s_i t_i = I\) and \(t_i \to t\), let \(v_i \to v\), and let \(e_i \geqslant 1\) satisfy \(e_i \|s_i v_i \| \to 0\) and \(e_i \rho (v_i) \to 0\). Then \(s_i \to s\), the functionals \(\lambda_i = \rho t_i\) are uniformly bounded, and \(e_i \| \lambda_i s_i - \rho \| \to 0\).

Proof. Put \(k_i = v_i - t_i s_i v_i\). Then \(s_i k_i = 0\), and \(k_i \to v\) since \(t_i\) is bounded and \(s_i v_i \to 0\). The maps \(\Phi_i (h, c) = t_i h + ck_i\) from \(H \times \mathbb{R}\) to \(E\) converge to \(\Phi (h, c) = th + cv\), which is injective (apply \(s\)) and hence invertible, as \(\dim E = \dim H + 1\). So \(\Phi_i^{- 1} \to \Phi^{- 1}\); since \(s_i \Phi_i (h, c) = h\), also \(s_i \to s\). For \(y \in E\) write \(\Phi_i^{- 1} y = (s_i y, c_i (y))\), so that \(y - t_i s_i y = c_i (y) k_i\) and \(|c_i (y) | \leqslant C \|y\|\) uniformly in \(i\). Then

\(\displaystyle \lambda_i s_i y - \rho (y) = - c_i (y) \rho (k_i) = - c_i (y) (\rho (v_i) - \rho (t_i s_i v_i)) .\)

Hence \(e_i \| \lambda_i s_i - \rho \| \leqslant C \left( e_i | \rho (v_i) | +\| \rho \| \sup_j \|t_j \| \hspace{0.17em} e_i \|s_i v_i \| \right) \to 0\). Boundedness of \(\lambda_i\) follows from \(t_i \to t\).\(\Box\)

The following reduction isolates the comparison needed to construct the right inverses. It requires no convergence rate for the inverses; the rate in Lemma 18.6 comes from the separate estimates on \(v_i\).

Lemma 18.7. (Right inverses from a fixed complement) Let \(E, H\) be finite-dimensional normed spaces and let \(s : E \to H\) be onto. Choose a complement \(C\) of \(\ker s\), and let \(t : H \to C \subset E\) be the inverse of \(s|_C\). If linear maps \(s_i : E \to H\) satisfy \(\varepsilon_i = \|(s_i - s) t\| \to 0\), then for all sufficiently large \(i\) the maps

\(\displaystyle t_i = t (s_i t)^{- 1} : H \longrightarrow E\)

are right inverses of \(s_i\) and converge to \(t\). More precisely, whenever \(\varepsilon_i < 1\),

\(\displaystyle \|t_i - t\| \leqslant \frac{\|t\| \varepsilon_i}{1 - \varepsilon_i} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Proof. Since \(st = I_H\), the map \(s_i t\) differs from \(I_H\) by an operator of norm \(\varepsilon_i\). The Neumann series therefore gives \((s_i t)^{- 1}\) and bounds its difference from \(I_H\) by \(\varepsilon_i / (1 - \varepsilon_i)\). The stated identities and estimate follow by composition with \(t\).\(\Box\)

The proof has five parts, and each has its own reason for smallness (Table 10). The current estimates are a contraction under repeated block transfer. The finite coefficient is accurate because of the thresholds and the exponential tails. The trace of the current is an exact forward difference; this, with the matching gain of marked transport, gives the equation direction its rates, and Lemma 18.6 turns that rate into the accuracy of \(\lambda_i\). The tensor traces are small for a different reason: the same forward difference becomes small after averaging over many sites. No single power count covers all five. The choices are made in this order: the block ratios and entry couplings of the stages, once and for all, so that transfer contracts; at a fixed level \(k\), the transfer depth and the period; then the ordered limit at that \(k\); and finally \(k = k_i\) along the radii. Here \(A_G = 3 d_{\mathfrak{g}} / \pi^4 > 0\) since \(d_{\mathfrak{g}} \geqslant 1\); identifying it uses \(d_2 = - \kappa_G / 4 \ne 0\). Thus \(\Theta \ne 0\).\(\Box\)

The following high-energy law follows from Theorem 6.18 and Lemma 18.2, as proved below.

Lemma 18.10. (High-energy trace limit) For every \(\varphi \in C_c (\mathbb{R}^4 ; \mathbb{C}) \cup \mathcal{S}\),

\(\displaystyle \lim_{R_{\text{E}} \to \infty} R_{\text{E}}^{- 8} (\log R_{\text{E}})^2 \int \varphi (\mathbf{p}/ R_{\text{E}}) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\Theta} (\mathbf{p}) = \frac{A_G}{1536 \pi} \int_{\bar{V}_+} \textsf{m} (\mathbf{p})^2 \varphi (\mathbf{p}) \hspace{0.17em} d^4 \mathbf{p} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Proof. The rescaled spectral measures are controlled through their Laplace transforms, which the trace law determines (Definition 6.17, Lemma 18.2). Put \(\nu_{R_{\text{E}}} = R_{\text{E}}^{- 8} \log^2 R_{\text{E}} \hspace{0.17em} \mu_{\Theta} (R_{\text{E}} \hspace{0.17em} \cdot)\). For every future timelike Laplace covector \(u\), with the Euclidean pairing \(u \cdot \mathbf{p}\) of item W0 of Definition 2.14, which is the Minkowski pairing of \(\mathbf{p}\) with the future timelike \((- u^1, - u^2, - u^3, u^4)\), Lorentz invariance reduces its transform to the time-axis transform at \(s = \sqrt{\textsf{m} (u)}\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \int e^{- u \cdot \mathbf{p}} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{R_{\text{E}}} = R_{\text{E}}^{- 8} \log^2 R_{\text{E}} \hspace{0.17em} G_{\Theta} (s / R_{\text{E}}) \longrightarrow A_G s^{- 8} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

To identify the constant, write \(t =\mathbf{p}^4\) and integrate the spatial radius \(q\):

\(\displaystyle \int_{\bar{V}_+} e^{- st} \textsf{m} (\mathbf{p})^2 \hspace{0.17em} d^4 \mathbf{p}= 4 \pi \int_0^{\infty} e^{- st} \int_0^t q^2 (t^2 - q^2)^2 \hspace{0.17em} dq \hspace{0.17em} dt = 1536 \pi s^{- 8} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The \(s = 1\) transform bounds compact masses, so diagonal extraction and Riesz representation [29, Theorem 2.14] give vague subsequential limits. The \(u / 2\) transform controls the \(u\)-damped tail, so every limit has the displayed transforms. Holomorphic uniqueness in each coordinate for timelike real part, then Fourier inversion on product Schwartz tests against the finite damped measures [29, Theorems 10.18 and 9.11], identify every limit with the cone density in the statement.

For Schwartz tests positivity gives

\(\displaystyle \nu_{R_{\text{E}}} \{\mathbf{p}^4 \leqslant R\} \leqslant e \hspace{0.17em} R_{\text{E}}^{- 8} \log^2 R_{\text{E}} \hspace{0.17em} G_{\Theta} ((R_{\text{E}} R)^{- 1}) \leqslant CR^8 \qquad (R \geqslant 1, R_{\text{E}} \geqslant R_{\text{E}, 0}) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Use the trace law at \(1 / (R_{\text{E}} R)\) and \(\log R_{\text{E}} \leqslant \log (R_{\text{E}} R)\). A Schwartz bound of order greater than eight sums the dyadic energy tails uniformly, since \(|\mathbf{p}| \leqslant \sqrt{2} \mathbf{p}^4\) on the cone. The \(C_c\) limit then gives the Schwartz limit.\(\Box\)

For \(\psi_{R_{\text{E}}} (x, y) = R_{\text{E}}^8 \psi (R_{\text{E}} x, R_{\text{E}} y)\), \(\mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} \psi_{R_{\text{E}}} (\mathbf{p}) =\mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} \psi (\mathbf{p}/ R_{\text{E}})\). For \(\psi \in \mathcal{S}_2\), \(\mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} \psi \in \mathcal{S}\). Substitution in Lemma 18.10 gives the position-space consequence

\(\displaystyle R_{\text{E}}^{- 8} \log^2 R_{\text{E}} \hspace{0.17em} W_2^{\xi_{\ast}} (\Theta, \Theta ; \psi_{R_{\text{E}}}) \longrightarrow \frac{A_G}{1536 \pi} \int_{\bar{V}_+} \textsf{m} (\mathbf{p})^2 \mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} \psi (\mathbf{p}) \hspace{0.17em} d^4 \mathbf{p} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

18.2Precise non-free comparisons

A finite Wick profile of Definition 2.18 is, at small distance, a finite sum of terms \(r^a \log^k r\) with \(a \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(k \geqslant 0\), plus a bounded remainder; the trace law requires the negative logarithmic power \(\log^{- 2} (1 / r)\). This gives the following exclusion; its proof follows.

Lemma 18.11. (Finite Wick obstruction) For every finite Wick profile \(W_{\text{Wick}}\) and every \(\delta > 0\), there is \(r \in (0, \delta)\) such that

\(\displaystyle G_{\Theta} (r) \ne W_{\text{Wick}} (r) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Proof. We expand each profile at small \(r\) and compare with the positive limit of Theorem 6.18. For one factor, polar coordinates and \(\omega_{m_{\text{sh}}} = \sqrt{m_{\text{sh}}^2 + q^2}\) give

\(\displaystyle K_{m_{\text{sh}}, k_E, h_{\text{mom}}} (r) = \frac{1}{2} \left( \int_{S^2} h_{\text{mom}} \right) \int_{m_{\text{sh}}}^{\infty} \omega_{m_{\text{sh}}}^{k_E} (\omega_{m_{\text{sh}}}^2 - m_{\text{sh}}^2)^{(j_h + 1) / 2} e^{- r \omega_{m_{\text{sh}}}} \hspace{0.17em} d \omega_{m_{\text{sh}}} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

For \(m_{\text{sh}} = 0\) this is proportional to \(r^{- j_h - k_E - 2}\). Otherwise split at \(2 m_{\text{sh}}\); the bounded interval is analytic. On the tail expand \((1 - m_{\text{sh}}^2 / \omega_{m_{\text{sh}}}^2)^{(j_h + 1) / 2}\). Taking enough terms makes the remainder integrable with any prescribed number of energy moments, hence gives a Taylor remainder of the required order. The retained terms are \(I_a (r) = \int_{2 m_{\text{sh}}}^{\infty} v^a e^{- rv} \hspace{0.17em} dv\), \(a \in \mathbb{Z}\). Integrate explicitly for \(a \geqslant 0\); \(I_{- 1}' = - e^{- 2 m_{\text{sh}} r} / r\) gives the logarithm, and \(I_a' = - I_{a + 1}\) gives all lower powers. Only nonnegative logarithmic powers occur. For products, choose each remainder order above the other factors' pole orders.

Thus \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} W_{\text{Wick}} = \sum_{a, k} c_{a, k} r^a \log^k r + O (1)\) with finitely many terms. After multiplying by \(r^8 \log^2 (1 / r)\), the terms with \(a > - 8\) vanish. Among the others, the least \(a\), then largest \(k\), gives a nonzero divergent leading term. A finite limit is therefore zero. Lemma 18.2 makes \(G_{\Theta}\) real, so equality with \(W_{\text{Wick}}\) implies equality with its real part. The positive trace limit contradicts equality on \((0, \delta)\), proving also Proposition 6.19.\(\Box\)

To describe polynomial mass-shell Wick contractions, put \(q_m (\mathbf{k}) = (\mathbf{k}, \sqrt{m^2 + |\mathbf{k}|^2})\), \(m \geqslant 0\). Fix a finite set of elementary field indices, with elements \(\textsf{x}, \textsf{x}'\); words \(u, u'\) list its elements. For words of length \(k\), \(\pi\) ranges over permutations of \(\{1, \ldots, k\}\). Comparison data specify finite mass–polynomial lists \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{pair}} (\textsf{x}, \textsf{x}')\), with \(P \in \mathbb{C} [\mathbf{p}^1, \ldots, \mathbf{p}^4]\), and complex contraction coefficients \(c (u, u', \pi)\).

Definition 18.12. (Polynomial mass-shell pairings) Set

\(\displaystyle v_{m, P} (\mathbf{k}) = \frac{P (q_m (\mathbf{k}))}{2 \omega_m (\mathbf{k})}, \qquad L_{m, P} (\varphi) = \int v_{m, P} (\mathbf{k}) \varphi (q_m (\mathbf{k})) \hspace{0.17em} d^3 \mathbf{k} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

For words \(u = (\textsf{x}_i)_{i = 1}^k\), \(u' = (\textsf{x}'_i)_{i = 1}^k\), define the convolutions of the functionals \(L_{m, P}\) by

\(\displaystyle \textsf{P} (u, u' ; \varphi) = \sum_{\pi} c (u, u', \pi) \sum_{(m_i, P_i) \in \prod_i \mathcal{M}_{\text{pair}} (\textsf{x}_i, \textsf{x}'_{\pi (i)})} \int \prod_i v_{m_i, P_i} (\mathbf{k}_i) \hspace{0.17em} \varphi \left( \sum_i q_{m_i} (\mathbf{k}_i) \right) \prod_i d^3 \mathbf{k}_i \hspace{0.17em} .\)

For unequal lengths put \(\textsf{P} = 0\). A zero denominator at zero momentum gives value zero.

For finite polynomial fields \(Q = \sum_i \alpha_i u_i\), including empty words, the bilinear pairing is \(W^{\text{pol}} (Q, Q' ; \varphi) = \sum_{i, j} \alpha_i \alpha'_j \textsf{P} (u_i, u'_j ; \varphi)\). Empty contractions evaluate \(\varphi\) at zero. The shell measure has no \((2 \pi)^{- 3}\) factor.

The next lemma separates each comparison by one product test; its proof follows.

Lemma 18.13. (Polynomial countertest) For every comparison data and every \(Q, Q'\), there are \(f, h \in \mathcal{S}\) with compactly supported \(\mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} (\bar{f} \otimes h)\) such that

\(\displaystyle \langle \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega, \Phi_{\Theta} (h) \Omega \rangle \ne W^{\text{pol}} (Q, Q' ; \mathcal{W}_{\text{rel}} (\bar{f} \otimes h)) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Proof. Equality for all product tests would identify the two spectral representations, hence \(G_{\Theta}\) with a finite Wick profile, which Lemma 18.11 excludes. In detail, equality for all product tests implies equality for every \(\varphi \in C_c^{\infty}\): choose \(\hat{h}_+ = \varphi\) and real \(\hat{f}_+ = 1\) on its support. Approximate \(e^{- r\mathbf{p}^4}\) by compact cutoffs. Dominated convergence applies: Lemma 18.2 controls \(\mu_{\Theta}\); exponential damping controls comparison polynomials at infinity, and \(d^3 \mathbf{k}/ |\mathbf{k}|\) is integrable at zero. Expanding the numerators into monomials factors the integrals into the kernels of Definition 2.18, with the empty words giving constants. Hence \(G_{\Theta} (r) = W_{\text{Wick}} (r)\) for all \(r > 0\), contrary to Lemma 18.11.\(\Box\)

For the photon comparison use Hilbert tensor products.

Definition 18.14. (Ambient and physical Maxwell Fock spaces) For \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) colours put

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} d \sigma (\mathbf{k}) = \frac{d^3 \mathbf{k}}{2 |\mathbf{k}|}, \qquad \Pi_{ab} (\mathbf{k}) = \delta_{ab} - \frac{\mathbf{k}_a \mathbf{k}_b}{|\mathbf{k}|^2},\\ \mathcal{K}_n = L^2 (d \sigma ; \mathbb{C}^n \otimes \mathbb{C}^3), \qquad \mathcal{F}_n = \bigoplus_{k \geqslant 0} \mathcal{K}_n^{\otimes k}, \qquad \mathcal{F}_n^{\text{phys}} = \bigoplus_{k \geqslant 0} \text{Sym}^k (\Pi \mathcal{K}_n) \hspace{0.17em} . \end{array}\)

The measure has no atom at zero momentum, so the value of \(\Pi\) there is immaterial, and \(\Pi\) acts pointwise almost everywhere. Thus a physical vector is symmetric and transverse in each slot. Translations multiply sector \(k\) by \(\exp \{iz \cdot \sum_j (\mathbf{k}_j, |\mathbf{k}_j |)\}\). The sector-zero unit is \(\Omega_0\).

Definition 18.15. (Coherent comparison vectors) For Schwartz tests \(h = (h_{\alpha, a})\), put

\(\displaystyle v_h (\alpha, a ; \mathbf{k}) = |\mathbf{k}| \sum_b \Pi_{ab} (\mathbf{k}) \hat{h}_{\alpha, b, +} (\mathbf{k}, |\mathbf{k}|), \qquad \mathcal{E} (h) = e^{- \|v_h \|^2 / 2} \bigoplus_{k \geqslant 0} \frac{v_h^{\otimes k}}{\sqrt{k!}} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

A comparison \(U : \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{F}_n\) is a complex linear isometry with image in \(\mathcal{F}_n^{\text{phys}}\), \(U \Omega = \Omega_0\), intertwining translations. Set \(\psi_U (f) = U \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega\).

Real tests have real components. Causal separation means \(\textsf{m} (x - y) < 0\) for every pair of points in their closed supports, where \(\textsf{m} (x) = (x^4)^2 - \sum_{a = 1}^3 (x^a)^2\).

Lemma 18.13 compares two-point functions only. The Fock test asks more: an embedding of the interacting states into photon Fock space under which the trace field is local relative to the free Maxwell field. Relative locality would impose the equality excluded below. Formally, let \(W (h)\) be Weyl operators of the free Maxwell field with \(W (h) \Omega_0 =\mathcal{E} (h)\) and \(W (h)^{\ast} = W (- h)\). If \(U \Phi_{\Theta} (f) U^{\ast}\) commuted with \(W (h)\) for \(h\) causally separated from \(f\), then, since \(U^{\ast} \Omega_0 = \Omega\) and \(\iota_4 \Theta\) is Hermitian,

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \langle \mathcal{E}(- h), \psi_U (f) \rangle & = \langle \Omega_0, W (h) U \Phi_{\Theta} (f) U^{\ast} \Omega_0 \rangle\\ & = \langle \Omega_0, U \Phi_{\Theta} (f) U^{\ast} W (h) \Omega_0 \rangle = \langle \psi_U (\bar{f}), \mathcal{E}(h) \rangle \hspace{0.17em} . \end{array}\)

Weyl operators enter only this motivation. The lemma compares the two matrix elements directly, and its proof uses spectral growth without restricting particle amplitudes.

Lemma 18.16. (Fock countertest) For every \(n \geqslant 0\) and every comparison \(U\), for every ball about zero there are Schwartz \(f\) compactly supported in that ball and real compactly supported Schwartz \(h_{\alpha, a}\), each causally separated from \(f\), such that

\(\displaystyle \langle \mathcal{E}(- h), \psi_U (f) \rangle \ne \langle \psi_U (\bar{f}), \mathcal{E}(h) \rangle \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Proof. The proof combines the spectral representation (Lemma 18.2), the high-energy law (Lemma 18.10) and translation covariance (Lemma 17.5) with the dilation behaviour of local photon amplitudes. Fix a ball and suppose equality always holds.

Sector amplitudes. Set \(p_j = (\mathbf{k}_j, |\mathbf{k}_j |)\) and \(p_{\text{tot}} = \sum_j p_j\). The convolution argument of Lemma 18.2 gives sector amplitudes \(Y_{k, s}\), with \(s\) listing colour and spatial indices:

\(\displaystyle \psi_U (f)_{k, s} = \hat{f}_+ (p_{\text{tot}}) Y_{k, s}, \qquad \mu_{\Theta} = (p_{\text{tot}})_{\ast} \sum_{k, s} |Y_{k, s} |^2 d \sigma^{\otimes k} \hspace{0.17em} .\) (18.13)

Box masses are finite. Cauchy–Schwarz and polynomial spectral growth give integrability against sufficiently high inverse polynomials in each momentum.

The common sector unitary \(i^k\) preserves the equality, physical vectors and translations. Write \(\textsf{Y} = i^k Y_{k, s}\).

Locality and doubling. Expand the coherent equality in a real parameter multiplying \(h\), justified by Cauchy–Schwarz and the exponential series. Polarization and complexification give independent complex slot tests. Transversality removes the projector in contractions with physical waves. Shrink nonnegative unit-integral \(f\) to zero. Then \(| \hat{f}_+ | \leqslant 1\) and \(\hat{f}_+ \to 1\); dominated convergence gives, for \(g_j\) supported spacelike to zero,

\(\displaystyle \int \textsf{Y} (\boldsymbol{k}) \prod_j [|\mathbf{k}_j | \hat{g}_{j, -} (p_j)] \hspace{0.17em} d \sigma^{\otimes k} = \int \overline{\textsf{Y} (\boldsymbol{k})} \prod_j [|\mathbf{k}_j | \hat{g}_{j, +} (p_j)] \hspace{0.17em} d \sigma^{\otimes k} \hspace{0.17em} .\) (18.14)

Here \(\hat{g}_- (p) = \hat{g}_+ (- p)\); the sector factors \(i^k\) give exactly these signs.

Fix other slots and use \(g (x, t) = a (x) \eta_{\epsilon}^{(b)} (t)\), with \(\eta_{\epsilon}\) approximating unit mass and \(b = 0, 1\). If \(0 \not\in \text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} a\), these are spacelike for small \(\epsilon\). Thus both Cauchy data of (18.14) are supported at zero. Weighted integrability bounds their order by \(J\), independently of the other tests.

Each datum depends only on the \(J\)-jet: multiplication by a cutoff in \(B (0, \epsilon)\) leaves the distribution unchanged. For a zero-\(J\)-jet test, Taylor's formula bounds derivatives through order \(J\) by \(O (\epsilon)\); let \(\epsilon \downarrow 0\).

The next paragraph proves a uniqueness implication: a combination of sector amplitudes whose two Cauchy data vanish is zero almost everywhere. It is applied below to a polynomial in the doubling operator applied to \(\textsf{Y}\), not to \(\textsf{Y}\) itself. The massless wave equation separates the frequency branches from these two Cauchy data away from zero momentum. After smearing other slots, each branch is integrable, bounded and holomorphic in its timelike tube, with continuous real boundary. For such a combination with zero Cauchy data, vanishing persists under small translations of the other spacelike tests. Schwarz reflection and holomorphic uniqueness [29, Theorems 11.14 and 10.18] make the zero set invariant under adding \(\mathbb{R}u\) for every future timelike \(u\). These vectors span \(\mathbb{R}^4\), giving vanishing at every real translation. Successive Fourier inversion [29, Theorem 9.11] of the smeared integrable amplitudes gives zero almost everywhere. Countably many shrinking bumps remove the other tests: their transforms are eventually nonzero at each momentum.

Let \(D_j \textsf{Y} (\boldsymbol{k}) = \textsf{Y} (\mathbf{k}_1, \ldots, 2\mathbf{k}_j, \ldots, \mathbf{k}_k)\). In the time-order-\(b\) Cauchy datum (\(b = 0, 1\)), this substitution gives \(2^{- 3 - b}\) times the datum at \(a (x) \mapsto 8 a (2 x)\), from \(d \sigma (2\mathbf{k}) = 4 d \sigma (\mathbf{k})\) and \(|\mathbf{k}|^{1 + b}\). Spatial jets of order \(l\) scale by \(2^{3 + l}\), giving eigenvalues \(2^{l - b}\). Choose a polynomial that annihilates this finite-dimensional dilation action on both Cauchy jets. The uniqueness implication then makes the same polynomial in \(D_j\) annihilate \(\textsf{Y}\): for some \(J'\),

\(\displaystyle \prod_{a = 0}^{J' - 1} (D_j - 2^{a - 1}) \textsf{Y} = 0 \qquad \text{in every slot } j \hspace{0.17em} .\) (18.15)

Growth. For \(R = 2^l\) let \(M_{\textsf{Y}} (R) = \int_{|\mathbf{k}_j | \leqslant R} | \textsf{Y} |^2 d \sigma^{\otimes k}\). By (18.13) and the trace law,

\(\displaystyle M_{\textsf{Y}} (2^l) \leqslant \mu_{\Theta} \{\mathbf{p}^4 \leqslant (k + 1) 2^l \}, \qquad 2^{- 8 l} M_{\textsf{Y}} (2^l) \longrightarrow 0 \hspace{0.17em} .\) (18.16)

For the limit, bound the energy mass by \(e \hspace{0.17em} G_{\Theta} (((k + 1) 2^l)^{- 1})\).

Lagrange interpolation in the commuting \(D_j\) splits \(\textsf{Y}\) into joint eigencomponents \(D_j \textsf{Y}_{\boldsymbol{e}} = 2^{e_j - 1} \textsf{Y}_{\boldsymbol{e}}\). Each is a finite combination of doubled copies, so retains (18.16). Moreover \(M_{\textsf{Y}_{\boldsymbol{e}}} (2^l) = 2^{2 l \sum_j e_j} M_{\textsf{Y}_{\boldsymbol{e}}} (1)\), since \(d \sigma (2\mathbf{k}) = 4 d \sigma (\mathbf{k})\). If \(e_j = 0\), contraction preserves box mass, contradicting local square integrability and absence of an atom at zero unless the component vanishes. Equation (18.16) excludes \(\sum_j e_j \geqslant 4\). Thus \(e_j \geqslant 1\), \(\sum_j e_j \leqslant 3\), and \(k \leqslant 3\), with box mass \(\leqslant CR^6\) for dyadic \(R \geqslant 1\). The vacuum sector vanishes by Lemma 18.1 and \(U \Omega = \Omega_0\).

Summing (18.13) gives \(\mu_{\Theta} \{\mathbf{p}^4 \leqslant R\} \leqslant CR^6\) at dyadic \(R \geqslant 1\). Multiplication by \(R^{- 8} \log^2 R\) gives zero in the limit, contradicting Lemma 18.10 on a nonnegative compact test in \(\{0 <\mathbf{p}^4 < 1, \textsf{m} (\mathbf{p}) > 0\}\).\(\Box\)
