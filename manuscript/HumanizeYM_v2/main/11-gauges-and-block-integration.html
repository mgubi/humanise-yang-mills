11Gauges and block integration

The block map of Definition 5.1 sends a fine-field density to a coarse-field density: it integrates over the fine links \(U\), while its kernel integrates to one over the coarse links \(V\) for each fixed \(U\). The predictor in that kernel averages fine parallel transports in local frames. A fixed axial frame would distinguish an ordering of the axes. We instead minimize a symmetric average of transport energies when the field is sufficiently small, and average over curvature-controlled axial frames elsewhere. Section 11.1 shows that these choices give a measurable, covariant probability kernel; consequently the block integration in Theorem 5.5 preserves the partition function.

The remaining subsections prepare the small-field integration used in Sections 12 and 13. Section 11.2 solves the free block problem: the free action at every level, the free interpolation \(W_0\) from coarse to fine fields, and an exact square completion. Section 11.3 constructs the stationary fine field about a coarse field and integrates the Gaussian fluctuations about it; the determinant, the Haar Jacobian and the inherited loop action form the one-loop action \(\textsf{T}\). Section 11.4 supplies what Section 12 needs to extract the curvature-square coefficient of \(\textsf{T}\): inverse bounds uniform in the block ratio, vertex bounds whose input-dependent part carries a factor \(\ell^{- 1}\), and a Ward identity showing that the predictor contact term (the chain-rule term containing the second derivative of the predictor) does not change that coefficient. Sections 11.5 and 11.6 give the local large-field energy (Lemma 11.12) and the simultaneous differentiated local integral (Lemma 11.14) used in Section 13.

Three uniformities require care.

    (i)

    The frame and predictor cutoffs must work on patches with arbitrarily many sites. Centred frame minimizers satisfy a contraction whose constant does not depend on the patch (Lemma 11.4).

    (ii)

    The free kernels must stay controlled after arbitrarily many blockings. We separate the principal Fourier pole before inverting (Lemma 11.5).

    (iii)

    The determinant comparison must not see the \(\ell^4\) sites of a fine cell. The difference of the two compared operators either factors through coarse spaces or contains the regulator \(\mathfrak{m}_{\mu} = \mu^2 (P_{\mu}^3)^3\), and \(P_{\mu}^3\) is trace class uniformly in \(\ell\) (Lemmas 11.8 and 12.8).

Induction scheme.
Sections 11–13 prove one descending induction on \(m\), started at \(m = N\) by the Wilson representation of Lemma 13.16. The hypothesis of the step \(m + 1 \to m\) has two parts: the path bounds and cancellations of Lemmas 12.3–12.4 at level \(m + 1\), for the input path action of Definition 12.2; and an input curve satisfying Definitions 13.1–13.3 and (5.12). The stationary integration of this section and the determinant comparison of Section 12 first construct the level-\(m\) path arrays; the joint proof after Lemma 12.9 shows that they satisfy Lemmas 12.3–12.4 at level \(m\). Lemmas 13.12 and 13.13 construct the output activities. Lemma 13.14 then fixes the output coupling; its proof uses Lemmas 13.9, 13.12, 13.13 and 13.15, none of which uses Lemma 13.14. Lemma 13.17 closes the step. Curl division (Lemma 12.5) and gauge completion (Lemma 12.7) do not use the induction.

Throughout, use the group and metric of Definition 2.1, the Wilson term (2.1), and the level choices (5.1). At a step \(m + 1 \to m\), put \(j = J_{m + 1}\), \(\ell = \ell_m\) and \(R = (j + 3) \ell\). Unless stated otherwise, periods satisfy the minimum-period requirement in Section 9. All finite operators use counting inner products and are periodizations of the kernels described below. An adjoint at complex momentum means the analytic continuation of the real-momentum adjoint.

11.1The block kernel

Before averaging transports, we express them in common endpoint frames. When the resulting connectors lie close to one another in \(G\), their weighted average lies close to \(G\), and its nearest point of \(G\) serves as a group-valued average. The other branches complete the kernel on the remaining configurations. Figure 11 shows the two cases, and Table 4 lists the tests.

Figure 11. The predictor of a coarse link \(e = (y, a)\). (a) Fine connectors between two blocks, each expressed in the frames at its two endpoints. (b) When the connectors lie close together in \(G\), their weighted average \(\bar{Y} = \sum_z w^{\text{av}}_z Y_{e, z}\) is a matrix just off \(G\); the predictor is its nearest point of \(G\), and the coarse link \(V_e\) fluctuates about it with weight \(e^{- \textsf{b} \beta \text{pr} (V_e Q_e^{- 1})}\). (c) When the connectors are spread out, a single connector \(Y_{e, z}\) is chosen with probability \(w^{\text{av}}_z\) and \(V_e\) fluctuates about it. This branch is part of the definition of the kernel on every configuration where the minimum test fails.

For \(o \in S_4\), let \(T_U (y \to z ; o)\) be the ordered product along the path that changes the four coordinates in the order \(o\), using inverse links for negative steps.

Definition 11.1. (Axial frames) The frame tagged by \((z, o)\), its normalization and its link coordinates are

\(\displaystyle g_y^{z, o} = T_U (z \to y ; o), \qquad h_y^{z, o} = (g_0^{z, o})^{- 1} g_y^{z, o}, \qquad U_{(y, a)}^{z, o} = g_y^{z, o} U (y, a) (g_{y + e_a}^{z, o})^{- 1} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

These frames provide the finite alternatives for the symmetric gauge choice.

In the patch \(\ell y +\mathbb{B}_R\) we abbreviate the frame \(h_{y, r}\) of (5.4) at offset \(r \in \mathbb{B}_R\) to \(h_r\). Here \(\delta_{\text{fr}} > 0\) is the frame cutoff and \(a_{\text{ax}} \geqslant 16\) the axial factor of item 2 of Table 3; patch sums count internal positive links and plaquettes. The frame test below compares transports around loops through the patch centre: under a gauge transformation such a loop transport is conjugated, so its chord is gauge invariant.

Definition 11.2. (Symmetric frame choice) For \(x, y \in \mathbb{B}_R\) and \(o, o_0 \in S_4\) let \(\mathcal{T}_{xy ; o}^{o_0} = g_x^{0, o_0} T_U (x \to y ; o) (g_y^{0, o_0})^{- 1}\), the transport from \(0\) to \(x\) in order \(o_0\), then to \(y\) in order \(o\), then back to \(0\) along the reversed route of order \(o_0\). Tags lie in \(\mathbb{B}_R \times S_4\).

    (a)

    Minimizing branch. If \(\text{ch} (\mathcal{T}_{xy ; o}^{o_0}) < \delta_{\text{fr}}\) for all \(x, y, o, o_0\), the frame \((h_r)_{r \in \mathbb{B}_R}\) is the minimizer with \(h_0 = 1\) of

    \(\displaystyle \frac{1}{24 |\mathbb{B}_R |^2} \sum_{r, r' \in \mathbb{B}_R} \sum_{o \in S_4} \text{pr} (h_r T_U (r \to r' ; o) h_{r'}^{- 1}),\)

    and every tag has mass \(|\mathbb{B}_R \times S_4 |^{- 1}\).

    (b)

    Axial branch. Otherwise a tag \((z, o)\) is eligible if

    \(\displaystyle \sum_{e \text{patch}} \text{ch} (U_e^{z, o}) \leqslant a_{\text{ax}} (2 R + 1) \sum_{\square \text{patch}} \text{ch} (U_{\square}) \hspace{0.17em} ;\)

    the frame is \(h^{z, o}\), and eligible tags have equal mass.

On the minimizing branch the frame does not depend on its tag. For a coarse edge \(e\) use the framed connector \(Y_{e, z}\) of (5.4) and the averaging weights \(w^{\text{av}}\) of (5.5); the weight is supported in \(\mathbb{B}_{j (\ell - 1) / 2} \subset \mathbb{B}_R\).

Definition 11.3. (Coarse predictors) If the minimum in (5.5) is below \(\varepsilon_{\text{pred}}\), then \(Q_e^{\text{pred}}\) is the minimizer there, and predictor tags have uniform mass on \(\mathbb{B}_R\). Otherwise a tag \(z\) selects \(Q_e^{\text{pred}} = Y_{e, z}\), with mass \(w_z^{\text{av}}\). The product of frame and predictor masses is \(\lambda^{\text{tag}} (U, \alpha_{\text{tag}})\). These choices define every summand of Definition 5.1.

The branch choices require constants independent of the patch radius. Their normalization then follows from finite probability sums and Haar invariance.

Lemma 11.4. (Branches and normalization) There are \(\delta_{\text{fr}}, \varepsilon_{\text{pred}} > 0\), depending only on \(G \subset U (n_G)\), such that for every patch \(\mathbb{B}_R\), \(R = (j + 3) \ell\), and every \(a_{\text{ax}} \geqslant 16\):

    (i)

    on the minimizing branch of Definition 11.2, the frame with \(h_0 = 1\) is unique;

    (ii)

    on the minimizing branch of Definition 11.3, the predictor is unique;

    (iii)

    for every \(o \in S_4\) some tag \((z, o)\) is eligible;

    (iv)

    the masses and the kernel are normalized:

    \(\displaystyle \sum_{\alpha_{\text{tag}}} \lambda^{\text{tag}} (U, \alpha_{\text{tag}}) = 1, \qquad \int k^{(m)}_{\beta} (U, V) \hspace{0.17em} dV = 1 \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The proof treats the predictor, the frame and the axial alternatives in turn, then the two normalizations.

Proof. Predictor branch. Work in the real Euclidean space of complex matrices with Frobenius norm. The tubular projection lemma of [12, p. 153, Lemma], applied to the compact smooth embedded submanifold \(G\), gives a neighbourhood with unique smooth nearest-point projection \(\pi\). At the identity its derivative is the orthogonal projection \(P_{\mathfrak{g}}\) onto \(\mathfrak{g}\). Consequently, for any \(\epsilon > 0\), shrinking this neighbourhood gives

\(\displaystyle \| \pi (A) - \pi (B) - P_{\mathfrak{g}} (A - B)\|_{\text{F}} \leqslant \epsilon \|A - B\|_{\text{F}}\) (11.1)

when \(A, B\) lie in a sufficiently small ball about \(1\). Integrate \(D \pi - P_{\mathfrak{g}}\) along their joining segment.

Put \(\bar{Y} = \sum_z w_z^{\text{av}} Y_{e, z}\). Expansion of the square gives

\(\displaystyle 2 n_G \sum_z w_z^{\text{av}} \text{pr} (Y_{e, z} u^{- 1}) = \sum_z w_z^{\text{av}} \|Y_{e, z} - \bar{Y} \|_{\text{F}}^2 + \| \bar{Y} - u\|_{\text{F}}^2 \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Thus the minimizers are precisely the nearest group points to \(\bar{Y}\). If the minimum is below \(\varepsilon_{\text{pred}}\), then \(\text{dist} (\bar{Y}, G) < \sqrt{2 n_G \varepsilon_{\text{pred}}}\). Choose this smaller than a tubular radius. This choice uses neither the weights nor their number.

Frame branch: a contraction uniform in \(R\). First make an origin-based axial change of gauge. Its route matrices, denoted \(\mathcal{T}_{rr' ; o}\), satisfy \(\mathcal{T}_{rr ; o} = 1\) and \(\mathcal{T}_{r' r ; o'} =\mathcal{T}_{rr' ; o}^{- 1}\) for the reversed order \(o'\). On the minimizing branch choose \(\delta_{\text{fr}}\) so that \(\|\mathcal{T}_{rr' ; o} - 1\|_{\text{F}} \leqslant d\), where \(d \leqslant 1 / 4\) will be fixed below. Write \(\text{av}\) for the uniform averages over the indicated indices. A global minimizer \(h\) exists by compactness. Varying its coordinate \(h_r\) and pairing the two orientations shows that \(h_r\) is a nearest group point to

\(\displaystyle F_r (h) = \text{av}_{r', o} \hspace{0.17em} h_{r'} \mathcal{T}_{rr' ; o}^{- 1} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The self term is harmless: replacing it by its value at a proposed competitor adds a nonnegative square. Comparison with the constant frame and the triangle inequality give

\(\displaystyle \text{av}_{r, r'} \|h_r - h_{r'} \|_{\text{F}}^2 \leqslant 4 d^2 \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Left-multiply \(h\) so that \(1\) is a nearest point to its centroid \(\bar{h} = \text{av}_r \hspace{0.17em} h_r\). Jensen's inequality then gives \(\| \bar{h} - 1\|_{\text{F}} \leqslant 2 d\) and \(\|F_r (h) - 1\|_{\text{F}} \leqslant 3 d\).

Center two minimizers \(h, k\) in this way, and put \(u = \max_r \|h_r - k_r \|_{\text{F}}\). Their centroids are normal to \(G\) at \(1\), so \(P_{\mathfrak{g}} (\bar{h} - \bar{k}) = 0\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \|F_r (h) - F_r (k)\|_{\text{F}} \leqslant u, \qquad \|F_r (h) - F_r (k) - (\bar{h} - \bar{k})\|_{\text{F}} \leqslant du \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Apply (11.1) with \(\epsilon = 1 / 4\), choosing \(3 d\) inside its ball. At a coordinate attaining \(u\) it yields \(u \leqslant (d + 1 / 4) u \leqslant u / 2\). Hence the centered minimizers coincide. All minimizers differ by a common left multiple, which \(h_0 = 1\) fixes uniquely. The contraction factor \(d + 1 / 4\) comes from averages only, so neither \(d\) nor \(\delta_{\text{fr}}\) depends on the number of sites.

Axial branch. For a rectangular slice \(X\) let \(\Sigma_{\text{link}} (X, U)\) and \(\Sigma_{\text{plaq}} (X, U)\) be the sums of link and plaquette chords internal to \(X\). We show, by induction on the number \(r\) of varying axes of \(X\), that some axial frame satisfies \(\Sigma_{\text{link}} (X, U^{\text{ax}}) \leqslant 4 r (2 R + 1) \Sigma_{\text{plaq}} (X, U)\). Choose a layer \(X_0\) perpendicular to the last axis whose plaquette sum is at most the slice average, so that \((2 R + 1) \Sigma_{\text{plaq}} (X_0, U) \leqslant \Sigma_{\text{plaq}} (X, U)\), and apply the induction hypothesis there. Extend its frame along the last axis, making those links equal to \(1\). A link in another layer differs from its transported link in \(X_0\) by a product of intervening plaquettes. Each plaquette lies between at most \(2 R\) such pairs. The chord triangle inequality and conjugation invariance, summed over the links, give

\(\displaystyle \Sigma_{\text{link}} (X, U^{\text{ax}}) \leqslant (2 R + 1) \{\Sigma_{\text{link}} (X_0, U^{\text{ax}}) + \Sigma_{\text{plaq}} (X, U)\} \leqslant 4 r (2 R + 1) \Sigma_{\text{plaq}} (X, U) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The second inequality uses the induction bound for \(X_0\) and the choice of the layer. At \(r = 4\) this proves eligibility, for each axis ordering, with factor \(16\).

Normalization. Each frame mass therefore sums to one; each predictor mass sums to one since \(w^{\text{av}} \geqslant 0\) and \(\sum_z w_z^{\text{av}} = 1\). Successively summing the finite tag choices proves the first identity. For fixed tags, every coarse-link factor in Definition 5.1 integrates to one by right invariance of Haar measure. Their product and the tag sum prove the second identity.\(\Box\)

Measurability and covariance of the block kernel.
The branch tests are Borel; unique compact minimizers depend continuously on their data. Axial eligibility is a closed inequality. Thus the finite tag sum is Borel, and it is bounded for fixed \(\beta\) since \(0 \leqslant \text{pr} \leqslant 2\) and the Haar denominator is positive. Gauge transformations act by conjugation on the rooted transports and transform both ends of a connector; lattice isometries permute the roots, orders and offsets, including inversion of reversed transports. The objective functions, eligibility inequalities and tag masses are preserved. Uniqueness on the minimizing branches and reindexing on the other branches therefore give
\(\displaystyle k^{(m)}_{\beta} (U^{\gamma}, V^{\gamma \circ \iota}) = k^{(m)}_{\beta} (U, V), \qquad k^{(m)}_{\beta} (s_{\ell b} U, s_b V) = k^{(m)}_{\beta} (U, V), \qquad \iota (y) = \ell y \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Here \(s_b\) is the pullback by \(x \mapsto Ox + b\), with \(O \in H_4\); \(s_{\ell b}\) uses the same \(O\) on fine sites. Fubini's theorem and Lemma 11.4 give (5.7). Thus the block kernel is measurable, covariant and normalized.

11.2Free quadratic forms

Write \(d_r\) for the forward lattice exterior derivative on \(r\)-forms, on either the fine or coarse lattice as indicated by its input. Thus \((d_1 a)_{ab} (x) = a_b (x + e_a) - a_b (x) - a_a (x + e_b) + a_a (x)\). A history at level \(m\) is the finite sequence \((\ell_i, J_{i + 1})_{m \leqslant i < N}\), read from fine to coarse. Its stages decrease by at most one. Both maps below take fine link fields to coarse link fields: \(\mathcal{Q}_m\) is the derivative of the predictor at the flat field, while

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{M}_m a (e) = \sum_z w_z^{\text{av}} \sum_{r = 0}^{\ell - 1} a (\ell e^- + z + re_{\text{dir} (e)}, \text{dir} (e))\)

is straight-link averaging. Their difference is a gradient, so they give the same coarse curl.

The free problem is Gaussian. At nonzero momentum, let a free fine field \(a\) have diagonal covariance \(\text{diag} (c_a)\); at the top level \(c_a = \lambda^{- 1}\), the Feynman-gauge covariance of the Wilson Gaussian. Its blocked image \(v =\mathcal{M}_m a + \textsf{b}^{- 1 / 2} \xi\), with independent standard noise \(\xi\), again has a diagonal covariance. The gradient difference between the two maps makes this recursion compatible with gauge reduction. Under blocking by \(\ell\), a coarse momentum \(p\) receives contributions from the fine momenta \(\theta_z = (p + 2 \pi z) / \ell\), indexed by \(z \in (\mathbb{Z}/ \ell \mathbb{Z})^4\); these are its aliases. Changing a representative of \(z\) changes \(\theta_z\) by a multiple of \(2 \pi\). Near \(p = 0\) the alias \(z = 0\) carries the massless pole; the other aliases are regular. Aliasing the straight-link filter and adding \(\textsf{b}^{- 1}\) gives the recursion (11.2) below. To write it, set

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} q_a (\theta) = \text{e}^{i \theta_a} - 1, \quad \lambda (\theta) = \sum_a q_a (\theta) q_a (- \theta),\\ r_{\ell} (t) = \ell^{- 1} \sum_{s = 0}^{\ell - 1} \text{e}^{ist}, \quad \hat{w}_{\ell} (\theta) = \prod_a \ell^{- 1} \sum_{|s| \leqslant (\ell - 1) / 2} \text{e}^{is \theta_a} . \end{array}\)

Here \(\hat{w}_{\ell}\) is the symbol of the uniform weight on \(\mathbb{B}_{(\ell - 1) / 2}\), so \(\hat{w}^{\text{av}} = \hat{w}_{\ell}^{\hspace{0.17em} j}\). Starting from \(c_a = \lambda^{- 1}\), update these auxiliary diagonal covariances at each step \(i + 1 \to i\) of the history, with \(\ell = \ell_i\) and \(j = J_{i + 1}\), by

\(\displaystyle c'_a (p) = \textsf{b}^{- 1} + \ell^{- 2} \sum_{z \bmod \ell} \hat{w}_{\ell} (\theta_z)^{2 j} r_{\ell} ((\theta_z)_a) r_{\ell} (- (\theta_z)_a) c_a (\theta_z), \qquad \theta_z = (p + 2 \pi z) / \ell .\) (11.2)

Gauge reduction keeps only the precision of the curl: minimizing \(\sum_a c_a^{- 1} |a_a + q_a \phi |^2\) over gradients \(\phi\) gives \(\langle d_1 a, \textsf{H}^0 d_1 a \rangle\) with the diagonal two-form metric below. For \(p \ne 0\), define this unmodified metric and the chosen metric by

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} \widehat{\textsf{H}}^{\hspace{0.17em} 0}_{ab, cd} (p) = \frac{\boldsymbol{1}_{(a, b) = (c, d)}}{c_a (p) c_b (p) \sum_h q_h (p) q_h (- p) / c_h (p)}, \qquad \widehat{\textsf{H}}^{\hspace{0.17em} 0} (0) = I,\\ \textsf{H}^{- 1} = (\textsf{H}^0)^{- 1} + \zeta_{J_m} (d_2 (\textsf{H}^0)^{- 1})^{\ast} d_2 (\textsf{H}^0)^{- 1} . \end{array}\)

Lemma 11.5 shows that the value at \(p = 0\) is the analytic extension of the formula. Since \(d_2 d_1 = 0\), the modification satisfies \(\textsf{H} d_1 = \textsf{H}^0 d_1\): the free action on curls is unchanged. It changes \(\textsf{H}\) only off the range of \(d_1\), where it makes the adjacent defect (11.3) positive, as the square completion of Lemma 11.12 requires. Fix \(\zeta_k = \max (\zeta^0_{k, k}, \zeta^0_{k + 1, k})\), where the positive thresholds for the two incoming stage pairs are chosen in the proof below. These constants are fixed before \(\gamma_k\) and the clipping coefficients. The ratio threshold also depends on the incoming \(\zeta\). The free action is

\(\displaystyle \textsf{S}^{\text{free}}_{N, m} (a) = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle d_1 a, \textsf{H}_{N, m} d_1 a \rangle, \qquad \textsf{K}_{N, m} = d_1^{\ast} \textsf{H}_{N, m} d_1 .\)

The rooted interpolation \(W_0 = W_{\text{r}}\) maps coarse link fields to fine link fields: \(W_{\text{r}} v\) minimizes the fine free action plus \(\textsf{b} \|\mathcal{Q}_m a - v\|^2 / 2\). The normal equations leave gradients vanishing at block roots undetermined. We choose the smooth rooted representative constructed in Step 6 below; the tree representative is obtained afterwards. We also construct a smooth centred joint representative \(W_{\text{s}}\) there. Its centred coarse-frame component is zero, whereas \(W_{\text{r}}\) is used in the rooted normal equations.

Let \(T\) be the free two-form transfer, a periodized analytic alias kernel from coarse to fine two-forms, constructed in Step 4 of Lemma 11.5. It satisfies

\(\displaystyle T^{\ast} d_1 = d_1 \mathcal{M}_m, \qquad T \textsf{H}_c^0 d_1 = \textsf{H}_f^0 d_1 W_0 .\)

The two conditions are compatible: \(\mathcal{Q}_m -\mathcal{M}_m\) takes values in gradients, \(\textsf{K}_c d_0 = 0\) and \(\textsf{K}_f W_0 =\mathcal{Q}_m^{\ast} \textsf{K}_c\), so

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \langle d_1 \mathcal{M}_m a, \textsf{H}_c^0 d_1 v \rangle & = \langle a, \mathcal{Q}_m^{\ast} \textsf{K}_c v \rangle = \langle a, \textsf{K}_f W_0 v \rangle\\ & = \langle d_1 a, \textsf{H}_f^0 d_1 W_0 v \rangle . \end{array}\)

This pairing determines only the projection onto exact fine two-forms. The construction below also fixes the coclosed component; it is not a componentwise straight-product extension. Lemma 11.5 shows that the adjacent defect factors through \(d_2\) on both sides,

\(\displaystyle \textsf{H}_c (\textsf{H}_c^{- 1} - T^{\ast} \textsf{H}_f^{- 1} T - \textsf{b}^{- 1} d_1 d_1^{\ast}) \textsf{H}_c = d_2^{\ast} \mathcal{W}_3 d_2,\) (11.3)

with the three-form kernel \(\mathcal{W}_3\) given in its proof. The factorization rests on one-leg exterior division. For a translation-invariant kernel \(f\) with coefficients \(f_x\) and a direction \(a\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{f (p) - f (p|_{p_a = 0})}{q_a (p)} = \sum_x f_x \text{e}^{ip \cdot x} \hspace{0.17em} \frac{1 - \text{e}^{- ip_a x_a}}{q_a (p)},\) (11.4)

and each quotient on the right is a finite geometric sum with \(|x_a |\) terms. Applied in a leg in which the kernel is closed, this division writes it through \(d_2\).

Write \(k = J_m\), and let \(C^{\text{tr}}_{j, k}\) be the stage-pair transfer bound obtained below. Proof. Step 1: the covariance recursion and the two-form metric. The straight-link multiplier is \(\ell \hspace{0.17em} \hat{w}_{\ell} (\theta)^j r_{\ell} (\theta_a)\), and \(\mathcal{Q}_m d_0 \phi = d_0 (\phi \circ \iota)\), so gauge reduction commutes with the block step. Taking aliases of \(\text{Cov} (v) =\mathcal{M}_m \text{diag} (c_a) \mathcal{M}_m^{\ast} + \textsf{b}^{- 1} I\) gives (11.2); \(\ell^{- 2} = \ell^{- 4} \ell^2\) combines counting adjoints with the two straight connectors. For \(w_a = 1 / c_a\), minimization of the diagonal precision over gradients gives

\(\displaystyle \min_{\phi} \sum_a w_a |a_a + q_a \phi |^2 = \frac{\sum_{a < b} w_a w_b |q_a a_b - q_b a_a |^2}{\sum_d w_d |q_d |^2} .\)

Indeed the minimizer is \(\phi = - \sum_a w_a \bar{q}_a a_a / \sum_a w_a |q_a |^2\); expanding its square proves the identity. This is precisely the two-form metric defined above.

Step 2: the cumulative filter. For total history dilation \(M\) at stage \(j\), the cumulative averaging filter is

\(\displaystyle f (\theta) = \hat{w}_M (\theta)^j \prod_{\text{stage increases at dilation } M'} \hat{w}_{M'} (\theta) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Successive dilations \(M'\) have ratio at least \(3\). On the enlarged coarse cube \(| \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} p_a | < 5 \pi / 4\), \(| \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} p_a | < \eta\), the geometric-sum formula gives

\(\displaystyle |f ((p + 2 \pi z) / M) | \leqslant C_j \prod_a (1 + |z_a |)^{- j}, \qquad |f (p / M) | \geqslant c_j > 0 \quad (p \text{ real}) \hspace{0.17em} .\) (11.6)

For the extra product, use \(| \hat{w}_{M'} (p / M) | \leqslant \text{e}^{4 \eta M' / M}\) and the corresponding real lower bound \(\text{e}^{- CM' / M}\). The sum of \(M' / M\) is bounded by a geometric series. The identities \(\hat{w}_{\ell} (\theta) \hat{w}_{\ell'} (\ell \theta) = \hat{w}_{\ell \ell'} (\theta)\) and \(r_{\ell} (t) r_{\ell'} (\ell t) = r_{\ell \ell'} (t)\) follow by writing each integer in its quotient and residue classes. They give the cumulative filter. The basic \(\hat{w}_M^{\hspace{0.17em} j}\) factor supplies the displayed alias decay. It also bounds \(r_M ((p + 2 \pi z)_a / M)\) by \(C / (1 + |z_a |)\), with a positive principal lower bound. Thus every nonprincipal alias is damped by the \(j\)-th power of the filter, uniformly in the number of blockings.

Step 3: the principal pole. Separating the principal alias isolates the massless pole; the other aliases and earlier noise can then be bounded before taking reciprocals in the two-form metric. In the iterated (11.2), with \(\lambda_M (p) = M^2 \lambda (p / M)\), this separation reads

\(\displaystyle c_a (p) = \frac{A_a (p)}{\lambda_M (p)} + c^{\text{np}}_a (p), \qquad A_a (p) = f (p / M) f (- p / M) r_M (p_a / M) r_M (- p_a / M) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The nonprincipal part \(c^{\text{np}}_a\) contains nonprincipal aliases, bounded by (11.6) and \(|M^2 \lambda ((p + 2 \pi z) / M) | \geqslant c |z|^2\) for \(z \ne 0\) on a smaller strip. Each earlier noise contributes \(C_j (M')^{- 2}\) by the squared filter bound. Summing these geometric contributions makes \(c^{\text{np}}_a\) analytic and uniformly bounded; it is nonnegative on real momenta.

Put \(B_a = A_a + \lambda_M c^{\text{np}}_a\) and \(F = f (p / M) f (- p / M)\), so \(c_a = B_a / \lambda_M\). The identity for a straight geometric sum implies

\(\displaystyle q_a (p) q_a (- p) F = M^2 q_a (p / M) q_a (- p / M) A_a \hspace{0.17em} .\)

It removes the apparent singularity in

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{h} (p) = F^{- 1} - \sum_a \frac{q_a (p) q_a (- p) c^{\text{np}}_a}{A_a B_a}, \qquad \lambda_M \mathfrak{h}= \sum_a \frac{q_a (p) q_a (- p)}{B_a} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

On the real enlarged cube \(B_a\) is bounded above and below by positive constants. Since \(\lambda (p)\) and \(\lambda_M (p)\) are uniformly comparable there, the last identity bounds \(\mathfrak{h}\) above and below, including \(\mathfrak{h} (0) = 1\). Thin the strip using the uniform analytic bounds. The unmodified plaquette metric is then

\(\displaystyle \widehat{\textsf{H}}^{\hspace{0.17em} 0}_{ab, cd} (p) = \frac{\boldsymbol{1}_{(a, b) = (c, d)}}{\mathfrak{h} (p) B_a (p) B_b (p)} \qquad (a < b, c < d) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Substitution of \(w_a = \lambda_M / B_a\) in the minimization of Step 1 gives exactly \(1 / (\mathfrak{h}B_a B_b)\). Thus this formula extends the defined metric analytically to zero. It and its inverse are uniformly positive on real momenta, with \(\widehat{\textsf{H}}^{\hspace{0.17em} 0} (0) = I\).

Step 4: the inverse modification and the defect. The inverse modification defining \(\textsf{H}\) has fixed stage coefficient \(\zeta_j\). This positive addition has uniform analytic bounds. Since \(d_2 d_1 = 0\), it gives \(\textsf{H} d_1 = \textsf{H}^0 d_1\); it also leaves \(\widehat{\textsf{H}} (0) = I\).

We first construct \(T\) for the unmodified metrics. Fix a nonzero real coarse momentum \(p\), and write \(k_z = (p + 2 \pi z) / \ell\), \(\delta_r = \ell d_r (k_z)\), \(H_z = \widehat{\textsf{H}}_f^0 (k_z)\), \(H = \widehat{\textsf{H}}_c^0 (p)\), \(D_z = \ell^{- 2} \text{diag} (c_{f, a} (k_z))\) and \(D = \widehat{\textsf{C}}_c (p)\). Put

\(\displaystyle F_{r, z} = m_z \bigwedge^r \text{diag} (r_{\ell} ((k_z)_a)), \quad m_z = \hat{w}_{\ell} (k_z)^j, \quad x = d_0 (p), \quad x_z = \delta_0, \quad R = d_1^{\ast} Hd_1 .\)

Stars in this real calculation are Hermitian adjoints. The geometric-sum identity gives \(F_{r + 1, z} \delta_r = d_r F_{r, z}\), and (11.2) becomes \(\sum_z F_{1, z} D_z F_{1, z}^{\ast} = D - \textsf{b}^{- 1} I\). Set

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} \varrho = x^{\ast} D^{- 1} x, \quad \varrho_z = x_z^{\ast} D_z^{- 1} x_z, \quad \pi = \varrho^{- 1} x^{\ast} D^{- 1},\\ e_z = \frac{\bar{m}_z \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} D_z}{\sum_w |m_w |^2 \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} D_w}, \quad y_z = |m_z |^2 \frac{\varrho}{\varrho_z}, \quad C = Hd_1 Dd_1^{\ast} . \end{array}\)

For each alias define the rescaled one- and two-form columns

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_z = D_z F_{1, z}^{\ast} R + e_z x_z \pi, \qquad \mathcal{T}_z = H_z \delta_1 D_z F_{1, z}^{\ast} d_1^{\ast} + y_z F_{2, z}^{\ast} (I - C) .\) (11.7)

The diagonal metric formula of Step 1 gives \(R = D^{- 1} - \varrho^{- 1} D^{- 1} xx^{\ast} D^{- 1}\) and \(Rd_0 = 0\). Consequently \(d_1^{\ast} (I - C) = 0\) and \((I - C) Hd_1 = 0\). The chain identity and its adjoint then give

\(\displaystyle \delta_1^{\ast} \mathcal{T}_z = F_{1, z}^{\ast} d_1^{\ast}, \qquad \mathcal{T}_z Hd_1 = H_z \delta_1 \mathcal{A}_z, \qquad \sum_z F_{1, z} \mathcal{A}_z = I - \textsf{b}^{- 1} R.\)

For example, the complement term vanishes in each of the first two equations; it remains essential for analytic continuation. With the scalar covariance chosen in Step 6, \(\mathcal{A}_z = \ell (W_{\text{s}})_z\) in physical alias amplitudes. Thus \(\delta_1 \mathcal{A}_z = \ell^2 (d_{1, f} W_{\text{s}})_z\), and the same curl is obtained from \(W_{\text{r}}\).

To remove the apparent poles, continue every adjoint by reflection without complex conjugation of the variable. On alias \(z\) the reflection is \(k_z (p) \mapsto - k_z (p)\), equivalently \((p, z) \mapsto (- p, - z)\). Write \(s = \lambda_c\), \(D_{aa} = B_a / s\), \(B_{0, a} = s (D_0)_{aa}\), and split the principal covariance as \(B_a = |m_0 r_{\ell} ((k_0)_a) |^2 B_{0, a} + sU_a\), including the penalty noise in \(U_a\). \(\displaystyle \mathcal{B}= H_0 \left[ \text{diag}_{a < b} (z_a + z_b) - s \bigwedge^2 \text{diag} (z_a) \right] .\)

The covariance split gives \(s\mathcal{B}= H_0 - y_0 F_{2, 0}^{\ast} HF_{2, 0}\), since \(1 - (1 - sz_a) (1 - sz_b) = s (z_a + z_b - sz_a z_b)\). Substitution yields the pole-free expression

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{T}_0 = y_0 F_{2, 0}^{\ast} + (\mathcal{B}Jd_1 M - H_0 Jd_1 U) d_1^{\ast} .\)

The pole separation gives \(\varrho = s^2 h_c\) and \(\varrho_z = \ell^4 \lambda_f (k_z)^2 h_f (k_z)\), with the analytic positive divisors of Step 3. For nonprincipal aliases \(y_z\) therefore contains two powers of \(s\), which cancel the possible poles in \(I - C\). The filter and denominator estimates of Steps 2–3 bound both formulas on a common smaller strip. At \(p = 0\) the rescaled principal columns are \(\mathcal{A}_0 = I\) and \(\mathcal{T}_0 = I\); the other columns vanish because their patch filters vanish. Thus the identities also hold at the harmonic mode.

The defect annihilates curls, for both the unmodified and the modified metrics: by the two conditions on \(T\) and \(\textsf{H}_f^{- 1} \textsf{H}_f^0 d_1 = d_1\), its value on \(d_1 v\) is

For the unmodified metrics, write \(E\) for the full defect on the left of (11.3), with \(\textsf{H}_c, \textsf{H}_f\) replaced by \(\textsf{H}_c^0, \textsf{H}_f^0\). It is closed in both legs. To lift both legs compatibly, first use the coefficientwise division (11.4) and the exterior identity \(d_r \iota_a + \iota_a d_{r + 1} = q_a I\) successively in the four coordinates to obtain \(E = d_2^{\ast} W\). Since \(d_2^{\ast} Wd_1 = 0\), a second left lift gives \(Wd_1 = d_3^{\ast} T_3\). Each coefficientwise division loses only a fixed power of path length, so the exponential bounds leave a smaller strip. Symmetrize the resulting three-form kernel \(F_0\). If \(S_0\) is the right lift of \(d_{2, f} (\textsf{H}_f^0)^{- 1} T \textsf{H}_c^0\) through \(d_{2, c}\), Woodbury inversion gives \(d_2 (\textsf{H}_c^0)^{- 1} \textsf{H}_c = D_c d_2\). \(\displaystyle \mathcal{W}_3 = D_c^{\ast} (F_0 + \zeta_k I - \zeta_j S_0^{\ast} S_0) D_c, \qquad D_c = (I + \zeta_k d_2 (\textsf{H}_c^0)^{- 1} d_2^{\ast})^{- 1} .\)

The pole-free alias estimates give \(\|F_0 \| \leqslant C\) and \(\|S_0 \|^2 \leqslant C^{\text{tr}}_{j, k} / \ell^2\) on real momenta. The unmodified defect has a uniform row bound: its Gram term sums \(\ell^4\) residues, each with two factors of order \(\ell^{- 2}\). Each fine difference commutes with the translation-invariant two-form operator \((\textsf{H}_f^0)^{- 1}\), and \(d_{2, f}\) applies their signed combinations; thus \(d_{2, f} (\textsf{H}_f^0)^{- 1} T\) gains one fine difference. Its lift has per-residue order \(\ell^{- 3}\), whose square is summed over \(\ell^4\) residues. Choose \(\zeta^0_{j, k} = C + 2\) and then \(\ell^2 \geqslant \zeta_j C^{\text{tr}}_{j, k}\) for the stage pair \((j, k)\). The parenthesis is at least \(I\). Both \(D_c\) and its inverse have uniform analytic bounds at the fixed stages. Consequently \(cI \leqslant \mathcal{W}_3 \leqslant CI\) on real momenta, uniformly in the history. This proves (iv); it is the input for the square terms in Lemma 11.12.

Step 5: coercivity and the row factor. Choose \(2 \gamma_k\) below the lower bounds for the top and the two incoming possibilities at stage \(k\). Then \(\textsf{H} - c_{\text{dir}, k} I \geqslant \gamma_k I\). Its positive square root \(\rho\) is analytic on a smaller common strip: expand the square root about a scalar exceeding the common upper bound. The resulting convergent matrix power series agrees with the positive root on the real torus. Contour displacement in Fourier inversion gives exponential coefficient decay and the same bound for all periodizations. This proves (ii) and (iii).

Step 6: the normal equations and the energy identity. The normal equations for (11.2), with the gradient chosen to match the rooted predictor, give

\(\displaystyle \textsf{K}_f W_0 =\mathcal{Q}_m^{\ast} \textsf{K}_c, \qquad \mathcal{Q}_m W_0 = I - \textsf{b}^{- 1} \textsf{K}_c \hspace{0.17em} .\)

At nonzero momentum write \(\textsf{C}_f = \text{diag} (c_a)\) and \(\textsf{C}_c =\mathcal{M}_m \textsf{C}_f \mathcal{M}_m^{\ast} + \textsf{b}^{- 1} I\). We now choose the representatives. Let \(A\) be the scalar average with the same weights as \(\mathcal{M}_m\); straight-path telescoping gives \(\mathcal{M}_m d_f = d_c A\). Put \(s_f = \tfrac{1}{4} \text{tr} \textsf{C}_f\) and

\(\displaystyle L = s_f A^{\ast} (As_f A^{\ast})^{- 1}, \quad W_F = \textsf{C}_f \mathcal{M}_m^{\ast} \textsf{C}_c^{- 1}, \quad \eta = \frac{d_c^{\ast} \textsf{C}_c^{- 1}}{d_c^{\ast} \textsf{C}_c^{- 1} d_c}, \quad W_{\text{s}} = W_F - (W_F d_c - d_f L) \eta .\)

The adjoints in these Fourier formulas use reflected momenta. On the real slice the scalar lift obeys \(AL = I\). With \(\textsf{K}_c = \textsf{C}_c^{- 1} - \textsf{C}_c^{- 1} d_c \eta\), the identities \(\mathcal{M}_m W_F = I - \textsf{b}^{- 1} \textsf{C}_c^{- 1}\) and \(\textsf{K}_c d_c = 0\) give

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{M}_m W_{\text{s}} = I - \textsf{b}^{- 1} \textsf{K}_c, \qquad \textsf{K}_f W_{\text{s}} =\mathcal{M}_m^{\ast} \textsf{K}_c .\) (11.8)

For the second identity, \(W_{\text{s}} = \textsf{C}_f \mathcal{M}_m^{\ast} \textsf{K}_c + d_f L \eta\). The covector \(\mathcal{M}_m^{\ast} \textsf{K}_c v\) annihilates fine gradients because \(\mathcal{M}_m d_f = d_c A\) and \(\textsf{K}_c d_c = 0\); on such covectors \(\textsf{K}_f \textsf{C}_f\) is the identity. The formula extends through zero. To see the cancellation, write \(\lambda_c = \ell^2 \lambda_f (p / \ell)\) for the inherited principal pole, \(\textsf{C}_c^{- 1} = \lambda_c D\), and \(\eta = q_c (- p)^T D / (\lambda_c h)\), where \(h = \lambda_c^{- 1} q_c (- p)^T Dq_c (p)\) is the pole-free divisor of Step 3. Put \(f_0 = \hat{w}_{\ell} (p / \ell)^j\), \(m_{0, a} = \ell f_0 r_{\ell} (p_a / \ell)\), \(B_{f, a} = \lambda_f c_{f, a}\) and \(B_f^s = \lambda_f s_f\). Separate the principal alias by

\(\displaystyle B_a^c = \ell^{- 2} B_{f, a} m_{0, a} m_{0, a}^- + \lambda_c R_a, \qquad B_s^c = B_f^s f_0 f_0^- + \lambda_c R_s,\)

where a superscript minus denotes evaluation at reflected momentum. Here \(R_a\) is the unscaled nonprincipal remainder of the coarse link covariance, including the penalty noise, while \(R_s\) is \(\ell^2\) times the nonprincipal remainder of \(As_f A^{\ast}\). The straight-sum identity gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{(W_F q_c)_{0, a} - q_{f, a} L_0}{\lambda_c} = \frac{q_{f, a}}{f_0} \left( \frac{R_s}{B_s^c} - \frac{R_a}{B_a^c} \right),\)

Here \(B_a^c = \lambda_c (\textsf{C}_c)_{aa}\) and \(B_s^c = \ell^2 \lambda_c As_f A^{\ast}\). Both denominators have uniform positive real lower bounds after their indicated normalization, and the nonprincipal remainders have the bounds from Step 2. Nonprincipal aliases have the same factor \(\lambda_c\) directly. Thus the quotient is analytic on a common smaller strip, including the complex zero set of \(\lambda_c\). The periodic covariance \(s_f\) makes the scalar lift compatible with alias relabelling at cell boundaries. The resulting alias sum gives the smooth bounds stated in Lemma 11.6(iv), also for \(W_{\text{s}}\).

Let \(\mathcal{F}_m\) be the frame functional of Lemma 11.6, so \(\mathcal{Q}_m =\mathcal{M}_m + d_c \mathcal{F}_m\). Choose a smooth compactly supported cardinal scalar interpolation \(I_{\ell}\) from coarse sites to fine sites, with identity restriction at block roots and fine \(r\)th differences bounded by \(C_r \ell^{- r}\). For instance, normalize an even smooth bump positive on \((- 1, 1)\) and supported in \([- 1, 1]\) by the sum of its integer translates, take the same tensor factor in each coordinate, and periodize; this also preserves signed permutations. Define

\(\displaystyle W_{\text{r}} = W_{\text{s}} - d_f I_{\ell} (\mathcal{F}_m W_{\text{s}}) .\) (11.9)

The frame functional is a normalized average of routes of length \(O_j (\ell)\), so \(\mathcal{F}_m W_{\text{s}}\) has a uniformly bounded, exponentially local coarse kernel. The interpolation therefore preserves the fine-difference bounds. Since \(\mathcal{Q}_m d_f \phi = d_c (\phi |_{\text{roots}})\), the first identity in (11.8) gives \(\mathcal{Q}_m W_{\text{r}} = I - \textsf{b}^{- 1} \textsf{K}_c\); the second gives \(\textsf{K}_f W_{\text{r}} =\mathcal{Q}_m^{\ast} \textsf{K}_c\). \(\displaystyle \langle a, \textsf{K}_f a \rangle + \textsf{b} \|\mathcal{Q}_m a - v\|^2 = \langle v, \textsf{K}_c v \rangle + \langle a - W_0 v, (\textsf{K}_f + \textsf{b} \mathcal{Q}_m^{\ast} \mathcal{Q}_m) (a - W_0 v) \rangle \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Set \(a = W_0 v\) and polarize. This is (i). The empty history gives \(\textsf{H}^0 = I\) and hence the Wilson form on curls, also after the inverse modification.

Step 7: the cutoff limit. Choose \(c > 0\) below the common decay rate of the forms, inverses, row factors and the kernels \(W_0, T\). For fixed \(m\) let \(N \to \infty\), so the cumulative dilation \(M \to \infty\). At each nonzero real momentum \(p\), \(\lambda_M (p) \to |p|^2\); \(A_a (p)\) converges as a product of filter factors whose deviations from one are summable; the nonprincipal aliases converge by dominated convergence with (11.6); and the noise contributions form a convergent series since \(M\) at least doubles at each step. Hence the symbols of \(\textsf{H}^0\), \(\textsf{H}^{\pm 1}\), \(W_{\text{s}}\), \(W_0\) and \(T\) converge at every such \(p\), and so does that of \(\rho\) by continuity of the positive square root. The uniform strip bounds make the coefficient arrays precompact under diagonal extraction; an array with these bounds is determined by its symbol at nonzero real momenta, so all subsequential limits coincide, and dominated convergence gives convergence at every smaller width. This proves (11.5). Absolute summation also gives convergence of every fixed periodization.\(\Box\)

11.3The stationary background and Gaussian coordinates

Tree gauge.
Given a spanning tree of a set of sites, rooted at \(x_0\), let \(g_x\) be the ordered product of the links along the tree path from \(x_0\) to \(x\), and apply the gauge transformation \(U (x, a) \mapsto g_x U (x, a) g_{x + e_a}^{- 1}\). Every tree link becomes \(1\). The axial frames of Definition 11.1 are of this form, for the tree of coordinate routes in the order \(o\). Figure 12 shows one plaquette. With root \(y\) and tree links \((y, 1)\), \((y + e_1, 2)\), \((y, 2)\), one has \(g_y = 1\), \(g_{y + e_1} = U (y, 1)\), \(g_{y + e_1 + e_2} = U (y, 1) U (y + e_1, 2)\) and \(g_{y + e_2} = U (y, 2)\), so the remaining link becomes
\(\displaystyle g_{y + e_2} U (y + e_2, 1) g_{y + e_1 + e_2}^{- 1} = U_{y ; 12}^{- 1}, \qquad U_{y ; 12} = U (y, 1) U (y + e_1, 2) U (y + e_2, 1)^{- 1} U (y, 2)^{- 1} .\)

Its chord is the plaquette chord, and a change of frame \(k\) at the root replaces it by \(kU_{y ; 12}^{- 1} k^{- 1}\). In a larger block, every non-tree link inside the block becomes the holonomy of a closed loop, a product of conjugated plaquette holonomies of a lattice surface spanning that loop; its chord is at most the sum of their chords. Lemma 11.4 (axial branch) proves the summed form of this bound, and the coercivity step in the proof of Lemma 11.6 is its linearization. A link joining two blocks becomes a transport between the two block roots and is compared with the coarse link. The gauge choice therefore does not make the remaining links small: their size is that of the curvature and of the coarse field.

Block norms.
A fine field that is smooth on the block scale has energy of order \(\mu^2 \|u\|^2\), with \(\mu = \ell^{- 1}\), so ordinary \(\ell^2\) norms would lose a factor of order \(\ell^2\) in inverse bounds. The block norm removes this loss by weighting each fine Fourier alias by \((\mu^2 + |q|^2)^{1 / 2}\). For this finite Fourier matrix use the residue representatives \(x, z \in \{0, \ldots, \ell - 1\}^4\); the kernel estimates below use centred representatives instead. Put \(\mu = \ell^{- 1}\), \(\theta_z (\xi) = (\xi + 2 \pi z) / \ell\), and
\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} \omega_z (\xi) = \left( \mu^2 + \sum_a |q_a (\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \theta_z (\xi)) |^2 \right)^{1 / 2},\\ \mathcal{U}_{\ell} (\xi)_{xz} = \ell^{- 2} \text{e}^{i \theta_z (\xi) \cdot x}, \quad T_{\ell} (\xi) =\mathcal{U}_{\ell} (\xi) \text{diag} (\omega_z (\xi)^{- 1}) \mathcal{U}_{\ell} (0)^{- 1} \hspace{0.17em} . \end{array}\)

A fine Bloch field at coarse momentum \(\xi\) obeys \(u (x + \ell n) = \text{e}^{i \xi \cdot n} \hspace{0.17em} u (x)\), and \(\hat{u} =\mathcal{U}_{\ell} (\xi)^{- 1} u\) are its alias coordinates on the fixed cell. For real \(\xi\) the block norm and its dual are

\(\displaystyle \|u\|_{\text{bl}}^2 = \sum_z \omega_z (\xi)^2 | \hat{u}_z |^2, \qquad \|f\|_{\text{bl}, \ast}^2 = \sum_z \omega_z (\xi)^{- 2} | \hat{f}_z |^2,\)

the fine \(H^1\) norm with mass \(\mu\) and its dual, in Bloch coordinates; coarse coordinates carry the Euclidean norm. Since \(\mathcal{U}_{\ell} (\xi)\) is unitary for real \(\xi\), \(\|u\|_{\text{bl}} = \|T_{\ell} (\xi)^{- 1} u\|\); the last factor of \(T_{\ell}\) fixes the reference cell. On the strip \(\mathcal{S}_{\eta} = \{\xi \in \mathbb{C}^4 : | \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} \xi_a | < \eta\}\) we use this formula as the definition: \(T_{\ell}\) on fine links or sites, the identity on coarse coordinates, direct sums on products, and \(T_{\ell} (- \xi)^{- T}\) on dual fields. Axial variables inherit the norm of their slice embedding. For kernels set

\(\displaystyle \|K\|_{\eta}^{\text{bl}} = \sup \|T_{\text{out}} (\xi_{\text{out}})^{- 1} K (\xi_{\text{out}}, \xi_{\text{in}}) T_{\text{in}} (\xi_{\text{in}})\|_{\text{op}} \hspace{0.17em},\)

the supremum over momenta whose indicated shifts all lie in \(\mathcal{S}_{\eta}\). Use tensor products for several inputs; field insertions are normalized coarse directions. The weights use \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \theta_z\) and need not be analytic. Write \(W_{\text{ax}} v = (v, D \psi (0) v)\) for the axial tangent, and retain \(W_0\) for the smooth free interpolation before axial gauge. They are distinct coordinate representatives.

The next lemma supplies the stationary configuration and identifies its first derivative with the free solution. Let \(\nabla_f^r\) denote any product of \(r\) forward fine differences. Fix an integer \(r_0 \geqslant 0\) with \(r_0 \leqslant 2\) or \(j \geqslant r_0 + 3\), and let \(x\) be any centred fine residue. The constants \(c_j > 0\) and \(C_{j, r}\) depend on the stage and derivative order, but not on the ratio, history or period.

Lemma 11.6. (Stationary interpolation)

    (i)

    Near zero there is an analytic stationary branch with

    \(\displaystyle D_{\psi} S (v, \psi (v)) = 0, \quad \psi (0) = 0, \quad \textsf{Q} \geqslant \nu_{j, \ell} I \hspace{0.17em},\)

    where \(\nu_{j, \ell} > 0\) does not depend on the period.

    (ii)

    \(D \psi (0)\) is the axial representative of the free interpolation: \(\text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} v + \iota_{\text{ax}} D \psi (0) v\) is the tree representative of \(W_0 v\).

    (iii)

    The averaging symbol equals one at zero and vanishes to order at least \(j\) at every nonzero alias.

    (iv)

    For \(0 \leqslant r \leqslant r_0\) and every bounded coarse field \(v\), the following bounds hold for \(W_0 = W_{\text{r}}\) and, with \(W_0\) replaced by \(W_{\text{s}}\), for the centred representative:

    \(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} | \nabla_f^r W_0 (\ell y + x, y') | \leqslant C_{j, r} \ell^{- 1 - r} e^{- c_j |y - y' |},\\ \| \nabla_f^r W_0 v\|_{\infty} \leqslant C_{j, r} \ell^{- 1 - r} \|v\|_{\infty} . \end{array}\)

The proof uses a local axial estimate for coercivity, a finite-dimensional implicit equation for the branch, and the alias decay of the averaging symbol for the kernel bounds.

Proof. The minimizing choices are analytic. At the flat field the frame objective has quadratic part \(\text{av}_{r, r'} |h_r - h_{r'} |^2 / 2\). On \(h_0 = 0\) this is positive definite. The predictor objective has Hessian \(I\) in its group variable. Their chart equations are real analytic. Their local complexifications and the analytic implicit function theorem make both minimizing choices real analytic near zero. Apply [20, Theorem 1.3.8] to the complexified chart equations, which vanish at zero and have the displayed invertible Hessians. The neighbourhoods may depend on the period.

For a fine infinitesimal field \(a\), let \(I_a (x \to x' ; o)\) be its signed sum along the route. Differentiating the frame equation gives

\(\displaystyle \tau_y (r) = - \text{av}_{r', o} \hspace{0.17em} I_a (\ell y + r \to \ell y + r' ; o), \quad h_{y, r}' = \tau_y (r) - \tau_y (0), \quad \mathcal{Q}_m a =\mathcal{M}_m a + d_0 \mathcal{F}_m a \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Here \(\mathcal{F}_m a (y) = \tau_y (0) - \sum_r w_r^{\text{av}} \tau_y (r)\). In particular \(\mathcal{Q}_m d_0 \phi = d_0 (\phi \circ \iota)\).

Coercivity on the axial slice. Telescoping the boundary of each coordinate rectangle expresses a free link as a block-constant coarse-link value plus curls in its enlarged block. Averaging the straight connectors replaces the coarse-link value by \(\mathcal{Q}_m a\); the frame differences are route sums of the same curls. For example, interchanging two positive steps gives

\(\displaystyle a_a (x) + a_b (x + e_a) - a_b (x) - a_a (x + e_b) = (d_1 a)_{ab} (x) .\)

Reverse steps change the sign and translate this identity. Repeated interchanges compare the tree route, a straight connector and its averaged translates. Thus, at each free edge \(e\),

\(\displaystyle |a_e | \leqslant C_{j, \ell} \sum_{\square \in \ell \hspace{0.17em} \text{parent} (e) +\mathbb{B}_{(j + 7) \ell}} | (d_1 a)_{\square} | + | (\mathcal{Q}_m a)_{\text{parent} (e), \text{dir} (e)} | \hspace{0.17em} .\)

This is an identity followed by a triangle inequality: tree-edge coordinates are zero, and every interchange of two successive route steps contributes exactly one signed curl. Cauchy–Schwarz and the bounded number of enlarged blocks containing a given plaquette give constants \(A_{j, \ell}, B_{j, \ell} < \infty\), independent of the period, with

\(\displaystyle \|a\|_2^2 \leqslant A_{j, \ell} \|d_1 a\|_2^2 + B_{j, \ell} \|\mathcal{Q}_m a\|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The row construction of Section 12.2, at the finer input level, identifies the leading Hessian with \(\textsf{K}_f = d_1^{\ast} \textsf{H}_f d_1\); Lemma 12.4 fixes its normalization at zero momentum. Cubic and quartic curvature atoms have zero quadratic derivative. Consequently, for the slice embedding \(\iota_{\text{ax}}\),

\(\displaystyle \langle \psi, \textsf{Q} \psi \rangle = \langle d_1 \iota_{\text{ax}} \psi, \textsf{H}_f d_1 \iota_{\text{ax}} \psi \rangle + \textsf{b} \|\mathcal{Q}_m \iota_{\text{ax}} \psi \|^2 \geqslant \nu_{j, \ell} \| \psi \|^2 \hspace{0.17em},\)

with \(\nu_{j, \ell} = \min (2 \gamma_j, \textsf{b}) / (1 + A_{j, \ell} + B_{j, \ell})\) (Lemma 11.5(ii)).

The branch and its tangent. The map \(\mathcal{E} (v, \psi) = D_{\psi} S (v, \psi)\) is analytic, \(\mathcal{E} (0, 0) = 0\), and \(D_{\psi} \mathcal{E} (0, 0) = \textsf{Q}\). The same implicit function theorem gives \(\psi (v)\). Differentiation yields

\(\displaystyle D \psi (0) = - \textsf{Q}^{- 1} D_v D_{\psi} S (0, 0) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

To identify this tangent, note that at the flat field the quadratic part of \(S (v, \cdot)\) in the fine field \(a = \text{rep} \hspace{0.17em} v + \iota_{\text{ax}} \psi\) is one half of \(\langle a, \textsf{K}_f a \rangle + \textsf{b} \|\mathcal{Q}_m a - v\|^2\). By the energy identity (Step 6 of the proof of Lemma 11.5), \(W_0 v\) minimizes it over all fine fields; a gradient \(d_0 \phi\) with \(\phi\) vanishing at the block roots changes neither term, and exactly one such modification of \(W_0 v\) has zero tree links. By coercivity it is the unique minimizer over the slice, which proves (ii). In centred coordinates \(c' (a) = -\mathcal{F}_m a\), so the centred tangent \((W_{\text{s}} v, 0)\) has rooted-frame component \(\mathcal{F}_m W_{\text{s}} v\). The joint gauge transformation with parameter \(I_{\ell} (\mathcal{F}_m W_{\text{s}} v)\) changes this pair to \((W_{\text{r}} v, 0)\) at the same coarse input. Tree gauge is applied after this transformation. In particular the tree projections of the two fine fields alone need not coincide. The implicit neighbourhood may depend on the volume; the coercivity constant does not.

Kernel bounds. First apply the alias calculation of Lemma 11.5 to \(W_{\text{s}}\); equation (11.9) then gives the same fine-difference bounds for \(W_0 = W_{\text{r}}\). Finally \(\hat{w}^{\text{av}} (\theta) = \hat{w}_{\ell} (\theta)^j\). At a nonzero alias \(2 \pi z / \ell\), at least one centred geometric sum has a simple zero, so the product has a zero of order at least \(j \geqslant 4\). At zero it equals one. For a word of \(r\) fine differences the alias multiplier is a product of \(r\) factors \(q_{a_h} (\theta_z)\), each bounded by \(C \ell^{- 1} (1 + |z|)\). This includes a difference crossing a fine cell boundary: its Bloch phase shifts the coarse base and gives exactly the same multiplier. After separating the principal pole as in Lemma 11.5, the interpolation alias has the bound \(C_j \ell^{- 1} \prod_{a = 1}^4 (1 + |z_a |)^{- j}\) on a common smaller strip. The centred-residue exponential is bounded there. Consequently the sum of absolute alias terms after \(r\) differences is at most

\(\displaystyle C_{j, r} \ell^{- 1 - r} \sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^4} (1 + |z|)^r \prod_{a = 1}^4 (1 + |z_a |)^{- j} \leqslant C'_{j, r} \ell^{- 1 - r} \qquad (r \leqslant j - 2) .\)

Indeed \((1 + |z|)^r \leqslant C_r \prod_a (1 + |z_a |)^r\) and each remaining exponent is at least two. The pole-free principal term has the same bound. This proves the required symbol estimate through order two for \(j \geqslant 4\), and through order \(r_0\) for \(j \geqslant r_0 + 3\). There are finitely many difference words at a fixed stage; take a common smaller strip for them. Shifting the four coarse Fourier contours towards the sign of the displacement gives the first bound in (iv). It includes all link components and is uniform in the centred fine residue \(x\). Summing over \(y'\) proves the second bound. Periodization preserves the row bound by the triangle inequality.

The same kernel bound permits polynomially growing inputs. For \(0 \leqslant c' < c_j\) and \(h \geqslant 0\) put

\(\displaystyle w_{c', h} (y) = e^{c' |y|} (1 + |y|)^h, \qquad \|v\|_{c', h} = \sup_y \frac{|v (y) |}{w_{c', h} (y)} .\)

The triangle inequality gives \(w_{c', h} (y') / w_{c', h} (y) \leqslant e^{c' |y - y' |} (1 + |y - y' |)^h\). Thus absolute kernel summation yields

\(\displaystyle \sup_{y, x} \frac{| \nabla_f^r W_0 v (\ell y + x) |}{w_{c', h} (y)} \leqslant C_{j, r, c', h} \ell^{- 1 - r} \|v\|_{c', h} .\) (11.10)

The sum converges since \(c' < c_j\). In particular (11.10) applies to polynomial jets of any fixed degree and to a cubic remainder when \(j \geqslant 6\).\(\Box\)

Gaussian coordinates.
Write \(U_v = U (v, \psi (v))\) and \(\textsf{Q} (v) = D_{\psi}^2 S (v, \psi (v))\). Let \(s\) change axial logarithmic increments to left increments at \(U_v\), and let \(J_{\text{ax}}\) be the product Haar density in these coordinates divided by its value at zero. For \(x = \ell y + r\) in its centred block, set \(\Psi (U, h_{\text{fr}}) (x) = h_{\text{fr}} (y) h_{y, r} (U)\), using the patch frame of Definition 11.2. This extends a coarse frame \(h_{\text{fr}}\) over each fine block. The joint variable is \((U, h_{\text{fr}})\), with gauge action \((U, h_{\text{fr}}) \mapsto (U^{\gamma}, h_{\text{fr}} (\gamma \circ \iota)^{- 1})\). Let \(\mathcal{R}\) insert fine site gauge parameters, and let \(\mathcal{I}\) embed free-edge increments, with zero coarse-frame component. Thus \((\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{I}) : \mathfrak{g}^{\text{fine sites}} \oplus \mathfrak{g}^{\text{free edges}} \to \mathfrak{g}^{\text{fine edges}} \oplus \mathfrak{g}^{\text{coarse sites}}\) is square: the number of tree edges is the number of fine sites minus the number of block roots. Write \(\textsf{J} (U, h_{\text{fr}})\) for the joint left Hessian of the leading action plus block penalty, with the coarse field fixed. We evaluate it along the stationary background; its value at the flat field has fine kinetic part \(\textsf{K}_f\). Put
\(\displaystyle X = D_{w = 0} [- \log (\Psi (U, h_{\text{fr}})^{- 1} \Psi (\text{e}^w (U, h_{\text{fr}})))], \qquad \mathcal{L}_{m + 1} = \sum_{x, i} \mathfrak{a}_i^{(2)} (\hspace{0.17em} \cdot \hspace{0.17em}, x) \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The last sum is the inherited quadratic loop action of Definition 12.2. The one-loop action is

\(\displaystyle \textsf{T} (v) =\mathcal{L}_{m + 1} (U_v) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det \textsf{Q} (v) - \log J_{\text{ax}} (v) .\)

It is the negative logarithm of the field-dependent Gaussian factor.

The determinant and coordinate density must be compared in the same counting coordinates. Let \(n\) be the number of real axial coordinates.

Lemma 11.7. (Gaussian coordinates) Near \(v = 0\) the Hessian \(\textsf{Q} (v)\) is positive,

\(\displaystyle J_{\text{ax}} = | \det Ds| > 0, \qquad J_{\text{ax}} (0) = 1, \qquad X\mathcal{R}= I, \qquad \det (\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{I})^2 = 1,\)

and, for every \(\beta > 0\),

\(\displaystyle J_{\text{ax}} (v) \hspace{0.17em} \text{e}^{-\mathcal{L}_{m + 1} (U_v)} \int \text{e}^{- \frac{\beta}{2} \langle w, \textsf{Q} (v) w \rangle} \hspace{0.17em} dw = \left( \frac{2 \pi}{\beta} \right)^{n / 2} \text{e}^{- \textsf{T} (v)} .\)

Thus the stationary Gaussian integral, including the inherited loop action and the Haar density, has field-dependent factor \(\text{e}^{- \textsf{T} (v)}\).

The proof computes the Haar density in axial coordinates at the stationary point. Lemma 12.7 then rewrites the determinant in gauge-completed form; (11.13) below collects the resulting normalization.

Proof. Tree gauge is an exact Haar change of variables. In tree order, each tree link is a Haar translate of its child gauge variable, whose integral is one. For fixed \(V\), gauge transformations fixing every block root leave the kernel of Definition 5.1 invariant. Integration of these gauge variables reduces its integral to product Haar measure on free links; root transformations are not integrated here. In exponential coordinates write this measure as \(J_{\ast}^{n_e} \prod_e J (\psi_e) \hspace{0.17em} d \psi\), with \(J (0) = 1\) and \(n_e\) the number of free links.

At a fixed stationary axial point \(y = \psi (v)\) the coordinate change on each free link is

\(\displaystyle s_y (\psi)_e = \log (\exp \psi_e \exp (- y_e)), \qquad s_y (y) = 0 \hspace{0.17em} .\)

Right invariance of Haar measure gives \(\prod_e J (\psi_e) = | \det Ds_y (\psi) | \prod_e J (s_y (\psi)_e)\). Evaluate at \(\psi = y\) to obtain \(J_{\text{ax}} (v) = | \det Ds_y (y) |\). Since \(Ds_0 (0) = I\), its value at zero is one, and it stays positive nearby. Stationarity removes the second-derivative term in the Hessian chain rule, giving

\(\displaystyle \textsf{Q} (v) = (Ds)^{\ast} (\mathcal{I}^{\ast} \textsf{J} \mathcal{I}) Ds\) (11.11)

for the joint left Hessian \(\textsf{J}\).

Gauge covariance of \(\Psi\) gives \(X\mathcal{R}= I\). In tree-height order, each tree-edge row of \((\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{I})\) has its child gauge coordinate \(\pm I\) and earlier gauge coordinates; each coarse-frame row has its root gauge coordinate \(- I\), and every free-edge slice column has its identity entry. Thus \(\det (\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{I})^2 = 1\). Positivity of \(\textsf{Q} (0)\) from Lemma 11.6 persists near zero.

The Gaussian integral of the positive form \(\frac{\beta}{2} \langle w, \textsf{Q} (v) w \rangle\) equals \((2 \pi / \beta)^{n / 2} (\det \textsf{Q} (v))^{- 1 / 2}\). Multiplying by \(J_{\text{ax}} (v) \text{e}^{-\mathcal{L}_{m + 1} (U_v)}\) gives the displayed identity by the definition of \(\textsf{T}\). The factors \(J_{\ast}^{n_e}\) and \((2 \pi / \beta)^{n / 2}\) do not depend on \(v\); they enter the scalar normalization.\(\Box\)

11.4Inverse, vertex and Ward estimates

Section 12 extracts the curvature-square coefficient of \(\textsf{T}\) by comparing it with an auxiliary fine determinant. This subsection supplies three inputs for that comparison: inverse bounds uniform in the block ratio (Lemma 11.8), vertex bounds (Lemma 11.9), and a Ward identity for the predictor contact term (Lemma 11.10).

The regulator.
For a fine site field put
\(\displaystyle (d_U \phi)_e = \phi (e^-) - \text{Ad} (U_e) \phi (e^+), \quad \Delta_U = d_U^{\ast} d_U, \quad P_{\mu} = \mu^2 (\Delta_U + \mu^2 I)^{- 1}, \quad \mathfrak{m}_{\mu} = \mu^2 P_{\mu}^9 \hspace{0.17em},\)

with \(\mu = \ell^{- 1}\). The regulator \(\mathfrak{m}_{\mu}\) acts as a mass at the block scale: by (11.15) below, \(\Delta_0 +\mathfrak{m}_{\mu} \geqslant 2^{- 10} (\Delta_0 + \mu^2)\), so below momentum \(\mu\) it is a mass of order \(\mu^2\), while above \(\mu\) it decays like \(\mu^2 (\mu / |k|)^{18}\). The ninth power is chosen because \(P_{\mu}^3\) is trace class at each coarse momentum, uniformly in \(\ell\) (Lemma 11.8(i)). After two field derivatives of \(\mathfrak{m}_{\mu} = \mu^2 (P_{\mu}^3)^3\) one undifferentiated factor \(P_{\mu}^3\) remains, and its trace norm keeps the \(\ell^4\) fine aliases out of the determinant comparison.

The predictor contact term.
At the stationary background for \(v\), let \(Q_v (w)\) be the framed predictor in left increments \(w\) of the joint fine-link and frame variables. Set \(Q_0 (v) = Q_v (0)\) and use the output chart based at this predictor:
\(\displaystyle p_v (w) = \log (Q_v (w) Q_0 (v)^{- 1}), \qquad \text{Pen}_v (p) = \textsf{b} \sum_e \text{pr} \left( (\exp v)_e (\exp p_e \hspace{0.17em} Q_{0, e} (v))^{- 1} \right) .\)

The base \(Q_0 (v)\) is held fixed when differentiating in \(w\); derivatives in \(v\) also differentiate this base. In these coordinates the penalty is unchanged. The second-derivative chain rule at \(w = 0\) gives

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} D_w^2 (\text{Pen}_v \circ p_v) & = H^{\ast} BH + \sum_{\lambda} d^{\lambda} C_{\lambda},\\ H & = D_w p_v, \quad B = D_p^2 \text{Pen}_v (0), \quad d = D_p \text{Pen}_v (0), \quad C_{\lambda} = D^2_w (p_v)_{\lambda} . \end{aligned}\) (11.12)

The index \(\lambda\) comprises a coarse link and a colour index. The last sum is the predictor contact term; this use of the word is unrelated to the contact extensions of Theorem 6.21. The internal derivatives defining \(H\) and \(C_{\lambda}\) use the rooted left increments. In centred fibres their tensors and \(\textsf{C}_{\mu}^{\text{rem}}\) are pulled back by the same triangular tangent at each background. In particular, \(C_{\lambda}\) is not replaced by the raw second derivative in a nonlinear centred chart. Along the stationary field, \(d (0) = 0\) and \(Dd (0) = - \textsf{K}_c\): at zero the based and absolute output charts have the same first derivative, and the normal equation gives \(\textsf{b} (I -\mathcal{Q}_m W_0) = \textsf{K}_c\) (Step 6 of the proof of Lemma 11.5).

The compared operators.
The gauge-completed Hessian, the ghost operator and the auxiliary fine operator are
\(\displaystyle \textsf{C}_{\mu} = \textsf{J} + F_{\mu}^{\ast} F_{\mu}, \quad F_{\mu} = d_U^{\ast} P_f +\mathfrak{m}_{\mu} X, \qquad \textsf{G}_{\mu} = \Delta_U +\mathfrak{m}_{\mu}, \qquad \textsf{A}^{\text{aux}}_{\mu} = \textsf{A} + d_U d_U^{\ast} + \textsf{M}_{\mu},\)

where \(P_f\) projects to fine links, \(\textsf{A} (U)\) is the fine leading Hessian and \(\textsf{M}_{\mu}\) applies \(\mathfrak{m}_{\mu}\) to each link component. Frame covariance gives \(F_{\mu} \mathcal{R}= \textsf{G}_{\mu}\). Write \(\textsf{C}^{\text{rem}}_{\mu}\) for \(\textsf{C}_{\mu}\) with the contact term of (11.12) removed; at the flat field \(\textsf{C}^{\text{rem}}_{\mu} = \textsf{C}_{\mu}\), since \(d (0) = 0\).

These operators give the one-loop action a single normalization. The second line below follows from (11.11) and \(J_{\text{ax}} = | \det Ds|\), the third from Lemma 12.7. For every \(\mu > 0\) and \(v\) in a neighbourhood of zero,

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{array}{|l|} \hline \begin{aligned} \textsf{T} (v) & =\mathcal{L}_{m + 1} (U_v) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det \textsf{Q} (v) - \log J_{\text{ax}} (v)\\ & =\mathcal{L}_{m + 1} (U_v) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det (\mathcal{I}^{\ast} \textsf{J} (v)\mathcal{I})\\ & =\mathcal{L}_{m + 1} (U_v) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det \textsf{C}_{\mu} (v) - \log \det \textsf{G}_{\mu} (v) . \end{aligned}\\ \hline \end{array}\) (11.13)

The factor \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) comes from the Gaussian integral; the Haar Jacobian converts the axial Hessian to left coordinates: \(\det \textsf{Q} = J_{\text{ax}}^2 \det (\mathcal{I}^{\ast} \textsf{J} \mathcal{I})\); the ghost determinant enters with power one because the gauge block of the completion contributes \((\det \textsf{G}_{\mu})^2\) to \(\det \textsf{C}_{\mu}\). The left side does not depend on \(\mu\). Field-independent factors, such as \((2 \pi / \beta)^{n / 2}\) and the Haar normalizations, form the scalar normalization; they cancel from every normalized expectation, so the convergence of correlations does not require them to converge. Only the dependence of \(\textsf{T}\) on \(v\) matters for correlations: Section 12 extracts the coupling coefficient from its quadratic kernel, and its higher Taylor terms enter the activities of Section 13.

Insertion bounds.

\(\displaystyle \sum_a q_a (- \xi) \textsf{b}_{(a, b'), \lambda} (\xi) = 0\)

for each colour \(b'\) and source index \(\lambda\). Restrict to \(\xi = te_a\). Only \(q_a (- te_a)\) remains, hence the corresponding component is zero for \(t \ne 0\). Continuity gives \(\textsf{b} (0) = 0\) for every component. Analyticity implies \(\textsf{b} = O (| \xi |)\) and \(\textsf{c} = O (1)\). On the tested ray \(W_i = O (t^3)\) and \(V_i = O (t^4)\). The displayed identity gives the same orders for \(H^{\text{con}}\) and hence for \(Y^{\text{con}} = H^{\text{con}} / 2\), proving the assertion.\(\Box\)

Removing the predictor contact term therefore changes the tested entry of the quadratic kernel only at order \(t^3\), and leaves the curvature-square coefficient unchanged.

11.5The local large-field energy

The Gaussian integral is performed only on part of each field configuration. Its complement must retain a positive energy per selected large-field test. This energy is the local sum of completed squares and signed error terms, on a consistent selection of tests. The estimate is deterministic and keeps every configuration: Lemma 11.12 gives the large field a positive energy, which Lemma 11.14 turns into a suppression factor.

Fix one hypercubic orientation. In Sections 11.5 and 11.6 write

\(\displaystyle t_{\text{f}} = t_{m + 1}, \qquad t_{\text{c}} = t_m, \qquad B = B_m, \qquad R_{\ast} = \lfloor B / 100000 \rfloor, \qquad t_{\text{hi}} = \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}^{- 49 / 100}\)

for the fine and coarse thresholds, the support radius, the path cutoff and the threshold of very large tests. Similarly \(c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}} = c_{\text{dir}, j}\) and \(c_{\text{dir}, \text{c}} = c_{\text{dir}, J_m}\), with the same convention for \(c_{\text{lin}}\). Recall \(\hat{\beta}_{m + 1} = \hat{\beta}_m + \kappa_G \log \ell\). Increase the coupling threshold so that \(B \geqslant j + 4\). In these two subsections the estimates hold on every coarse torus of period \(n_{\text{tor}} \geqslant 100 (B + 1)\), with fine period \(\ell n_{\text{tor}}\). These periods also satisfy \(\ell n_{\text{tor}} \geqslant 100 ((j + 3) \ell + 1)\), so the patch-size requirement used in Lemmas 11.4–11.7 holds. Use the same periodized kernels and local sector conditions. By the formula for \(t_m\) in Section 5 and \(M^{\text{cut}}_m = \exp ((\log \log \hat{\beta}_m)^2)\) in Section 9,

\(\displaystyle t_{\text{f}} / t_{\text{c}} \to 1, \qquad B^n t_{\text{f}} / t_{\text{hi}} \to 0 \qquad (\hat{\beta}_m \to \infty, \ell \text{ fixed})\)

for every fixed \(n\). All limits below are taken in this way.

Square completion.
The input and output row maps on two-forms are \(\mathcal{B}_f = (\sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}}} I, \rho_f)^T\) and \(\mathcal{B}_c = (\sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{c}}} I, \rho_c)^T\). They satisfy \(\mathcal{B}_f^{\ast} \mathcal{B}_f = \textsf{H}_f\) and \(\mathcal{B}_c^{\ast} \mathcal{B}_c = \textsf{H}_c\). The free square-completion maps are
\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} M_f =\mathcal{B}_f \textsf{H}_f^{- 1} T\mathcal{B}_c^{\ast}, \qquad M_n = \textsf{b}^{- 1 / 2} d_1^{\ast} \mathcal{B}_c^{\ast},\\ N = \left( \begin{array}{c} I -\mathcal{B}_c \textsf{H}_c^{- 1} \mathcal{B}_c^{\ast}\\ \mathcal{W}_3^{1 / 2} d_2 \textsf{H}_c^{- 1} \mathcal{B}_c^{\ast} \end{array} \right) . \end{array}\)

They satisfy \(M_f^{\ast} M_f + M_n^{\ast} M_n + N^{\ast} N = I\). Indeed \(P_c =\mathcal{B}_c \textsf{H}_c^{- 1} \mathcal{B}_c^{\ast}\) is the orthogonal projection onto the range of \(\mathcal{B}_c\), so the first block of \(N\) contributes \(I - P_c\), while multiplying (11.3) by \(\mathcal{B}_c \textsf{H}_c^{- 1}\) on the left and by its adjoint on the right gives

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{B}_c \textsf{H}_c^{- 1} d_2^{\ast} \mathcal{W}_3 d_2 \textsf{H}_c^{- 1} \mathcal{B}_c^{\ast} = P_c - M_f^{\ast} M_f - M_n^{\ast} M_n .\)

Thus a coarse row vector splits isometrically into a part transferred from the fine rows (\(M_f\)), a part carried by the penalty (\(M_n\)), and a remainder (\(N\)) seen only off the range of \(\mathcal{B}_c\) and by the three-form defect. For an infinitesimal pair of fine and coarse fields, let \(X_f^{\text{lin}}, X_c^{\text{lin}}\) be their curls followed by \(\mathcal{B}_f, \mathcal{B}_c\), and let \(Z^{\text{lin}}\) be \(\sqrt{\textsf{b}}\) times their linear predictor discrepancy. The free interpolation identities give

\(\displaystyle M_f^{\ast} X_f^{\text{lin}} + M_n^{\ast} Z^{\text{lin}} = X_c^{\text{lin}} .\) (11.16)

These identities apply to the untruncated flat kernels. We retain them when estimating the errors below.

For the actual rows, transport every kernel coefficient along the fixed coordinate route joining its endpoints, using the block-constant lift of the coarse configuration \(V\). Retain a coefficient only when that route, measured in fine units, has length at most \(R_{\ast}\). For a coefficient joining a fine site to a coarse site, also retain the fine residue and every endpoint of that route. Denote the resulting maps, respectively truncating \(M_f, M_n, N\), by \(\Phi, \Phi_n, \mathcal{N}\). Write \(L_{f, R_{\ast}}\) and \(L_{c, R_{\ast}}\) for the path rows of Definition 12.2 truncated by their true lengths in fine units. At a plaquette \(p\) put

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} X_f (p) = \left( \sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}}} F_p (U), L_{f, R_{\ast}} (U, p) \right), \quad X_c (p) = \left( \sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{c}}} F_p (V), L_{c, R_{\ast}} (V, p) \right),\\ v_c (p) = \left( \min (1, t_{\text{c}} / \text{ch} (V_p)) \sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{c}}} F_p (V), \chi_p (V) L_{c, R_{\ast}} (V, p) \right),\\ Z (e) = \sqrt{\textsf{b}} \hspace{0.17em} F (V_e Q_e^{- 1}), \qquad Q = Q^{\text{pred}} (U) . \end{array}\)

Here \(F (g) = P_{\mathfrak{g}} (g - g^{- 1}) / 2\), the ratio in \(v_c\) is one at zero chord, and \(\chi_p\) is the relaxed threshold test \(\chi^{\text{rel}}\) of (5.9) at the plaquette base. At each plaquette \(X_f\), \(X_c\) and \(v_c\) have two components, direct and path; norms and inner products are taken over both. The capped coarse coordinates \(v_c\) are bounded by \(Ct_{\text{f}}\) everywhere. Put

\(\displaystyle K^{\text{loc}}_f (p) = \text{clip}_{t_{\text{f}}} (\text{ch} (U_p)) + \tfrac{1}{2} \chi_p (U) |L_{f, R_{\ast}} (U, p) |^2, \quad K^{\text{loc}}_c (p) = \text{clip}_{t_{\text{c}}} (\text{ch} (V_p)) + \tfrac{1}{2} \chi_p (V) |L_{c, R_{\ast}} (V, p) |^2,\)

the clips and tests using the input and output coefficients, respectively. We split the local kinetic-energy difference into six terms, labelled on the right by what each accounts for:

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} E_f (p) & = K^{\text{loc}}_f (p) + \tfrac{1}{2} | \Phi v_c (p) |^2 - \langle \Phi v_c (p), X_f (p) \rangle & & \text{fine energy},\\ E_n (e) & = \tfrac{\textsf{b}}{2} \text{ch} (V_e Q_e^{- 1})^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} | \Phi_n v_c (e) |^2 - \langle \Phi_n v_c (e), Z (e) \rangle & & \text{mismatch penalty},\\ E_c (p) & = \langle v_c (p), X_c (p) \rangle - \tfrac{1}{2} |v_c (p) |^2 - K^{\text{loc}}_c (p) & & \text{coarse subtraction},\\ E_N (q) & = \tfrac{1}{2} |\mathcal{N}v_c (q) |^2 & & \text{complement square},\\ E_L (p) & = \langle v_c (p), \Phi^{\ast} X_f (p) + \Phi_n^{\ast} Z (p) - X_c (p) \rangle & & \text{linear defect},\\ E_D (p) & = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle v_c (p), (I - \Phi^{\ast} \Phi - \Phi_n^{\ast} \Phi_n -\mathcal{N}^{\ast} \mathcal{N}) v_c (p) \rangle & & \text{quadratic defect} . \end{array}\)

The fine and mismatch terms supply the energy of selected large-field tests, and \(E_N \geqslant 0\). The defects \(E_L, E_D\) vanish when \(\Phi, \Phi_n, \mathcal{N}\) are replaced by \(M_f, M_n, N\) and the rows by their linearizations, by (11.16) and \(M_f^{\ast} M_f + M_n^{\ast} M_n + N^{\ast} N = I\). The index \(q\) runs over the two-form complement and three-form components of \(N\). Summing over every anchor, the cross terms of \(E_f, E_n, E_c\) cancel against \(E_L\), and the squares \(\frac{1}{2} | \Phi v_c |^2 + \frac{1}{2} | \Phi_n v_c |^2 + \frac{1}{2} | \mathcal{N}v_c |^2 - \frac{1}{2} |v_c |^2\) cancel against \(E_D\):

\(\displaystyle \sum E_f + \sum E_n + \sum E_c + \sum E_N + \sum (E_L + E_D) = \sum K^{\text{loc}}_f + \tfrac{\textsf{b}}{2} \sum_e \text{ch} (V_e Q_e^{- 1})^2 - \sum K^{\text{loc}}_c .\) (11.17)

The adjoints in this identity are those of the truncated transported matrices, not the flat matrices. The right side is the truncated clipped kinetic energy difference; the higher atoms and omitted tails are separate factors in Section 13. The identity concerns the sum over all anchors. Lemma 11.12 bounds instead the sum of the six terms over the anchors near a selection; on such a partial sum the cross terms need not cancel and individual terms can be negative, so the defects \(E_L, E_D\) must be estimated.

Fix a mismatch multiplier \(W > 0\), chosen after \(\ell\) below, and write \(n_e = \text{ch} (V_e Q_e^{- 1})\). The fine field is taken in the tree slice of a rooted block tree, that is, in the tree gauge of Figure 12 for a rooted spanning tree of each block.

Definition 11.11. (Selections and allocation sectors) A selection \(A = (A_f, A_n, A_c)\) consists of fine plaquettes, coarse mismatch edges and coarse sites; \(|A| = |A_f | + |A_n | + |A_c |\).     (i)

    (ii)

    \(n_e \geqslant Wt_{\text{f}}\) for \(e \in A_n\), and \(n_e \leqslant 2 Wt_{\text{f}}\) for \(e \not\in A_n\);

    (iii)

    in \(A^{[5 B]}\), \(A_c\) is the set of coarse sites touched by an output plaquette with \(\text{ch} (V_p) > t_{\text{c}}\), and every chosen frame tag has positive mass.

Proof. The path bounds of Lemmas 12.3–12.4 give the row and column sums of the transported maps, and the free kernels of Lemma 11.5 give the flat identities; both are taken at the input and output levels already fixed in the descending construction.

Step 1: fine, mismatch and coarse terms. Exponential row bounds, together with unitary parallel transport, give row and column sums for \(\Phi\) bounded by \(C \ell^{- 2}\) and \(C \ell^2\) respectively. A row fixes a fine output and sums over coarse inputs; a column fixes a coarse input and sums over fine outputs. Both sums for \(\Phi_n, \mathcal{N}\) are at most \(C\). Truncation restricts every coarse displacement to \(B / 50\). The capped coordinates give \(\| \Phi v_c \|_{\infty} \leqslant C \ell^{- 2} t_{\text{f}}\) and \(\| \Phi_n v_c \|_{\infty} \leqslant Ct_{\text{f}}\).

Write \(R_f = \sum_{A_f : r^{\text{f}}_p > t_{\text{f}}} r^{\text{f}}_p\) and \(R_n = \sum_{A_n} n_e\). For the direct component, \(|F (g) | \leqslant \text{ch} (g)\) gives

\(\displaystyle E_f^{\text{dir}} (p) \geqslant \tfrac{1}{2} (\sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}}} r^{\text{f}}_p - | \Phi v_c (p, 0) |)^2 \quad (r^{\text{f}}_p \leqslant t_{\text{f}}) .\)

Choose \(\ell\) so \(| \Phi v_c | \leqslant \epsilon_{\ell} t_{\text{f}}\), with \(\epsilon_{\ell} \leqslant \sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}}} / 8\). Each selected \(t_{\text{f}} / 4 \leqslant r^{\text{f}}_p \leqslant t_{\text{f}}\) contributes \(c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}} t_{\text{f}}^2 / 128\). For \(r^{\text{f}}_p > t_{\text{f}}\), the linear clip and \(\epsilon_{\ell} \sqrt{c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}}} \leqslant c_{\text{lin}, \text{f}} / 4\) leave \(3 c_{\text{lin}, \text{f}} t_{\text{f}} r^{\text{f}}_p / 4\).

The path component is a square when its relaxed threshold test holds. When the test fails the term has a cross term of either sign, which must be summed. If a path row based at \(p\) is dropped, some plaquette \(q\) obeys \(r^{\text{f}}_q > t_{\text{f}} \exp (\text{dist} (p, q) / R_j^{\text{rel}})\). For a fixed \(q\), the number of possible \(p\) is at most \(C_{R_j^{\text{rel}}} (r^{\text{f}}_q / t_{\text{f}})\): its radius is \(R_j^{\text{rel}} \log (r^{\text{f}}_q / t_{\text{f}})\), and \((1 + R_j^{\text{rel}} \log y)^4 \leqslant C_{R_j^{\text{rel}}} y\) for \(y \geqslant 1\). Since \(r^{\text{f}}_q \leqslant 2\), each witness is within \(R_j^{\text{rel}} \log (2 / t_{\text{f}}) \leqslant B / 50\) for large \(\hat{\beta}_m\). It therefore lies in the neighbourhood where consistency applies and belongs to \(A_f\). Split a path row into endpoints with chord at most \(t_{\text{f}}\) and those above \(t_{\text{f}}\). The row bound controls the first part by \(Ct_{\text{f}}\) times the number of dropped rows; the column bound controls the second by \(CR_f\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{dropped } p} |L_{f, R_{\ast}} (U, p) | \leqslant C (C_{R_j^{\text{rel}}} + 1) R_f .\)

Multiplication by \(| \Phi v_c | \leqslant \epsilon_{\ell} t_{\text{f}}\) loses at most \(c_{\text{lin}, \text{f}} t_{\text{f}} R_f / 4\) after increasing \(\ell\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \sum E_f \geqslant \tfrac{1}{2} c_{\text{lin}, \text{f}} t_{\text{f}} R_f + \tfrac{c_{\text{dir}, \text{f}}}{128} t_{\text{f}}^2 |A_f \setminus \{r^{\text{f}}_p > t_{\text{f}} \}| .\)

For a selected mismatch \(n_e \geqslant Wt_{\text{f}}\), \(| \Phi_n v_c | \leqslant Ct_{\text{f}}\). Choose \(W\) so this is at most \(\sqrt{\textsf{b}} \hspace{0.17em} n_e / 2\). The same square inequality gives

\(\displaystyle \sum E_n \geqslant \tfrac{\textsf{b}}{8} \sum_{A_n} n_e^2 .\)

For \(E_c\) the path component cancels exactly, including its relaxed threshold test. Put \(\alpha_p = \min (1, t_{\text{c}} / r^{\text{c}}_p)\). The direct component is

\(\displaystyle E_c (p) = c_{\text{dir}, \text{c}} (\alpha_p - \alpha_p^2 / 2) |F (V_p) |^2 - \text{clip}_{t_{\text{c}}} (r^{\text{c}}_p) .\)

The nearest-point Taylor expansion gives \(|F (g) |^2 \geqslant \text{ch} (g)^2 - C \text{ch} (g)^4\) for small chord: the deficit is the squared normal displacement of \(g - 1\), whose normal part is \(O (\text{ch} (g)^2)\). Fix \(\delta\) small enough that also \(|F (g) |^2 \geqslant 3 \text{ch} (g)^2 / 4\) below \(\delta\). Since \(c_{\text{dir}, \text{c}} > 16 c_{\text{lin}, \text{c}}\), the three ranges yield

\(\displaystyle E_c (p) \geqslant \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} - Ct_{\text{c}}^4, & r^{\text{c}}_p \leqslant t_{\text{c}},\\ c_{\text{dir}, \text{c}} t_{\text{c}} r^{\text{c}}_p / 4, & t_{\text{c}} < r^{\text{c}}_p \leqslant \delta,\\ - c_{\text{lin}, \text{c}} t_{\text{c}} r^{\text{c}}_p, & r^{\text{c}}_p > \delta . \end{array} \right.\)

The last range uses only nonnegativity of the first term. Fix the constant \(a\) above and set \(\epsilon = a / 4\).

Step 2: signed terms near very large tests. We first compare large output plaquettes with fine data. Let \(\mathcal{P}_y (U) = \sum_q m_y (q) \text{ch} (U_q)\) be the fine plaquette sum in the lifted macro-patch centred at \(\ell y\), with \(m_y (q)\) its actual multiplicity there. The macro radius is \((j + 7) \ell\), so \(\sum_y m_y (q) \leqslant K_j\) independently of \(\ell\) and the period. On its axial frame, denote a straight connector by \(Y_z\) and its two root adjustments by \(p_0, p_1\). The averaging weight satisfies \(\ell^4 w_z^{\text{av}} \leqslant 1\). Telescoping each length-\(\ell\) connector and the two end routes, then summing their crossings, gives

\(\displaystyle \ell^4 \sum_z w_z^{\text{av}} \text{ch} (p_0^{- 1} Y_z p_1) \leqslant C_j \ell^2 \mathcal{P}_y (U) .\)

For the straight segment, a fine edge occurs in at most \(\ell\) translated segments, and the eligible axial link sum is at most \(C_j \ell \mathcal{P}_y (U)\). The two end-frame sums obey the same \(C_j \ell^2 \mathcal{P}_y (U)\) bound: write their coordinate routes, count each link at most \(C_j \ell\) times after the normalized transverse average, and use the nearest-point comparison for the minimizing frame. The axial alternatives use their defining link-sum bound instead. The predictor branch comparison bounds its adjusted chord by \(C_{\text{pred}}\) times this weighted chord sum, on either branch. Around the four sides of an output plaquette the root adjustments cancel. Comparing its four links with \(Q\) gives

\(\displaystyle r^{\text{c}}_p \leqslant \sum_{e \in \partial p} n_e + C_j \ell^{- 2} \mathcal{P}_y (U) .\)

A coarse plaquette thus sees fine curvature only with weight \(\ell^{- 2}\), and each fine plaquette enters boundedly many such comparisons. In an eligible axial frame a fine link differs from its replicated coarse link by at most \(C\) times the macro-patch plaquette sum, plus the mismatch on a boundary edge. Compare the four sides of a coarse plaquette, average the straight paths, and use the predictor equation. Each fine plaquette occurs with total coefficient at most \(C \ell^{- 2}\); mismatch chords enter linearly. Split each fine chord into its part at most \(t_{\text{f}}\) and its selected excess, and each mismatch into its part at most \(2 Wt_{\text{f}}\) and its selected part. The resulting bound has the form

\(\displaystyle r^{\text{c}}_p \leqslant C_{j, \ell, W} t_{\text{f}} + \Delta_p, \qquad \sum_p \Delta_p \leqslant C \left( \ell^{- 2} \sum_{A_f : r^{\text{f}}_q > t_{\text{f}}} r^{\text{f}}_q + \sum_{A_n} n_e \right) .\)

The excess constant depends on the fixed stage, predictor and penalty bounds and is fixed before \(\ell, W\); only the bounded-field term may depend on them. This follows by summing the actual route multiplicities before enlarging a macro-patch. Applying the same bound to \(\Phi^{\ast} X_f\) and \(\Phi_n^{\ast} Z\), with the column estimates above, bounds their sums on \(\text{Aff}\) by \(C\) times that excess sum plus \(C_{j, \ell, W} t_{\text{f}} | \text{Aff} |\). Since \(|v_c | \leqslant Ct_{\text{f}}\), these estimates give

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{Aff}} (E_L + E_D) \geqslant - Ct_{\text{f}} (\ell^{- 2} R_f + R_n) - C_{j, \ell, W} t_{\text{f}}^2 | \text{Aff} | .\)

For \(E_D\), expand its four operator terms. The bounds on \(v_c, \Phi v_c, \Phi_n v_c, \mathcal{N}v_c\), together with both row and column sums of the three maps, bound each term by \(Ct_{\text{f}}^2\) per anchor. The constants also depend on the predictor and penalty bounds.

Every output plaquette with \(r^{\text{c}}_p > \delta\) lies in \(\text{Aff}\) for large \(\hat{\beta}_m\): outside \(\text{Aff}\) the macro-patch comparison has no fine or mismatch test above \(t_{\text{hi}}\), so \(r^{\text{c}}_p \leqslant C_{j, \ell, W} t_{\text{hi}} < \delta\). On \(\text{Aff}\) the same comparison and \(t_{\text{c}} \leqslant 2 t_{\text{f}}\) give

\(\displaystyle c_{\text{lin}, \text{c}} t_{\text{c}} \sum_{r^{\text{c}}_p > \delta} r^{\text{c}}_p \leqslant C_{j, \ell, W} t_{\text{f}}^2 | \text{Aff} | + Ct_{\text{f}} (\ell^{- 2} R_f + R_n) .\)

Add this inequality to the previous lower bound. There is a geometric constant \(K\), fixed before the coupling threshold, with \(| \text{Aff} | \leqslant KB^4 |D|\) and at most \(KB^4 |A|\) local output anchors. Increasing the constants defines \(Z_{\text{aff}} = C_{j, \ell, W} K\) and gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{Aff}} (E_L + E_D) \geqslant c_{\text{lin}, \text{c}} t_{\text{c}} \sum_{r^{\text{c}}_p > \delta} r^{\text{c}}_p - Ct_{\text{f}} (\ell^{- 2} R_f + R_n) - Z_{\text{aff}} t_{\text{f}}^2 B^4 |D| .\)

The positive term cancels the third coarse-plaquette range of Step 1; the fine excess (through \(\ell\)), the mismatches (through \(W\)) and the tests in \(D\) absorb the losses.

Step 3: signed terms in the small-field region. For an anchor outside \(\text{Aff}\), no fine chord or mismatch above \(t_{\text{hi}}\) occurs in the surrounding macro-patches. The preceding plaquette comparison gives coarse plaquette chords at most \(C_{j, \ell, W} t_{\text{hi}}\) on this region. Choose an axial gauge in a radius-\(O (B)\) coarse cube. Lemma 11.4 bounds its link chords by \(CB\) times the sum of its \(O (B^4)\) plaquette chords, hence by \(C_{j, \ell, W} B^5 t_{\text{hi}}\). Its block-constant extension acts on the fine field. Comparing a fine link with its replicated coarse link uses the local block-tree route; its fine plaquette sum is \(O_{j, \ell} (t_{\text{hi}})\) and its boundary mismatch is at most \(t_{\text{hi}}\). Both fields are consequently small in this common gauge, with the convenient bound

\(\displaystyle \delta_{\text{loc}} = C_{j, \ell, W} B^{10} t_{\text{hi}}\)

through distance \(2 B / 5\). In this region compare each transported kernel with its flat uncut kernel. A path of length \(n\) has transport error at most \(Cn \delta_{\text{loc}}\), by telescoping its product. Let \(\tau_0 > 0\) be a common exponential decay rate of the fixed-stage kernels. Their tails are at most \(Ce^{- \tau_0 R_{\ast}}\). Each curvature or predictor differs from its linearization by \(C \delta_{\text{loc}}^2\). With \(\epsilon_{\text{loc}} = (1 + R_{\ast}) \delta_{\text{loc}} + \text{e}^{- \tau_0 R_{\ast}}\), subtract (11.16) and \(M_f^{\ast} M_f + M_n^{\ast} M_n + N^{\ast} N = I\) before applying the triangle inequality. This gives

\(\displaystyle \| \Phi^{\ast} X_f + \Phi_n^{\ast} Z - X_c \| \leqslant C \delta_{\text{loc}} \epsilon_{\text{loc}}, \qquad \|(I - \Phi^{\ast} \Phi - \Phi_n^{\ast} \Phi_n -\mathcal{N}^{\ast} \mathcal{N}) v_c \| \leqslant Ct_{\text{f}} \epsilon_{\text{loc}} .\)

The assertion is local: extend the small logarithms by zero off the neighbourhood; the cut rows do not change there. Their range fits inside \(2 B / 5\) for sufficiently large \(\hat{\beta}_m\). Thus each signed term is bounded below by \(- C (t_{\text{f}} \delta_{\text{loc}} + t_{\text{f}}^2) \epsilon_{\text{loc}}\). Since \(B^n t_{\text{hi}}^2 / t_{\text{f}} \to 0\) and \(B^n e^{- \tau_0 R_{\ast}} / t_{\text{f}} \to 0\) for every fixed \(n\), their total is at least \(- \epsilon t_{\text{f}}^2 |A|\). Away from very large tests, the transported identities thus hold up to errors that are small compared with \(t_{\text{f}}^2\) per selected test.

Step 4: absorption of the losses. The fine and mismatch bounds leave \(at_{\text{f}}^2\) per selected fine or mismatch test, \(at_{\text{f}} t_{\text{hi}}\) per test in \(D\), and still retain the losses \(Ct_{\text{f}} (\ell^{- 2} R_f + R_n)\) from the signed terms. First increase \(\ell\) to handle the fine loss, then \(W\) to handle the mismatch loss. Coarse flags can be assigned to incident output plaquettes with chord above \(t_{\text{c}}\), with at most four flags per plaquette. Plaquettes above \(\delta\) number at most \(K |D|\) by the macro-patch comparison; all local output anchors number at most \(KB^4 |A|\). Using \(t_{\text{f}} \leqslant 2 t_{\text{c}}\), the middle range of Step 1 leaves \(4 at_{\text{f}}^2\) per remaining such plaquette and so covers every other coarse flag.

The large output plaquette loss cancels against the positive term of Step 2; \(E_N \geqslant 0\). The residual losses are at most \((4 aK + Z_{\text{aff}} B^4) t_{\text{f}}^2 |D | + Ct_{\text{c}}^4 KB^4 | A| + at_{\text{f}}^2 |A| / 4\). Increase the coupling threshold, with \(\ell, W, a\) fixed, until

\(\displaystyle (4 aK + Z_{\text{aff}} B^4) t_{\text{f}}^2 \leqslant at_{\text{f}} t_{\text{hi}}, \qquad Ct_{\text{c}}^4 KB^4 \leqslant at_{\text{f}}^2 / 4.\)

The limits stated at the beginning of this subsection justify both choices. At least \(at_{\text{f}}^2 |A| / 2\) remains. Take \(\kappa = a / 2\). Every multiplicity used a fixed-radius local count or a row or column sum; none counts the period. This proves the asserted uniformity and the stated order of choices.\(\Box\)

Lemma 11.14 uses no identity between the two covariances: the difference between the sector integrand and the reference law is carried exactly by the determinant and Gaussian-density correction factors listed before Lemma 11.14.

Fix the allocation, its actual block trees, the selected tests, all tags, and the frozen fine links \(Y\). The tree links equal \(1\) by the gauge choice of Section 11.3 and are not variables; the frozen links are free links, held fixed in the Gaussian calculation and integrated afterwards with Haar measure. Proof. Coercivity. The linearized square terms and an eligible predictor control a fine link by those term values in a fixed macro-patch plus \(C \delta_x\) times the surrounding fine increments, where \(\delta_x = C_{j, \ell} t_{\text{c}}\) tends to zero. Summing squares, using the bounded number of macro-patches meeting an edge, gives

\(\displaystyle \|a\|_2^2 \leqslant C_{j, \ell} \| \textsf{D} a\|_2^2 + C_{j, \ell} \delta_x^2 \|a\|_2^2 .\)

For large \(\hat{\beta}_m\) the last coefficient is at most \(1 / 2\), proving \(\textsf{A}_Y \geqslant cI\). All-frozen sectors have zero-dimensional active space and the assertion is vacuous there. Adding identity coordinates for inactive links preserves this lower bound.

Decay. Exponential path coefficients bound the weighted row and column sums of \(\textsf{D}\). Products give the same Schur bound for \(\textsf{A}_Y\). By the Combes–Thomas argument, for sufficiently small fixed \(\theta > 0\) multiply the coordinates by \(\text{e}^{\theta \text{dist} (i, \cdot)}\) on the left and by its inverse on the right. The resulting matrix differs from \(\textsf{A}_Y\) by at most \(C \theta\) in operator norm: \(| \text{e}^{\theta d} - 1| \leqslant \theta d \text{e}^{\theta d}\) is summable against the exponential coefficient bound. Choose \(C \theta < c / 2\). The inverse Neumann series now gives exponential row and column bounds for \(\textsf{A}_Y^{- 1}\) and every principal compression, with constants independent of the number of coordinates.

Windows. A compressed inverse column solves \(\textsf{A}_Y Pe_i = e_i\) inside its window. \(\displaystyle \|P - \textsf{A}_Y^{- 1} \|_{\text{Schur}} \leqslant Ce^{- cB}, \qquad \| \Gamma - \textsf{A}_Y^{- 1} \|_{\text{Schur}} \leqslant Ce^{- cB} .\)

\(\displaystyle \Gamma - \textsf{A}_Y^{- 1} = \textsf{A}_Y^{- 1} (\textsf{A}_Y \Gamma - I) .\)

Mean. The affine source is formed from the same retained terms and from a bounded smooth extension of the frozen logarithms. Its derivatives are \(O (t_{\text{f}})\) with the same spatial weights. The bondwise change restricts to \(I\) when \(I\) contains every colour coordinate of each bond it reads. The matrix in this density is the inverse of the marginal covariance \(\Gamma_I\), not the compression of \(\Gamma^{- 1}\). The Jacobian and the Gaussian density in \(\mathcal{R}_I\) depend on all the reads of the product together, not factor by factor; this is why the whole product is estimated in one integral under the common law. For a fixed finite product and a sufficiently small neighbourhood of \(V_0\), the displacement is bounded and the covariance has uniform positive lower and finite upper bounds. The transported density and its fixed-order derivatives are then dominated, in Lebesgue coordinates, by a polynomial times a decaying Gaussian. Together with the held-link supremum envelopes and protected cutoffs, this justifies differentiation under this finite integral. The uniform read-count and simultaneous-warning estimates needed for the expansion are proved separately in the lemma. The next lemma bounds its ordered derivatives at \(V_0\) under the same base law, including products containing incoming measurable forced activities. It is the second local input to Section 13. Its bounds may depend on the fixed mismatch multiplier \(W\) and energy constant \(\kappa\) of Lemma 11.12. Clauses (i)–(iv) are conditional on the frozen-source, retained-plateau, warning-support and localized determinant inputs identified in the proof; the present section does not yet establish those inputs for the actual expansion.

For an admitted polymer put \(A = A (M)\). For a large-field component with selection \(A = (A_f, A_n, A_c)\) and \(A_c \ne \varnothing\), let \(F_A\) contain its full local sector factor and every read meeting it. Adjoin every large-field component met by those reads. In this value estimate only, replace the healthy-support condition within this component by the consistency condition of Definition 11.11. Retain the incoming-support disjointness and inventory incidence. The other factors attached to \(F_A\) have the bounds \(f_i\) above; their product excludes the component's selected-test suppression and cutoff remainders already included in that component. Fix \(0 < \kappa'_B < \kappa\) before the coupling threshold. Let \(s_A\) count the radius-\(B\) blocks of its complete read support, including retained path lengths in those units. The component itself is estimated in value; derivatives fall only on attached smooth factors and the common Gaussian density. Fix the orders \(k, q\) and take odd \(\ell\) above the stage-data threshold. A function has upper rate \(r\) if it is at most \(C_{\epsilon} \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}^{r + \epsilon}\) for every \(\epsilon > 0\) once \(\hat{\beta}_m\) is large. Write \(P = P_{j, \ell, k, q}\), of upper rate zero, and \(d = d_{j, \ell, k, q}\); the large-field component bound may also depend on \(\kappa'_B\). Directions and sector parameters have the restrictions of Lemma 11.13.

Lemma 11.14. (Simultaneous local integration) Assume \(\beta \in \mathfrak{B}_{m + 1}\), as in the reference law above.

    (i)

    Admitted polymers \(M\) have integrable majorants \(G_M\) for their ordered derivatives through order \(k\), with

    \(\displaystyle \int G_M \hspace{0.17em} d \nu \leqslant P (\hat{\beta}_m) (1 + s_M)^d \prod_{i \in M} f_i .\)
    (ii)

    If \(A_c = \varnothing\), the selected-test envelopes satisfy, through order \(k\),

    \(\displaystyle \prod_{i \text{selected test}} a_i \leqslant e^{- \kappa'_B \beta t_{\text{f}}^2 |A|} .\)
    (iii)

    A large-field component with \(A_c \ne \varnothing\) has a value majorant \(G_A\) with

    \(\displaystyle \int G_A \hspace{0.17em} d \nu \leqslant P (\hat{\beta}_m) (1 + s_A)^d e^{- \kappa'_B \beta t_{\text{f}}^2 |A|} \prod_{i \text{attached}} f_i .\)
    (iv)

    Simultaneous cutoff remainders at \(r\) distinct addresses have probability at most \(\varepsilon_w^{2 r}\).

    (v)

    Disjoint complete read supports factorize, including Haar and tag coordinates.

The window bounds of Lemma 11.13 control the Gaussian scores and exponentials, the energy of Lemma 11.12 supplies the suppression, and finite range gives the factorization.

Proof. Step 1: the affine Gaussian substitution. For any chart-supported \(F_Y\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \int \text{e}^{- \beta Q_Y (a)} \hspace{0.17em} F_Y (a) \hspace{0.17em} da = & (2 \pi g^2)^{n / 2} (\det \Gamma)^{1 / 2} \text{e}^{- \beta Q_Y (m_Y)}\\ & \times \int F_Y (m_Y + gz) \exp \left\{ \langle \eta_Y, z \rangle + \tfrac{1}{2} \langle z, (\Gamma^{- 1} - \textsf{A}_Y) z \rangle \right\} \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_{\Gamma} (z),\\ \eta_Y = & - g^{- 1} (\textsf{A}_Y m_Y + f_Y) . \end{array}\)

Here \(n\) counts active real coordinates; the Haar density and chart cutoffs are included in \(F_Y\). Indeed, \(da = g^n \hspace{0.17em} dz\) and

\(\displaystyle \beta Q_Y (m_Y + gz) = \beta Q_Y (m_Y) + g^{- 1} \langle \textsf{A}_Y m_Y + f_Y, z \rangle + \tfrac{1}{2} \langle z, \textsf{A}_Y z \rangle .\)

Dividing the resulting density by the normalized \(\gamma_{\Gamma}\) density gives both the linear residual and the quadratic correction in the displayed identity, as well as its determinant. The identity holds for every vector \(m_Y\) and every positive \(\Gamma\), in particular for either window prescription. The choices (11.18) only make the reference law finite range; the exponents of the retained factor vanish exactly when \(m_Y = - \textsf{A}_Y^{- 1} f_Y\) and \(\Gamma = \textsf{A}_Y^{- 1}\), and Step 4 estimates that factor. The local expansion in Section 13 uses \(\Omega^{\text{loc}}\).

For the window part of the correction, put \(\textsf{E}_{\Gamma} = \textsf{A}_Y \Gamma - I\). When \(B\) is sufficiently large, Lemma 11.13 gives \(\| \textsf{E}_{\Gamma} \|_{\text{Schur}} < 1\), and therefore

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \Gamma^{- 1} - \textsf{A}_Y & = \sum_{h \geqslant 1} (- \textsf{E}_{\Gamma})^h \textsf{A}_Y,\\ \log \det \Gamma + \log \det \textsf{A}_Y & = \sum_{h \geqslant 1} \frac{(- 1)^{h + 1}}{h} \text{tr} (\textsf{E}_{\Gamma}^h) . \end{array}\)

The order in the first series follows from \(\textsf{A}_Y \Gamma = I + \textsf{E}_{\Gamma}\); the second uses positivity of \(\textsf{A}_Y\) and \(\Gamma\). The same estimates give convergence after any fixed number of derivatives, with the selected windows held fixed. Expanding the matrix products gives ordered chains of coordinate indices; their complete reads include the windows and the row stencils at every link of the chain. In an exponentially weighted Schur norm, the absolute sum over either endpoint of a length-\(h\) quadratic chain, at fixed other endpoint, is bounded by \(\|| \textsf{E}_{\Gamma} |\|_{\text{Schur}}^h \|| \textsf{A}_Y |\|_{\text{Schur}}\). Fixed-order derivatives add at most a fixed polynomial in \(h\), and a smaller exponential weight absorbs the spatial direction weights. For a selected collection of quadratic chain terms, \(|z_i z_{i'} | \leqslant (z_i^2 + z_{i'}^2) / 2\) assigns each absolute term to its endpoints; summing the two endpoint marginals before Gaussian integration controls the whole collection without a bound on the number of chains at one anchor. These identities isolate the window correction relative to \(\textsf{A}_Y^{- 1}\); they do not identify \(\det \textsf{A}_Y\) with the stationary determinant of Lemma 11.7.

Unresolved localization. The endpoint estimate alone does not prove the support-weighted summability of the resulting ordered chains with their complete window and field reads. The proof also needs a separate comparison of \(\det \textsf{A}_Y\) and the stationary determinant, including the selected allocation and its scalar factors. Until those two inputs are established for the same sector, the determinant and covariance factor bounds used in Step 4 and in Lemma 13.5 are conditional.

Step 2: scores. For the common change of coordinates defined above, the first logarithmic derivative of the Gaussian density is

\(\displaystyle - \tfrac{1}{2} \text{tr} (\Gamma_I^{- 1} \dot{\Gamma}_I) + \tfrac{1}{2} z_V^T \Gamma_I^{- 1} \dot{\Gamma}_I \Gamma_I^{- 1} z_V - z_V^T \Gamma_I^{- 1} \dot{z}_V .\)

Add the logarithmic Jacobian derivative. Repeated differentiation bounds every score through order \(k\) by a polynomial in the absolute values of the marginal Gaussian coordinates, with coefficients controlled by Lemma 11.13 and the fixed smooth chart extension. The number of placements is a fixed polynomial in \(1 + s_M\), since \(k\) is fixed and \(|I| \leqslant C_{j, \ell} B^4 s_M\). Connectivity and the chosen root bound all distances from \(o\) by \(C_{j, \ell} B (1 + s_M)\), so the spatial direction weights give another fixed polynomial.

The normalized coarse directions have size \(O (t_{\text{c}})\) with their stated spatial weights. On a fixed chart, derivatives of its multiplication, logarithm and bounded extension are bounded. The mean derivatives are \(O (t_{\text{f}})\), while \(t_{\text{c}} / t_{\text{f}}\) stays bounded. In \(z_V\) the additional normalization is \(g^{- 1}\). Thus the score coefficients are bounded by a fixed power of \(g^{- 1} t_{\text{c}}\), \(B\) and \(1 + s_M\). Since

\(\displaystyle g^{- 1} t_{\text{c}} \leqslant C \exp \{(\log \log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1})^2 \},\)

all fixed powers have upper rate zero. The small inverse and stationarity residuals in Lemma 11.13 have the same property after this normalization.

Step 3: square corrections. The chart-supported square corrections have deterministic envelopes: on their support all logarithms are \(O (t_{\text{f}})\), the affine remainder is \(O (t_{\text{f}}^2)\), and \(| \tfrac{1}{2} | r + e |^2 - \tfrac{1}{2} | r |^2 | \leqslant |r| |e| + |e|^2 / 2\) gives an energy correction \(O (\beta t_{\text{f}}^3)\). The exponentially summable path coefficients bound the same quantities with fixed path-length powers after differentiation. These bounds enter \(a_i\).

This use of the small logarithms also requires a same-field plateau for every frozen link read by a retained row, or a separately bounded factor for a failed plateau. The stated consistency of Definition 11.11 reaches \(5 B\), whereas the active/frozen boundary is at \(10 B\); the proof here does not yet show that the necessary physical cutoff and tag factors remain on that boundary after the expansion. Thus the claimed square-correction and annular bounds in this step and in Steps 7–8 are conditional on that missing support argument. Exact compensation of the surrogate in the density does not by itself make the remainder small.

Step 4: unbounded exponentials. The unbounded exponentials come from the residual Gaussian density and its local covariance comparisons. Write their upper exponents as \(\eta_i \cdot z + z^T K_i z / 2\), with \(K_i \geqslant 0\). At the base field the Gaussian likelihood is one; its derivatives are the polynomial scores already estimated. Its exponent tends to zero with \(V - V_0\), so on a sufficiently small neighbourhood its weighted row sums are at most \(\delta_B = C_{j, k} B^d \text{e}^{- cB}\). The residual Gaussian density has this bound by Lemma 11.13. The resolvent identity for each compressed inverse gives \(C \delta_B\) for its local quadratic corrections. For the finitely many types of test and covariance-ratio addresses, bounded multiplicity and decay from the address bound their sum at coordinate \(a\) by \(C \delta_B \sum_y \text{e}^{- c \text{dist} (y, a)} \leqslant C' \delta_B\). For the arbitrarily many ordered defect chains use instead the two endpoint Schur sums of Step 1. The support-weighted factorization of these chains remains conditional on the localization input stated there. In the linear terms, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz at each coordinate gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \Gamma^{1 / 2} \sum_i \eta_i \right|^2 \leqslant C \sum_i \Lambda_i, \qquad \left| \Gamma^{1 / 2} \sum_i K_i \Gamma^{1 / 2} \right| \leqslant C \delta_B .\)

To specify the linear bound, let \(w_{ia}\) be the sum of the absolute path weights contributing to coordinate \(a\) of \(\eta_i\); bounded endpoint multiplicity gives \(\sup_a \sum_i w_{ia} \leqslant C\). Put

\(\displaystyle \Lambda_i = \sum_a \frac{| \eta_i (a) |^2}{w_{ia}} + \text{tr} (\Gamma^{1 / 2} K_i \Gamma^{1 / 2}),\)

with zero summand if both numerator and denominator vanish. This quantity assigns the common linear exponential bound to the individual factors. To see the first bound, use \(| \sum_r x_r |^2 \leqslant (\sum_r w_r) \sum_r |x_r |^2 / w_r\) with the absolute path weights \(w_r\); their sums are the preceding bounded row sums. The Schur bound for \(\Gamma\) completes the estimate. In particular, this bounds \(| \Gamma^{1 / 2} \eta_0 |^2\) with \(\eta_0 = \sum_i \eta_i\), including all terms \(\eta_i^T \Gamma \eta_{i'}\) with \(i \ne i'\). This is the factor \(\exp (C \Lambda_i)\) in \(a_i\). Put \(K = \sum_i K_i\). Since \(0 \leqslant K_i \leqslant K\), each \(\| \Gamma^{1 / 2} K_i \Gamma^{1 / 2} \|\) is at most \(\| \Gamma^{1 / 2} K \Gamma^{1 / 2} \| < 1 / 8\) for large \(B\), also after the fixed enlargement needed for two moments. Diagonalization and square completion, with \(- \log (1 - u) \leqslant 2 u\) for \(0 \leqslant u \leqslant 1 / 2\), give

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_{\Gamma} \text{e}^{2 \eta_0 \cdot z + z^T Kz} \leqslant \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( C \sum_i \Lambda_i \right) .\)

Under this tilt the covariance is at most \(2 \Gamma\) and its mean is \((I - 2 \Gamma K)^{- 1} (2 \Gamma \eta_0)\). Polynomial moments are bounded by the preceding linear estimate; increasing the fixed constant in \(\exp (C \Lambda_i)\) includes their mean contributions. Thus the differentiated factors and likelihood have, after removing the cutoff-remainder amplitudes, one \(L^2\) majorant bounded by \(P_{j, \ell, k, q} (\hat{\beta}_m) (1 + s_M)^d \prod a_i\).

Step 5: cutoff remainders. We next bound the simultaneous event defined above, including all derivative supports. Let \(p_0\) bound the number of Gaussian coordinates per coarse site and \(m_0\) the number of distinct cutoff-remainder addresses per site. These are fixed once the stage and ratio are fixed. For \(r\) distinct addresses, choose one violating coordinate, a witness, for each. There are at most \((p_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} |)^r\) choices. Distinct addresses may share a witness, but a single coordinate can serve at most \(m_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} |\) addresses, so the set \(J\) of distinct witnesses satisfies

\(\displaystyle r \leqslant m_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} | \hspace{0.17em} |J|, \qquad |J| \leqslant r.\)

The Schur bound gives covariance operator norm at most \(M_0\). The exponential Markov inequality for each signed sum \(\sum_{i \in J} \pm z_i\), and summation over the \(2^{|J|}\) signs, give

\(\displaystyle \Pr \{|z_i | \geqslant T (i \in J)\} \leqslant 2^{|J|} \exp \{(M_0 / 2 - T) |J|\} .\)

Take \(T = \xi t_{\text{f}} / (2 g)\) and choose \(H > 0\) with \(M_0 / 2 + Hm_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} | \leqslant T\). Then \((T - M_0 / 2) |J| \geqslant Hm_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} | |J| \geqslant Hr\): although witnesses are shared, the large deviations still gain \(\text{e}^{- H}\) per address. Summing over the witness choices gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr (E_{\mathcal{J}(M)}) \leqslant (p_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} |)^r \hspace{0.17em} 2^r \text{e}^{- Hr} = (2 p_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} | \text{e}^{- H})^r .\)

The factor \(t_{\text{f}} / g\) grows faster than every fixed power of \(B\) and \(\log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}\). Consequently \(H\) can exceed \(2 \tau_w \log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1} + \log (2 p_0 |\mathbb{B}_{2 B} |)\), proving (iv). A cutoff remainder is thus rare because its violating coordinate lies \(t_{\text{f}} / g \gg 1\) standard deviations out.

The probability calculation is conditional on the excursion implication stated before the lemma. For a cutoff that reads frozen links, the implication needs the actual retained component masks or a verified physical-link plateau; a small Gaussian vector alone does not control arbitrary frozen Haar variables. In particular, separation of a warning address from selected tests is a separate condition from the radius \(2 B\) of its Gaussian witness. This missing implication also leaves the application of (iv) conditional.

Step 6: the common majorant. Take the finite sum of the absolute differentiated smooth expressions and scores, multiplied by the fixed incoming measurable envelopes, the cutoff-remainder amplitudes \(\prod_w M_w\), and the simultaneous indicator. The product rule bounds every derivative through order \(k\) by this same nonnegative function \(G_M = G_M^0 \boldsymbol{1}_{E_{\mathcal{J} (M)}}\). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, Step 4 and (iv),

\(\displaystyle \int G_M \hspace{0.17em} d \nu \leqslant \|G_M^0 \|_{L^2 (\nu)} \Pr (E_{\mathcal{J}(M)})^{1 / 2} \leqslant P (\hat{\beta}_m) (1 + s_M)^d \prod_{i \not\in \mathcal{J} (M)} a_i \prod_{w \in \mathcal{J} (M)} M_w \hspace{0.17em} \varepsilon_w,\)

so the square root of the simultaneous bound leaves exactly one factor \(\varepsilon_w\) per address. Since \(f_w = M_w \varepsilon_w\), this proves (i).

Step 7: selected-test suppression. Distribute the local energy of Lemma 11.12 among the selected-test factors before differentiating. When \(A_c = \varnothing\) the admitted support is healthy. The physical-link change holds the fine links and selected test indices fixed; differentiated test cutoffs retain the selected-test inequalities on their supports. The exponential energy is retained in each differentiated envelope. Annular affine errors have size \(E (\hat{\beta}_m) |A|\) with \(E\) of upper rate \(- 3 / 2\), and the remaining per-test logarithmic corrections have a fixed-power bound \(C_{j, \ell, k, q} (1 + \log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1})^{d_0} |A|\). Derivatives of the smooth factors through order \(k\) multiply this bound by fixed powers of \(B\), \(g^{- 1} t_{\text{c}}\) and the number of selected tests. Their logarithms per test are \(o (\beta t_{\text{f}}^2)\), since \(\beta t_{\text{f}}^2\) is comparable to \((M^{\text{cut}}_{m + 1})^2\), which exceeds every fixed power of \(\log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}\). Also \(E (\hat{\beta}_m) / t_{\text{f}}^2 \to 0\). Increase the coupling threshold after fixing \(\kappa'_B < \kappa\); absorption into \((\kappa - \kappa'_B) \beta t_{\text{f}}^2 |A|\) gives (ii) through order \(k\). The support factor \((1 + s_M)^d\) remains outside this per-test estimate. The determinant and covariance corrections use the exponential bounds above. Integrating the independent frozen Haar variables and summing the finite tag probabilities preserve the bound.

Step 8: large-field components. Retain the full sector masks of the component and all frozen links read by it. The lower bound in Lemma 11.12 gives \(\kappa t_{\text{f}}^2 |A|\) in its local energy. The enlarged neighbourhood used by the component adds annular affine remainders bounded by \(E (\hat{\beta}_m) |A|\), where \(E\) has upper rate \(- 3 / 2\): the term error is \(O (t_{\text{f}}^3)\), and the number of positions per test is a fixed power of \(B\). The chart-density, tag, local determinant and normalized frozen-coordinate factors contribute at most \(C_{j, \ell} (1 + \log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1})^{d_0} |A|\) to the upper exponent, for a fixed \(d_0\). Indeed, their read counts are at most \(C_{j, \ell} B^4 |A|\); the logarithm of each inverse Gaussian normalization is \(O (1 + \log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1})\), the chart logarithms are bounded on the protected chart, and the local determinant terms have the bounds of Lemma 11.13. The retained value envelope is thus at most

\(\displaystyle \exp \hspace{-0.17em} [- \kappa \beta t_{\text{f}}^2 |A| +\{\beta E (\hat{\beta}_m) + C_{j, \ell} (1 + \log \hat{\beta}_{m + 1})^{d_0} \}|A|] .\)

As in Step 7, both corrections are \(o (\beta t_{\text{f}}^2)\) per test. Choose \(\kappa''_B\) strictly between \(\kappa'_B\) and \(\kappa\) and increase the coupling threshold. This bounds the component envelope by \(e^{- \kappa''_B \beta t_{\text{f}}^2 |A|}\). Use this component envelope and the incoming measurable supremum envelopes. The attached factors and Gaussian scores have exactly the \(L^2\) bound proved above. Cauchy–Schwarz with their simultaneous event retains every attached \(f_i\). Polynomial factors in the number of component reads are bounded by a fixed polynomial in \(1 + s_A\) times a fixed power of \(B\), the latter entering \(P\). Any remaining fixed per-test logarithmic loss is absorbed by \(\kappa''_B - \kappa'_B\). Frozen Haar integration has mass one. This proves (iii), without differentiating its flag masks or taking a supremum of a Gaussian coordinate.

Step 9: factorization. The Gaussian marginal on \(I\) has covariance \(\Gamma_I\). Every complete read support contains \(\Omega_i^{\text{loc}}\) for each coordinate \(i\) it reads. The windows are symmetric. Hence if \(i, i'\) belong to disjoint complete read supports, \(i' \not\in \Omega_i^{\text{loc}}\) and \(i \not\in \Omega_{i'}^{\text{loc}}\), so \(\Gamma_{ii'} = 0\) by (11.18). The Gaussian characteristic function therefore factorizes over these supports. The frozen Haar and reference tag coordinates are disjoint as well, proving (v).\(\Box\)
