15Infinite volume and field calculus

We pass from the fixed-volume kernels of Theorem 5.9 (fixed-volume limit) to correlations on \(\mathbb{R}^4\). The order of limits is that of Definition 2.6 and Figure 1: at every fixed admissible volume the spacing tends to zero along the full cutoff sequence; only then does the volume grow, along one refinement of the prescribed volume sequence that serves every field list, derivative and test. Section 15.1 extracts this volume limit, with all derivatives, from the uniform bounds of Proposition 5.8 (Lemma 15.1). Section 15.2 bounds separated correlations cell by cell and shows that negligible errors vanish in them (Lemma 15.3). Section 15.3 identifies the quotient fields: it constructs coefficient maps for inclusions and positional derivatives and proves \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}} F_d = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}\) (Lemma 15.5, which proves Proposition 6.4). Section 15.4 passes reflection positivity to the limit and proves the power bounds of E7 (Lemma 15.6, which completes Theorem 6.1). Section 15.5 bounds repeated reflected insertions, the input for the slab operators of Lemma 16.12.

The Wilson family and separated tests are those of Section 2.2, the insertion labels those of Section 2.3, and the differences those of Section 5.6. Write \(M_{p, v} = M^{\text{UV}}_{p, v}\) and \(\langle F \rangle_{N, p} = \int F \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{N, p}\). Constants may depend on the fixed construction; further dependence is indicated.

The applications of the fixed-volume limit and uniform bounds below depend on the remaining inputs identified in Sections 14.2 and 14.3: propagation of the complete higher-marker support bounds and their stopping-level restart at support exponent \(98\); the same-sample guarded Gaussian comparison with its scalar and forced estimates, together with an exact density and coupling tie if its course is restarted; and finite-cutoff continuity of the actual marked graph operator. In particular, the deductions of the stated limits, quotient rank and repeated-word bounds for the selected Wilson family remain conditional on these inputs. The local deductions from those inputs are proved here.

15.1Volume limits

Fix a strictly increasing \(\textsf{p} : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\). Proposition 5.8 bounds the lattice kernels and their differences on separated sets, uniformly in the volume; one further difference makes these bounds equicontinuous.

For comparison, \(\text{Conf}_n\) requires each pair of points to differ in at least one coordinate, while \(\text{Gen}_n\) requires them to differ in every coordinate. At physical period \(L\), \(\text{NoHit}_{L, n}\) requires, for each pair, at least one coordinate difference outside \(L\mathbb{Z}\); the periodic coordinate-generic domain requires every such difference to lie outside \(L\mathbb{Z}\). The latter is contained in both \(\text{Gen}_n\) and \(\text{NoHit}_{L, n}\). For two points with difference \((L / 4, 0, 0, 0)\), the pair lies in \(\text{NoHit}_{L, 2}\) but not \(\text{Gen}_2\); with difference \((L, L, L, L)\) it lies in \(\text{Gen}_2\) but not \(\text{NoHit}_{L, 2}\). Proposition 5.8 gives its cutoff threshold independently of the period; the fixed-period limit of Theorem 5.9 permits its cutoff threshold to depend on the period.

Lemma 15.1. (Volume limits) There are a strictly increasing \(\textsf{q}\) and a complex multilinear table \(S\) such that, with \(P = \textsf{p} \circ \textsf{q}\), for every \(n\), label list \(v\) and difference list \(\boldsymbol{l}\),

\(\displaystyle S_n (v ; \cdot) \in C^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_n), \qquad \partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} M_{P (k), v} \longrightarrow \partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} S_n (v ; \cdot) \quad \text{locally uniformly on } \text{Conf}_n \hspace{0.17em} .\) (15.1)

Every compact \(K \subset \text{Conf}_n\) eventually lies in \(\text{NoHit}_{L_{P (k)}, n}\), and for every separated test \(f\),

\(\displaystyle C_{P (k)}^{\text{UV}} (v ; f) \longrightarrow \int f (x) S_n (v ; x) \hspace{0.17em} dx.\) (15.2)

Both convergences persist along every further refinement of \(\textsf{q}\).

Proof. Fix compact sets \(K \subset K' \subset \text{Conf}_n\), with \(K'\) a neighbourhood of \(K\). For large \(p\), \(K'\) lies in \(\text{NoHit}_{L_p, n}\) with torus separation at least some \(2 r > 0\) independent of \(p\). Proposition 5.8 bounds \(\Delta_{\boldsymbol{l}} M^{\text{lat}}_{N, p}\) by \(B (v, \boldsymbol{l}, r)\) near \(K'\) for large \(N\), uniformly in \(p\), and by Lemma 14.37 the multilinear interpolants converge at each fixed \(p\) to \(\partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} M_{p, v}\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \sup_{p \geqslant p_0} \sup_{x \in K'} | \partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} M_{p, v} (x) | \leqslant B (v, \boldsymbol{l}, r),\) (15.3)

although the cutoff threshold may depend on \(p\). One more derivative gives equicontinuity on \(K\). Arzelà–Ascoli [28, Theorem 7.25] and a diagonal extraction over compact exhaustions of \(\text{Conf}_n\), arities, degree lists, basis coefficient lists and difference lists, a countable family, give \(\textsf{q}\). Multilinearity extends the limits to every label list, and integration by parts against compactly supported tests identifies the limit of each derivative with the derivative of the limit. This proves (15.1).

A compact separated set has bounded diameter, so it eventually lies in \(\text{NoHit}_{L_{P (k)}, n}\). Theorem 5.9 gives, at every admissible \(p\), the full inner limit \(C_p^{\text{UV}} (v ; f) = \int f \hspace{0.17em} M_{p, v}\); the support of \(f\) is eventually admissible along \(P\), and local uniform convergence proves (15.2). Both properties pass to subsequences.\(\Box\)

Lemma 15.5 refines \(\textsf{q}\) once more without changing \(S\); from then on \(P\) denotes the final refinement. At each volume the kernel limits remain the full ultraviolet limits of Theorem 5.9: no spacing subsequence enters (15.2).

Symmetries of the limit.
Permutation symmetry, the empty correlation, deletion of the distinguished unit, conjugation and signed-permutation covariance hold on the lattice once \(N\) exceeds the reference levels (Definition 2.8, Lemma 14.8). Approximating separated configurations by lattice points, Lemma 14.37 and (15.1) pass them to \(S\). For translations, approximate the translation vector on the same lattice; translation invariance and (15.3) give arbitrary real translations. This proves E1–E3, and (15.1) gives E6.

15.2Separated cells and negligible errors

Chessboard seminorms, interior observables, signed images, aligned tilings, anchors and margins are those of Definition 14.10 and of the paragraphs before Lemma 14.11 and Definition 14.17. For observables \(F_1, \ldots, F_n\) in distinct cells \(C_1, \ldots, C_n\) of a translated bond tiling \(\mathcal{T}\), each interior to its cell, write \(\|F_h \|_{\mathcal{T}} = \|G_h \|_{\mathcal{T}}\), where \(G_h\) is the base-cell observable with \((G_h)_{C_h} = F_h\). The chessboard inequality (14.19), with the constant one in the remaining cells, gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle \prod_{h = 1}^n F_h \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant \prod_{h = 1}^n \|F_h \|_{\mathcal{T}} \hspace{0.17em} .\) (15.4)

The seminorm measures the cost of repeating one observation throughout the box, so this bound is independent of the volume and of the distances between the cells.

For a centered bound, the insertion's stencil links have both endpoints strictly inside the cell centered at its anchor, and its seminorm in that centered tiling is bounded as in Definition 14.10 and Lemma 14.16. There the constant is fixed before the cell level \(m\); the sufficiently fine cutoff may depend on \(m\) but is uniform in \(p\) and the anchor.

Separated anchors must therefore be placed in distinct cells, away from the boundaries. In each axis, the translations of a tiling of side \(a\) that bring one of \(n\) anchors within distance \(w\) of a cell boundary form, modulo \(a\), at most \(n\) intervals of total length \(2 nw\). If \(2 nw < a\), some lattice translation avoids them once \(a_N\) is small. We call this the strip count. Anchors at torus sup-distance greater than \(a\) never share a cell.

Lemma 15.2. (Separated cells) Let \(m \leqslant k\) and \(n \geqslant 1\) with \(6 na_k < a_m\), and suppose the insertions \(O_{N, p} (v_h, \cdot)\), \(h \leqslant n\), satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma 14.11 at level \(k\) with a common bound \(B\). For all sufficiently large \(N\) and all anchors with pairwise torus sup-distance greater than \(a_m\),

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle \prod_{h = 1}^n O_{N, p} (v_h, y_h) \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant B^n .\)

Proof. The strip count with \(a = a_m\) and \(w = 3 a_k\) gives a tiling of side \(a_m\) with the anchors in distinct cells, at distance at least \(3 a_k\) from their boundaries. Lemma 14.11 bounds each seminorm by \(B\), and (15.4) gives the claim.\(\Box\)

A negligible error has a tolerance chosen before the cell scale (Definition 14.17), and this order makes it vanish against each fixed list of spectators.

Lemma 15.3. (Vanishing errors) For every negligible error \(E\) of Definition 14.17, fixed label list \(v\), distinguished slot \(i\) and \(\delta > 0\), there is \(C (v, \delta) < \infty\) such that, eventually in (14.28),

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle E_{N, p} (y_i) \prod_{h \ne i} O_{N, p} (v_h, y_h) \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant C (v, \delta) \eta_{N, p} \qquad (\text{sep}_{\text{tor}} (y) \geqslant \delta) \hspace{0.17em} .\) (15.5)

Consequently its smear against any fixed separated test tends to zero in that order.

The error and the spectators receive distinct cells; the error pays its tolerance \(\eta_{N, p}\), and each spectator a constant.

Proof. Let \(n\) be the arity. Take \(m\) with \(a_m < \delta\), and let \(k \geqslant m\) be the level that Definition 14.17 assigns to the arity \(n\) and the lower level \(m\); the error tiling has side \(a_k\). Choose \(k_{\text{sp}} \geqslant k\), beyond the reference levels of the spectators, with \(3 a_{k_{\text{sp}}} \leqslant a_k / [8 (n + 1)]\). Lemma 14.16 bounds the centered seminorms of all signed and conjugated images of the spectators at level \(k_{\text{sp}}\) by some \(B_{\text{sp}}\). The strip count with \(a = a_k\) and \(w = a_k / [4 (n + 1)]\), and \(\delta > a_k\), give a tiling of side \(a_k\) with the anchors in distinct cells at distance at least \(w\) from their boundaries. This margin contains the integer margin (14.29), including its extra lattice unit, and exceeds \(3 a_{k_{\text{sp}}}\). Translating by a multiple of \(a_k\), we may take the error's cell to be the base cell. Definition 14.17 bounds the seminorm of the error by \(\eta_{N, p}\), and Lemma 14.11, with tiling side \(a_k\) and centered level \(k_{\text{sp}}\), bounds those of the spectators by \(B_{\text{sp}}\). Now (15.4) gives (15.5) with \(C = B_{\text{sp}}^{n - 1}\). On a compact test support the number of lattice tuples times \(a_N^{4 n}\) is bounded independently of \(N, p\), which proves the smeared assertion.\(\Box\)

15.3Quotient fields

Two labels represent the same field when every separated correlation gives the same answer for them (Definition 2.12). For a degree \(d\), a slot, fixed spectators \(v\) and a separated test \(f\), the one-slot functionals on \(V_d\) are

\(\displaystyle L_{N, p} (c) = C^{\text{lat}}_{N, p} ((d, c), v ; f), \qquad L (c) = \int f (x_0, x) \hspace{0.17em} S_{n + 1} (c (x_0), v (x)) \hspace{0.17em} dx_0 \hspace{0.17em} dx.\)

As \(S\) is smooth, \(\mathcal{N}_d (S)\) is the intersection of the kernels of all such \(L\): it consists exactly of the labels that no separated smear detects. Four inputs from Section 14.2 identify it, each with one task. (C1) Lemma 14.21 represents a degree-\(d\) insertion in a degree \(e \geqslant d\), and (C2) Lemma 14.22 its lattice difference quotient in degree \(d + 1\), up to negligible errors. (C3) Lemma 14.20 replaces a tame family in \(\ker \Pi_{d, N, p}\) by observables with vanishing separated moments, up to a negligible error; tame means the subpower bound (14.27). (C4) The free test rows of Lemma 14.23 and the relative rank comparison of Lemma 14.28 keep the quotient from losing dimension. The families supplied by (C1)–(C3) may have unbounded coefficients; Lemma 15.4 turns them into fixed coefficient vectors.

The coefficient limits are taken along a common spacing subsequence \(\textsf{n}\) at every volume and a further volume refinement, in the order (14.28). They do not affect the kernel limits, which at each admissible volume remain the full ultraviolet limits required by Definition 2.6. A family in a finite-dimensional space is bounded if its norm is bounded eventually in (14.28); an ordered limit point of countably many bounded families is their joint limit along an ultrafilter containing the ordered tails. It exists by compactness, and families that converge along the ordered tails keep their limits, so passing to ordered limit points changes neither \(S\) nor \(P\).

The next lemma, finite-dimensional linear algebra, converts approximate representatives, which need not be bounded, into one exact representative.

Lemma 15.4. (Representation of limits) Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex normed space and \(\mathcal{F}\) a proper filter on an index set. Let \(\Pi_u, \Pi\) be idempotents on \(V\) with \(\Pi_u \to \Pi\), and \(L_{i, u}, L_i\), \(i \in I\), linear functionals with \(L_{i, u} \to L_i\) and \(\ker \Pi = \bigcap_i \ker L_i\). Suppose \(x_u \in V\) satisfy, for every \(i\), along \(\mathcal{F}\),

\(\displaystyle L_{i, u} (x_u) \longrightarrow t_i, \qquad L_{i, u} ((x_u - \Pi_u x_u) / (1 +\| \Pi_u x_u \|)) \longrightarrow 0.\) (15.6)

Then some \(z \in V\) satisfies \(\Pi z = z\) and \(L_i (z) = t_i\) for every \(i\).

The second condition makes the null part of \(x_u\) negligible relative to its retained part. If the retained parts \(\Pi_u x_u\) escaped to infinity, normalizing them would produce a unit vector in the range of \(\Pi\) annihilated by every \(L_i\): a null vector in the retained range, which \(\ker \Pi = \bigcap_i \ker L_i\) excludes.

Proof. Put \(b_u = (1 +\| \Pi_u x_u \|)^{- 1}\) and \(z_u = b_u \Pi_u x_u\), so \(b_u + \|z_u \| = 1\) and \(\Pi_u z_u = z_u\). The pairs \((b_u, z_u)\) have a cluster point \((\lambda, z)\) along \(\mathcal{F}\) in \([0, 1] \times \{\|z\| \leqslant 1\}\). As \(L_{i, u} (z_u) = b_u L_{i, u} (x_u) - L_{i, u} (b_u (x_u - \Pi_u x_u))\),

\(\displaystyle L_i (z_u) - b_u t_i = b_u (L_{i, u} (x_u) - t_i) - L_{i, u} (b_u (x_u - \Pi_u x_u)) + (L_i - L_{i, u}) (z_u) \longrightarrow 0,\)

and \(\Pi z_u - z_u = (\Pi - \Pi_u) z_u \to 0\). Hence \(L_i (z) = \lambda t_i\), \(\Pi z = z\) and \(\lambda + \|z\| = 1\). If \(\lambda = 0\), then \(z \in \bigcap_i \ker L_i = \ker \Pi\), so \(z = \Pi z = 0\), contradicting \(\|z\| = 1\). Thus \(\lambda > 0\), and \(z / \lambda\) has the required properties.\(\Box\)

Lemma 15.5. (Corrected coefficient maps) After a strictly increasing refinement of \(\textsf{q}\), which does not change \(S\), there are complex linear maps \(\hat{\jmath}_{d, e} : V_d \to V_e\) for \(d \leqslant e\) and \(\hat{\partial}_{d, a} : V_d \to V_{d + 1}\) such that, for every \(c \in V_d\), spectator word \(v (x)\) and \((x_0, x) \in \text{Conf}_{n + 1}\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} S_{n + 1} (\hat{\jmath}_{d, e} c (x_0), v (x)) = S_{n + 1} (c (x_0), v (x)),\\ S_{n + 1} (\hat{\partial}_{d, a} c (x_0), v (x)) = \partial_{x_0^a} S_{n + 1} (c (x_0), v (x)) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(15.7)} \end{array}\)

Moreover \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}} F_d = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}\), and the induced maps \(\jmath_{d, e} [c] = [\hat{\jmath}_{d, e} c]\) and \(\partial_a [c] = [\hat{\partial}_{d, a} c]\) satisfy Proposition 6.4.

The proof identifies the null space with the kernel of a limiting projection, then applies Lemma 15.4 to the families of (C1) and (C2).

Proof. Limiting projections. Bounded coefficient families are tame: Lemma 14.7 bounds their inverse terminal coordinates by a fixed factor times \(\prod_{m < N} C^{\text{col}}_{J_{m + 1}}\), and \(\log C^{\text{col}}_{J_{m + 1}} \leqslant J_{m + 1}^{- 2} \log \ell_m\) (Section 9) with \(J_m \to \infty\) makes this product grow more slowly than every power of \(a_N^{- 1}\). A countable diagonal extraction, first in \(N\) at every \(p\), then in the volume, retaining (15.1), makes the following converge: the projections of Lemma 14.19, which are bounded by \(C^{\Pi}_d\), the comparison lifts of Lemma 14.28, and the couplings \(\beta_{N, p, k} \in \mathfrak{B}_k\), \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), of Theorem 5.6. In operator norm,

\(\displaystyle \Pi_{d, \textsf{n} (j), p} \longrightarrow \Pi_d^{(p)}, \qquad \Pi_d^{(P (k))} \longrightarrow \Pi_d, \qquad \Pi_d^2 = \Pi_d \hspace{0.17em},\) (15.8)

and by (14.28) this iterated limit is an ordered limit.

The null space. By finite dimensionality, the full fixed-volume limits and (15.2), \(L_{N, p} \to L\) in operator norm in the ordered limit. For fixed \(c\), the bounded family \(c - \Pi_{d, N, p} c\) is tame and lies in \(\ker \Pi_{d, N, p}\), so (C3) and Lemma 15.3 give \(L_{N, p} (c - \Pi_{d, N, p} c) \to 0\). Thus \(L ((1 - \Pi_d) c) = 0\) for every \(L\):

\(\displaystyle \ker \Pi_d \subset \mathcal{N}_d (S) .\) (15.9)

For the reverse inequality, use (C4). Rearranging (14.39) isolates \(\mathcal{G}^{\nu}_I\); every other term contains a nonempty \(R_J\), whose expectation is at most its seminorm \(\gamma_{\nu}\) by (15.4) with one occupied cell. Thus

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle \prod_{h \in I} F^{\text{tst}}_h \right\rangle_{N, p} -\mathcal{G}^{\nu}_I \right| \leqslant \gamma_{\nu} \sum_{J \subsetneq I} |\mathcal{G}^{\nu}_J |,\) (15.10)

and the sum stays bounded as \(y^{\nu, i} \to y^i\). Pass to the coefficient and kernel limits. Each entry of the resulting matrix is a fixed spectator functional, vanishing on \(\mathcal{N}_d (S)\), applied to the limiting lift of \(\mathfrak{m}_j\); so the matrix factors through \(F_d\) and has rank at most \(\dim F_d\). As \(\nu \to \infty\) these matrices tend to the rank-\(\dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}\) matrix \(\mathcal{G}\), so \(\dim F_d \geqslant \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}\). Ranks of converging idempotents are eventually constant (if \(\|P - Q\| < 1\), then \(P\) is injective on \(\text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} Q\)), so the dimension inequality of Lemma 14.19 passes to \(\Pi_d\), and with (15.9), \(\dim F_d \leqslant \dim V_d - \dim \ker \Pi_d \leqslant \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{N}_d (S) = \ker \Pi_d, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}} F_d = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}} .\) (15.11)

Targets. For the inclusion of \(c \in V_d\), (C1) and Lemma 15.3 give a tame family \(w_{N, p} \in V_e\) whose one-slot functionals tend to \(t_L = \int fS (c, \ldots)\). For the derivative in slot \(i\) and axis \(a\), (C2) gives \(w_{N, p} \in V_{d + 1}\), and torus summation by parts gives exactly

\(\displaystyle a_N^{4 n} \sum_y f (\hat{y}) \Delta_{i, a} M^{\text{lat}}_{N, p} (v ; y) = a_N^{4 n} \sum_y \frac{f (\hat{y} - a_N e_{i, a}) - f (\hat{y})}{a_N} M^{\text{lat}}_{N, p} (v ; y),\) (15.12)

with \(e_{i, a}\) the coordinate vector of slot \(i\), axis \(a\). The backward quotient tends to \(- \partial_{i, a} f\) with residual at most \(a_N \| \partial_{i, a}^2 f\|_{\infty} / 2\), which Proposition 5.8 at order zero controls, so the target is \(t_L = - \int (\partial_{i, a} f) S (c, \ldots)\).

Representation. Apply Lemma 15.4 in \(V_e\), with \(\mathcal{F}\) the filter of ordered tails \(\{(j, k) : k \geqslant k_0, j \geqslant j_0 (k)\}\), \(\Pi_u = \Pi_{e, \textsf{n} (j), P (k)}\) and the one-slot functionals of degree \(e\). They converge as shown above, and \(\ker \Pi_e = \bigcap \ker L\) by (15.11). With \(\Pi = \Pi_{e, N, p}\), the family \((w - \Pi w) / (1 +\| \Pi w\|)\) lies in \(\ker \Pi\) and is tame, being a bounded multiple of \(w\) minus a family of norm at most one; (C3) and Lemma 15.3 give the second condition of (15.6). The lemma gives one vector representing the target against every spectator and test at once; no further subsequence is chosen for these uncountably many tests. Choose such vectors on a basis of \(V_d\) and extend linearly; (15.1) turns the distributional identities into (15.7).

Induced maps. By (15.7), \(\hat{\jmath}_{d, e}\) and \(\hat{\partial}_{d, a}\) carry \(\mathcal{N}_d (S)\) into null spaces, so they descend to \(F_d\) and satisfy the identities of Proposition 6.4; with (15.11) this proves that proposition. The further properties listed after it (injective responses, units, conjugation, \(H_4\) covariance, the positional identity in every slot) follow from these identities, E1 and E2, as explained there.\(\Box\)

Example: the curvature square (continued).
Section 10.5 follows the label \(c_{F^2} \in V_4\) of \(\frac{1}{4} \sum_{a, b, \alpha} F^{\alpha}_{ab} F^{\alpha}_{ab}\) to its insertion; in degree four the quotient removes nothing. Every jet coordinate has weight at least two, and since \(\mathfrak{g}\) is simple no nonzero \(\text{Ad} (G)\)-invariant polynomial is linear in a \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued jet; so an element of \(V_4\) is a constant plus an invariant quadratic form in the lowest jet \(\mathcal{A}_0\), that is, in the curvature at the origin. The Maxwell equations do not constrain the curvature at one point, so no nonzero such polynomial vanishes on every Maxwell solution: the equation space \(\mathcal{I}_4\) of Lemma 14.19 is zero. That lemma gives \(\Pi_{4, N, p} = 1\), and (15.11) gives \(\mathcal{N}_4 (S) = 0\). Distinct degree-four labels give distinct fields, and \([c_{F^2}] \ne 0\) in \(F_4\). The quotient first acts on derivatives: the lattice difference quotient of the insertion of \(c_{F^2}\) is not a degree-five insertion, (C2) represents it by a tame family in \(V_5\), and Lemma 15.4 replaces that family by one vector, with \(\partial_a [c_{F^2}] = [\hat{\partial}_{4, a} c_{F^2}]\). In degree five, for any fixed axis \(a\), \(\mathcal{I}_5\) contains the invariant contraction \(\sum_{b, c, \alpha} F^{\alpha}_{ab} \hspace{0.17em} \partial_c F^{\alpha}_{cb}\) of a curvature with the Maxwell expression. By Lemma 14.19 and (15.11), \(\mathcal{N}_5 (S) = \ker \Pi_5\) is therefore nonzero, and \(\hat{\partial}_{4, a} c_{F^2}\) is determined only modulo these limits of transported equation directions.

15.4Euclidean properties

Lattice symmetries and positivity pass through the ordered limits; Lemma 15.2 controls separated points as they approach one another, uniformly in their diameter.

Lemma 15.6. (Euclidean properties) The table \(S\) of Lemma 15.1 satisfies E1–E7.

Proof. E1–E3 and E6 were proved after Lemma 15.1; the positive derivative orders of E7 use (15.7).

Reflection positivity. We prove E5 and deduce E4. Fix joint smooth tests \(f_i\) with compact support on positive-side separated configurations for an axis \(a\). Use the bond reflection \(x^a \mapsto a_N - x^a\), a lattice symmetry with planes \(x^a = a_N / 2\) and \(x^a = (a_N + L_p) / 2\). For large \(p, N\) the lattice smears \(F_i\) are supported strictly inside the positive half-torus, with stencils avoiding both planes, since the supports keep a positive distance from \(x^a = 0\) and the stencils have physical radius tending to zero. Periodic Wilson reflection positivity [30, Theorem 2.1, pp. 14–15] gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i, j} \bar{z}_i z_j \langle \overline{F_i \circ \textsf{r}_{a, N}} F_j \rangle_{N, p} \geqslant 0,\)

where \(\textsf{r}_{a, N}\) reflects configurations. Insertion covariance reflects the positions and applies \(\textsf{s}_a^{\text{refl}}\) to the labels; conjugation acts on the labels of the first word. The reflected joint tests converge with all derivatives to \(\bar{f}_i \otimes f_j\) with the first group reflected through \(x^a = 0\), and all supports lie in one compact separated set. Proposition 5.8 at order zero bounds the effect of the moving tests by their uniform difference times a bounded weighted site count. Lemma 14.37 and (15.2) then give a positive semidefinite limiting matrix; smoothness of \(S\) makes every pairing absolutely integrable. This is E5. Concentrating normalized bumps at positive-side words gives E4 by continuity.

Power bound at order zero. Take a nonempty label list and put \(d_{\text{pow}} = 1 + \max_i d_i\). Lemma 14.16 with \(\varepsilon = 1\), for the finitely many signed and conjugated images of the labels, gives \(k_0\) and \(C_{\text{ctr}}\) such that their centered seminorms at levels \(k \geqslant k_0\) are at most \(C_{\text{ctr}} a_k^{- d_{\text{pow}}}\); these constants precede the separation. The block ratios grow slowly: \(\log \ell_u / s_u \to 0\) (Lemma 14.2) gives \(\ell_u \leqslant a_u^{- 1}\) eventually, so for some \(c_{\text{mesh}} > 0\) and all \(u\)

\(\displaystyle a_{u + 1} \geqslant c_{\text{mesh}} a_u^2 .\) (15.13)

Thus the first \(u \geqslant k_0\) with \(a_u < \delta \leqslant 1\) satisfies \(a_u \geqslant c_0 \delta^2\), where \(c_0 = \min (c_{\text{mesh}}, a_{k_0})\): a discrete scale lies within a fixed power of any prescribed length. Fix \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) and \(x\) with \(\text{sep}_{\infty} (x) \geqslant r\). Choose the first \(m \geqslant k_0\) with \(a_m < r / 2\), then the first \(k \geqslant k_0\) with \(a_k < a_m / [6 (n + 1)]\); then \(k \geqslant m\) and \(a_k \geqslant c' (n) r^4\). Lattice approximations of \(x\) in large volumes have torus separation greater than \(a_m\), so Lemma 15.2 bounds the lattice correlation by \((C_{\text{ctr}} a_k^{- d_{\text{pow}}})^n\). Lemma 14.37 and (15.1) give

\(\displaystyle |S_n (v ; x) | \leqslant C (n, d, c) r^{- 4 d_{\text{pow}} n},\) (15.14)

with constants independent of the positions and the volume. Each insertion costs its centered seminorm at a cell scale comparable to \(r^4\), and the chessboard bound makes the total independent of the diameter of \(x\).

Higher orders. By (15.7), every coordinate derivative is a correlation with fixed degree-raised labels, finitely many at each order. Apply (15.14) to each, take the largest exponent, and bound the Fréchet norm by the sum of the coordinate coefficients in an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbb{R}^{4 n}\). This proves E7; for arity zero, \(S_0 = 1\).\(\Box\)

Proof of Theorem 6.1. Compose the volume refinements of Lemmas 15.1 and 15.5. Equation (15.2) proves ordered convergence, with the full inner limit of Theorem 5.9 at every admissible volume, and Lemma 15.6 proves E1–E7. The coefficient corrections change no kernel limit.\(\Box\)

15.5Repeated reflected words

Use the OS space of Definition 6.5. For a word \(w\), let \(w^{\ast}\) reverse it and apply conjugation to its labels, and let \(\theta w = \textsf{s}_4^{\text{refl}} w^{\ast}\) be its antilinear OS reflection. For \(s > 0\) and a word \(w\) with times in \((0, s)\) set

\(\displaystyle A_w v = w \hspace{0.17em} \tau_{se_4} v, \qquad w^{\dagger} = \tau_{se_4} \theta w ;\)

\(A_w\) acts on words, and boundedness on OS classes is not assumed. A word has buffer \(r\) in a slab of width \(h_{\text{sl}}\) if its points have pairwise sup-distance at least \(r\) and times in \([r, h_{\text{sl}} - r]\). Fix a degree bound \(d\) and a coefficient bound \(R \geqslant 0\), and put \(Q = 4 (d + 1)\). The next estimate bounds repeated insertion before any operator extension.

Lemma 15.7. (Repeated reflected words) There is \(C_0 = C_0 (d, R) > 0\) such that every nonempty length-\(n\) word \(w\) with these bounds and buffer \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) in a slab of width \(h_{\text{sl}}\) admits a width \(0 < s \leqslant h_{\text{sl}}\) in which \(w\) has buffer \(r / 2\) and, for every separated exterior word \(v\) with positive times,

\(\displaystyle \|[(A_{w^{\dagger}} A_w)^j v]\|_{\text{OS}}^2 \leqslant B_v [C_0^n (n / r)^{Qn}]^{4 j} \qquad (j \geqslant 0),\)

where \(B_v = B_v (w, r, s) < \infty\) is independent of \(j\).

The operators use the chosen width \(s\), and \(s\), \(C_0\) and \(Q\) precede \(v\). The main insertions occupy cells of a size chosen from \(r\) alone and the exterior insertions a finer aligned tiling, so the cost of the exterior does not grow with the number \(j\) of repetitions.

Proof. Enlarge the coefficient ball to contain all signed and conjugated images in degrees at most \(d\). Lemma 14.16 with \(\varepsilon = 1\) gives \(k_{\ast}\) and \(B_{\ast} > 0\) such that their centered seminorms at levels \(k_{\text{w}} \geqslant k_{\ast}\) are at most \(B_{\ast} a_{k_{\text{w}}}^{- d_{\text{pow}}}\), \(d_{\text{pow}} = d + 1\); this precedes \(n, r, w\). Choose the first \(m \geqslant k_{\ast}\) with \(a_m < r / 32\) and put \(s = a_m \lfloor h_{\text{sl}} / a_m \rfloor\). As \(w\) is nonempty with buffer \(r\), \(h_{\text{sl}} \geqslant 2 r\), so \(0 < s \leqslant h_{\text{sl}}\) and \(h_{\text{sl}} - s < a_m\), and \(w\) has buffer \(r / 2\) in width \(s\). By (15.13), \(a_m \geqslant c_1 r^2\) with \(c_1 > 0\) depending on \(k_{\ast}\). Fix \(0 < \alpha \leqslant 1\) with \(20 \alpha < c_1\), and choose the first \(k_{\text{w}} \geqslant k_{\ast}\) with \(a_{k_{\text{w}}} < \alpha (r / n)^2\). Then

\(\displaystyle 20 na_{k_{\text{w}}} < a_m, \qquad a_{k_{\text{w}}} \geqslant c_2 (r / n)^4, \qquad (B_{\ast} a_{k_{\text{w}}}^{- d_{\text{pow}}})^n \leqslant (B_{\ast} c_2^{- d_{\text{pow}}})^n (n / r)^{4 d_{\text{pow}} n} .\) (15.15)

Expanding the OS norm gives a correlation of \(4 jn\) main insertions and two copies of \(v\). The main insertions are separated from one another and from the exterior by at least \(r / 2 > a_m\). Translations by multiples of \(s\) preserve residues modulo \(a_m \mathbb{Z}^4\), so the main anchors have at most \(2 n\) residue patterns, those of \(w\) and \(\theta w\), for every \(j\). For nonempty \(v\), let \(t_{\min} > 0\) be its minimum time and choose \(m_{\text{v}} \geqslant k_{\text{w}}\) with \(a_{m_{\text{v}}} < 2 t_{\min}\) and \(a_{m_{\text{v}}}\) below every distance between distinct anchors of \(v\). The two exterior copies have times \(\pm (2 js + t)\), \(t\) a time of \(v\), so they are more than \(2 t_{\min} > a_{m_{\text{v}}}\) apart for every \(j\). Choose \(k_{\text{v}} \geqslant m_{\text{v}}\), beyond the exterior reference levels, with \(12 |v| a_{k_{\text{v}}} < a_{m_{\text{v}}}\), and let \(X \geqslant 0\) bound the centered seminorms of the exterior labels and their images at level \(k_{\text{v}}\) (Lemma 14.16). Neither \(m_{\text{v}}\) nor \(k_{\text{v}}\) depends on \(j\).

The coarse tiling has side \(a_m\) and separates the main insertions; the fine tiling has side \(a_{m_{\text{v}}}\), shares its origin and separates the exterior insertions. Their side ratio is odd, so coarse reflections preserve the fine grid and its interior margins. The strip count for the \(2 n\) main residues modulo \(a_m\) with strip width \(5 a_{k_{\text{w}}}\), possible by (15.15), keeps the main anchors at distance at least \(5 a_{k_{\text{w}}}\) from the coarse boundaries. A further translation by less than \(a_{m_{\text{v}}} \leqslant a_{k_{\text{w}}}\) puts the \(2 |v|\) exterior anchors at distance at least \(3 a_{k_{\text{v}}}\) from the fine boundaries, since \(12 |v| a_{k_{\text{v}}} < a_{m_{\text{v}}}\), and leaves the main anchors margin at least \(3 a_{k_{\text{w}}}\). The main anchors occupy distinct coarse cells, none containing an exterior anchor.

Apply (15.4) to the coarse tiling, treating the product of the exterior factors in one coarse cell as one observable. Lemma 14.11 bounds each main factor by \(B_{\ast} a_{k_{\text{w}}}^{- d_{\text{pow}}}\). For a coarse cell with \(n_C\) exterior factors, reflect their product into every coarse cell; in each copy the exterior anchors are more than \(a_{m_{\text{v}}}\) apart and occupy distinct fine cells, so (15.4) for the fine tiling and Lemma 14.11 bound the coarse seminorm of that product by \(X^{n_C}\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \|[(A_{w^{\dagger}} A_w)^j v]\|_{\text{OS}}^2 \leqslant X^{2 |v|} (B_{\ast} a_{k_{\text{w}}}^{- d_{\text{pow}}})^{4 jn} .\)

For each fixed \(j\), lattice approximations of the anchors preserving these strict margins, Lemma 14.37 and (15.1) pass this bound to the limit; no cutoff threshold uniform in \(j\) is needed. Take \(B_v = \max (1, X^{2| v|})\), \(C_0 = B_{\ast} c_2^{- d_{\text{pow}}}\) and \(Q = 4 d_{\text{pow}}\), and use (15.15). For empty \(v\) omit its factors and take \(B_v = 1\).\(\Box\)
