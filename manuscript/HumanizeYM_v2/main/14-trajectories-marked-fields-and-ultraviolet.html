14Trajectories, marked fields and ultraviolet convergence

This section develops the trajectories, renormalized insertions, volume-uniform bounds and full fixed-volume limits of Theorem 5.6, Proposition 5.8 and Theorem 5.9. The proofs of the bounds and limits still require the higher-mode attachment and stopping estimates, the actual marked Gaussian comparison, and the fixed-level cutoff comparison identified at their points of use below. The section also develops the cutoff equation identities, coefficient corrections and finite-rank comparisons from which Section 15 builds the quotient fields. All these results concern the Wilson measures of Definition 2.3 and the insertions of Definition 2.8.

Three different tasks are involved, and each has its own mechanism. A continuous shooting argument chooses the bare coupling so that the trajectory reaches one prescribed coarse coupling (Section 14.1). Weighted estimates for the specified recursion give uniqueness and analytic dependence near that endpoint. Finally, at fixed volume, convergence of the effective coordinates at each fixed level is combined with a bound on the error made by stopping the insertion transfer there (Section 14.3). The additive scalar normalization cancels from normalized expectations, so its convergence is never needed.

The main difficulty in the bounds of Section 14.2 is differentiation in position. Each lattice difference introduces an inverse spacing, so a bound for undifferentiated insertions does not suffice. Local comparison replaces a differentiated insertion by one of higher degree, with a negligible chessboard error. Exact equation identities remove its equation component. A finite matrix of free Maxwell correlation tests bounds the remaining coefficient. This argument takes place at finite cutoff, before any continuum field space has been constructed.

Throughout, the group, metric, penalty, scales and admissible classes are those of Section 5 and Section 9. The trajectories are built from the path actions of Lemmas 12.3–12.4, which fix the classical and one-loop terms, the Wilson initialization of Lemma 13.16, and the inductive step of Theorem 5.5, which carries an admissible representation one level down. Additional dependence of constants is indicated. Write \(M_{N, p} = M^{\text{lat}}_{N, p}\) and \(\langle F \rangle_{N, p} = \int F \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{N, p}\).

For an insertion label \(v = (d, c)\) put \(O_{N, p} (v, y) = O^{(d)}_{N, p} (c, y)\). Physical points \(x\) in a moment are read as lattice sites \(x / a_N\), modulo \(L_p D_N\). We use the torus separation

\(\displaystyle \text{sep}_{\text{tor}} (y) = \min_{i \ne j} \min_{z \in L_p \mathbb{Z}^4} |a_N (y_i - y_j) + z|_{\infty},\)

with value \(+ \infty\) for at most one slot. Multilinear interpolation on the physical grid means the affine interpolation in each coordinate of each grid cube, using its vertex values.

14.1Trajectories and tuning

The entry levels allow the ratios and stage constants to increase sufficiently slowly. Besides the conditions of Section 9, we use the following quantitative choices. Let \(c_{\text{dir}, j}\) be the direct clipping coefficient at stage \(j\), and let \(\nu_G^{\text{mat}}\) be the real dimension of the ambient matrix space. Put

\(\displaystyle Q_j (b) = 2 (2 + 4 A_j^{\text{path}}) + 1 + 6 (c_{\text{dir}, j} + (A_j^{\text{path}})^2) + 4 \nu_G^{\text{mat}} (\log 16 + \tfrac{1}{2} \log b), \qquad q_m = Q_{J_m} (\hat{\beta}_m) .\) (14.1)

Here \(A_j^{\text{path}}\) is the path bound of Lemma 12.3. For an admissible unmarked representation \(\rho_m\) on \(V\) sites write \(\Phi_m = v_{\text{sc}, m} V - P^{\text{path}}_{N, m} - \sum_i \boldsymbol{1}_{X_i \hspace{0.17em} \text{healthy}} E_i^{\text{reg}}\) and \(K_{m, Y}\) for its forced activities. The next estimate bounds, per site, the ratio obtained by replacing the positive density by absolute values in a normalized integral.

Lemma 14.1. (Normalization cost) For every sufficiently late level \(m\) and every admissible unmarked representation \(\rho_m\) on \(V\) sites,

\(\displaystyle \frac{\int e^{\Phi_m (U)} \sum_{\mathcal{A}} \prod_{Y \in \mathcal{A}} |K_{m, Y} (U) | \hspace{0.17em} dU}{\int \text{dens} \hspace{0.17em} \rho_m \hspace{0.17em} dU} \leqslant e^{Vq_m} .\) (14.2)

Proof. Put \(\hat{g}_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 2}\). The scalar-free unmarked exponent is at most \(VE_m\), and its absolute forced sum is at most \(e^{V \epsilon_m^{\text{for}}}\) with \(\epsilon_m^{\text{for}} \leqslant 1\); at every sufficiently late level the path bounds give \(E_m \leqslant 2 + 4 A_{J_m}^{\text{path}}\). Restrict the denominator to links of chord at most \(\hat{g}_m / 4\). There are no flags there, the forced sum is one, and its scalar-free exponent is at least \(- V (E_m + 6 (c_{\text{dir}, J_m} + (A_{J_m}^{\text{path}})^2))\). The elementary Haar-ball bound gives this set measure at least \((\hat{g}_m / 16)^{4 V \nu_G^{\text{mat}}}\). The common scalar normalization in numerator and denominator cancels, so the ratio is at most

\(\displaystyle \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \{ V [2 E_m + \epsilon_m^{\text{for}} + 6 (c_{\text{dir}, J_m} + (A_{J_m}^{\text{path}})^2) + 4 \nu_G^{\text{mat}} \log (16 / \hat{g}_m)] \} \leqslant e^{Vq_m} .\)

\(\Box\)

The per-site exponent \(q_m = O (1 + \log \hat{\beta}_m)\) comes from the Haar-ball loss \(\hat{g}_m^{- \nu_G^{\text{mat}}}\) on every link. Lemma 14.27 improves it to a constant depending only on the stage: in an axial gauge the denominator contributes Haar-ball factors on \(3 V + 1\) links and the numerator contributes one-link integrals on \(3 V - 3\) links, so only a loss of order \(\log \hat{\beta}_k\) for the whole torus remains. The source comparison needs that improvement, because its gain per cell is only a power of \(M^{\text{cut}}_t\). Enlarge \(Q_j\) at the finitely many initial levels, so that \(q_m \geqslant 1\) and (14.2) hold at every level. The choices include

\(\displaystyle B_{i + 1}^2 \leqslant 2 B_i^2, \qquad (\ell_i B_i^2)^4 q_{i + 1} = o (s_{i + 1}), \qquad \varpi_{J_i} = o (s_i) .\) (14.3)

Define \(k (m)\) to be the first \(k \geqslant m\) with \(B_k^2 \leqslant a_m / a_k\).

The short-distance comparison uses the following comparison windows of levels. For \(b > 0\) and a level \(t\) in stage \(j\), write \(L = \ell_j^{\text{st}}\) and

\(\displaystyle h_t (b) = \left\lceil \frac{(b + 2) \log M^{\text{cut}}_t}{\log L} \right\rceil, \quad v_t = \left\lceil \frac{10 \log B_t}{\log L} \right\rceil, \quad k_t = t - h_t (b) - v_t .\) (14.4)

The numerical requirements of the marked proofs serve three purposes: contraction of one-insertion remainders (items 1–3), control of replicated sources and comparison with the free field (items 4–5), and the initialization of insertions (item 6). At each stage the finite-dimensional and support constants are fixed first, then the block ratio, and finally the coupling entry. The constants named here are introduced with their estimates; we list them so that the order of choice is visible in one place.

For each stage \(j\), let \(K_j \geqslant 1\) be a nondecreasing bound for the leading coefficient maps and their inverses through that stage's finitely many degrees and symmetry averages. These bounds are fixed before the column bounds \(C^{\text{col}}_j\). Let \(A_d^{\text{sym}}\) bound the finite basis recombination in degree \(d\), and let \(C_j^{\text{loc}}\) bound the local marked error of the one-step estimate before the ratio is chosen (proof of Lemma 14.6). Each step below has \(j = J_{m + 1}\) and \(L = \ell_m\); in items 2–3 and in the proofs of Lemmas 14.6, 14.18 and 14.31 we write \(K = K_j\), \(S = C^{\text{col}}_j\) and \(\varepsilon_L = L^{- 3 / 8}\). For the retained incoming regular term, and for the unsubtracted incoming contribution in Section 19, let \(R_j^{\text{supp}} \in \mathbb{N}\) bound the enlargement of its projected incoming read support in units of \(B_m\), over the finitely many adjacent stage pairs with fine stage \(j\). Put

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_m = \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \{ (\sigma_{J_m} + 2) (2 R_{J_{m + 1}}^{\text{supp}} + 13)^4 \} .\) (14.5)

This factor converts an incoming support weight into the outgoing weight. Indeed, for fine and coarse covering numbers \(s_f, s_c\), the connected-cover bound gives

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} Ls_c & \leqslant (L + 10 s_f) (2 R_j^{\text{supp}} + 13)^4,\\ (\sigma_{J_m} + 2) s_c & \leqslant \log \mathcal{A}_m + \tfrac{1}{2} \sigma_{J_{m + 1}} s_f, \end{array}\)

provided \(20 (\sigma_{J_m} + 2) (2 R_j^{\text{supp}} + 13)^4 \leqslant L \sigma_{J_{m + 1}}\). Thus half the incoming exponent remains for the other support factors. The constant \(\mathcal{C}_j \geqslant 2\) includes these bounds and the finite source comparison constants. In item 6, \(C_d\) is the section bound in (10.6). For the source estimates, choose an integer \(q^{\text{ht}}_d\) bounding the powers of the inverse health threshold in degree \(d\), and put

\(\displaystyle \zeta_{d, i} = \hat{\beta}_i^{- (q^{\text{ht}}_d + 4)} .\)

This bound controls attachments of other marked factors to a distinguished mark. Let \(A_{j, d}\) bound the finite section, derivative and support-conversion constants in these attachments at stage \(j\), and let \(\eta^{\text{att}}_i > 0\) be the attachment threshold in Lemma 13.5 for the step at level \(i\). Decrease it so that \(\eta^{\text{att}}_i \leqslant 1 / 4\). All these constants are fixed before the coupling entry. Lemma 14.14 uses

\(\displaystyle 2 A_{j, d} (2 B_i + 1)^4 (1 + B_i)^4 \hat{\beta}_i^{q^{\text{ht}}_d} \zeta_{d, i} \leqslant \eta^{\text{att}}_i / 4, \qquad j = J_{i + 1}, \quad d \leqslant J_i .\) (14.6)

The factor \((1 + B_i)^4\) bounds the spatial sum from marked anchors to a site; it is derived in the proof of that lemma. For transport to finer levels we also require

\(\displaystyle \hat{\beta}_{i + 1}^{- 1} \ell_i^d C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}} \zeta_{d, i} \leqslant \zeta_{d, i + 1} .\) (14.7)

  1. Coefficient and support bounds. Choose nondecreasing columns with \(C^{\text{col}}_j \geqslant 64 K_{j + 1}^2\), above the derivative factorials and finite basis-recombination constants at stage \(j\). The support exponent is \(\sigma_j = 100\); we also retain the estimates at every exponent in \([98, 100]\).

  2. One-insertion contraction (Lemma 14.6). Choose \(L^{3 / 5} > 2 C_j^{\text{loc}}\), \(\mathcal{A}_m \leqslant L\) and \(20 (\sigma_{J_m} + 2) (2 R_j^{\text{supp}} + 13)^4 \leqslant L \sigma_{J_{m + 1}}\). The local marked error \(\epsilon^{\text{mk}}_m\) satisfies \(\epsilon^{\text{mk}}_m \leqslant L^{- 2 / 5}\), and

    \(\displaystyle \epsilon^{\text{mk}}_m (1 + S) A_d^{\text{sym}} \leqslant \varepsilon_L, \quad 2 K \varepsilon_L (1 + S) \leqslant 1, \quad 4 S \varepsilon_L \leqslant 1, \quad 2 K \leqslant S, \quad 4 K^2 \leqslant C^{\text{col}}_{J_m} .\) (14.8)

  3. Backward matching (Lemmas 14.18 and 14.31). Require

    \(\displaystyle 4 K \hspace{0.17em} 32 (1 + S)^2 \varepsilon_L \leqslant 1, \qquad (4 K^2 + 1) L^{1 / 8} \varepsilon_L \leqslant 1.\) (14.9)

    For each auxiliary section through the stage's degrees, require also \(2 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_j) H_{j, d} A_d^{\text{sym}} L^{- 1 / 8} \leqslant 1 / 4\), where \(H_{j, d}\) bounds its normalized healthy derivatives.

  4. Accumulated transport losses. Choose ratios to satisfy (14.11), including every constant in \(\mathcal{C}_j\).

  5. Gaussian comparison and source bounds (Lemmas 14.25–14.27). On a comparison window, for an arity bound \(n \geqslant 2\), put \(\textsf{w}_t = (M^{\text{cut}}_t)^{1 / (8 n)}\). Let \(\mathcal{E}_t^{\text{G}}\) be the sum of the covariance, rounding, Taylor and localization errors in the fixed finite source comparison. Require

    \(\displaystyle \textsf{w}_t^n \mathcal{C}_j^{h_t (b) + v_t + 2} \mathcal{E}_t^{\text{G}} \leqslant 1, \qquad Q_j^{\text{cmp}} \leqslant \log M^{\text{cut}}_t / (16 n), \qquad \hat{\beta}_i \in [\hat{\beta}_t / 2, \hat{\beta}_t] \quad (k_t \leqslant i \leqslant t) .\) (14.10)

    Here \(Q_j^{\text{cmp}}\) is the stage normalization constant in (14.48), enlarged to cover the source modes and amplitudes of the finitely many arity bounds \(n \geqslant 2\) used at stage \(j\), and the second bound is required for each of these \(n\). These constants are fixed first; the entry is then raised until \(\log M^{\text{cut}}_t \geqslant 16 n \hspace{0.17em} Q_j^{\text{cmp}}\). The first bound is imposed with the stronger smallness used in (14.46). The entry also meets the local marked bound in item 2, the forced-source bounds at every exponent between \(98\) and \(100\), and (14.6)–(14.7). The unmarked attachment sum in Lemma 13.5 is at most \(\eta^{\text{att}}_i / 2\), and \(64 \log \hat{\beta}_{i + 1} / B_i \leqslant 1 / 4\).

  6. Initialization of insertions (Lemma 14.7). At every level \(m\) and \(d \leqslant J_m\), require \(B_m\) at least the radius of \(\Sigma_d\) and \(C_d t_m^{- d} e^{\sigma_{J_m}} (e^{| \Sigma_d | \epsilon_m^{\text{for}}} - 1) \leqslant (\epsilon_m^{\text{for}})^{1 / 2}\).

Lemma 14.2. (Scale and period choices) All requirements 1–7 and (14.3) can be met with

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} \ell_m \geqslant 3 \text{ odd}, \qquad \log \ell_m \to \infty, \qquad \log \ell_m / s_m \to 0, \qquad 4 \mid L_p,\\ (a_m / a_{k (m)})^4 q_{k (m)} = o (s_{k (m)}) . \end{array}\)

The sides \(L_p\) increase strictly; every positive integer divides infinitely many. For every finite set of exponents \(b \geqslant 1\) and finite comparison requests, arbitrarily late terminal levels \(t\) meet the comparison requirements simultaneously on their windows (14.4), with \(k_t \to \infty\) and \(J_i = J_t\) throughout each window.

Proof. The choices follow the order of Table 3: stage constants, then ratios, then entries. First fix the entire sequence \(K_j\) and the finite section bounds. Choose \(C^{\text{col}}_j \geqslant 64 K_{j + 1}^2\), and then fix \(\mathcal{C}_j\) to include these columns and all source constants. Since \(J_{m + 1} \leqslant J_m + 1\), this gives \(4 K_{J_{m + 1}}^2 \leqslant C^{\text{col}}_{J_m}\) even at a stage transition. Next choose nondecreasing odd stage ratios \(\ell_j^{\text{st}} \geqslant \max (3, j)\) large enough that

\(\displaystyle \frac{\omega_j^{\text{RG}}}{\log \ell_j^{\text{st}}} \leqslant \min (\kappa_G / 4, j^{- 2}), \qquad \frac{\log \mathcal{C}_j}{\log \ell_j^{\text{st}}} \leqslant j^{- 2} .\) (14.11)

Each denominator can be increased after its numerator is fixed. The ratio requirements in items 2 and 3 are lower bounds on this same later choice: \(L^{- 2 / 5} / L^{- 3 / 8} = L^{- 1 / 40} \to 0\) and \(L^{1 / 8} L^{- 3 / 8} = L^{- 1 / 4} \to 0\). Choose also \(L^{3 / 5} > 2 C_j^{\text{loc}}\), so \(C_j^{\text{loc}} / L < \tfrac{1}{2} L^{- 2 / 5}\). For this fixed \(L\), raise the coupling entry until the absolute value of the \(o_{\hat{\beta}_m} (1)\) term in the one-step estimate is at most \(\tfrac{1}{2} L^{- 2 / 5}\). The local error \(C_j^{\text{loc}} / L + o_{\hat{\beta}_m} (1)\) is then at most \(L^{- 2 / 5}\). The column and ratio choices also give the fixed auxiliary-section inequality. The support radii and exponents in (14.5) are fixed before \(L\), so its compression condition and \(\mathcal{A}_m \leqslant L\) are further fixed lower bounds on the ratio. Thus no column is chosen in response to a ratio or to a later-stage column.

Choose consecutive constant-stage intervals, postponing each new stage until all its fixed coupling requirements hold. On a fixed stage \(s_i\) grows linearly, while \(B_i = O ((\log (2 + s_i))^2)\) and \(q_i = O (1 + \log (2 + s_i))\). Thus

\(\displaystyle (\ell_i B_i^2)^4 q_{i + 1} / s_{i + 1} \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{on that stage as its entry is postponed} .\)

For the initial stage, choose \(\bar{\beta}\) after its fixed ratio \(L_0\). For \(B (b) = \lceil (\log b)^2 \rceil\), the quotient \(B (b + \kappa_G \log L_0)^2 / B (b)^2\) tends to one as \(b \to \infty\). Choose \(\bar{\beta}\) beyond a threshold where it is at most two for every \(b \geqslant \bar{\beta}\). This covers every step of the first stage, including \(i = 0\). At a transition to a later ratio, impose the same bound at its entry and thereafter, with that ratio fixed. Impose the second condition of (14.3) with bound \(1 / j\) after entry to stage \(j\). Impose also \(\varpi_j \leqslant s_i / j\) and \(\log \ell_j^{\text{st}} \leqslant s_i / j\) at its entry. These are lower bounds on that entry. By item 9 of Table 3, \(t_m^{- 1} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) and \((\epsilon_m^{\text{for}})^{1 / 2} \hat{\beta}_m^{d + 1} \to 0\), so item 6, including the finite stencil-radius requirement, is another entry bound. Since \(\log \hat{\beta}_m \to \infty\) at a fixed ratio, item 7 is also an entry bound, imposed for both ratios that occur at the levels of a stage. At each fixed ratio, the same threshold formula gives \(t_m / t_{m + 1} \to 1\) as \(\hat{\beta}_m \to \infty\); impose \(\tfrac{1}{2} \leqslant t_m / t_{m + 1} \leqslant 2\) at entry and thereafter, with \(t_m \leqslant 1\) supplied by item 7 of Table 3. The preceding entry restrictions give both little-oh assertions and \(\log \ell_i / s_i \to 0\); \(s_i \geqslant i \log 3\) at every level.

For each stage, the length \(h_t (b) + v_t\) in (14.4) is \(O_j ((\log \log (2 + s_t))^2 + \log \log (2 + s_t)) = o (t)\). Lengthen that constant-stage interval to contain a terminal point with the whole window (14.4) in the same stage and with its left endpoint beyond all earlier requested levels. Do this with the longest window \(b = j\), all arities \(n \leqslant j\) and the finitely many comparison orders at stage \(j\), on the compact sets of test configurations with point separations at least \(1 / j\) and coordinates of absolute value at most \(j\). Every \(1 \leqslant b \leqslant j\) uses a subinterval of this window. The length and error bounds are uniform for \(b\) in any fixed compact subinterval of \([1, j]\); take the maximum of the finite comparison constants before postponing the common terminal level. Their finitely many derivative bounds are uniform on each such compact set. The estimates (14.44)–(14.46) show that the first requirement in (14.10) holds once that entry is late enough. The second is a lower bound on \(\log M^{\text{cut}}_t\) after \(Q_j^{\text{cmp}}\) is fixed. The total window length is \(o (t)\), so its coupling variation is \(o (\hat{\beta}_t)\) and gives the third requirement. These are lower bounds on a later entry or interval length. Each fixed request is then met at arbitrarily large stages. The source requirements are also entry bounds. On a fixed stage, the left side of (14.6) is a fixed constant times \((2 B_i + 1)^4 (1 + B_i)^4 \hat{\beta}_i^{- 4}\) and tends to zero. In (14.7), division by \(\zeta_{d, i + 1}\) leaves

\(\displaystyle \ell_i^d C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}} \hat{\beta}_{i + 1}^{- 1} (\hat{\beta}_{i + 1} / \hat{\beta}_i)^{q^{\text{ht}}_d + 4},\)

which tends to zero at fixed ratio. Include both adjacent stages at a transition. There are finitely many degrees at each entry. The unmarked attachment sum tends to zero by the factor rates in the proof of Lemma 13.5, so its half-threshold requirement is another entry bound. These choices do not alter earlier intervals. Taking \(\ell_i = \ell^{\text{st}}_{J_{i + 1}}\) gives odd ratios, and \(\ell_j^{\text{st}} \geqslant j\) gives \(\log \ell_i \to \infty\).

The level \(k (m)\) exists: \(a_i B_i^2\) decreases by at least the factor \(2 / 3\) at each step. If \(k (m) = u + 1 > m\), minimality gives

\(\displaystyle a_m / a_{k (m)} \leqslant \ell_u B_u^2 .\) (14.12)

Consequently (14.3) gives \((a_m / a_{k (m)})^4 q_{k (m)} = o (s_{k (m)})\). The case \(k (m) = m\) satisfies the same conclusion directly from \(q_m = o (s_m)\).

Choose \(L_p = 4 (M_{\text{per}} + p + 1)\). For \(j = J_{m + 1}\), the required period inequalities, including item 7 of Table 3, are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} L_p e^{s_m} \geqslant 100 (B_m + 1), \qquad L_p e^{s_{m + 1}} \geqslant 100 ((j + 3) \ell_m + 1),\\ L_p e^{s_m} \geqslant v_j^{\text{per}} (1 + B_m + B_{m + 1})^{h_j^{\text{per}}} . \end{array}\)

The first follows by choosing \(M_{\text{per}}\) large, since \((B_m + 1) e^{- s_m}\) is bounded. After canceling \(\ell_m\) in the second, postpone each stage until \(e^{s_m} \geqslant 100 (j + 4)\). For the third, \(v_j^{\text{per}}\) and \(h_j^{\text{per}}\) are fixed before entry to stage \(j\), and \(v_j^{\text{per}} (1 + B_m + B_{m + 1})^{h_j^{\text{per}}} e^{- s_m} \to 0\) on that stage. Postpone each later stage until this expression is at most one thereafter, and enlarge \(M_{\text{per}}\) for the finite initial interval. This proves all three inequalities. The third supplies the period bound for the five-torus localization in the proof of Lemma 13.7. The periods are strictly increasing, divisible by four, and every integer divides infinitely many of them.\(\Box\)

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} \rho_{N, p, N} = \rho^{\text{W}}_{\beta_{N, p}}, \qquad | \beta_{N, p, m + 1} - \beta_{N, p, m} - \kappa_G \log \ell_m | \leqslant \omega_{J_{m + 1}}^{\text{RG}},\\ \int \text{dens} \hspace{0.17em} \rho_{N, p, m} = Z_{L_p D_N, \beta_{N, p}} . \end{array}\)

All representations are admissible, and the terminal integral has the same value.

Proof of Lemma 14.3 and Theorem 5.6. The trajectory is obtained by bracketing, level by level, along the continuous Wilson curve of Lemma 13.16, carried down by the continuous step of the specified recursion in Lemma 13.17; the drift margin comes from (14.11). Set

14.2.2Chessboard seminorms and replicated sources

A bond tiling has cell boundaries through link midpoints. Its reflected copies place an observable in every cell, with conjugation on odd reflections. The copy \((F)_C\) is the pullback of \(F\) under the composition of bond reflections taking the base cell to \(C\), conjugated when that composition has odd parity.

Definition 14.10. (Chessboard seminorm) For a tiling \(\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}\) into congruent reflected cells and an observable \(F\) in its base cell, set

\(\displaystyle \|F\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}} = \left| \left\langle \prod_{C \in \mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}} (F)_C \right\rangle_{N, p} \right|^{1 / |\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}} |} .\)

Interior means dependence only on links with both endpoints strictly inside the base cell. The centered tiling \(\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m (y)\) has physical cell side \(a_m\) and base cell centered at \(y\).

The seminorm measures the size of a repeated observation throughout the box; it converts reflected-product estimates into bounds on one insertion. For interior complex observables these quantities are seminorms and satisfy

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle \prod_{C \in \mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}} (F_C)_C \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant \prod_{C \in \mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}} \|F_C \|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}} .\) (14.19)

Apply the abstract chessboard theorem, [14, Theorem 4.1], successively in the four axes. The real vector space is the space of complex cell functions, and the reflection includes conjugation. Translation invariance gives cyclicity; reflection positivity gives the required Schwarz inequality. For the Wilson measure this positivity is [30, Theorem 2.1, pp. 14–15], with compact matrix group, positive coupling and observables on one side of the bond planes. For \(\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m\), there are \(L_p D_m\) cells per axis, an even integer. The cell side \(a_m / a_N = D_N / D_m\) is odd, so the centered boundaries pass through link midpoints. Bounded Borel cell functions follow by approximation in the finite-dimensional configuration measure.

The centered seminorm estimate must also control anchors near arbitrary, separated configurations. Write \(v = (d, c)\) for the insertion label. A signed image means the image under \(H_4\); conjugation is also allowed. Two tilings are aligned when their cells have the same axes and every coarse boundary is a fine boundary.

Lemma 14.11. (Interior seminorm bound) Fix \(m \leqslant k \leqslant N\), \(N \geqslant m_{\text{ref}} (d)\), and \(B_{\text{rt}} \geqslant 0\). Suppose, for every anchor \(y'\), every signed image or image under conjugation of \(O_{N, p} (v, y')\) has seminorm at most \(B_{\text{rt}}\) in \(\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_k (y')\) and stencil within \(a_k\) of \(y'\). In every translated bond tiling \(\mathcal{T}\) of side \(a_m\), an anchor at distance at least \(3 a_k\) from its cell boundary satisfies

\(\displaystyle O_{N, p} (v, y) \text{ is interior}, \qquad \|O_{N, p} (v, y)\|_{\mathcal{T}} \leqslant B_{\text{rt}} \hspace{0.17em} .\) (14.20)

Proof. At an aligned fine-cell center the bound follows from (14.19); between centers it follows from log-convexity in each axis, using the symmetry of Lemma 14.8. Since \(a_k / a_N\) is odd, the fine-cell centers are lattice sites. At an aligned fine-cell center, reflect all coarse copies into distinct fine cells. Their signs and conjugations are among the assumed images. Apply (14.19) with \(1\) in every unoccupied fine cell. Taking the coarse seminorm bounds it by \(B_{\text{rt}}\).

For intermediate anchors, let \(H (z)\) be the coarse seminorm after \(z\) lattice translations in one axis, with the other coordinates fixed. Translation invariance and reflection give

\(\displaystyle H (z)^2 \leqslant H (z - 1) H (z + 1) \hspace{0.17em} .\) (14.21)

Indeed, translate the reflected product by one lattice step. Alternate cells then contain shifts \(z - 1\) and \(z + 1\); apply (14.19). On a finite interval a nonnegative sequence satisfying this inequality cannot exceed its endpoint maximum: an interior positive maximum extends to both neighbours and hence to an endpoint. This includes zero endpoints.

Bracket each coordinate of \(y\) by its two neighbouring fine-center coordinates. All anchors in the resulting rectangle have margin at least \(2 a_k\), so their stencils are interior. Induct on \(i = 0, \ldots, 4\): the seminorm is at most \(B_{\text{rt}}\) when the first \(i\) coordinates are arbitrary lattice coordinates in these intervals and the remaining coordinates are endpoints. The case \(i = 0\) is the aligned-center bound. In the next axis, the endpoint bounds hold uniformly in the coordinates already moved; (14.21) supplies the intervening bounds. The case \(i = 4\) proves (14.20).\(\Box\)

Replicated sources.
Several insertions are carried at once by giving each a nilpotent mark. This is the device of the observable field in [5, Section 2.3], where only terms of order at most one in each external field are retained. For a finite set of slots \(I_0\), work in the commutative algebra \(\mathbb{C} [z_i : i \in I_0] / (z_i^2 : i \in I_0)\). Write \(z_I = \prod_{i \in I} z_i\) for the mode \(I\). The exact source density starts at
\(\displaystyle \mathcal{H}_N (z ; U) = \text{dens} \hspace{0.17em} \rho_N (U) \prod_{i \in I_0} (1 + z_i F_i (U)), \qquad \mathcal{H}_m =\mathcal{B}^{(m)}_{\beta_{N, p, m + 1}} \mathcal{H}_{m + 1} .\) (14.22)

Thus its normalized \(z_I\) integral is the cutoff moment of the corresponding physical insertions. At level \(m\) write its local representation as

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} & e^{\Phi_m (U) + \sum_i z_i f_{i, m} (U) + \sum_X H_{m, X} (z ; U)}\\ & \quad \cdot \sum_{\mathcal{A} \text{eligible}} \prod_{Y \in \mathcal{A}} \left( K_{m, Y} (U) + \sum_i z_i k_{i, m, Y} (U) + K^{\geqslant 2}_{m, Y} (z ; U) \right) . \end{aligned}\) (14.23)

Here \(\Phi_m\) is the unmarked exponent, \(f_{i, m}\) and \(k_{i, m, Y}\) are the complete one-insertion exponential and forced terms, including their physical spacing factors. The polynomials \(H_{m, X}\) and \(K^{\geqslant 2}_{m, Y}\) contain only modes \(z_I\) with \(|I| \geqslant 2\). Their support contains every anchor indexed by a nonzero mode. The regular terms include their health masks, and each forced term retains its eligibility test. For a single mark, \(z_1^2 = 0\) gives \(e^{\Phi_m + z_1 f_{1, m}} = e^{\Phi_m} (1 + z_1 f_{1, m})\), and (14.23) is the representation of Definition 14.5 with

\(\displaystyle f_{1, m} = a_m^{- d} \left\{ x_0 +\boldsymbol{1}_{\Sigma_d \hspace{0.17em} \text{healthy}} P_d [t_m^{- \textsf{N}} \mathbf{x}_m^{\circ}] + \sum_i \boldsymbol{1}_{Z_i \hspace{0.17em} \text{healthy}} R_i \right\}, \qquad k_{1, m, Y_i} = a_m^{- d} Q_i,\)

at \(\mathbf{v}= \textsf{Z}^{- 1}_{N, p, d} c\). The higher modes record the connected terms that integration creates between different marks. Rescaling mark \(h\) by \(\textsf{w}_h > 0\), its mark weight, multiplies the coefficient of \(z_I\) by \(\prod_{h \in I} \textsf{w}_h\); a bound of order one on the rescaled source therefore bounds that coefficient by \(\prod_{h \in I} \textsf{w}_h^{- 1}\).

At the cutoff, the healthy and unhealthy parts of different insertions must be assembled together. A single forced support can meet more than one unhealthy stencil, even when their anchors are far apart. The following construction gives the higher forced modes required in that case; no activity is divided by.

For a two-mark example, let \(K (U)\) be a selected eligible forced activity, and let \(b_1 (U), b_2 (U)\) be two unhealthy complement factors whose stencils meet its support. Suppressing the common field \(U\), the part formed using these factors is

\(\displaystyle K (1 + z_1 b_1) (1 + z_2 b_2) = K + z_1 Kb_1 + z_2 Kb_2 + z_1 z_2 Kb_1 b_2 .\)

The coefficient of \(z_1 z_2\) contains the original activity once. A product that repeats a mark, such as \(z_1 (z_1 z_2)\), is zero. This exact algebra does not by itself give the support estimates needed for propagation.

Lemma 14.12. (Joint terminal forced modes) Fix a finite set of allowed degrees \(\mathcal{D}\) and a finite family of primary insertions of degrees \(d_h \in \mathcal{D}\), with distinct anchors. Assume their stencils have the fixed radii of their degrees and their positive mark weights at \(N\) satisfy \(\textsf{w}_{h, N} a_N^{- d_h} |\mathbf{x}_{h, N} |_1 \leqslant \zeta_{d_h, N}\). The singleton terms agree with the construction in Lemma 14.7.

Proof. Expand the finite product in (14.22), and split each chosen section into its healthy term and its unhealthy complement. Fix a term and an eligible family of original forced activities. Join each complement stencil to the selected supports it meets, and join intersecting complement stencils. Every complement contains a flag, so exact coverage gives it at least one incident selected support. Collapse each connected component containing a complement to one new address: its support and inventory are the unions of those of its constituents, and its value is the product of the complements and selected activities, with their original eligibility tests and the exact flag test on the union. Leave untouched selected supports in the cover. The collapsed supports are disjoint, and expansion of their constituent indices recovers the original selection and the assignment of marks uniquely. Thus this is an identity coefficient by coefficient, including at zeros of the original activities. Add zero unmarked slots for these indexed addresses when needed. For one complement the component is exactly the merged address of Lemma 14.7.

For the bound, use the complete stencil as the read support and the absolute majorant of each complement. The weighted singleton estimate used in (14.6) bounds its size by \(A_{j, d_h} \hat{\beta}_N^{q^{\text{ht}}_{d_h}} \zeta_{d_h, N}\), with the fixed stencil and support factors included. It is at most \(A_{j, d_h} \hat{\beta}_N^{- 4}\). At most a fixed number of stencils of each admitted degree can meet a site, because anchor occupancy is one; the finite maximum of their radii and constants gives one site bound, independent of the number of marks. The original Wilson forced majorants have, at every fixed larger support exponent, a site sum bounded by a polynomial in \(B_N\) times \(\exp (- c \beta_N t_N^2)\), by the counting estimate in the proof of Lemma 13.16. The extra exponent absorbs the union support. Every new forced component contains at least one original forced support. Charge half of each such support's Wilson suppression before applying the rooted tree bound of Lemma 13.4; root at a constituent containing the site being tested, which may be an original forced support rather than a stencil. Summing the possible site roots and dividing by \(\epsilon_N^{\text{for}}\) gives, for fixed stage constants \(C_j, c_j > 0\),

\(\displaystyle (2 B_N + 1)^4 \sup_x \sum_{a : x \in Z_{a, N}} v^{\text{new,for}}_{a, N} \leqslant C_j (1 + B_N)^{C_j} \hat{\beta}_N^{C_j} \frac{e^{- c_j \beta_N t_N^2}}{\epsilon_N^{\text{for}}} \leqslant \frac{\eta_N^{\text{att}}}{4} .\)

The remaining halves of the suppressions make the forced-support tree sum convergent; the stencil site bound includes all degrees together. Since \(\beta_N t_N^2 \geqslant (M^{\text{cut}}_N)^2 / 2\) and \(\epsilon_N^{\text{for}} = e^{- (M^{\text{cut}}_N)^{1 / 5}}\), the prefactor tends to zero on the selected late terminal levels. Increasing the entry makes this contribution and the regular singleton contribution each fit one quarter of the attachment threshold; the latter uses the strengthened entry bound (14.6). Their sum fits the combined site bound in Lemma 14.14. All estimates precede summation over the finite marker family and are uniform in the period. Absolute convergence permits the countable forced-address sums and their regrouping.\(\Box\)

Connected terms with at least two marks need large supports, because distinct marked anchors are far apart. We record this once, since the stopping estimate uses it again.

Lemma 14.13. (Marked covering estimate) Let \(B \geqslant 8\). If a support \(X\) contains \(n \geqslant 2\) anchors at pairwise periodic max-distance at least \(B^2\), then

\(\displaystyle Bn \leqslant 64 \text{cov}_B (X) .\) (14.24)

Proof. Take a minimal cover of \(X\) by \(M = \text{cov}_B (X)\) radius-\(B\) balls with connected union. Join their centers by a tree with edges of length at most \(2 B + 1\). Its doubled traversal has length at most \(6 B (M - 1)\). Assign each anchor to one covering center. Distinct anchors have distinct centers, and the distance between any two assigned centers is at least \(B^2 - 2 B\). Visit these centers in their cyclic order in the traversal. The triangle inequality gives \(n (B^2 - 2 B) \leqslant 6 B (M - 1)\), so \(n (B - 2) \leqslant 6 M\). For \(B \geqslant 8\) we have \(B - 2 \geqslant \tfrac{3}{4} B\), hence \(Bn \leqslant 8 M \leqslant 64 M\). The argument uses the periodic max-distance and needs no nonwrapping assumption.\(\Box\)

The estimate permits each source weight to be enlarged by \(e^{\delta B / 64}\) per mark when \(\delta\) units of the support exponent are removed.

The rooted expansion needs a bound on all the other marked factors. We verify it for the primary insertions of Definition 2.8, with their selected coefficient graphs, before taking any replicated integral. For mark \(h\) of degree \(d_h\) at level \(i\) let \(\mathbf{x}_{h, i}\) be its dimensionless singleton coefficient, let \(b_{h, i} = a_i^{- d_h} |\mathbf{x}_{h, i} |_1\), and let \(v_{a, i}^{\text{src}}\) be the weighted integrated majorants of the other marked factors on their complete read supports \(Z_{a, i}\), with derivative and support weights as in Lemma 13.5.

Lemma 14.14. (Marked attachments) Fix a finite set of degrees \(\mathcal{D}\) and let \(\max_{d \in \mathcal{D}} m_{\text{ref}} (d) \leqslant k \leqslant N\), with \(d \leqslant J_i\) for every \(d \in \mathcal{D}\) and \(i \geqslant k\). \(\displaystyle (2 B_i + 1)^4 \sup_x \sum_{a : x \in Z_{a, i}} v_{a, i}^{\text{src}} \leqslant \eta^{\text{att}}_i / 2,\)

uniformly in the cutoff, the period and the number of marks.

Proof. The proof is an induction down the levels: the rooted clause of Lemma 13.5 bounds each step, the covering estimate controls the changing mark weights. Closing the bound also requires the higher-mode estimate identified below. The singleton coefficients are controlled by Lemma 14.6 and their format by Lemma 14.9.

For the local expansion, give a mode \(z_I\) the weight \(\prod_{i \in I} w_i\), with \(w_i > 0\). The resulting coefficient norm is submultiplicative: multiplication sums over disjoint splittings of \(I\), exactly the splittings in the product of the two weighted norms. The coefficientwise rooted clause of Lemma 13.5 applies in this algebra, with the attachment bound at the preceding stage of the construction. Derivative and support weights are included in the separate majorants \(v_{a, i}\); no submultiplicativity is asserted for them. Connected terms with at least two marks obey (14.24) with \(B = B_k\), because distinct anchors are separated by \(B_k^2\) and \(B_k \geqslant 8\).

The weight change from \(\textsf{w}_{h, i + 1}\) to \(\textsf{w}_{h, i}\) introduces the factor \(\hat{\beta}_{i + 1}^{|I|}\) on a mode \(I\). For \(|I| \geqslant 2\), Lemma 14.13 at level \(i\) gives

\(\displaystyle \hat{\beta}_{i + 1}^{|I|} \leqslant \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left\{ \frac{64 \log \hat{\beta}_{i + 1}}{B_i} \text{cov}_{B_i} (X) \right\} .\)

The coefficient of the covering number tends to zero at fixed stage, since \(B_i \asymp (\log \hat{\beta}_i)^2\) and \(\hat{\beta}_{i + 1} / \hat{\beta}_i \to 1\). The source entry bounds therefore absorb this increase into the extra support exponent at each step. The singleton terms use the coefficient and graph bounds established below. This controls the varying weights on all modes with at least two marks.

The weights \(\textsf{w}_{h, i}\) estimate the intermediate source polynomials; the weight in the final integral remains \(\textsf{w}_{h, k}\). The inverse estimate of Lemma 14.6 gives \(b_{h, i + 1} \leqslant \ell_i^{d_h} C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}} b_{h, i}\). Applying (14.7) to each \(d_h\) gives

\(\displaystyle \textsf{w}_{h, i} b_{h, i} \leqslant \zeta_{d_h, i} \qquad (k \leqslant i \leqslant N) .\)

Here is the conversion from a marked-anchor bound to the site sum required by Lemma 13.5. Each support contains its marked anchor. If it also contains a site \(x\) at distance \(R\) from that anchor, its connected covering number is at least \(R / (2 B_i + 1)\). One reserved unit of support exponent thus gives a factor at most \(e^{- R / (2 B_i + 1)}\). Distinct anchors have occupancy one, so summing this factor over them is bounded by

\(\displaystyle \sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^4} e^{- |z|_{\infty} / (2 B_i + 1)} \leqslant C (1 + B_i)^4 .\)

This estimate also bounds the periodic sum, by lifting the distinct anchors to nearest representatives. For a mark of degree \(d_h\), the finite derivative products and polynomial evaluations are bounded by \(A_{j, d_h} \hat{\beta}_i^{q^{\text{ht}}_{d_h}}\). Rooted attachment sums use the other reserved support unit to absorb \(e^{\eta^{\text{att}}_i n_{\star}}\); summing their convergent tree majorants enlarges the bound by at most two, after decreasing \(\eta^{\text{att}}_i\) if necessary. For the singleton weighted majorants \(v_{a, i}^{(1)}\) this gives

\(\displaystyle (2 B_i + 1)^4 \sup_x \sum_{a : x \in Z_{a, i}} v_{a, i}^{(1)} \leqslant 2 A_{j, \ast} (2 B_i + 1)^4 (1 + B_i)^4 \hat{\beta}_i^{- 4} \leqslant \eta^{\text{att}}_i / 4, \qquad A_{j, \ast} = \max_{d \in \mathcal{D}} A_{j, d} .\)

The last inequality is the maximum of the finitely many corresponding inequalities (14.6); occupancy is used once for the combined family, not once per degree. The joint terminal representation of Lemma 14.12 also supplies a forced higher-mode site bound at the initial level. To complete the induction, one must propagate a stronger anchored majorant for the already-formed higher modes and the new joins, at a fine support exponent that retains the output reserve after reweighting. The singleton estimate above does not by itself establish that propagation. The application of the complete attachment bound at later levels is therefore conditional on this remaining estimate.\(\Box\)

The next estimate is the source of all volume-uniform bounds. Place one copy of the insertion in every cell of the level-\(m\) tiling, give each copy a nilpotent mark, and integrate down to a level \(k\) at which every cell contains \((a_m / a_k)^4\) sites. The marks stay at distance at least \(B_i^2\) at every intermediate level. The attachment condition for their complete source depends on the higher-mode propagation still required in Lemma 14.14; the following bound is conditional on that input. At level \(k\) each mark contributes the factor \(e^6 / \textsf{w}_k\), where \(\textsf{w}_k^{- 1} = a_k^{- d} e^{o (s_k)}\) is the size of the transported insertion, and each site contributes the unmarked normalization \(e^{q_k}\) of Lemma 14.1. Taking the \(|\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m |\)-th root gives the per-insertion bound \(\textsf{w}_k^{- 1} e^{6 + (a_m / a_k)^4 q_k}\).

We also use the estimate for a residual observable \(E\) with an exact source representation. Call \(\textsf{w} E\) controlled at level \(k\) when its weighted singleton exponential bound is at most \(3\), its forced singleton bound is at most \(1\), and the connected regular and forced bounds contribute at most one each per mark, with the original eligibility tests. The source evolution from its initial level down to \(k\) must retain these bounds and satisfy the attachment bounds (14.6) at every intervening level. This condition concerns the complete source, including its forced terms. Fix \(d\) and a bounded coefficient ball. A stopping pair is a pair of levels \(m_{\text{ref}} (d) \leqslant m \leqslant k\) with \(B_k^2 \leqslant a_m / a_k\).

Lemma 14.15. (Replicated source bound) There are mark weights \(\textsf{w}_k > 0\), independent of \(p\), such that, for each stopping pair, some \(N_0 (d, \text{ball}, m, k)\) gives, for all \(N \geqslant N_0\) and all \(p\),

\(\displaystyle \textsf{w}_k^{|\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m |} \left| \left\langle \prod_{C \in \mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m} (O (c))_C \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant e^{| \Lambda_{n_{p, k}} | q_k + 6 |\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m |} .\)

For every \(\varepsilon' > 0\), there is \(k_0 (\varepsilon')\) with \(6 - \log \textsf{w}_k \leqslant (d + \varepsilon') s_k\) for all \(k \geqslant k_0 (\varepsilon')\). The same integral bound holds with \(O (c), \textsf{w}_k\) replaced by \(E, \textsf{w}\) whenever \(\textsf{w} E\) is controlled at \(k\).

The proof bounds the complete source before taking its replicated integral.

Proof. The marks are attached through Lemma 14.14, the expansion of Lemma 13.5 bounds the higher modes, and Lemma 14.1 bounds the unmarked ratio; Lemmas 14.6 and 14.9 control the singleton coefficients and their format. Enlarge the fixed coefficient radius to include its signed and conjugated images. Put

\(\displaystyle E_k = R' C^{\text{col}}_{J_k} \prod_{i < k} C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}}, \qquad \textsf{w}_k = a_k^d \hat{\beta}_k^{- w_d} / E_k,\)

where \(R' > 0\) depends on the coefficient ball and \(w_d\) is a fixed nonnegative integer large enough for its finite polynomial evaluations and forced counterparts. The transport inequality of Lemma 14.7, iterated from \(m_{\text{ref}} (d)\), bounds the level-\(k\) coefficients by the product in \(E_k\). Threshold powers introduce only a fixed power of \(\hat{\beta}_k\); enlarging \(w_d\) includes them. Increase \(w_d\) to at least \(q^{\text{ht}}_d + 4\). Multiplying every mark by \(\textsf{w}_k\) then bounds its full singleton size by \(\hat{\beta}_k^{- w_d} \leqslant \zeta_{d, k}\).

The reflected anchors at level \(k\) have spacing \(a_m / a_k \geqslant B_k^2\), and their parents agree under each block map. Since \(B_{i + 1}^2 \leqslant 2 B_i^2\) and \(\ell_i \geqslant 3\), their spacing is at least \(B_i^2\) at every finer level. Apply Lemma 14.14 with initial weight \(\textsf{w}_k\). Its full-interval bound verifies the hypothesis of the rooted clause of Lemma 13.5. Use the mode-weighted coefficient norm of that proof; derivative and support weights remain in the separate majorants.

Singleton terms are bounded directly by their one-mark norms. Taylor's product rule introduces at most the factorial of the derivative order; the stage column constant was chosen above this factorial. With \(j = J_{k + 1}\), the rooted clause of Lemma 13.5 supplies the derivative bound at support exponent \(\sigma_{J_k} + 3\) and the forced bound \(v_{\star} e^{\eta^{\text{att}}_k n_{\star}} e^{- r_j \beta'_u t_{k + 1}^2}\) at exponent \(\sigma_{J_k} + 2\), where \(v_{\star}\) is the integrated majorant of the distinguished factor and \(n_{\star}\) its covering number. Multiplying by a mark weight changes only the distinguished factor in its tree sum. Item 1 bounds the derivative factorial. The weighted sums therefore retain those bounds; decreasing the final exponent from \(100\) to any value in \([98, 100]\) only decreases the sums. The forced normalization uses half the suppression, leaving the other half for the marked derivative and attachment losses. Their spatial anchor is always one of the marks; a nonzero mode is assigned only to a support containing all its marked anchors. This proves the support assertion in (14.23), as well as its absolute convergence. Restricting the set of marks commutes with the construction, since every operation takes disjoint unions of their modes.

At the stopping level, the weighted supremum of each complete singleton exponential term is at most \(3\), and that of its forced profile is at most \(1\). Indeed, its polynomial part is at most its coefficient bound, its marked regular sum is at most that bound, and the normalization of the forced part is at most one; Lemma 14.7 and the definition of \(E_k\) bound the sum by \(3\). The forced profile follows from its \(\kappa_k\) normalization. For (14.23), the local absolute sums give an additional two per mark: exponentiation of the connected regular terms and the eligible forced sum each contribute at most one. Integrating the remaining scalar-free unmarked exponent and dividing by the unmarked partition function gives at most \(e^{| \Lambda_{n_{p, k}} | q_k}\), by Lemma 14.1. Thus coefficient extraction in the weighted source norm yields

\(\displaystyle \textsf{w}_k^{|I_0 |} \left| \left\langle \prod_{i \in I_0} F_i \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant \exp \{| \Lambda_{n_{p, k}} |q_k + (3 + 1 + 2) |I_0 |\} .\)

Exact integration preserves these normalized coefficients, proving the first assertion. The ratio of site and cell counts is \((a_m / a_k)^4\). For a residual source controlled at weight \(\textsf{w}\), the same coefficient extraction uses \(\textsf{w}\) in place of \(\textsf{w}_k\); only the bounds \(3 + 1 + 2\) and the unmarked ratio enter this calculation. Taking the cell root gives

\(\displaystyle \|E\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m} \leqslant \textsf{w}^{- 1} \exp \{(a_m / a_k)^4 q_k + 6\} .\) (14.25)

In particular \(\textsf{w} = c \textsf{w}_0\) gives a bound \(C_{m, k, \textsf{w}_0} / c\), uniformly in the cutoff and period. This proves the residual clause, for which \(E\) need not be a primary insertion.

Finally (14.11), summed as in (14.13), gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i < k} \log C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}} = o (s_k), \qquad \log \hat{\beta}_k = o (s_k) .\)

The separate final column is included in this sum for \(k > 0\). Hence, for every \(\varepsilon' > 0\), there is \(k_0 (\varepsilon')\) with \(6 - \log \textsf{w}_k \leqslant (d + \varepsilon') s_k\) for all \(k \geqslant k_0 (\varepsilon')\). All choices precede the period, and all the estimates are on full cutoff tails.\(\Box\)

The centered estimate fixes its constant before the physical cell size. A sufficiently fine cutoff then places the fixed insertion stencil strictly inside that cell.

Lemma 14.16. (Centered bound) For every \(d\) and \(R, \varepsilon > 0\), there is \(A (d, R, \varepsilon) \geqslant 0\) such that, for every \(m \geqslant m_{\text{ref}} (d)\), some \(N_0 (d, R, \varepsilon, m) \geqslant m\) gives, for \(N \geqslant N_0\), every \(p, y\) and \(\|c\| \leqslant R\),

\(\displaystyle O_{N, p}^{(d)} (c, y) \text{ is interior}, \qquad \|O_{N, p}^{(d)} (c, y)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m (y)} \leqslant Aa_m^{- d - \varepsilon} .\)

Proof. The replicated source bound of Lemma 14.15 gives the seminorm at the stopping level \(k = k (m)\) of Lemma 14.2; the choice of \(k (m)\) makes the per-cell site cost negligible against \(s_k\). For \(0 < \varepsilon' < 1\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \|O (c, 0)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m (0)} & \leqslant \exp \{(a_m / a_k)^4 q_k + 6 - \log \textsf{w}_k \},\\ 6 - \log \textsf{w}_k & \leqslant (d + \varepsilon') s_k \quad (k \geqslant k_0 (\varepsilon')),\\ (a_m / a_{k (m)})^4 q_{k (m)} & \leqslant \varepsilon' s_{k (m)} \quad (m \geqslant m_0 (\varepsilon')) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(14.26)} \end{array}\)

The cutoff threshold is uniform in the coefficient ball and in \(p\).

Put \(\tau = s_{k (m)}\), \(\upsilon = s_m\) and \(W = (a_m / a_k)^4\). Since \(q_k \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle \tau - \upsilon = \log (a_m / a_k) \leqslant W \leqslant Wq_k \leqslant \varepsilon' \tau .\)

The exponent in the first line of (14.26) is therefore at most \((d + 2 \varepsilon') \tau \leqslant (d + 2 \varepsilon') \upsilon / (1 - \varepsilon')\). Choose \(\varepsilon'\) so that \((d + 2 \varepsilon') / (1 - \varepsilon') \leqslant d + \varepsilon\). This proves the required estimate with constant one for all sufficiently large \(m\). For the finitely many remaining \(m \geqslant m_{\text{ref}} (d)\), use the first line of (14.26) at the selected fixed \(k (m)\) and take the maximum of its ratio to \(a_m^{- d - \varepsilon}\) and one. This fixes \(A\) before \(m\).

Finally the stencil of Definition 2.8 has fixed lattice radius. For fixed \(m\) its physical radius tends to zero as \(N \to \infty\), uniformly in \(p\). Translation covariance moves the centered calculation to any \(y\).\(\Box\)

14.2.3Negligible errors and local matching

The following estimates concern the insertions of Definition 2.8, with the terminal maps \(\textsf{Z}_{N, p, d}\) of Lemma 14.7. They are used to identify the quotient fields in Section 15. A statement holds on full cutoff tails if it holds for all \(N \geqslant N_0\) and every \(p\). A nonnegative family \(b_{N, p}\) has subpower growth when, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), \(b_{N, p} \leqslant a_N^{- \varepsilon}\) on such a tail. A coefficient family is tame if

\(\displaystyle \forall \varepsilon > 0 \exists N_0 \forall N \geqslant N_0 \forall p : \| \textsf{Z}_{N, p, d}^{- 1} c_{N, p} \|_{\infty} \leqslant a_N^{- \varepsilon} .\) (14.27)

For later volume extraction we also use strictly increasing subsequences \(N = \textsf{n} (j)\), \(p = P (k)\) and the ordered limit

\(\displaystyle \text{olim} \hspace{0.17em} X_{N, p} = x \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \forall \varepsilon > 0 \exists k_0 \forall k \geqslant k_0 \exists j_0 (k) \forall j \geqslant j_0 (k) : |X_{\textsf{n} (j), P (k)} - x| < \varepsilon .\) (14.28)

The ordered versions of tameness and negligibility replace full cutoff tails by these quantifiers. We prove the full-tail versions of the bounds below; the same constructions work for ordered-tame families, with their ordered thresholds, and this is the form used in Section 15.

An anchor is the lattice site at which an insertion is placed; translates of a bond tiling are by lattice vectors. In a base cell of side \(a_k\), let \(z^a\) be the anchor's integer coordinate measured from the first site in direction \(a\), and put

\(\displaystyle M_{n_{\ast}, k, N} = \left\lfloor \frac{a_k}{8 (n_{\ast} + 1) a_N} \right\rfloor, \qquad M_{n_{\ast}, k, N} \leqslant z^a, \quad z^a + M_{n_{\ast}, k, N} + 1 \leqslant a_k / a_N \hspace{0.17em} .\) (14.29)

These inequalities specify a relative interior margin.

Definition 14.17. (Negligible errors) An observable family \(E_{N, p} (y)\) is negligible if there are \(\eta_{N, p} \geqslant 0\) with \(\sup_p \eta_{N, p} \to 0\) as \(N \to \infty\) such that, for every arity \(n_{\ast}\) and level \(m\), there are \(k \geqslant m\) and \(N_0 \geqslant k\) with the following property: for all \(N \geqslant N_0\), all \(p\), every translated bond tiling \(\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}\) of side \(a_k\) and every base-cell anchor \(y\) satisfying (14.29),

\(\displaystyle E_{N, p} (y) \text{ is interior}, \qquad \|E_{N, p} (y)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}} \leqslant \eta_{N, p} \hspace{0.17em} .\)

The tolerance is chosen before \(n_{\ast}\) and \(m\); the level and the cutoff tail may depend on these two requests. Here \(n_{\ast}\) is the number of insertions in a later product test, and the lower level \(m\) requires the chosen cell scale to be arbitrarily fine. All residuals below are translation covariant, and their signed and conjugated images obey the same estimates. To pass from centered to arbitrary interior seminorms, choose \(k' \geqslant k\) with \(24 (n_{\ast} + 1) a_{k'} \leqslant a_k\) and apply the finite reflection argument of Lemma 14.11. It uses only fixed stencils and the chessboard inequality, so it also applies to these residuals on full cutoff tails.

Local matching converts a local observable of controlled size into an insertion of the same degree. It makes no assumption on the size of the physical coefficient it produces; it bounds its inverse terminal coordinate, which is the quantity in (14.27). The derivative bound below controls Taylor extraction, and the supremum bound controls the forced complement.

Lemma 14.18. (Local comparison)

    (i)

    Local matching. Let \(F_{N, p} (y)\) be local observables such that \(a_N^{d'} F_{N, p}\) is continuous on a fixed stencil and regular on healthy configurations in the sense of Definition 5.3, with derivative bounds and a supremum bound over all configurations of subpower growth. Then there are tame \(b_{N, p} \in V_{d'}\) with \(O^{(d')} (b) - F\) negligible.

    (ii)

    Auxiliary equation insertions. For the auxiliary section of Lemma 10.4, there are invertible \(P_{N, p}\), with \(\|P_{N, p}^{- 1} \|_1 \leqslant 2\) eventually uniformly in \(p\), such that, with \(B_{d, N, p} = \textsf{Z}_{N, p, d} P_{N, p} t_N^{\textsf{N}} \textsf{R}_d\), the comparison (14.30) holds for every equation label \(\pi_{N, p} \in \mathcal{I}_d\) with tame \(B_{d, N, p} \pi_{N, p}\).

Here \(\mathcal{I}_d\) is the equation ideal described in the proof of Lemma 14.19, and the comparison is

\(\displaystyle O^{(d)}_{N, p} (B_{d, N, p} \pi_{N, p}, y) - a_N^{- d} V_d^{\text{aux}} [\textsf{R}_d \pi_{N, p}] (y) \quad \text{is negligible} .\) (14.30)

Both parts use the same backward coefficient correction.

Proof. We represent the observable as a one-mark source at the cutoff, carry the difference with the selected primary insertion backwards with the marked step (Lemma 14.6), and bound its replicated seminorm by Lemma 14.15. Write \(h_N = a_N^{d'} F_{N, p} (0)\). Enlarge its stencil by the section stencil. The radial Taylor coefficients through degree \(d'\) (in the sections of Lemma 10.2) give a coefficient \(v_N\) and a remainder. They are bounded by a fixed multiple of the derivative bound: it is a linear map on a finite jet space. Subtract the section with that coefficient on the healthy part. Its regular remainder has zero value and zero radial jet through degree \(d'\). Assign the complementary observable to the forced supports meeting the enlarged stencil. This gives an exact one-mark representation of \(\text{dens} \hspace{0.17em} \rho_N h_N\), with

\(\displaystyle |v_N |_1 + r_N \leqslant E_{N, p}, \qquad \text{forced majorant} \leqslant E'_{N, p},\) (14.31)

where \(E, E'\) have subpower growth. The support weight of this fixed enlarged stencil is bounded by \(e^{81 \sigma_{J_N}}\) eventually; its \(B_N\) covering number is at most \(81\). Threshold powers, the Taylor-coefficient bound and this support weight are included in \(E, E'\). The entry conditions make each of them subpower. No division by the forced normalization is made on the uncontrolled complement: it is retained with its original eligibility tests and the supremum bound \(E'\).

To compare with the selected graph, carry this representation backwards and at the reference level choose the primary coefficient with the same polynomial coordinate. Subtract the corresponding selected inserted density. Its polynomial coordinate at the reference level is zero. The following calculation carries this matching condition towards the fine scale.

For one step, let \(u, v\) be the coarse and fine coordinates, \(R, T\) their remainder bounds, \(Y = | \textsf{S}_L v|_1\), \(K\) the norm bound for the leading map and its inverse, \(\varepsilon_L = L^{- 3 / 8}\) and \(\vartheta_L = L^{1 / 8}\). The marked-step inequalities give

\(\displaystyle Y \leqslant K (|u|_1 + \varepsilon_L (Y + T)), \qquad R \leqslant KY + \varepsilon_L T.\) (14.32)

Suppose \(a |u|_1 \leqslant R\), with \(a = 32 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_i})^2 \geqslant 4 K^2\). Then \(4 K |u|_1 \leqslant Y + \varepsilon_L T\). Substitution in the first inequality, using \(4 K \varepsilon_L \leqslant 1\), gives \(Y \leqslant 4 K \varepsilon_L T\). Item 3 in Lemma 14.2 gives

\(\displaystyle 4 K \hspace{0.17em} 32 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}})^2 \varepsilon_L \leqslant 1, \qquad (4 K^2 + 1) \vartheta_L \varepsilon_L \leqslant 1.\)

Consequently \(32 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}})^2 |v|_1 \leqslant T\) and \(\vartheta_L R \leqslant T\). The matching condition starts this induction with \(u = 0\) at the reference level. Multiplying the second inequality through the interval gives the inverse-scale gain, while the finest-level bound is closed by absorbing the coefficient contribution into its remainder bound. In particular, with subpower \(E''\) absorbing \(E, E'\),

\(\displaystyle | \Delta_N |_1 \leqslant \frac{2 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_N}) E''_{N, p}}{\ell_{N - 1}^{1 / 8}}, \qquad R_k \leqslant 2 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_N}) E''_{N, p} e^{- (s_N - s_k) / 8} .\) (14.33)

Here \(\Delta_N\) is the difference from the selected fine polynomial coordinate. The same bounds hold when a local source is first represented at any level \(n \leqslant N\), with its polynomial coordinate matched to the selected graph at a lower reference level. If \(E''_{n ; N, p}\) bounds its starting data after the coefficient contribution is absorbed, then

\(\displaystyle | \Delta_n |_1 \leqslant \frac{2 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_n}) E''_{n ; N, p}}{\ell_{n - 1}^{1 / 8}}, \qquad R_k \leqslant 2 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_n}) E''_{n ; N, p} e^{- (s_n - s_k) / 8} \quad (m_{\text{ref}} (d') \leqslant k < n) .\) (14.34)

Indeed the cone starts at the same zero matched coordinate, and its induction uses only the steps through \(n\). The boundary absorption uses the starting bound there. The product \(\prod_{i = k}^{n - 1} \ell_i^{- 1 / 8}\) is exactly \(e^{- (s_n - s_k) / 8}\). Taking \(n = N\) recovers (14.33). The factor \(e^{- (s_n - s_k) / 8}\) is the inverse-scale gain: each matched step contracts the remainder by \(\ell^{- 1 / 8}\).

To estimate the replicated residual, for a source of size \(E\) started at \(N\), use at level \(k\) the mark weight

\(\displaystyle \textsf{w} = \frac{\zeta_{d', k} / 4}{a_k^{- d'} E \exp \{- (s_N - s_k) / 8\}},\)

where \(\zeta_{d', k} = \hat{\beta}_k^{- (q^{\text{ht}}_{d'} + 4)}\) is the source threshold fixed before \(N, p\) in (14.6). Its reciprocal is a fixed power of the reference coupling. At every finer step multiply this weight by the source contraction factor of that step. Its product with the dimensionless singleton size is at most one quarter of that source threshold; the other three quarters bound the graph remainder, the forced complement and their sum. This follows inductively from the two inequalities in (14.17). For the first step the forced complement is bounded by \(E'\) in (14.31); the large-field suppression absorbs its fixed support and derivative factors before division by the new forced normalization. The radius transport (14.7) and the singleton site-sum calculation in Lemma 14.14 control the singleton attachments. The complete residual is controlled at level \(k\) in the sense of Lemma 14.15 only after the higher-mode propagation identified there has been established. The singleton polynomial coordinate at the reference level is zero, so the representation constructed this way is exactly the difference with the selected primary coefficient.

Here \(m\) labels the tiling and \(k\) the finer stopping level at which its replicated sources are integrated. Replicate the difference at a stopping pair \(m \leqslant k\), and use the weighted source representation (14.23). Coefficient extraction contributes \(\textsf{w}^{- 1}\) per mark; the complete singleton bounds and the normalized unmarked ratio (14.2) contribute a constant depending on \(m, k\). Consequently, for every fixed such pair,

\(\displaystyle \|O^{(d')} (b) - F\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_m (0)} \leqslant C_{m, k} \mathfrak{e}_{N, p} a_N^{1 / 8}, \qquad \| \textsf{Z}_{N, p, d'}^{- 1} b_{N, p} \|_{\infty} \leqslant 2 E'''_{N, p},\) (14.35)

where \(\mathfrak{e}, E'''\) have subpower growth. The factor \(e^{s_k / 8}\) and the fixed weight factor \(\zeta_{d', k}^{- 1}\) belong to \(C_{m, k}\). The same argument applies after each signed permutation and conjugation, with translation covariance and the union of their fixed stencils. Absorbing two factors \(a_N^{- 1 / 32}\) bounds the first expression by \(a_N^{1 / 16}\) on every fixed pair. The interior-seminorm comparison after Definition 14.17 gives the common tolerance \(a_N^{1 / 32}\), chosen before all cell requests. This proves negligibility and tameness on full cutoff tails, and part (i).

For part (ii), perform the construction on all its basis polynomials together, with the original Taylor normalization. Let \(u_j\) be the primary bare coefficient matching its \(j\)th basis vector, and set \(Px = \sum_j x_j u_j\), after the finite symmetry averaging. For this fixed section the regular derivative bound \(H_{j, d}\) at stage \(j\) is fixed before its ratio, independently of the later coupling. The sharper coefficient estimate in (14.33) therefore has numerator bounded by \(2 (1 + C^{\text{col}}_j) H_{j, d}\) on each basis input. The ratio condition, including the finite basis-recombination factor, gives

\(\displaystyle | x - Px |_1 \leqslant \tfrac{1}{4} |x|_1 .\)

Indeed, before summing over the fixed basis the right side is the last-step factor \(\ell_{N - 1}^{- 1 / 8}\) times the stage coefficient bound; the stage ratio was chosen after that bound. 14.2.4Equation identities and coefficient corrections

The equation projections separate the coefficient directions controlled by exact cutoff identities. The free equation space consists of polynomial labels that vanish on curvature jets satisfying the Maxwell equations. Its transported image is the kernel of the projections chosen below, and the free quotient has dimension \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}\). The projections and the equation observables in Lemmas 14.19–14.20 are chosen together, before the spacing and volume subsequences.

Lemma 14.19. (Equation projections) For every \(d\), there are complex linear idempotents \(\Pi_{d, N, p} : V_d \to V_d\) and \(C^{\Pi}_d < \infty\) such that

\(\displaystyle \| \Pi_{d, N, p} \| \leqslant C^{\Pi}_d, \qquad \dim \text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} \Pi_{d, N, p} \leqslant \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}}, \qquad \ker \Pi_{d, N, p} = B_{d, N, p} \mathcal{I}_d,\)

with the maps \(B\) of Lemma 14.18(ii).

Proof. The kernel is prescribed by the transported equation ideal, and the bound comes from choosing orthogonal projections in a fixed norm. Let \(\mathcal{I}_d \subset V_d\) be the polynomials of Definition 2.7 belonging to the ideal generated by the linear Maxwell equations in radial jet coordinates. Radial coordinates already satisfy the linear Bianchi identities. Taking invariant polynomials commutes with the on-shell quotient: average any representative over the compact adjoint group to obtain an invariant representative. Thus the on-shell map is onto \(M_d^{\text{Max}}\) (Definition 6.3) and

\(\displaystyle \dim V_d - \dim \mathcal{I}_d = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_d^{\text{Max}} .\) (14.36)

Lemma 14.18(ii) constructs invertible coefficient maps \(B_{d, N, p} : V_d \to V_d\). They transport \(\mathcal{I}_d\) to the coefficient equations for auxiliary local representatives, including their change to the selected insertion coordinates. Set \(U_{d, N, p} = B_{d, N, p} \mathcal{I}_d\). In the Euclidean norm on the fixed basis take the orthogonal projection \(Q_{d, N, p}\) onto \(U_{d, N, p}\) and set \(\Pi_{d, N, p} = 1 - Q_{d, N, p}\). Then \(\Pi^2 = \Pi\) and \(\ker \Pi = U_{d, N, p}\). Equivalence with the fixed coefficient norm bounds \(\| \Pi \|\) independently of \(N, p\), even when \(B\) is poorly conditioned. Since \(B\) is invertible, \(\dim \ker \Pi = \dim \mathcal{I}_d\), and (14.36) gives the dimension bound.

The same local comparison maps will be used in Lemma 14.20. They and these projections are chosen for all cutoffs before any subsequences.\(\Box\)

The next estimate converts a coefficient equation into an observable with exact separated moment identities.

Lemma 14.20. 14.2.6Bounds for lattice differences

Positional differences are a separate problem. A difference quotient multiplies the insertion by the inverse spacing,

\(\displaystyle \frac{O^{(d)}_{N, p} (c, y + e_a) - O^{(d)}_{N, p} (c, y)}{a_N} = a_N^{- (d + 1)} (P_d [t_N^{- \textsf{N}} \textsf{Z}_{N, p, d}^{- 1} c] (y + e_a) - P_d [t_N^{- \textsf{N}} \textsf{Z}_{N, p, d}^{- 1} c] (y)),\) (14.50)

so Lemma 14.16, applied to the two terms on the left, bounds its seminorm only by \(2 Aa_m^{- d - \varepsilon} / a_N\), which diverges as \(N \to \infty\). The remedy has three steps. Local matching represents the difference quotient as a degree-\((d + 1)\) insertion up to a negligible error (Lemma 14.22); the factor \(a_N^{- (d + 1)}\) becomes the normalization of the new insertion, but its coefficient is only tame, not bounded. The equation projection removes the directions of that coefficient whose insertions vanish in separated correlations (Lemmas 14.19–14.20). The remaining coefficient lies in a space of dimension at most \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_{d + 1}^{\text{Max}}\) and is bounded by testing it against the finite free matrix (Lemmas 14.23 and 14.28).

The tests of the difference must be bounded without any bound on differentiated insertions. Averaging the difference quotient over \(Q_N \approx h_{\text{av}} / a_N\) consecutive translates telescopes to a difference over the fixed physical distance \(Q_N a_N\), which is bounded by undifferentiated insertions alone; this is why the argument is not circular. Comparing averaged tests at nearby positions uses the following displacement estimate, which also involves only undifferentiated insertions. For a grid configuration \(x\) define its minimum coordinate separation on the physical torus by

\(\displaystyle g_p (x) = \min_{i \ne j} \min_{a = 1, \ldots, 4} \min_{z \in L_p \mathbb{Z}} |x_i^a - x_j^a + z|,\)

with value \(+ \infty\) for one slot. This separation supplies the empty slabs for reflection positivity. A coordinate hybrid of \(x, y\) chooses each coordinate from either configuration. Assume the insertions and their single-axis reflections are interior at a centered level \(D \leqslant N\), with all their seminorms at most \(A \geqslant 0\).

Lemma 14.29. (Displacement of correlation tests) For every \(r > 0\) with \(a_D < r\), a one-coordinate grid move \(x \mapsto y\) of size at most \(r\) from \(g_p (x) \geqslant 4 r\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle |M_{N, p} (v ; x / a_N) - M_{N, p} (v ; y / a_N) | \leqslant A^n |x - y| / r.\)

\(\displaystyle |M_{N, p} (v ; x / a_N) - M_{N, p} (v ; y / a_N) | \leqslant 4 nA^n |x - y| / r.\)

Proof. A move changes the lengths of the two empty slabs next to the moved insertion, and the spectral theorem for the transfer operator bounds the effect of that change; the reflection inequality (14.19) bounds what remains. Lemma 14.16 supplies the assumed seminorms for bounded coefficient lists on full cutoff tails. The separation hypothesis gives every directed coordinate distance at least \(4 r\), at any period. No fixed test neighbourhood is used.

Fix an axis and all other coordinates. Reflection positivity and translation invariance give a positive transfer operator, rescaled to \(0 \leqslant T \leqslant 1\). On the periodic axis the expectation is a normalized trace. If \(X\) denotes the insertion being moved, its two adjacent empty slabs have lengths \(n_1^{\text{sl}}, n_2^{\text{sl}}\) in fine sites. A move by \(l \geqslant 0\) changes their factor by

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} T^{n_1^{\text{sl}} + l} XT^{n_2^{\text{sl}} - l} - T^{n_1^{\text{sl}}} XT^{n_2^{\text{sl}}} & = T^{n_1^{\text{sl}}} (T^l X - XT^l) T^{n_2^{\text{sl}} - l}\\ & = - T^{n_1^{\text{sl}}} (1 - T^l) XT^{n_2^{\text{sl}} - l} + T^{n_1^{\text{sl}}} X (1 - T^l) T^{n_2^{\text{sl}} - l} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(14.51)} \end{array}\)

Both expressions are inserted in the same remaining cyclic product and divided by the same partition trace. The stencil is contained in a centered cell of lattice side \(d_{\text{cell}} = a_D / a_N\). Since \(a_D < r\), the directed separations exceed \(2 d_{\text{cell}} + 2 r / a_N\), and \(la_N \leqslant r\) by assumption. Splitting the two surrounding empty slabs at their reflection planes leaves \(n_{\text{sl}} + 1 \geqslant 2 r / a_N\) fine sites next to each occurrence of \(1 - T^l\). The spectral theorem gives

\(\displaystyle \|(1 - T^l) T^{n_{\text{sl}}} \| \leqslant \sup_{0 \leqslant t \leqslant 1} l (1 - t) t^{n_{\text{sl}}} \leqslant l / (n_{\text{sl}} + 1) \leqslant la_N / (2 r) .\)

For each normalized trace in (14.51), reflection Schwarz at the slab ends and the chessboard inequality bound the remaining factors by the product of their centered seminorms, at most \(A^n\). One applies this inequality to the complete cyclic product, so no supremum norm of \(X\) is required. The two terms together are bounded by \(A^n la_N / r\). An arity-one moment is translation invariant and has change zero.

For a negative move reverse the chosen axis. Coordinate separation is unchanged and the assumed reflected seminorms have the same bound, so the same slab estimate applies. Replace the \(4 n\) coordinates one at a time. Each starting configuration is a coordinate hybrid and hence has the required separation. Each move is at most \(|x - y| \leqslant r\). Summing proves the second bound. The constants depend only on the seminorms, arity and slab margin, uniformly in \(N, p\).\(\Box\)

All these bounds hold on full cutoff tails and use no positional difference estimate. Thus \(\|\mathcal{P}_{N, p} c^{\boldsymbol{a}} \| \leqslant C_{\text{test}}\) eventually, uniformly in \(p\).

Step 3: Invertibility of the tests. The finite-dimensional lower bound is obtained before any cutoff limit. The free matrix has a positive lower bound on the unit sphere. The relative-error estimate and the equation-seminorm bound put the tests of the projected lifts within \(O (\gamma_{\nu}) + o_{N \to \infty} (1)\) of the free matrix, uniformly in \(p\). Choose the fixed \(\nu\) with this error smaller than its matrix lower bound. Averaging over sufficiently small \(h_{\text{av}}\) preserves the lower bound: the undifferentiated correlation tests have uniform positional continuity by Lemma 14.29, with margin \(b\), the centered level \(D\) and the coordinate-hybrid separations just chosen. The level and its seminorm bounds are fixed before reducing \(h_{\text{av}}\), so the displacement bound tends to zero with \(h_{\text{av}}\). The projected lifts are therefore linearly independent. Their number equals \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}} M_{d'}^{\text{Max}}\), whereas Lemma 14.19 bounds \(\dim \text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} \Pi_{d'}\) by that number. They consequently span the range. Their uniform coefficient bounds give

\(\displaystyle \|c^{\text{pr}} \| \leqslant C_{\text{det}} \|\mathcal{P}_{N, p} c^{\text{pr}} \|\) (14.52)

with \(C_{\text{det}}\) independent of \(N, p\) on the retained tails. Choose an invertible square minor \(H_0\) of the free matrix and the corresponding interacting minor \(H\). For \(\|H - H_0 \| \leqslant (2\|H_0^{- 1} \|)^{- 1}\), the inverse bound \(\|H^{- 1} \| \leqslant 2 \|H_0^{- 1} \|\) follows.

Step 4: The bound on \(c^{\text{pr}}\). The equation coefficient \(z = (c^{\boldsymbol{a}} - c^{\text{pr}}) / (1 +\|c^{\text{pr}} \|)\) is tame. Its tests tend to zero, so eventually \(C_{\text{det}} \|\mathcal{P}_{N, p} z\| \leqslant 1 / 2\). Equation (14.52) yields

\(\displaystyle \|c^{\text{pr}} \| \leqslant C_{\text{det}} C_{\text{test}} + \tfrac{1}{2} (1 +\|c^{\text{pr}} \|), \qquad \|c^{\text{pr}} \| \leqslant 2 C_{\text{det}} C_{\text{test}} + 1.\)

Lemma 14.16 now bounds the seminorms of \(O^{(d')} (c^{\text{pr}})\) at each fixed sufficiently fine scale. Now \(c^{\boldsymbol{a}} - c^{\text{pr}}\) is tame, so its equation residual has vanishing seminorm. Together with the matching residual, the triangle inequality for seminorms gives the same type of bound for \(\Delta_{\boldsymbol{a}} F\). All thresholds and constants in this argument are independent of \(p\).

Step 5: Products at separated points. Distribute the differences in \(\boldsymbol{l}\) among their indicated slots. A difference in slot \(i\) acts only on that insertion, so the resulting moment is exactly the expectation of the product of these differentiated insertions. Choose a fixed tiling side smaller than \(r\) and translate its boundaries away from all anchors. In each axis the excluded boundary strips occupy less than one full cell length if their widths are chosen below \(a_m / [4 (n + 1)]\). For sufficiently large \(N\) a lattice translation with these margins exists. Distinct anchors occupy distinct cells, since their torus separation exceeds the cell side. The interior-seminorm comparison, with a finer centered scale, bounds all the factors uniformly. Apply (14.19) and put one in every unused cell. Their product gives the asserted \(B\). Empty difference lists and arities at most one obey the same argument. This proves the lemma and Proposition 5.8.\(\Box\)

14.2.7Bounded approximations

The later phase argument needs bounded approximations with a bound independent of the cutoff. Compactness at each cutoff gives no such bound. We obtain it by stopping at one fixed coarse lattice and pulling bounded observables back through the probability block kernels.

In the next two lemmas, a statement holds eventually in the ordered limits when it holds for all sufficiently large indices of the chosen volume sequence and, at each such volume, all sufficiently fine cutoffs of the chosen spacing sequence. No common cutoff threshold in other subsequently fixed parameters is asserted. The chessboard seminorms used below are defined in Definition 14.10.

Lemma 14.31. (Bounded coarse approximations) For every \(d\), \(k \geqslant m_{\text{ref}} (d)\), real \(c \in V_d\) and \(\delta > 0\), there are measurable real observables \(\mathcal{B}_{N, p} (c, y)\) and \(M_{\text{bnd}} < \infty\), independent of \(N, p, y\), with \(|\mathcal{B}_{N, p} (c, y) | \leqslant M_{\text{bnd}}\) and

\(\displaystyle \|\mathcal{B}_{N, p} (c, y) - O^{(d)}_{N, p} (c, y)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_k (y)} \leqslant \delta\)

eventually in the ordered limits. Both observables are interior to that cell, and the same holds for their signed and reflected images.

Proof. We stop at a fixed coarse level \(h\), replace the insertion by a bounded coarse polynomial pulled back through the probability block kernels (Definition 5.1), and bound the discrepancy by replicating it in every cell (Lemma 14.15); the marked step of Lemma 14.6 matches the coefficients. The construction is independent of the phase decomposition. Choose a fixed \(m \geqslant k\) with \(B_m^2 \leqslant a_k / a_m\), using Lemma 14.2. At a finer level \(h \geqslant m\), the bounded coarse polynomial with coordinate \(v\) is

\(\displaystyle \textsf{V}_h (v ; U) = a_h^{- d} \left( v_0 +\boldsymbol{1}_{\Sigma_d \hspace{0.17em} \text{healthy}} P_d [t_h^{- \textsf{N}} v^{\circ}] (U) \right) .\)

Its supremum for each basis input is bounded by a constant depending on \(d, h\), uniformly in \(N, p\): the healthy polynomial uses a fixed compact coordinate range, and the unit term is constant. Its inspected stencil has a fixed radius, including the links needed for the relaxed threshold test.

Let \(\mathcal{K}_{N, h} (U, dV)\) be the probability kernel obtained by composing the block kernels from \(N\) to \(h\), and set

\(\displaystyle (\mathcal{P}_{N, h} F) (U) = \int F (V) \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{K}_{N, h} (U, dV) .\)

It preserves real values and does not increase the supremum norm. The locality of the block kernels confines its dependence to the fine preimage of the inspected stencil. For reflected cells these ancestor cones are disjoint once \(h\) has the stated margin. At each intermediate level the block kernels are products in the coarse-link variables given the preceding field; integrating disjoint ancestor cones therefore gives conditional independence, and hence \(\prod_C \mathcal{P}_{N, h} F_C =\mathcal{P}_{N, h} (\prod_C F_C)\). Choose \(h\) sufficiently large that this preimage lies strictly inside the centered level-\(k\) cell, including its boundary-link margin.

To compare with the actual insertion, choose auxiliary fine coefficients \(u_j\) whose selected level-\(h\) polynomial coordinates differ by at most \(1 / 4\) from the basis vectors. They are obtained by carrying the pure coarse polynomial back through the inverse graph construction in Lemma 14.6. Apply (14.32) to the difference with this primary coarse polynomial. Write \(S_h = C^{\text{col}}_{J_h}\), and let \(\mathcal{J}_h\) be the matrix of the selected level-\(h\) coordinates of these trials. For a basis input, the cone and the finest-level boundary estimate give

\(\displaystyle 32 (1 + S_h)^2 |e_j -\mathcal{J}_h e_j |_1 \leqslant R_h, \qquad R_h \leqslant 2 (1 + S_h), \qquad |e_j -\mathcal{J}_h e_j |_1 \leqslant \frac{1}{16 (1 + S_h)} \leqslant \frac{1}{4} .\) (14.53)

Taking the maximum over columns gives \(\|\mathcal{J}_h - 1\|_1 \leqslant 1 / 4\), which implies \(\|\mathcal{J}_h^{- 1} \|_1 \leqslant 2\). If \(c\) is the required reference coefficient, linear recombination therefore gives

\(\displaystyle O (c) = \sum_j t_j O (\textsf{Z}_{N, p, d} u_j), \qquad \sum_j |t_j | \leqslant 2 e^{\sum_{i < h} \log C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}}} |c|_1 .\)

Replace each term on the right by \(\mathcal{P}_{N, h} \textsf{V}_h (e_j)\). This defines a bounded complex observable \(\mathcal{B}'_{N, p}\); its bound depends on \(h, d, c\), and hence may depend on \(\delta\), but is independent of \(N, p\).

To control the discrepancy, take one reflected copy in each cell of the tiling. For a fine field \(U\), let \(O_C (U)\) be the copy of \(O (\textsf{Z} u_j)\) in cell \(C\); for a coarse field \(V\), let \(\textsf{V}_{h, C} (V)\) be the corresponding copy of the coarse polynomial. Given \(U\), the ancestor cones are disjoint, so the conditional factorization above gives the exact identity

\(\displaystyle \prod_C ((\mathcal{P}_{N, h} \textsf{V}_{h, C}) (U) - O_C (U)) = \int \prod_C (\textsf{V}_{h, C} (V) - O_C (U)) \mathcal{K}_{N, h} (U, dV) .\)

Formula (14.22) applies to the fine insertions; at level \(h\) multiply the source density by the corresponding coarse factors. The probability kernel supplies no additional supremum factor. Conditional on the higher-mode propagation input identified in Lemma 14.14, weighted coefficient extraction, Lemma 14.15 and (14.2) give, after recombination,

\(\displaystyle \|\mathcal{B}'_{N, p} - O (c)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\text{tile}}_k} \leqslant C_{k, m, d, c} \hspace{0.17em} e^{\sum_{i < h} \log C^{\text{col}}_{J_{i + 1}}} (1 + C^{\text{col}}_{J_h}) e^{- (s_h - s_m) / 8} .\) (14.54)

The prefactor has subpower growth in \(a_h^{- 1}\) by (14.11); the last factor tends to zero. Choose \(h\) so that this expression is at most \(\delta\), before choosing the cutoff. This also meets the stencil-fit condition.

For real \(c\), the original insertion is real by Lemmas 10.2 and 14.8. Set \(\mathcal{B}= \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{B}'\). In the centered cell,

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{B}- O (c) = \tfrac{1}{2} (\mathcal{B}' - O (c)) + \tfrac{1}{2} \overline{\mathcal{B}' - O (c)} .\)

The chessboard seminorm and its invariance under conjugation show that this does not increase (14.54); the supremum bound also does not increase. Translation, signed permutation and conjugation use the same finite collection of stencils and bounds. This proves the lemma on the stated ordered tails.\(\Box\)

Fix a degree bound \(d\), a lower level \(m\) and an ordered extraction. A real grid word is a list \(w = ((d_i, c_i), x_i)_{i = 1}^n\) with \(d_i \leqslant d\), \(\bar{c}_i = c_i\) and distinct spacing-\(a_k\) positions. We assume its configuration is generic. Let \(\vartheta\) denote linear time reflection on positions and labels, without complex conjugation of scalar coefficients. For an ordered word \(w\), write \(w^{\ast}\) for conjugation and reversal of order, and \(\theta w = \vartheta w^{\ast}\) for the antilinear reflection of Osterwalder and Schrader. Put

\(\displaystyle F_{N, p} (w) = \prod_i O^{(d_i)}_{N, p} (c_i, x_i / a_N), \qquad z_j (T) = Tja_k (1, 1, 1, 1),\)
\(\displaystyle f_{j, N, p, T} = F_{N, p} (\tau_{z_j (T)} w) - F_{N, p} (\tau_{z_j (T)} \vartheta w) .\)

These are the real word differences used in the off-diagonal variance estimate in Section 17.

Lemma 14.32. (Bounded real word approximations) There is \(k \geqslant m\), chosen before \(w\), such that every such word has, for each \(\varepsilon > 0\), measurable real approximations \(g_{j, N, p, T}\) and \(R_{\varepsilon} < \infty\) with \(|g_{j, N, p, T} | \leqslant R_{\varepsilon}\) for all \(N, p, T, j\). For each \(i < j\) and every sufficiently large integer \(T\), eventually in the ordered limits,

\(\displaystyle | \langle g_{i, N, p, T} g_{j, N, p, T} \rangle_{N, p} - \text{Re} \langle f_{i, N, p, T} f_{j, N, p, T} \rangle_{N, p} | \leqslant \varepsilon .\)

Proof. Each insertion of the word is replaced by its bounded approximation from Lemma 14.31; the chessboard inequality (14.19), with the seminorm bounds of Lemmas 14.16 and 14.11, controls the replacement error by seminorms alone. Choose \(k \geqslant \max (m, \max_{e \leqslant d} m_{\text{ref}} (e))\). It depends only on \(d, m\), before the length, labels and positions of \(w\). All its finer comparison levels remain available by Lemma 14.2.

Include every label in \(w\) and its signed and conjugated images in a finite list. Lemma 14.16 gives a bound \(C\) for their centered seminorms at any one fixed sufficiently fine level. Choose that level so that its cells and the margins required by Lemma 14.11 fit between distinct spacing-\(a_k\) points. Apply Lemma 14.31 with error \(\delta\) at a still finer centered level, preserving these margins. Denote its real bounded approximations by \(\mathcal{B}_i\). The same family, translated to any anchor, is used for every copy of the word. Its seminorms are at most \(C + \delta\), independently of its possibly much larger supremum bound.

For a product of \(n_{\text{word}}\) such insertions at distinct grid sites, telescope the products, replacing one factor at a time. Put all \(n_{\text{word}}\) factors into distinct cells with the chosen margins. The interior-seminorm comparison and (14.19) give

\(\displaystyle \left| \left\langle \prod_{a = 1}^{n_{\text{word}}} \mathcal{B}_a - \prod_{a = 1}^{n_{\text{word}}} O_a \right\rangle_{N, p} \right| \leqslant n_{\text{word}} \delta (C + \delta)^{n_{\text{word}} - 1} .\) (14.55)

Only the seminorms, not the supremum bounds on \(\mathcal{B}_a\), occur on the right. For each fixed finite configuration, sufficiently large periods avoid wrapping identifications; its stencils then lie in the prescribed cells on the inner cutoff tail. This explains the order of the two eventualities.

Let \(G_{j, N, p, T}\) be the product of the \(\mathcal{B}_i\) for the translated word, and let \(G^{\theta}_{j, N, p, T}\) be the corresponding product for its reflected word. Set \(g_j = G_j - G^{\theta}_j\). Choose \(M_i^{\text{bnd}}\) to bound both the original and reflected approximations in slot \(i\). Then

\(\displaystyle |g_j | \leqslant 2 \prod_i M_i^{\text{bnd}} = : R_{\varepsilon},\)

independently of \(N, p, T, j\). Every \(g_j\) is real and measurable. Set \(g_j = 0\) outside the ordered tails on which the coarse approximations are defined, so this supremum bound holds for all \(N, p, T, j\). For fixed \(i < j\), sufficiently large \(T\) separates the two translated finite lists, for each of the four combinations of a word and a reflected word. Indeed their first coordinates differ by \(T (j - i) a_k\) plus a fixed finite difference. Each list is itself distinct because the original word is generic and reflection is injective.

Expand \(g_i g_j\) and \(f_i f_j\) into their four product terms and apply (14.55) with \(n_{\text{word}} = 2 n\). Choose \(0 < \delta \leqslant 1\) so that \(4 (2 n) \delta (C + 1)^{2 n - 1} \leqslant \varepsilon\). Taking real parts cannot increase the error. Intersecting the four eventual sets proves the assertion. The empty word has zero difference and may be assigned \(g_j = 0\). There is no common cutoff threshold in \(T, i, j\); a finite list of pairs is handled by intersecting its eventual sets. The choice gives such a grid level above every prescribed lower level \(m\), which is the cofinality required in Section 17.\(\Box\)

14.3Full ultraviolet convergence

We prove convergence as the ultraviolet cutoff increases, at fixed volume. The argument is a triangle inequality through a common fixed level \(m\). At a separated configuration \(x\) on a fixed common grid,

\(\displaystyle |M_{N, p} - M_{N', p} | \leqslant \underbrace{|M_{N, p} - C_{N, p, m} |}_{\text{stopping error}} + \underbrace{|C_{N, p, m} - C_{N', p, m} |}_{\text{fixed-level difference}} + \underbrace{|C_{N', p, m} - M_{N', p} |}_{\text{stopping error}},\) (14.56)

where \(M_{N, p} = M_{N, p} (v ; x / a_N)\) is the cutoff moment and \(C_{N, p, m} = C_{N, p, m} (v ; x)\) is its approximation computed at level \(m\) from the unmarked representation and the one-mark families (14.58). The intended bounds are \(2 F_0 e^{- B_m / 64}\) for each stopping error, with \(F_0\) independent of \(m\), and convergence of the fixed-level difference as \(N, N' \to \infty\) at fixed \(m\). The proofs of Lemmas 14.35 and 14.34 identify estimates still needed for these bounds. Once those estimates are established, choose \(m\) to make both stopping errors small, hold it fixed, and then send both cutoffs to infinity.

The first lemma treats one fixed coarse level. Its proof uses a comparison of the actual marked steps on a common class of graphs. The elementary observation below shows which convergence of those steps is sufficient.

Lemma 14.33. (Contracting steps on a common core) Let \(X_i\) be nonempty complete metric spaces of diameter at most \(D\), and let \(T_{N, i} : X_{i + 1} \to X_i\), defined for all sufficiently large \(N\), have a common Lipschitz constant \(0 \leqslant q < 1\). Suppose that, for each fixed \(i\), the sequence \(T_{N, i} g\) is Cauchy for every \(g\) in a dense subset of \(X_{i + 1}\). Suppose also that, for every fixed \(h\) and all sufficiently large \(N\), the selected trajectory is defined through level \(h\), lies in the respective spaces and obeys \(g_{N, i} = T_{N, i} g_{N, i + 1}\) at every \(i < h\). Then \(g_{N, m}\) is Cauchy for each fixed \(m\).

Proof. If \(g_N \to g\) in \(X_{i + 1}\) and \(a\) belongs to the dense subset, then

\(\displaystyle d_i (T_{N, i} g_N, T_{N', i} g_{N'}) \leqslant q \hspace{0.17em} d_{i + 1} (g_N, a) + d_i (T_{N, i} a, T_{N', i} a) + q \hspace{0.17em} d_{i + 1} (a, g_{N'}) .\)

First let \(N, N' \to \infty\), then choose \(a\) arbitrarily close to \(g\). Completeness gives a common limit. Fix \(h > m\) and start each cutoff recursion at the same point of \(X_h\). Applying this argument at the finitely many levels from \(h\) to \(m\) gives Cauchy auxiliary outputs. Each differs from the selected output by at most \(Dq^{h - m}\). Given a tolerance, choose \(h\) first and then choose the cutoffs, proving the claim.\(\Box\)

For a fixed graph, finite address truncations give a candidate core: the polynomial coordinate space has a finite \(\ell^1\) basis, and the weighted remainder norm is an anchored sum over addresses. The tail of each basis column tends to zero; a single finite union controls all columns. Closing that union under the finite symmetry and conjugation orbits preserves those symmetries. The retained functions keep their local cancellations and original eligibility tests. This is approximation of each fixed graph, not a uniform tail estimate over the graph ball. Its use below requires a common address dictionary and admissibility of the truncated graphs for the actual steps.

Proof. The proof describes the comparisons in Table 8, subject to the remaining verification identified below. A truncated graph generally represents a different marked density from the selected one; its admissibility must be proved by applying the same marked construction to its own perturbation. In particular, adding an address with zero unmarked amplitude preserves the unmarked density, but a nonzero mark in that slot still contributes to its first variation. Its original eligibility and the corresponding bounds must be retained.

For each fixed finite-address input, establish convergence of the actual step in the full regular ordered-derivative and forced supremum norms, including the output tails and forward coefficient readout. The latter also needs a uniform same-cutoff Lipschitz bound in the input graph, as in the coefficient estimate of Lemma 14.6; together with fixed-core convergence, it transfers convergence to the selected, cutoff-dependent graphs. A weighted kernel and derivative-score comparison in fixed physical links is one possible route. For the finite coefficient changes use \(M^{- 1} - \tilde{M}^{- 1} = M^{- 1} (\tilde{M} - M) \tilde{M}^{- 1}\) with common inverse bounds. These are statements about the actual finite-cutoff maps, not consequences of contraction alone. Subject to their verification, Lemma 14.33 gives the Cauchy property at level \(m\). The finitely many coefficient maps between the reference level and \(m\) converge as well. Solving their fixed-dimensional linear systems then gives Cauchy polynomial coordinates for each fixed reference label. Substituting in the graph gives the complete singleton functions.

On the fixed finite torus, weighted convergence bounds the sums of the regular terms uniformly. For the forced gas, the product telescope in Lemma 13.15 bounds the difference while retaining each original eligibility test. Equivalently, incorporate those tests into the entries before grouping identical supports and inventories; there are only finitely many such supports and inventories. The resulting scalar-free density and the singleton functions are uniformly Cauchy on their fixed health domains. The health threshold at level \(m\) is fixed; no moving indicator is being differentiated or replaced. Formula (14.23), with its higher modes set to zero, is a finite polynomial in the grouped functions and their exponentials. Its scalar-free coefficients are consequently uniformly Cauchy and integrable against Haar probability measure.

The denominator has a common positive lower bound. Choose \(g > 0\) below every admitted \(\beta_{N, p, m}^{- 1 / 2}\) on the retained fixed-level tail. Restrict every actual link to a fixed chord neighbourhood of the identity of radius below \(\min \{t_m / 4, g / 4\}\), shrinking it once if necessary so that every plaquette has chord below \(t_m\) and no flag is present. This set has positive product Haar measure. The forced sum there is one, and the scalar-free path action and activities are uniformly bounded above on the cutoff tail. The same positive lower bound for the integral therefore applies to every cutoff on that tail. Dividing the Cauchy numerator integrals by these positive Cauchy denominators gives (14.60), subject to the fixed-level convergence input above.\(\Box\)

The source density transfers exactly, but its singleton part does not: connected components with two or more marks remain. Two marks show what the stopping removes and what it keeps. Write \(H_{m, X} = z_1 z_2 h_X\) and \(K^{\geqslant 2}_{m, Y} = z_1 z_2 \kappa_Y\), put \(K^{\mathcal{B}} = \prod_{Y \in \mathcal{B}} K_{m, Y}\), and suppress \(m\) and \(U\) on the singleton functions. Since \(z_1^2 = z_2^2 = 0\), expanding the exponential and the product in (14.23) gives

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} {}[z_1 z_2] \mathcal{H}_m = & e^{\Phi_m} \sum_{\mathcal{A}} \left[ f_1 f_2 K^{\mathcal{A}} + \sum_{Y \in \mathcal{A}} (f_1 k_{2, Y} + f_2 k_{1, Y}) K^{\mathcal{A} \setminus \{Y\}} + \sum_{Y \ne Y'} k_{1, Y} k_{2, Y'} K^{\mathcal{A} \setminus \{Y, Y' \}} \right]\\ & + e^{\Phi_m} \sum_{\mathcal{A}} \left[ \left( \sum_X h_X \right) K^{\mathcal{A}} + \sum_{Y \in \mathcal{A}} \kappa_Y K^{\mathcal{A} \setminus \{Y\}} \right], \end{aligned}\) (14.61)

with sums over eligible families and ordered pairs of distinct \(Y, Y' \in \mathcal{A}\). The contribution in the first bracket is \([z_1 z_2] \mathcal{H}^0_{N, p, m}\). It keeps the marked forced terms \(k_{i, Y}\), which enter through the same eligible families as the unmarked activities. The contribution in the second bracket is removed, and its normalized integral is the stopping error for two marks. On a configuration without flags only the empty family is eligible, and the coefficient reduces to \(e^{\Phi_m} (f_1 f_2 + \sum_X h_X)\).

The next estimate bounds the effect of omitting all components with two or more marks. Its quantifiers are those needed in (14.56), where \(m\) is chosen before the cutoffs; so the constant \(F_0\) below must not depend on \(m\) or \(N\). Convergence at each fixed level does not supply such a bound, since it concerns \(C_{N, p, m}\) and not its distance from the cutoff moment.

Lemma 14.35. (Stopping estimate) For fixed \(p, v, x\), there are \(F_0 < \infty\) and \(m_0\) such that, for each \(m \geqslant m_0\) and every sufficiently large \(N\),

\(\displaystyle |M_{N, p} (v ; x / a_N) - C_{N, p, m} (v ; x) | \leqslant 2 F_0 e^{- B_m / 64} .\) (14.62)

The constant \(F_0\) is independent of \(m, N\); the cutoff threshold may depend on \(m\).

Every mode with at least two marks has support covering number at least \(B_m / 64\), by Lemma 14.13. Multiply all such modes, and only those, by a complex \(z\). The complete singleton functions, the original forced eligibility tests and the unmarked representation are unchanged. Lowering the support exponent by two absorbs \(|z| < r_m = e^{B_m / 64}\), so the normalized integral \(F_m (z)\) is holomorphic on this disk, with \(F_m (1)\) the cutoff moment and \(F_m (0) = C_{N, p, m}\). The argument needs a bound by a constant \(F_0\) at one base level \(k_0\) chosen before \(m\); the required propagation is identified in the proof. Given that bound, Cauchy's estimate on circles of radius \(r_m / 2\) centered on \([0, 1]\) gives

\(\displaystyle |F_m (1) - F_m (0) | \leqslant \frac{F_0}{r_m / 2} = 2 F_0 e^{- B_m / 64} .\)

The error is the bound on the disk divided by half its radius; it is small because the radius grows with \(m\) while the bound does not.

Proof. The expansion is that of (14.23) for the exact source evolution (14.22); the proposed base-level bound uses Lemma 14.15 and the finite one-mark coefficient and graph bounds below. Choose a base level \(k_0\) above the reference levels and the grid containing \(x\). At all sufficiently fine levels, distinct ancestral sites have separation at least \(B_i^2\): their separation is a fixed positive physical number divided by \(a_i\), while \(B_i^2\) grows only as a power of \(\log \hat{\beta}_i\). Their occupancy is one. Enlarge \(k_0\) so these assertions and the entry conditions for the marked source estimates hold at every subsequent level.

Choose mark weights \(\textsf{w}_h > 0\) at this base level so the weighted complete singleton sizes and coefficient norms lie below their prescribed bounds. The reference coefficient of each primary insertion is prescribed in Definition 2.8. Iterating the inverse coefficient estimate of Lemma 14.6(i) over the finitely many steps from that mark's reference level to \(k_0\) bounds \(b_{h, k_0} = a_{k_0}^{- d_h} |\mathbf{x}_{h, k_0} |_1\) uniformly in \(N\). The graph bound then controls the full singleton norms. Choose the fixed weights small enough that \(\textsf{w}_h b_{h, k_0} \leqslant \zeta_{d_h, k_0}\) as well as the stated singleton bounds. This choice does not use cross-cutoff graph convergence. Let \(s_0^{\text{reg}}, s_0^{\text{for}}\) bound the weighted regular singleton supremum and the sum of forced singleton suprema at \(k_0\). Set

\(\displaystyle F_0 = \frac{\exp \{| \Lambda_{n_{p, k_0}} |q_{k_0} + n (s_0^{\text{reg}} + s_0^{\text{for}} + 2)\}}{\prod_h \textsf{w}_h} .\) (14.63)

The numerator is the normalized-integral bound of the several-mark expansion, now with these fixed base weights. It depends on the base level, not on the later stopping level.

At level \(m > k_0\), every nonzero mode of \(H_{m, X}\) or \(K^{\geqslant 2}_{m, Y}\) contains at least two marks and its support contains their anchors. The covering estimate of Lemma 14.13 gives

\(\displaystyle \text{cov}_{B_m} (X) \geqslant B_m / 64.\)

Multiply every one of these higher-mode functions by a common complex variable \(z\). For \(|z| \leqslant e^{B_m / 64}\), its amplitude increase is absorbed by decreasing the support exponent by two:

\(\displaystyle |z| e^{(\sigma - 2) \text{cov}_{B_m} (X)} \leqslant e^{\sigma \text{cov}_{B_m} (X)} .\)

Singleton functions are not multiplied by \(z\). The source representation therefore remains integrable on \(|z| < e^{B_m / 64}\), with its prescribed singleton functions, original forced eligibility tests and two fewer units of support exponent. Here \(\sigma = \sigma_{J_m} = 100\). For \(s \geqslant 0\) and \(98 \leqslant \tau \leqslant 100\), \(e^{\tau s} \leqslant e^{100 s}\), so every regular or forced weighted sum decreases when its exponent decreases. The coefficientwise rooted clause of Lemma 13.5 bounds the connected regular terms at exponent \(\sigma + 3\) and the forced terms at \(\sigma + 2\). The entry chosen in item 5 controls the marked product factors. Applying these bounds in the weighted source norm of (14.23) leaves the two units used here, with final exponent \(98\).

To carry this modified density back to the same base level \(k_0\), one needs the coefficientwise marked attachment induction restarted at level \(m\), with the actual modified higher modes admitted at exponent \(98\) and with weights satisfying the base graph and coefficient hypotheses. Its anchored higher-mode bound and this restart are not yet established in this draft. Multiplication by \(z\) preserves the original eligibility tests and cannot lower marker degree; it does not by itself prove the required bound. If that induction supplies the stated base estimates, the singleton exponential and forced factors contribute at most \(n (s_0^{\text{reg}} + s_0^{\text{for}})\) to the logarithmic majorant, and the remaining connected regular and forced sums contribute at most \(2 n\). Equation 14.2 contributes \(| \Lambda_{n_{p, k_0}} | q_{k_0}\). Coefficient extraction divides by \(\prod_i \textsf{w}_i\), giving (14.63). The resulting bound on the normalized full-mode integral \(F_m (z)\) is

\(\displaystyle |F_m (z) | \leqslant F_0 .\)

This avoids the level-\(m\) volume factor that would arise from estimating the normalized integral only where it was stopped.

For \(e^{B_m / 64} > 2\), the Cauchy derivative estimate on circles of radius \(e^{B_m / 64} / 2\) centered on \([0, 1]\) gives

\(\displaystyle |F_m (1) - F_m (0) | \leqslant 2 F_0 e^{- B_m / 64} .\)

At \(z = 1\) this is the exact multiple-source integral. Its equality to the original cutoff moment follows from Fubini and the probability normalization of every block kernel in (14.22). At \(z = 0\) it is exactly (14.58). Substitution proves the estimate. For each fixed \(m\), the singleton bounds use the finite one-mark estimates and the propagated weight relation. The cutoff threshold may depend on \(m\), while \(F_0\) stays fixed.\(\Box\)

The fixed-level, stopping and positional estimates would give the full limit once the remaining inputs to those estimates are established.

Lemma 14.36. (Lattice-point convergence) For every \(p, n, v\), there is \(f_{p, v} \in C^{\infty} (\text{NoHit}_{L_p, n})\) such that, for every \(\boldsymbol{l}\), compact \(K \subset \text{NoHit}_{L_p, n}\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\), some \(N_0 (p, n, v, \boldsymbol{l}, K, \varepsilon)\) gives

\(\displaystyle | \Delta_{\boldsymbol{l}} M_{N, p} (v ; x / a_N) - \partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} f_{p, v} (x) | < \varepsilon\)

at every spacing-\(a_N\) point of \(K\) in the periodic lift, for every \(N \geqslant N_0\).

Proof. Pointwise convergence on the common grids is (14.56); the difference bounds of Lemma 14.30 extend it to uniform convergence of all derivatives. By Lemmas 14.34–14.35, the right side of (14.56) is at most \(4 F_0 e^{- B_m / 64} + |C_{N, p, m} - C_{N', p, m} |\); taking first \(m\) and then \(N, N'\) large proves the Cauchy property at every separated configuration on a fixed common grid. Since every ratio is an integer, each coarser grid is contained in every finer grid. The union of these grids is dense in the periodic noncollision domain.

We next extend this limit and identify its derivatives. Choose a compact neighbourhood \(K'\) of \(K\) contained in that domain, and a positive lower bound for its torus separation. Lemma 14.30 bounds the lattice functions and all their fixed differences there. One more difference gives, by telescoping along coordinate lattice paths,

\(\displaystyle |G_N (z) - G_N (z') | \leqslant B \hspace{0.17em} |z - z' |_1\)

for nearby physical grid points, where \(G_N = \Delta_{\boldsymbol{l}} M_{N, p}\). For order zero, a finite dense grid in \(K'\) and this estimate extend the pointwise Cauchy property to uniform convergence on \(K\). The multilinear interpolants obey the same bound, with an additional \(O (a_N)\) displacement to their vertices. The resulting limit \(f_{p, v}\) is continuous and periodic.

Suppose \(G_N\) already converges locally uniformly to \(g\). Bounds for two more differences compare its first forward difference with a difference over a fixed physical interval. Put \(h_N = Q_N a_N \to h > 0\), with \(Q_N \in \mathbb{N}\). Summing successive first differences and bounding their variation by the second difference gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \frac{G_N (z + h_N e) - G_N (z)}{h_N} - \Delta_e G_N (z) \right| \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2} Bh_N .\) (14.64)

Choose \(h\) small and then let \(N \to \infty\). The endpoint values converge uniformly, so (14.64) proves that \(\Delta_e G_N\) is uniformly Cauchy on \(K\). Call its continuous limit \(g_e\). The exact telescoping identity on grid segments, followed by Riemann summation of the uniformly convergent first differences, gives

\(\displaystyle g (z + te) - g (z) = \int_0^t g_e (z + ue) \hspace{0.17em} du.\)

Thus \(\partial_e g = g_e\). Induct on the length of the difference list. Every required additional order is available from Lemma 14.30, so all derivative limits exist and \(f_{p, v}\) is smooth. Finite covers by such neighbourhoods give the asserted uniform statement on \(K\).\(\Box\)

The same limit applies when observation points move across periodic representative boundaries. Smearing then follows from a compact-support Riemann sum.

Lemma 14.37. (Interpolation and smearing) For fixed \(p, n, v, \boldsymbol{l}\) and \(x_N \to x \in \text{NoHit}_{L_p, n}\), the multilinear interpolants of \(\Delta_{\boldsymbol{l}} M_{N, p}\) at \(x_N\) converge to \(\partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} f_{p, v} (x)\). For every \(p\)-admissible separated test \(t\),

\(\displaystyle t \partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} f_{p, v} \in L^1, \qquad a_N^{4 n} \sum_y t (\hat{y}) \Delta_{\boldsymbol{l}} M_{N, p} (v ; y) \longrightarrow \int t \partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} f_{p, v} .\)

Proof. Both assertions follow from the locally uniform convergence of Lemma 14.36, with the placement and moment conventions of Definition 2.4 and admissibility from Section 2.2. Choose a compact neighbourhood of \(x\) inside the periodic noncollision domain. Every vertex used by the interpolant at \(x_N\) lies there eventually and tends to \(x\). The interpolant is a convex combination of the vertex values. Uniform convergence at these vertices and continuity of the limiting derivative give the moving-point assertion. Work on the periodic lift throughout; no continuity of the choice of centered representatives is needed.

The closed support of \(t\) is a compact subset of that domain, strictly inside the chosen period box. The smooth limiting derivative is bounded there, so its product with \(t\) is integrable. On a fixed compact neighbourhood, Lemma 14.36 makes the supremum of the difference between the lattice values and the limiting derivative tend to zero. Its contribution to the sum is at most that supremum times \(\|t\|_{\infty}\) times the total volume of the grid cubes meeting the support. This volume is bounded independently of \(N\). The remaining sum is the Riemann sum for the continuous compactly supported function \(t \partial_{\boldsymbol{l}} f_{p, v}\) and converges to its integral.\(\Box\)

Proof of Theorem 5.9. Set \(M^{\text{UV}}_{p, v} = f_{p, v}\). Lemma 14.36 gives its smoothness on the full periodic noncollision domain, and Lemma 14.37 gives the asserted interpolation and smearing limits for every fixed derivative list. Thus every inner limit in Definition 2.6 exists at every admissible fixed volume along the full cutoff sequence.\(\Box\)
