9Transfer and stability of local insertions

9.1Differentiating the nonlinear insertion expansion

The elementary insertion is obtained by differentiating a real tilt of the same nonlinear block integral used for the background. This matters for two reasons. The background normalization must cancel exactly, and the derivative of the gas must retain the original activity addresses. We first prove the summation argument which makes this differentiation legitimate, then account for the nonprincipal terms.

9.1.1A joint bound for values and derivatives

Fix a number \(D \geqslant 0\) and an open interval \(I\) containing zero. For a differentiable real function \(f\) and a nonnegative number \(p\), say that \(f\) has joint bound \(p\) at \(\theta\) if there are \(a, b \geqslant 0\) with

\(\displaystyle a + b \leqslant p, \qquad |f (\theta) | \leqslant a, \qquad |f' (\theta) | \leqslant Db.\) (9.1)

The witnesses \(a, b\) may depend on \(\theta\). This is stronger than bounding the value by \(p\) and the derivative by \(Dp\) separately. Its advantage is that products have no extra factor equal to their degree. Indeed, if \(f_1, f_2\) have witnesses \((a_1, b_1), (a_2, b_2)\), take

\(\displaystyle a = a_1 a_2, \qquad b = a_1 b_2 + b_1 a_2 .\)

The product rule gives the required value and derivative bounds, and \(a + b \leqslant (a_1 + b_1) (a_2 + b_2) \leqslant p_1 p_2\). Induction gives joint bound \(\prod_{j \in q} p_j\) for any finite product. A constant multiplier \(c\) multiplies the bound by \(|c|\). These statements include \(D = 0\). For the affine tilt \(f + \theta g\), if \(|f| \leqslant a\), \(|g| \leqslant Db\) and \(| \theta | D \leqslant 1\), the witnesses \((a + b, b)\) give joint bound \(a + 2 b\).

Here is the precise infinite-sum consequence. Suppose \(f_i\) is differentiable on \(I\) and has joint bound \(p_i\) at every point of \(I\), where \(\sum_i p_i < \infty\). Both \(\sum_i f_i (\theta)\) and \(\sum_i f_i' (\theta)\) converge absolutely. For a closed segment joining \(\theta\) and \(\theta + h\) inside \(I\), the mean value theorem gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \frac{f_i (\theta + h) - f_i (\theta)}{h} \right| \leqslant Dp_i .\)

Dominated convergence on the counting measure therefore yields

\(\displaystyle \frac{d}{d \theta} \sum_i f_i (\theta) = \sum_i f_i' (\theta) .\) (9.2)

This proof needs no continuity of the individual derivatives. It also shows that the choice of an enumeration of the countable index set has no effect on the result.

9.1.2The gas derivative and its support

Work on a fixed finite coarse lattice \(\Lambda\). Let \(A_j \ne \varnothing\) be the support of forced activity \(j \in \mathbb{N}\). The ordinary activities, the support and inventory of each forced activity, and the admissibility test are held fixed. Only the values \(K_j\) change, along a real family \(F_j (\theta, U)\), with \(F_j (0, U) = K_j (U)\). Assume that, for \(| \theta | D \leqslant 1\), these are measurable, gauge invariant forced activities with the same locality and flag conditions as \(K_j\). Also assume

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} & F_j (\theta, \cdot) = F_j (0, \cdot) \quad \text{if } o \not\in A_j,\\ & F_j (\cdot, U) \text{ has joint bound } p_j \geqslant 0,\\ & \sup_{x \in \Lambda} \sum_{j : x \in A_j} p_j e^{uC_B (A_j)} \leqslant s, \qquad u \geqslant 0. \end{aligned}\) (9.3)

The middle line is required on an open neighborhood of zero contained in \(\{| \theta |D \leqslant 1\}\), for every \(U\). Such a neighborhood exists also when \(D = 0\). Since each support is nonempty,

\(\displaystyle \sum_j p_j \leqslant | \Lambda | s < \infty .\)

For a finite family \(q\) put \(p_q = \prod_{j \in q} p_j\). Expanding finite products and taking increasing finite subsets of \(\mathbb{N}\) proves

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} q \subset \mathbb{N} \hspace{0.27em} \text{finite} \end{array}$}}} p_q = \prod_j (1 + p_j) \leqslant \exp \left( \sum_j p_j \right) < \infty .\)

For fixed \(U\), admissibility does not depend on \(\theta\). Thus each admissible product \(\prod_{j \in q} F_j (\theta, U)\) has joint bound \(p_q\), and an inadmissible term is zero. Equation (9.2) now proves that the gas derivative at zero is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{G}^{[1]} (k) (U) = \sum_{q \text{admissible}} \sum_{j \in q} k_j (U) \prod_{\ell \in q \setminus \{j\}} K_{\ell} (U), \qquad k_j (U) = \partial_{\theta} F_j (\theta, U) |_0 .\) (9.4)

There is also absolute convergence before the marked index is summed. For joint-bound witnesses \(a_j, b_j\) at zero,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j \in q} |F_j' (0, U) | \prod_{\ell \ne j} |K_{\ell} (U) | \leqslant D \sum_{j \in q} b_j \prod_{\ell \ne j} a_{\ell} \leqslant D \prod_{j \in q} (a_j + b_j) \leqslant Dp_q .\)

The middle inequality keeps just the terms with exactly one \(b\) in the product expansion. Summing over \(q\) proves absolute convergence of the full pointed series. This is the marked gas on the original addresses.

The marked family retains all required structural properties. If \(o \not\in A_j\), its derivative is zero by the first line of (9.3). Wherever the flag condition requires \(F_j (\theta, U) = 0\), it also requires \(k_j (U) = 0\). Gauge invariance and locality follow by differentiating the corresponding equalities in \(\theta\). Measurability follows by choosing any nonzero sequence \(\theta_n \to 0\) in the permitted interval and writing \(k_j\) as the pointwise limit of measurable difference quotients. Finally,

\(\displaystyle |k_j (U) | \leqslant Dp_j \hspace{0.17em} \boldsymbol{1}_{\{o \in A_j \}}, \qquad \sum_j Dp_j \boldsymbol{1}_{\{o \in A_j \}} e^{uC_B (A_j)} \leqslant Ds.\)

This proves the flagged cost (7.82), with exactly the insertion debt \(D\) and the site bound \(s\) in (9.3).

9.1.3The logarithmic derivative and the exact raw identity

The localized regular terms arise from the logarithm of the positive normalization. Write this normalization as

\(\displaystyle a (\theta, U) = \exp \left( \sum_i \ell_i (\theta, U) \right), \qquad a_0 (U) = a (0, U) > 0.\)

Suppose that each logarithmic term has a summable joint majorant, uniformly for \(\theta\) near zero, and that its derivative is the localized insertion

\(\displaystyle \ell_i' (0, U) = \chi_{X_i} (U) h_i (U) .\)

The product bound above applies, in particular, to each connected cluster term \(c \prod_j f_j (\theta) / r\)!: its majorant is \(|c| \prod_j p_j / r\)!. Thus an absolutely summable connected-cluster majorant justifies differentiating the logarithmic expansion. Equation (9.2) and the chain rule give

\(\displaystyle a' (0, U) = a_0 (U) z (U), \qquad z (U) = \sum_i \chi_{X_i} (U) h_i (U) .\) (9.5)

The exact tilted block identity, after the two activity regroupings, has the form

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{T} \rho_{\text{fine}} (U) + \theta \mathcal{T}f (U) = e^{E (U)} \frac{a (\theta, U)}{a_0 (U)} \hspace{0.17em} b (\theta, U), \qquad b (0, U) =\mathcal{G} (K) (U),\) (9.6)

where \(b\) is the gas of \(F (\theta)\). All quantities use the same background and history. This identity is required for the actual block integral, for \(| \theta | D \leqslant 1\). Differentiating it, using (9.4) and (9.5), yields

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{T}f (U) = e^{E (U)} \mathcal{G} (K) (U) z (U) + e^{E (U)} \mathcal{G}^{[1]} (k) (U) .\)

As \(\rho_{\text{coarse}} = e^E \mathcal{G} (K)\), this is exactly (7.79). In particular, the derivative of the normalization is multiplied by the full coarse density. Omitting \(a_0^{- 1}\) before differentiation would give an incorrect factor.

The normalization cancellation in (9.6) is algebraic, but uses exact identities. To spell it out, let \(G_{\theta}\) be the gas before the two regroupings, whose logarithmic shifts are \(R\) and \(S\). Their identities read

\(\displaystyle b (\theta) = G_{\theta} e^{R + S}, \qquad e^{E + R + S} = \mathscr{O} a_0, \qquad \mathscr{O} a (\theta) G_{\theta} =\mathcal{T} \rho_{\text{fine}} + \theta \mathcal{T}f.\)

Substitution proves (9.6). The two exact gas regroupings and their numerical majorants are inputs here, as is the exact block expansion in the last identity. The middle identity is (8.250): proved there by the output-exponent and regular-accounting identities (8.248) and (8.249) on the same data. Their application must retain the same support, inventory and flag data. The scalar differentiation argument does not by itself establish the additional transferred singleton-profile identity required in Subsection 7.12.

9.1.4Separating the principal term from the tail

Here is the quantitative bookkeeping for the nonprincipal regular terms. It identifies precisely which connected estimates are needed. Let \(j\) be the derivative order, \(p\) the spatial weight exponent, \(B\) the cover radius, and \(a \geqslant 0\), \(e > 0\) the support exponent and its reserve. Put

\(\displaystyle r = pj, \qquad \Phi (C) = [\sigma (1 + C)]^r, \qquad P_{e, r} = e^{- r} r! \exp (e), \qquad A_{e, r} = \sigma^r P_{e, r},\)

where \(\sigma = 4 (2 B + 1) > 0\) and \(e^{- r}\) denotes the inverse power of the reserve \(e\). The exponential series gives \([e (1 + C)]^r / r! \leqslant \exp (e (1 + C))\) for \(C \geqslant 0\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle \Phi (C) e^{aC} \leqslant A_{e, r} e^{(a + e) C} .\) (9.7)

The localized logarithmic terms have a nonnegative integer order. An order-zero term corresponds to a single thin molecule; the higher orders are connected clusters. Terms which do not contain the insertion vanish. The remaining terms have supports containing the chosen anchor \(o\). Write \(C_i\) for their cover numbers and \(\omega_i \geqslant 0\) for their cluster majorants. Suppose the connected estimate gives, for every \(z \geqslant 0\) and every site \(x\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i : \hspace{0.17em} x \in X_i, \hspace{0.17em} \text{ord} (i) \geqslant z} \omega_i e^{(a + e) C_i} \leqslant t \gamma^z (1 - \gamma)^{- 1}, \qquad 0 \leqslant \gamma < 1.\) (9.8)

For positive-order terms the derivative estimate is \(\|h_i \| \leqslant D \omega_i \Phi (C_i)\). Keeping only terms containing the insertion, using \(o \in X_i\), and applying (9.7) and (9.8) at \(z = 1\) gives the total bound

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{ord} (i) \geqslant 1} e^{aC_i} \|h_i \| \leqslant DA_{e, r} \hspace{0.17em} t \gamma (1 - \gamma)^{- 1} .\) (9.9)

Order zero must be treated separately, because its single-insertion molecule is the principal term. If an order-zero molecule containing the insertion is not principal, it has at least two elements. Indeed, a singleton containing the marked element consists exactly of that element. Thus its excess cardinality is at least one. Let \(v_i\) be its nonnegative marked-molecule majorant, with the restriction that excess cardinality is at least one. The mixed marked estimate needed here is

\(\displaystyle \sup_x \sum_{i : \hspace{0.17em} x \in X_i} v_i e^{(a + e) C_i} \leqslant C_1 .\)

Assume the order-zero derivative bound \(\|h_i \| \leqslant DB_{\ast} v_i \Phi (C_i)\) for a nonnegative derivative bound \(B_{\ast}\). The molecule-to-order-zero-cluster map is injective, so no multiplicity is lost in the sum. Applying the reserve inequality and the site estimate at \(o\) proves

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} \text{ord} (i) = 0\\ i \text{nonprincipal} \end{array}$}}} e^{aC_i} \|h_i \| \leqslant DB_{\ast} A_{e, r} C_1 .\)

Together with (9.9), this gives

\(\displaystyle M \leqslant DA_{e, r} \left( B_{\ast} C_1 + \frac{t \gamma}{1 - \gamma} \right) .\) (9.10)

To obtain the reserved mass of Subsection 7.12, take \(a\) at least its reserved support exponent. The same calculation with a larger \(a\) continues to apply if the displayed estimates have that reserve.

In the actual insertion normalization, \(D = m \exp (- \varepsilon C_{\text{fine}}) / g\), where \(g > 0\) is the gain and \(\varepsilon > 0\) is the fine support reserve. Therefore, defining

\(\displaystyle T_{\text{tail}} = \frac{A_{e, r}}{g} \left( B_{\ast} C_1 + \frac{t \gamma}{1 - \gamma} \right),\)

we have \(M \leqslant me^{- \varepsilon C_{\text{fine}}} T_{\text{tail}}\). This accounts for both the support decay and the normalization gain; they are not independent assumptions on \(M\).

9.1.5The strict power reserve

For completeness, the tail comparison used in choosing the coupling can be verified directly from the bound exponents. Write \(H (x) = \beta_{\text{fine}} (x)\), with \(H (x) \to \infty\). A function has upper exponent \(q\) if for every \(v > q\), eventually \(|f (x) | \leqslant H (x)^v\). Products add upper exponents and sums with the same upper exponent retain it: use half the available exponent margin in each bound and absorb the fixed factor two into the remaining power of \(H\). Fixed powers of a function with upper exponent zero still have upper exponent zero.

Let \(q < 0\) be the connected-expansion exponent. The bounds used above have the following exponents, with \(L\), derivative orders and \(e\) fixed before \(x\) is taken large:

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c|cccccc} \text{bound} & A_{e, r} & B_{\ast} & C_1 & t & \gamma & g^{- 1}\\ \hline \text{upper exponent} & 0 & 0 & 3 q / 2 & q & q / 2 & - q. \end{array}\)

Here \(\gamma = H^{q / 2}\) and \(g = H^q\); hence eventually \(0 \leqslant \gamma \leqslant 1 / 2\) and \((1 - \gamma)^{- 1} \leqslant 2\). Both terms defining \(T_{\text{tail}}\) consequently have upper exponent \(q / 2\). Since \(q / 2 < q / 4\), the definition gives, eventually,

\(\displaystyle T_{\text{tail}} \leqslant H^{q / 4}, \qquad M \leqslant me^{- \varepsilon C_{\text{fine}}} H^{q / 4} .\) (9.11)

This is a strict exponent comparison. An upper exponent zero by itself would not justify paying a bound from a fixed power of \(\log H\).

Sections 8.1 and 8.2 prove the connected and mixed site estimates from their vertex bounds; Subsection 8.32 proves the two gas regrouping identities under their exact flag and reserve hypotheses. The remaining analytic work is to construct the actual tilted block expansion, establish its vertex bounds and localized derivative estimates, and purchase all these bounds and their stated upper exponents on the selected history. With those inputs, the differentiation, flagged cost, nonprincipal tail accounting and strict-power comparison above supply the corresponding inputs to the elementary projection estimate.

9.2Paying for the elementary projection on the selected scales

We now verify the principal reserve and the numerical projection allowance used in Subsection 7.12. All quantities refer to the marked expansion constructed in Sections 8.33 and 8.34. In particular the regular logarithmic terms and the differentiated forced array are those of the same background transition. We retain the incoming sector and path estimates of that construction.

9.2.1The singleton identity before evaluating the gas

For each real elementary insertion \(M\), denote by \(F^M_j (\theta, U)\) the actual tilted forced coefficient after the two regroupings of Subsection 8.33. The index \(j\) includes its fixed support and flag inventory. Define one common transition map on elementary insertions by

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{D} (M)_j (U) = \left. \frac{d}{d \theta} F^M_j (\theta, U) \right|_{\theta = 0} .\)

The coefficientwise differentiability and domination were proved in Sections 8.34 and 9.1. The raw forced coefficient \(k_j\) in that expansion is this very derivative. Hence

\(\displaystyle k_j (U) =\mathcal{D} (M)_j (U)\) (9.12)

for every original output address and every configuration. This fixes the transition map from the constructed coefficient array itself; it is not selected from its gas evaluation.

Let \(\mathcal{P}\) denote the profile map, which sums coefficients with a common support and inventory. Its absolute convergence follows from the dominated forced bound. For real insertions \(M_1, M_2\) of the same type and majorant, and \(v = L^d\), the raw complex forced array is \(v (\mathcal{D}(M_1) + i\mathcal{D}(M_2))\). The local splitting of Subsection 7.9 produces a coefficient field \(Q\) and a response \(\psi\) with

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{P} (Q_{\text{forced}}) - \text{hook} (\psi) = v \{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}(M_1)) + i\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}(M_2))\} .\) (9.13)

Indeed splitting adds exactly \(\text{hook} (\psi)\) to the raw profile, and the profile map is linear by absolute convergence. Subtracting that hook proves the identity. Its sign agrees with the compensating response in the ordinary part of the density.

The input profile is zero for an ordinary insertion. For a flagged insertion it is its inserted value at its original support and inventory, and zero at other addresses. Thus the input ordinary part and input profile in this transition are precisely those of \(M_1 + iM_2\). Their fine cost is at most \(2 m\). Taking \(M_2 = 0\) proves the claimed singleton relation for a real insertion, with the same available cost \(2 m\).

These identities persist under affordable linear combinations. For a countable family with coefficients \(z_i\) and \(\sum_i |z_i | m_i < \infty\), the forced-estimate bounds the sum of absolute output coefficients by a fixed multiple of this sum. Tonelli's theorem applies to the nonnegative majorants on the product of insertion and output indices. One may therefore interchange those sums in the profile and obtain the linear combination of (9.13). The permutation covariance of the actual arrays also survives: differentiate their coefficientwise relabelling identity and then apply the same absolutely convergent profile map. This verifies the marked-profile requirement of Subsection 7.12 on the selected transition.

9.2.2The principal cluster already has a paid majorant

For an ordinary insertion there is exactly one principal term: the order-zero cluster whose molecule consists of the insertion letter alone. Its support is the selected enlarged insertion support \(X_{\ast}\), and its majorant is denoted by \(\omega_{\ast}\). The distinction between this term and the order-zero tail is the excess-cardinality condition in Subsection 9.1. For a flagged insertion this cluster cannot be regular, so there is no principal term.

Write \(C_{\ast} = C_B (X_{\ast})\), and use the positive bounds \(t, v, u\) and \(\gamma\) of Subsection 8.34. The rooted logarithmic estimate at order zero, evaluated at the principal anchor, gives

\(\displaystyle \omega_{\ast} e^{(a + e) C_{\ast}} \leqslant \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} i : \hspace{0.17em} o \in X_i \end{array}$}}} \omega_i e^{(a + e) C_B (X_i)} \leqslant \frac{t}{1 - \gamma} \leqslant 2 t \leqslant 1.\)

The first inequality retains one nonnegative summand. The last uses \(u = 10 (t + v) \leqslant 1 / 2\). In the combined expansion one may instead use the common bound \(4 v + 8 t \leqslant u\); either bound leaves the same unit reserve. No separate smallness assumption on this individual cluster is needed.

The local derivative estimate already proved there has amplitude \(D \omega_{\ast} \Phi (X_{\ast})\), where

\(\displaystyle D = \frac{me^{- \varepsilon C_{B_f} (X)}}{g} \leqslant \frac{m}{g}, \qquad \Phi (X_{\ast}) \leqslant Ve^{C_{\ast} / 2} .\)

Here \(m\) is the fine reserved mass and \(g > 0\) the normalization gain. At fixed derivative order \(p\) and spatial power \(r\), one can take

\(\displaystyle V = [4 (2 B + 1)]^{rp} C_{rp}, \qquad C_{rp} = \sup_{z \geqslant 0} (1 + z)^{rp} e^{- z / 2} < \infty .\)

Finiteness follows from polynomial growth versus exponential decay. If \(a \geqslant b + 3\) and \(e \geqslant 0\), where \(b\) is the outgoing support exponent, then

\(\displaystyle e^{(b + 2) C_{\ast}} \|h_{\ast} \| \leqslant DV \omega_{\ast} e^{(b + 5 / 2) C_{\ast}} \leqslant DV \omega_{\ast} e^{(a + e) C_{\ast}} \leqslant Vm / g.\)

This proves the principal estimate in (7.81) for the actual localized term. If \(C_{\ast} \geqslant B\), the same proof retains \(e^{- B / 2}\), because \(a + e - (b + 5 / 2) \geqslant 1 / 2\).

For a complex insertion the real and imaginary parts have the same support, type and mass majorant. Their principal terms are at the same index. The triangle inequality therefore gives total principal mass at most \(2 |z| Vm / g\) after multiplication by \(z\). For a flagged input both principal contributions are zero. This verifies the principal contribution used in the complex raw expansion, including the convention of pairing a real insertion with an identically zero imaginary part.

9.2.3Combining the three bounds

Fix a degree \(d\) and block ratio \(L\). Let \(H_d\) be the local splitting factor of Subsection 7.12, and put

\(\displaystyle c_d = \text{rate} (L) / H_d > 0.\)

We first pay the two small bounds in (7.83). Put \(H = \beta_f (x)\) and

\(\displaystyle P (x) = e^{(\log \log x)^2}, \qquad P_f = P (H), \qquad \kappa = e^{- P (x)^{1 / 5} / 2}, \qquad s = 2 e^{- \mu P_f^2 / 16}, \quad \mu > 0.\)

These are the actual square root of the outgoing forced allowance and the second forced-gas bound. Eventually \(e < x \leqslant H\), so \(1 \leqslant P (x) \leqslant P_f\). If \(P_f \geqslant 64 / \mu\), then

\(\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2} P (x)^{1 / 5} \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2} P_f \leqslant \mu P_f^2 / 32, \qquad \kappa^{- 1} s \leqslant 2 e^{- \mu P_f^2 / 32} .\)

The last bound has every negative upper exponent in \(H\). Indeed, with \(y = \log \log H\), the logarithm of its ratio to \(H^{- a}\) is at most \(\log 2 - (\mu / 32) e^{2 y^2} + ae^y\), which tends to \(- \infty\) for every fixed \(a > 0\). Since \(g^{- 1} = H^{- q}\) with \(q < 0\),

\(\displaystyle 2 L^d g^{- 1} \kappa^{- 1} s \longrightarrow 0.\)

The tail bound (9.11) likewise gives \(2 L^d H^{q / 4} \to 0\). The debt and tail both contain the factor \(me^{- \varepsilon C_{B_f} (X)} \leqslant m\). Choose one coupling threshold after which the two preceding scalar factors are at most \(c_d\). Multiplying by this nonnegative mass factor proves both small-bound inequalities uniformly over all insertions, including \(m = 0\); no division by \(m\) is used.

The remaining local splitting condition is paid at the same time. Since \(B = \lceil (\log x)^2 \rceil\), the logarithm of \((2 B + 1)^4 \kappa\) is bounded above, for large \(x\), by

\(\displaystyle C + 8 \log \log x - \tfrac{1}{2} e^{(\log \log x)^2 / 5},\)

which tends to \(- \infty\). Hence \((2 B + 1)^4 \kappa \leqslant 1\) eventually. This establishes all three inequalities in (7.83), including the hit-width bound. The thresholds depend on the fixed stage and degree, but not on the finite volume or insertion.

Define

\(\displaystyle E_d (x) = 2 L^d V (x) / g (x), \qquad Q_d (x) = (c_d + |E_d (x) |) H_d .\)

The principal bound is at most \(|E_d (x) | m\). Hence both it and the tail bound are bounded by \(e_0 = (c_d + |E_d (x) |) m\). The local splitting proof gives coefficient, remainder and response bounds at most \(H_d e_0 = Q_d (x) m\). Its density is exactly \(L^d \mathcal{T}f\), and the fine singleton expansion costs at most \(2 m\). This uses the separate marked-profile identity of the expansion as well as its scalar density identity.

The quantity \(Q_d\) may grow as a power of the fine coupling. That is harmless for this last projection: its permitted allowance is

\(\displaystyle Q_d (x) \leqslant e^{\sqrt{\beta_f (x)}} .\) (9.14)

We verify this inequality without changing the selected block or insertion. The bound \(V (x)\) has upper exponent zero, since it is a fixed power of \(1 + B (x)\) times a constant. The inverse gain \(g (x)^{- 1} = \beta_f (x)^{- q}\) has upper exponent \(- q\). Thus \(Q_d\) has some finite upper exponent, with \(L, d\) and the stage profile fixed. For any function \(F\) of finite upper exponent \(v\), choose \(w > \max (v, 0)\). Eventually \(|F (x) | \leqslant \beta_f (x)^w\). Writing \(y = \sqrt{\beta_f (x)} \to \infty\), we have

\(\displaystyle \beta_f (x)^w e^{- \sqrt{\beta_f (x)}} = y^{2 w} e^{- y} \longrightarrow 0.\)

For example, \(2 w \log y - y \to - \infty\), since \(\log y / y \to 0\). Consequently \(F (x) \leqslant e^{\sqrt{\beta_f (x)}}\) eventually. Applying this to \(Q_d\) proves (9.14). The threshold is uniform in the finite volume and the insertion because \(Q_d\) depends only on the displayed stage data.

9.2.4The simultaneous multiple-insertion requirements

There are a few additional numerical inequalities on this same transition. We verify them explicitly, since their order of selection matters. Fix an occupancy bound \(N \in \mathbb{N}\), a support exponent \(b \geqslant 0\), and a positive suppression constant. Write

\(\displaystyle H = \beta_f (x), \qquad \eta = H^{- 2}, \qquad \alpha = b + \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad s (x) = 2 (1 + N) \eta (Ne^{\alpha} + 1) + (1 + N) \tau (x),\)

where \(\tau\) is the forced bound from the second exclusion estimate. Its Gaussian suppression gives any prescribed negative upper exponent, in particular \(- 2\); this is the estimate used in Subsection 3.15. Thus \(s\) has upper exponent \(- 2\). Since \(B (x)\) has upper exponent zero, so does every fixed polynomial in \(B (x)\). It follows that eventually

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} (2 B + 1)^4 s (x) \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad e^{s (x)} - 1 \leqslant 1, \qquad \tau (x) \leqslant 1,\\ \alpha (2 \cdot 1024 B + 1)^4 \leqslant \sqrt{H} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(9.15)} \end{array}\)

Indeed the first and third quantities tend to zero. For the second first arrange \(s (x) \leqslant \log 2\). For the fourth compare its upper exponent zero with the strictly larger exponent \(1 / 2\). The expression for \(s\) is the sum of the two ordinary copied contributions and the forced contribution; in particular the factor \(1 + N\) is retained in each of the three terms.

The geometric floor required for the local transport is selected earlier. If \(R_{j, i}\) is its fixed support-enlargement charge for a transition from stage \(j\) to stage \(i\), choose the integer length floor

\(\displaystyle n_j \geqslant \max_{i \leqslant j} \lceil e^{R_{j, i}} \rceil .\)

Every block length \(L_j \geqslant n_j\) then satisfies \(e^{R_{j, i}} \leqslant L_j\) for each permitted transition. Only finitely many indices occur in this maximum. This requirement is independent of the coupling and is therefore included before selecting the lengths in Subsection 19.2.

After the lengths are fixed, at stage \(j\) there are finitely many permitted predecessor stages \(i\) and degrees \(d \leqslant i\). Choose the maximum of their thresholds for (9.14), (9.15), and the tail and forced payments. Raise the common entry threshold to this maximum. Subsection 19.2 produces one realization paying all these requirements. Every later compatible realization paying enlarged thresholds retains them.

Thus the elementary projection allowance can be taken to be \(e^{\sqrt{\beta_f}}\) simultaneously for every permitted degree at each stage, on the same nonlinear transition as the ordinary and multiple-insertion estimates. With the marked-profile identity, this supplies the elementary input in Subsection 15.1; its finite linear assembly then gives the terminal bound there. The underlying sector and path estimates are still those of the construction, rather than consequences of this numerical selection.

9.3Comparing the principal insertion with its Gaussian reference

The principal cluster is not itself a small remainder. Its leading part is a local Gaussian reference observable. We compare these two objects on one probability space, keeping the insertion fixed while differentiating the surrounding densities. This is necessary even when the insertion is only bounded and measurable.

9.3.1The common reference integral

Fix a forest, its tags, a regular insertion of amplitude \(\tau \geqslant 0\), and a healthy base configuration. Let \(y \in \mathbb{R}^j\), \(j \leqslant n\), parameterize an ordered local variation about that configuration, with base point \(0\). The read coordinates form a finite set of size \(N_0\), including every colour of each read bond. Write \(C_6 (y), C_9 (y)\) for the actual-sector and Gaussian-reference covariances on these coordinates, and \(w_6 (y, z), w_9 (y, z)\) for the two compensated coordinate maps. At the base point

\(\displaystyle C_0 = C_6 (0), \qquad w_6 (0, z) = z.\)

Both covariances are positive in a neighborhood of zero. Let \(J (y, z)\) be the common compensated Jacobian and put

\(\displaystyle R_a (y, z) = J (y, z) \frac{p_{C_a (y)} (w_a (y, z))}{p_{C_0} (z)}, \qquad a = 6, 9,\)

where \(p_C\) is the Gaussian density in (8.192). The reference probability measure is always \(\gamma_{C_0}\).

Let \(h (z)\) be the original masked insertion evaluated at the fixed base configuration and its original Gaussian coordinates. It is measurable and \(|h (z) | \leqslant \tau\). The exact two integrands are

\(\displaystyle R_6 (y, z) \chi_6 (y, z) h (z), \qquad R_9 (y, z) \chi_9 (y, z) h (z) .\) (9.16)

Here \(\chi_6\) is the sector cutoff and \(\chi_9\) the reference cutoff, both smooth with values in \([0, 1]\). The first identity follows from the compensated change of variables of Subsection 8.26: on the support of its cutoff the new configuration agrees with the fixed one on the insertion's read support. Locality therefore identifies their insertion values. For the second identity, the reference cutoff implies that the first cutoff is one and that the fixed configuration is healthy on the insertion support. The mask can then be removed, and the same local agreement identifies the unmasked reference insertion with \(h (z)\). Where a cutoff vanishes both sides of its identity are zero. Thus no derivative of \(h\) is taken in either formula, and no insertion-dependent change of Gaussian measure has been made.

We state the quantitative finite-dimensional data used below explicitly. They are uniform over all the forests, tags and local variations under consideration. There are \(N, Q, S \geqslant 1\), \(\delta \geqslant 0\), and a fixed \(M > 0\) such that:

  1. \(N_0 \leqslant N\) and \(C_0 \leqslant MI\);

  2. the entries of \(C_6, C_9\) have jets through order \(n\) bounded by \(Q\) at zero, their inverses at zero have entries bounded by \(Q\), and the jets of \(C_9 - C_6\) are bounded by \(\delta\);

  3. for \(\|z\|_{\infty} \leqslant S\), the entries of \(w_6, w_9\) and the scalar \(J\) have jets bounded by \(Q\), and those of \(w_9 - w_6\) are bounded by \(\delta\);

  4. on a common neighborhood of zero both integrands in (9.16) vanish for \(\|z\|_{\infty} > S\). The cutoff jets on this ball are bounded by a fixed polynomial in \(N, Q, S\). For every \(z\) with \(\|z\|_{\infty} < v\), both cutoffs are identically one on some neighborhood of zero.

Here a jet bound includes the value and the operator norms of derivatives of all orders at most \(n\). The last neighborhood may depend on \(z\); the common compact support neighborhood does not. The covariances, coordinate maps and Jacobian are smooth on the compact coordinate ball and a neighborhood of the base parameter. These hypotheses are the specific comparison data; they are not consequences of an abstract equality between the two gas profiles.

9.3.2A quantitative density difference

There is a polynomial \(P_n\) with positive coefficients, depending only on \(n\), such that, after increasing \(P_n\) if necessary and provided

\(\displaystyle \delta P_n (N, Q, S) \leqslant 1,\) (9.17)

the following bounds hold for \(\|z\|_{\infty} \leqslant S\):

\(\displaystyle \|R_6 (\cdot, z)\|_{J^n_0} \leqslant P_n (N, Q, S), \qquad \|R_9 (\cdot, z) - R_6 (\cdot, z) \|_{J^n_0} \leqslant \delta P_n (N, Q, S) .\) (9.18)

We prove the polynomial form of these estimates rather than leave the dependence on the number of read coordinates implicit.

For matrices use the maximum entry norm. Multiplication costs at most \(N\) per contracted index. Differentiating \(CC^{- 1} = I\) gives, for a nonempty labelled derivative set \(I\),

\(\displaystyle D_I (C^{- 1}) = - C^{- 1} \sum_{\varnothing \ne A \subseteq I} (D_A C) D_{I \setminus A} (C^{- 1}) .\)

Induction in \(|I| \leqslant n\) bounds all inverse jets by a polynomial in \(N, Q\). The exact identity

\(\displaystyle C_9^{- 1} - C_6^{- 1} = C_9^{- 1} (C_6 - C_9) C_6^{- 1}\)

and the Leibniz rule give a factor \(\delta\) in every derivative of the inverse difference. The remaining factors have the polynomial bounds just obtained.

For the logarithmic determinant, its positive-order derivatives follow by repeatedly differentiating \(D \log \det C = \text{tr} (C^{- 1} DC)\). Telescoping each product shows that the difference of these derivatives for \(C_9\) and \(C_6\) is \(\delta\) times a polynomial. For the zeroth order join \(C_6 (0)\) to \(C_9 (0)\) by the straight positive segment. Since the endpoint inverse operator norms are at most \(NQ\), both endpoints are at least \((NQ)^{- 1} I\); the whole segment has the same lower bound. Hence

\(\displaystyle | \log \det C_9 (0) - \log \det C_6 (0) | \leqslant \int_0^1 N \|C_s^{- 1} \| \|C_9 (0) - C_6 (0)\| \hspace{0.17em} ds \leqslant N^3 Q \delta .\)

The two quadratic forms in the Gaussian exponent differ by

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} w_9^T C_9^{- 1} w_9 - w_6^T C_6^{- 1} w_6 = & (w_9 - w_6)^T C_9^{- 1} w_9\\ & + w_6^T C_9^{- 1} (w_9 - w_6) + w_6^T (C_9^{- 1} - C_6^{- 1}) w_6 . \end{aligned}\)

Every derivative therefore has the same \(\delta\) factor.

Let \(\ell_a = \log [p_{C_a (y)} (w_a (y, z)) / p_{C_0} (z)]\). The preceding calculations bound all jets of \(\ell_9 - \ell_6\) by \(\delta\) times a polynomial. All positive-order jets of \(\ell_6\) have polynomial bounds, and \(\ell_6 (0, z) = 0\). The labelled-partition formula

\(\displaystyle D_I e^{\ell} = e^{\ell} \sum_{\pi \in \text{Part} (I)} \prod_{A \in \pi} D_A \ell\)

thus gives polynomial jets of \(e^{\ell_6}\). For \(e^{\ell_9 - \ell_6} - 1\), the value is bounded by \(e | \ell_9 - \ell_6 |\) under (9.17). Each positive-order partition has at least one derivative factor, so all its terms retain a factor \(\delta\). The other factors can be bounded by one times an enlarged polynomial, using (9.17). Finally

\(\displaystyle R_9 - R_6 = Je^{\ell_6} (e^{\ell_9 - \ell_6} - 1)\)

and the Leibniz rule prove (9.18). All operations involve at most \(n\) derivatives, so the number and degree of polynomial factors are fixed, independently of \(N\).

9.3.3The good-coordinate region and the Gaussian tail

On \(\|z\|_{\infty} < v\), the cutoff jets equal those of the constant one. The difference of the two integrands consequently has jet norm at most \(\tau \delta P_n (N, Q, S)\). On the rest of the compact ball, the product rule and (9.18) bound its jets by \(\tau P_n (N, Q, S)\), after enlarging the polynomial once more. Outside that ball the integrands vanish on a parameter neighborhood, so their jets vanish too.

The reference Gaussian obeys the dimension-explicit tail estimate

\(\displaystyle \gamma_{C_0} \{\|z\|_{\infty} \geqslant v\} \leqslant 2 Ne^{- v + M / 2} .\) (9.19)

For a single coordinate, Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{z_i}\) and \(e^{- z_i}\) gives \(\mathbb{P} (|z_i | \geqslant v) \leqslant 2 e^{- v + (C_0)_{ii} / 2}\). Sum over the at most \(N\) coordinates and use \((C_0)_{ii} \leqslant M\). No independence of these coordinates is required.

Differentiation under the integral is justified even though \(h\) need not be continuous. On a fixed compact coordinate ball the smooth density-cutoff factors and all their derivatives through order \(n\) are bounded on a smaller parameter neighborhood. Multiplication by the fixed measurable \(h\) supplies an integrable constant bound. Difference quotients are dominated using the fundamental theorem of calculus along a short parameter segment. Apply dominated convergence, successively through order \(n\), as in Subsection 8.26. Integration of the two pointwise bounds and (9.19) gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \int (R_6 \chi_6 - R_9 \chi_9) h \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_{C_0} \right|_{J^n_0} \leqslant \tau P_n (N, Q, S) (\delta + 2 Ne^{- v + M / 2}) .\) (9.20)

Finite forest and tag averages preserve this estimate, because their weights are nonnegative and sum to one. The insertion and the reference transition remain paired before taking these averages.

9.3.4How the comparison is paid

Write \(\beta_f (x)\) for the fine inverse coupling, and use the upper-exponent convention of Subsection 19.1. Suppose \(N, Q, S\) have upper exponent zero, \(\delta\) has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\), and \(e^{- v}\) has every negative upper exponent. Then the multiplier of \(\tau\) in (9.20) has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). Indeed a fixed polynomial in exponent-zero quantities still has upper exponent zero. Its product with \(\delta\) has exponent \(- 1 / 2\), whereas its product with \(e^{- v}\) can be assigned any negative exponent. A finite sum preserves the larger exponent. The smallness requirement (9.17) therefore holds beyond a finite post-length threshold.

Here are the exact scales that produce the comparison data in the construction. The local support diameter is at most \(D_x = B_x (4 B_x + 2) + 2 B_x\), and its read dimension is at most

\(\displaystyle N (x) = (2 D_x + 1)^4 L^4 \hspace{0.17em} 4 \dim \mathfrak{g}.\)

This has upper exponent zero for fixed \(L\). The local direction bound is \(2 \theta_f (x) (1 + D_x)^p\), of upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). Let \(J_0 (x)\) bound the unscaled displacement of the two means, and \(V_0 (x)\) the covariance difference, including their jets. The construction requires the bounds

\(\displaystyle J_0 : - 1, \qquad V_0 : - 1 / 2,\) (9.21)

where the numbers denote upper exponents. Here \(\beta\) is the actual fine inverse coupling of the incoming representation, within its prescribed window about \(\beta_f (x)\). Gaussian coordinates divide the mean displacement by \(g = \beta^{- 1 / 2}\), so a common jet difference bound is

\(\displaystyle \delta (x) = |V_0 (x) | + g^{- 1} |J_0 (x) | .\)

Since \(g^{- 1}\) has upper exponent \(1 / 2\), this has exponent \(- 1 / 2\). Absolute mean and coordinate bounds, covariance inverse bounds, and the fixed-order Jacobian bounds are exponent zero after this rescaling. The support radius in Gaussian coordinates is bounded by \(g^{- 1} (5 a \theta_f + |c| \theta_f)\), also exponent zero. The product cutoffs have polynomial fixed-order jet bounds: in a derivative of order at most \(n\), at most \(n\) factors are differentiated; the others have magnitude at most one. There are at most \(N^n\) choices of these factors. The one-factor derivatives are bounded by the fixed smooth cutoff and its rescaled aperture.

Choose the good-coordinate radius

\(\displaystyle v (x) = \frac{\varepsilon}{128 (\dim \mathfrak{g}+ 1)} \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{M}_f (x),\)

where \(\mathcal{M}_f\) is the fine monitor. The monitor estimate gives every negative upper exponent for \(e^{- v}\). The construction's aperture comparison is \(\theta_f / g \geqslant \mathcal{M}_f / 2\). Eventually \(\delta \leqslant \varepsilon \mathcal{M}_f / [128 (\dim \mathfrak{g}+ 1)]\); hence

\(\displaystyle g \dim \mathfrak{g} \hspace{0.17em} (v + \delta) < \varepsilon \theta_f / 8.\)

Each reference coordinate then lies strictly inside its cutoff plateau when \(\|z\|_{\infty} < v\). Continuity gives the required parameter neighborhood; the cutoff implication in the common integral gives the other plateau as well. This proves the tail and numerical purchases directly, once the primitive mean and covariance comparison (9.21) and their absolute jet bounds have been supplied. Those primitive estimates concern the actual sector and reference covariances; the value-only remote mean bound does not by itself prove them.

9.4The mean and covariance jets in the principal comparison

We prove the extra smallness used in (9.21). The two means need not agree away from the flat configuration. At the flat configuration their values vanish and their first derivatives differ only by the local inverse error. This gives one more small factor for their difference than for the covariance difference.

9.4.1Uniform derivatives of the two local kernels

We first record why the kernel bounds are uniform in the period. All row estimates in this subsection use the maximum of weighted row and column sums from Subsection 8.13. For a fixed derivative order \(n\), suppose a family of precisions \(A (b)\) has uniformly positive lower bound \(r > 0\), uniform exponential row bound, and derivatives through order \(n\) with weighted row bounds at most \(C (x)\), where \(C\) has upper exponent zero. The logarithmic fields \(b\) range over a ball of radius \(v (x)\), with upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). These hypotheses hold for the unmasked local kernels under the fixed path and positive precision bounds used in Subsection 8.21.

The inverse derivative formula, in multilinear notation, is

\(\displaystyle D_I A^{- 1} = \sum_{(I_1, \ldots, I_k)} (- 1)^k A^{- 1} (D_{I_1} A) A^{- 1} \cdots (D_{I_k} A) A^{- 1} .\) (9.22)

The sum is over ordered partitions of the labelled set \(I\) into nonempty blocks. Differentiate \(AA^{- 1} = I\) and induct on \(|I|\) to prove this formula: the first differentiated block is the nonempty subset falling on \(A\), and the induction partitions the remaining labels. Submultiplicativity and the inverse decay estimate of Subsection 8.13 bound each term by a fixed power of \(C (x)\) and of the inverse row bound. The number of terms depends only on \(n\). Thus all inverse jets have upper exponent zero.

The same argument applies to every principal window, with a small reduction of the exponential weight when the windows vary. Indeed, at some fixed weight \(\gamma > 0\) the ordered-partition formula bounds each inverse derivative on every window by \(K_n\). An assembled column is one such inverse column extended by zero; thus each of its entries is at most \(K_n e^{- \gamma \text{dist} (a_i, a_j)}\). At weight \(\gamma / 2\), both row and column sums are bounded by

\(\displaystyle K_n m \sum_{q \geqslant 0} (2 q + 1)^4 e^{- \gamma q / 2},\)

where \(m\) bounds the number of coordinates at an anchor. This bound does not require two columns to use the same window. All windows and coordinate projections remain fixed during differentiation. Symmetrizing preserves the estimate.

On the reference side the coupling is the matrix of the unmasked curvature and predictor derivatives, restricted by the fixed keep test to the active coordinates. Denote it by \(D (b)\); its precision is exactly \(D (b)^{\ast} D (b)\). Away from zero this coupling differs from the completed-sector coupling: it uses the actual derivatives of curved plaquettes and of the predictor, whereas the completed sector transports their flat derivatives. We bound the reference derivatives separately. A derivative of order \(j\) of a plaquette derivative is a derivative of order \(j + 1\) of its finite product of link matrices; the product rule bounds it by the same finite path-length moments, now through order \(j + 1\). The predictor contribution is treated with its fixed-ball derivative bound through that order. Purchase these bounds through \(n + 1\) when requesting \(n\) coupling derivatives. All coefficients are bounded by the absolute path sums already used in Subsection 8.21. Summing their exponentially weighted rows and columns, and then applying the fixed keep and active projections, gives an exponent-zero bound for each reference coupling derivative. The product rule gives the precision derivative bound, with a finite sum over subsets of the derivative labels. The flat identity proved below identifies \(D (0)^{\ast} D (0)\) with the active principal block of the completed sector precision; hence Subsection 8.12 gives its lower bound \(r\). Its perturbation on the logarithmic ball is bounded by \(C (x) v (x)\), so it remains at least \(r / 2\) eventually. Thus the inverse argument applies to the reference matrix as well as the completed one.

The reference panels have a different geometric size from the sector windows. For \(B \geqslant 120\) they contain every active coordinate whose parent anchor is within \(\lfloor B / 60 \rfloor\) of the column anchor. Each endpoint of a bond lies within one coarse step of its parent anchor. The triangle inequality therefore bounds the separation of any two such endpoints by \(\lfloor B / 60 \rfloor + 2\); the reference middle-panel criterion follows from \(20 \lfloor B / 60 \rfloor + 80 \leqslant B\). These are the two panel inclusions required here. The inverse-error proof uses only this inner radius, not an equality of the two window systems. Accordingly the reference error costs \(e^{- c \lfloor B / 60 \rfloor}\) in place of \(e^{- cB}\). Both have every negative upper exponent.

For clarity, these derivatives are taken with the forest, tags, read windows and sector indicators fixed. The unmasked matrix entries are products of path derivatives and parallel transports. Their row costs are finite sums of the absolute path bounds multiplied by fixed powers of path length. The fixed-ball predictor bounds in Subsection 8.21 make those costs exponent zero. On the shrinking ball, the change from the flat precision is at most \(C (x) v (x)\) in operator norm, by the fundamental theorem of calculus. Eventually this is less than \(r / 2\), so the same inverse estimates apply throughout the ball. This proves the required neighborhood of positivity without changing any sector indicator.

Both affine sources are products of these kernel rows, their coupling matrices and the unmasked residual vector. Their derivatives therefore have exponent-zero bounds as well. The residual and its derivatives have the fixed-ball bounds (8.160). A row acting on a bounded vector uses its absolute row sum, not the total number of lattice coordinates. The preceding product estimates are consequently uniform over all finite periods and all allowed forests and tags.

9.4.2The exact comparison at the flat configuration

Let \(A\) denote the full flat sector precision, padded by the identity on inactive coordinates, and let \(U\) denote its active coordinate set. At a flat field all parallel transports are identities. Differentiating a plaquette gives its signed exterior difference, and differentiating the predictor noise gives the flat coarse response with the same sign in both constructions. The normalization of the signed average is the same \(|\mathcal{S}|^{- 1 / 2}\) in both matrices. Evaluating on each colour coordinate vector therefore identifies the restricted sector coupling with the reference coupling. Taking its Gram matrix gives the exact flat identity

\(\displaystyle A_{UU} = A_{\text{ref}} .\) (9.23)

The padding contributes zero on \(U\). Rows discarded by the reference keep test already vanish in this coupling, so their removal does not change the product.

Since inactive rows and columns of \(A\) are identity rows and columns, \(A\) is the direct sum of \(A_{UU}\) and an identity matrix. Hence

\(\displaystyle (A^{- 1})_{UU} = A_{UU}^{- 1} .\)

This conclusion uses the actual direct-sum property; it would not follow merely by compressing an arbitrary inverse. Let \(P_6\) be the sector window inverse and \(P_9\) the reference window inverse at the flat point. The window-error calculation of Subsection 8.13, applied to their respective windows, gives

\(\displaystyle \|(P_6)_{UU} - P_9 \|_0 \leqslant t (x),\) (9.24)

where \(t\) has every negative upper exponent. Indeed each error is bounded by its fixed inverse-decay constant times \(e^{- c \lfloor B_x / 60 \rfloor}\), and the cutoff radius \(B_x\) dominates every fixed multiple of \(\log \beta_f (x)\). Taking the sum of the two errors preserves this property. Symmetrizing (9.24) gives the same bound for the flat covariance difference.

Write the actual and reference means in logarithmic coordinates as

\(\displaystyle m_6 (b) = - P_6 (b)^{\ast} s_6 (b), \qquad m_9 (b) = - P_9 (b)^{\ast} s_9 (b) .\)

On the actual side the formula is restricted to \(U\) after the matrix-vector product. Each source is its coupling transpose applied to the residual. The residuals vanish at \(b = 0\): all curvature strokes are zero, the predictor is the identity, and all coarse guides are linear in zero strokes. Thus both sources and both means vanish there.

The source derivatives agree after restriction:

\(\displaystyle (Ds_6 (0) h) |_U = Ds_9 (0) h.\) (9.25)

To see this, write \(s = M^{\ast} a\). At zero, \(a (0) = 0\), so its derivative is \(M (0)^{\ast} Da (0) h\) and the derivative of \(M\) contributes zero. The two residual formulas are the same unmasked curvature and noise strokes, after restricting the kept rows. On omitted rows the coupling is zero, as in (9.23). This proves (9.25). The actual source derivative also vanishes outside \(U\), because the corresponding coupling columns are zero.

Differentiating the mean products now gives the exact identity

\(\displaystyle D (m_9 - m_6) (0) h = ((P_6 (0))_{UU} - P_9 (0))^{\ast} Ds_9 (0) h.\) (9.26)

Derivatives of \(P_a\) multiply the zero source and disappear. By the row bound and the exponent-zero source derivative bound,

\(\displaystyle \|D (m_9 - m_6) (0)\| \leqslant t (x) C (x) .\)

This proves a first-derivative comparison, not merely an equality of quadratic forms.

9.4.3Transferring the estimates along a local variation

We give the elementary Taylor argument used for this transfer. Let \(f : E \to F\) be \(C^n\), \(n \geqslant 2\), on a neighborhood of the closed ball of radius \(v \leqslant 1\), and suppose

\(\displaystyle f (0) = 0, \qquad \|Df (0)\| \leqslant Ct, \qquad \|D^k f (b)\| \leqslant C \quad (2 \leqslant k \leqslant n, \|b\| \leqslant v) .\)

Let \(u\) be a \(C^n\) chart about the parameter base point with \(\|u (0)\| \leqslant v\) and \(\|D^k u (0)\| \leqslant v^k\) for \(1 \leqslant k \leqslant n\). Taylor's formula along the segment from \(0\) to \(b\) gives

\(\displaystyle \|f (b)\| \leqslant Ct \hspace{0.17em} v + Cv^2 / 2, \qquad \|Df (b)\| \leqslant C (t + v) .\)

For a derivative of \(f \circ u\) of order \(k \geqslant 1\), a partition with \(r\) blocks contributes \(D^r f (u (0))\) applied to the derivatives of \(u\) of those block sizes. Their product costs \(v^k\). When \(r = 1\) this is at most \(C (t + v) v\); when \(r \geqslant 2\) it is at most \(Cv^2\), since \(k \geqslant 2\) and \(v \leqslant 1\). There are at most \(n! 2^n\) partitions for any \(k \leqslant n\). Together with the value estimate this proves

\(\displaystyle \|f \circ u\|_{J^n_0} \leqslant n! 2^n \{C (t + v) v + Cv^2 \} .\) (9.27)

Similarly, if \(g (0)\) has norm at most \(t\) and all its positive-order derivatives on the ball are bounded by \(2 C\), the mean-value theorem and the same partition formula give

\(\displaystyle \|g \circ u\|_{J^n_0} \leqslant n! 2^n (t + 2 Cv) .\) (9.28)

Apply (9.27) to \(f = m_9 - m_6\), using (9.26). Apply (9.28) to each entry of the covariance difference, using (9.24). The common logarithmic chart from Sections 8.18 and 8.21 supplies the required \(u\). For a support anchored at \(o\), its radius is at most \(D_x\). The value bound needs the base field as well as the direction bounds. Here is a direct local estimate. On the nonwrapping read box of radius \(R = 45 (1 + B)\), choose the gauge given by parallel transport from its anchor along coordinate-ordered paths. A gauged bond is the holonomy of the two such paths and that bond. Interchanging adjacent coordinate steps reduces this loop to the empty path using at most \(C (1 + R)^2\) plaquettes in the box, with \(C\) depending only on dimension. Indeed each path has at most \(4 (2 R + 2)\) steps; moving each step past the earlier steps uses at most the square of this number of swaps. Each swap inserts a conjugate of one elementary plaquette. The representation norm is invariant under conjugation and satisfies \(\|UV - 1\| \leqslant \|U - 1\| + \|V - 1\|\). Health therefore bounds the gauged bond chord by \(C (1 + R)^2 \theta_c\). The logarithm is Lipschitz on its fixed smaller chart, so it obeys the same bound after enlarging \(C\). The period and chart conditions used below ensure that this box lifts and that all these logarithms are defined. The positive-order derivatives instead follow from the direction bound. A common size and derivative scale is therefore

\(\displaystyle v (x) = C_n \{ (1 + R)^2 \theta_c (x) + 2 \theta_f (x) (1 + \text{reach} (x))^p (1 + D_x)^p \} .\)

For the derivative part apply the weighted direction bound at \(o\) to every read site and using the uniform derivatives of the logarithm on the fixed chart. An ordered product of \(k\) local variations contributes the \(k\)th power of that scale. It has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\) and is eventually at most one.

9.4.4Returning from the local logarithm to the physical field

The preceding comparison is made with blank local sector data. We must identify its readings with the physical ones; global agreement of the fields or of their sector indicators is not available. Let \(Y\) be the allocated coarse support, disjoint from the sector's seed set. For each coordinate read by the insertion, with parent anchor \(p\), its allocation includes

\(\displaystyle \text{halo}_{R + 4} \{p\} \subset Y, \qquad R = 45 (1 + B) .\)

The field is healthy on \(Y\), and the period and chart bounds ensure that this read region has the local gauge chart of Subsection 8.18. Fix that gauge \(g\) once, at the base configuration. For every nearby configuration \(W\) define

\(\displaystyle b (W)_e = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \log ((g \cdot W)_e), & \text{dist} (p, e_-) \leqslant R,\\ 0, & \text{dist} (p, e_-) > R. \end{array} \right.\)

The finite set of logarithms stays in its common chart on a neighborhood of the base point. Thus \(b\) is smooth there, and \(\exp b (W) = g \cdot W\) on the entire read halo. No assertion is made about plaquettes crossing the boundary of its zero extension. The derivative estimates used above hold for precisely this extension: the entries outside the halo have zero derivatives.

Here is why this local agreement suffices. A seed in the read halo would belong to \(Y\), contrary to seed separation. The local indicator tests therefore agree with the blank tests there. The forest, signed choices, widths and fixed path data are the same. Every entry used by a sector window, and every source entry used in its local mean, reads at most \(17 B\) from the column anchor. This fits inside \(R\). In the reference construction the middle and end panels and their field queries are contained in the same allocated halo, with the extra four sites allowing for the bond endpoints. For the reference construction this containment can be checked numerically. The panel radius is at most \(\lfloor B / 20 \rfloor\). If \(E\) denotes the extent of a single path query, the coupling read radius is \(E + 10 B + 4\), and the covariance read radius is this quantity plus \(2 \lfloor B / 20 \rfloor\). The mean adds the residual read radius, the coupling range, and one more panel radius; write the resulting radius as \(S\). The fixed range inequalities give \(S + E \leqslant 36 B\). Even an allowed displacement of the query anchor by \(7 (1 + B)\) therefore costs at most \(7 (1 + B) + 36 B \leqslant 45 (1 + B) = R\). These inequalities cover the whole local product, including source entries in a mean row. Consequently their restricted coupling and precision entries, and their source entries, agree after the local gauge change. Equal principal matrices have equal inverses; their restricted matrix products also agree. This proves equality of the mean readings and window covariance blocks. It uses the local tests only, so sector indicators outside the halo can be arbitrary.

More explicitly, write \(O_j\) for the colour adjoint of the fixed gauge at the parent of coordinate \(j\). For a mean read at \(k\), and a covariance block between \(k\) and \(l\), the preceding identification gives

\(\displaystyle O_k m_a^{\text{phys}} (W)_k = m_a^{\text{loc}} (b (W))_k, \qquad C_a^{\text{phys}} (W)_{kl} = O_k^{\ast} C_a^{\text{loc}} (b (W))_{kl} O_l, \qquad a = 6, 9.\)

For the sector covariance its locality initially identifies the column at \(k\); symmetry gives the displayed row identity. The reference identity reads that row directly. Thus the same chart at \(k\) compares any requested block, including \(l\) outside the insertion's support. The mean identity on the reference side holds for every fixed fine field and auxiliary parameter: its local affine source is the same one just identified. All these equalities hold on a neighborhood, so their derivatives through the required order agree as well.

A colour adjoint is an isometry, so returning from the local mean does not change its norm. Each entry of \(O_j\) has magnitude at most one, so the covariance entry bound acquires at most \((\dim \mathfrak{g})^2\). The gauge is fixed while differentiating. Consequently the actual unscaled mean difference has upper exponent \(- 1\), whereas the covariance difference has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\), exactly as in (9.21).

The argument uses the same local histories, flags and reference windows on both sides. It does not identify the two precisions on nonflat fields. The hypotheses inherited from the earlier kernel and chart sections are the common path bounds, their local smooth formulas, and the positive precision estimates; these remain part of the selected construction throughout.

9.5Attenuation under the principal Gaussian reference

We apply the polynomial cancellation just proved to the reference integral in Subsection 9.3. The important feature is the order of constants: the coefficient of \(L^{- d - 1}\) is chosen before \(L\). Bounds for the nonlinear chart and the Gaussian can be chosen after \(L\), because their additional costs tend to zero as the entrance coupling increases. Throughout, \(n \geqslant \max \{2, d + 1\}\), the insertion has the fine regularity through order \(n\) used below, and its polynomial moments through degree \(d\) vanish. The coarse weight exponent does not exceed the fine one. The stage smoothing order \(j\) also satisfies \(d \leqslant j - 2\), so that the installed kernel supplies every spatial difference used in the polynomial decomposition. In particular degree-four attenuation uses \(j \geqslant 6\).

9.5.1The first derivative of the reference mean

Let \(u\) be a coarse logarithmic field and \(v_F (u)\) the stationary relative field in tree coordinates from Subsection 8.14. At the identity the full affine row satisfies

\(\displaystyle D_{\infty} v_F (u) + r'_0 (u) = 0.\)

This is (8.111), with the same signed normalization on the two terms. The reference construction uses the cut row \(D_R\) and its cut residual derivative \(r'_R\). The exponential path tail gives, uniformly in the period,

\(\displaystyle \|D_R v_F (u) + r'_R (u)\|_{\infty} \leqslant C (1 + R)^a e^{- \sigma R} \|u\|_{\infty} .\) (9.29)

To see this, subtract the full identity. The row difference acts on \(v_F (u)\), whose sup norm is at most \(C_{\text{resp}} \|u\|_{\infty}\). The guide difference acts on \(u\). Each is a sum of omitted path rows; their weighted absolute tails give the displayed bound, including the finite path-length power. All inactive coordinates are omitted on both sides, and tree coordinates of \(v_F\) are zero.

Let \(A_R = D_R^{\ast} D_R\), let \(P_R\) be the reference principal-window inverse, and put \(E_R = A_R P_R - I\). The reference mean in active coordinates is \(m (u) = - P_R (u)^{\ast} D_R (u)^{\ast} r_R (u)\). Since \(r_R (0) = 0\), its derivative at zero is

\(\displaystyle Dm (0) u = - P_R (0)^{\ast} D_R (0)^{\ast} r'_R (u) .\)

The elementary normal-equation identity therefore gives

\(\displaystyle Dm (0) u - v_F (u) = E_R (0)^{\ast} v_F (u) - P_R (0)^{\ast} D_R (0)^{\ast} (D_R (0) v_F (u) + r'_R (u)) .\)

The window and row bounds of Subsection 9.4, together with (9.29), imply

\(\displaystyle \|Dm (0) - v_F \|_{\infty \to \infty} \leqslant C \{e^{- c \lfloor B / 60 \rfloor} + (1 + R)^a e^{- \sigma R} \} .\) (9.30)

Here and below the restriction to active counting coordinates and the fixed colour reassembly contribute only fixed factors. Because \(R = \lfloor B / 100000 \rfloor\) and \(B\) grows quadratically in the logarithm of the coupling, the right side is eventually at most the coarse small-field threshold \(\theta_c\). This proves the required first-order approximation for the selected windowed mean, without replacing it by the exact inverse mean.

9.5.2The nonlinear local display

The tree-straightening potential is a linear site field \(\phi (u)\). Its discrete gradient corrects the tree response to the actual installed \(A_0\) response, denoted \(\mathcal{A}u\):

\(\displaystyle \phi (u) (e_-) - \phi (u) (e_+) + v_F (u)_e + \bar{u}_e = (\mathcal{A}u)_e .\)

This follows by summing the tree potential along a bond: the interior terms telescope, leaving its endpoint difference. The root correction is included in the same potential, as in the response bound of Subsection 8.14.

For noise \(w\) in fine link logarithmic units define the local logarithmic display

\(\displaystyle \Phi (u, w)_e = \log (e^{\phi (u) (e_-)} e^{\tilde{m} (u)_e + w_e} e^{\bar{u}_e} e^{- \phi (u) (e_+)}),\)

where \(\tilde{m}\) is the colour-reassembled mean, zero on tree links. The logarithm is taken on one common fixed ball. Its existence and uniform derivatives follow from the fixed-dimensional group chart, the mean row bounds, the tree-potential bound and the replica bound. Shrink the input ball so that the four entries of the logarithm's argument lie in that chart. The derivative of \(\log (e^a e^b e^c e^{- d})\) at zero is \(a + b + c - d\). Consequently, with \(T (u, w) =\mathcal{A}u + w\), there are \(Q \geqslant 1\) and \(r > 0\), uniform over all periods, forests and tags, such that

\(\displaystyle \Phi (0) = 0, \quad \|D \Phi (0) - T\| \leqslant Q \theta_c, \qquad \|D^j \Phi (z)\| \leqslant Q \quad (j \leqslant n, \|z\| \leqslant r) .\) (9.31)

The constants \(Q, r\) may depend on the already chosen block factor. The tangent error is (9.30); the other bounds follow by the finite product and composition rules and the separate reference inverse estimates already proved.

Let \(a (y) = (u (y), w)\), where \(u (y)\) is the fixed-gauge local logarithm along the ordered coarse variation. Write \(s\) for its common size and derivative scale, so that

\(\displaystyle \|a (0)\| \leqslant s, \qquad \|D^j a (0)\| \leqslant s^j \quad (1 \leqslant j \leqslant n), \qquad \theta_c \leqslant s \leqslant \min \{1, r\} .\)

The noise is controlled separately from the health condition. In counting Gaussian coordinates \(z\), it is \(w = g \hspace{0.17em} \text{tape} (z)\), up to the fixed colour isometries. We use a compactly supported \(C^n\) cutoff \(\Gamma\) with \(| \Gamma | \leqslant 1\) and

\(\displaystyle z \in \text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} \Gamma \quad \Longrightarrow \quad g \| \text{tape} (z)\| \leqslant \varepsilon \theta_f \leqslant s.\)

The last inequality is an actual cutoff purchase. For fixed block factor the thresholds are \(\theta_c = P (x) / \sqrt{x}\) and \(\theta_f = P (x + c_L) / \sqrt{x + c_L}\), where \(P (x) = \exp ((\log \log x)^2)\) and \(c_L\) is fixed. Their ratio tends to one: the square-root ratio does, and the mean-value theorem gives

\(\displaystyle | (\log \log (x + c_L))^2 - (\log \log x)^2 | \leqslant |c_L | \sup_{\xi \text{between } x \text{ and } x + c_L} \frac{2 | \log \log \xi |}{\xi \log \xi} \longrightarrow 0.\)

Thus \(\theta_c / \theta_f \geqslant 1 / 2\) eventually. Choose the cutoff constant \(\varepsilon \leqslant 1 / 2\); then \(\varepsilon \theta_f \leqslant \theta_c \leqslant s\). Increasing the entrance coupling achieves this after \(L\) is fixed. The chart bound gives \(s = C \theta_c\) times a fixed polynomial in its read radius, with the local-gauge constant \(C\) purchased before \(L\). The nonlinear display constants \(Q, r\) are chosen after \(L\). Thus the asserted bound for \(a\) holds on the cutoff support, including its boundary. Only the coarse component varies. Integrating the first two derivative bounds in (9.31) gives

\(\displaystyle \| \Phi (z) - Tz\| \leqslant Q (\theta_c +\|z\|) \|z\|, \qquad \|D \Phi (z) - T\| \leqslant Q (\theta_c +\|z\|) .\)

Apply the partition chain rule to \((\Phi - T) \circ a\). A term with one block costs at most \(2 Qs \cdot s^j \leqslant 2 Qs^2\). A term with at least two blocks costs at most \(Qs^j \leqslant Qs^2\); linear maps have zero derivatives of those orders. The value is bounded by \(2 Qs^2\) too. Thus for a constant \(c_n\) depending only on the derivative order,

\(\displaystyle \|D^j (\Phi \circ a) (0) -\mathcal{A}D^j u (0)\| \leqslant c_n Qs^2 + \|w\|, \qquad j \leqslant n.\) (9.32)

The noise term is needed only at \(j = 0\) but is harmless at all orders.

Each \(\mathcal{A}D^j u (0)\) has the graded decomposition of Subsection 7.11, with cost \(M\) chosen before \(L\). Put the error in (9.32) entirely into its last piece. Since the fine direction width is at least \(\theta_f\), all the display derivatives have a graded decomposition at scale \(L^{- 1}\) and cost

\(\displaystyle M + \frac{c_n Qs^2 + \|w\|}{\theta_f} L^{d + 1} .\) (9.33)

This operation requires no polynomial property of the error.

9.5.3The common healthy segment

For a short fine support, meaning that its cover number \(q\) obeys \(q^3 < L\), the whole inspected support lies in the read halo of one coarse chart. Seed separation, identical local flags, and the mean-locality argument of Subsection 9.4 identify the displayed configuration with the physical Gaussian-reference configuration on that support, after two gauge transformations. One gauge is the fixed coarse chart gauge; the second is the exponential of \(\phi (u)\). Gauge invariance of the insertion removes both. The equality holds as a germ in the coarse variation, so derivatives of the insertion agree. Neither the frozen fine field nor the Gaussian coordinates are resampled in this identity.

The cutoff restricts to configurations healthy on \(X\). We also need health on the radial segment of the display. Here is the small elementary estimate that supplies it. If a plaquette has four logarithmic bonds of norm at most \(h \leqslant 1\), let \(K (t)\) be the matrix chord \(K (t) = (\text{mat} (\text{plaquette} (t)) - I) / c_G\), where \(c_G\) is the fixed colour normalization and the norm is the ambient matrix norm of the health test. The group product and this chord map have bounded second derivatives on this fixed ball, so

\(\displaystyle \|K (t) - tK (1)\| \leqslant C_G t (1 - t) h^2, \qquad 0 \leqslant t \leqslant 1.\)

Indeed subtract the affine interpolation of \(K\) at its endpoints and integrate its second derivative against the Dirichlet Green kernel on \([0, 1]\); the kernel integral is \(t (1 - t) / 2\). All four bonds in the touched-plaquette halo are included. The chord at zero is zero. Hence, if the endpoint is healthy and \(C_G h^2 \leqslant \theta_f\),

\(\displaystyle \|K (t)\| \leqslant t \theta_f + t (1 - t) \theta_f \leqslant \theta_f .\)

Take \(h = Qs\). The two required smallness conditions \(Qs \leqslant 1\) and \(C_G (Qs)^2 \leqslant \theta_f\) hold eventually: \(s\) is \(\theta_c\) times a fixed polynomial in the read radius, and \(\theta_c^2 / \theta_f\) has negative upper exponent. The direction width bound follows likewise from \(\theta_f (1 + 6 (1 + B) L)^p \leqslant 1\). These purchases occur after \(L\). We can therefore apply the displaced cancellation bound to every partition in the derivatives of \(f \circ \exp \circ \Phi \circ a\). It gives the fine-insertion bound

\(\displaystyle C_{n, d} \tau L^{- d - 1} \left( M + \frac{c_n Qs^2 +\|w\|}{\theta_f} L^{d + 1} \right)^n .\) (9.34)

The finite number of partitions is included in \(C_{n, d}\).

9.5.4Separate local gauges for a winding support

For a general support \(X\), one common logarithm need not exist. We instead base the insertion chart at the actual received configuration \(W_0\). Keep the frozen fine field, the Gaussian coordinates, and every localization tag fixed. For each tested fine bond \(i\), choose the coarse tree gauge \(g_i\) on the read halo centered at its coarse parent. The local mean and covariance agreement proved earlier identifies this local calculation with the same received configuration. The gauges may differ with \(i\).

Let \(b_i (y)\) be the coarse logarithm in this gauge along the coarse variation, and let \(w_i\) be the adjoint transform of the same noise. Use the unstraightened tree display

\(\displaystyle S_i (y) = e^{\tilde{m} (b_i (y))_i + (w_i)_i} e^{\overline{b_i (y)}_i} .\)

Its linear response is the tree response \(\mathcal{W}b = v_F (b) + \bar{b}\), whose weighted bound is \(M\). Take \(\log (S_i (y) S_i (0)^{- 1})\) and return it by the fixed adjoint \(g_i^{- 1}\) at the initial endpoint. The variable straightening potential \(\phi (b_i (y))\) is absent from this relative display. Denote the result by \(Z_i (y)\); set it to zero on untested bonds. The relative logarithm is defined near \(y = 0\) because both arguments lie in the fixed chart. It satisfies

\(\displaystyle Z (0) = 0, \qquad W (y)_i = \exp (Z_i (y)) (W_0)_i \quad \text{on every tested bond} .\)

This is an identity of germs: the relative exponential cancels the base display in that bond's own gauge. Finitely many tested bonds admit one common neighborhood. Local dependence of \(f\) then gives \(f (W (y)) = f (\exp (Z (y)) W_0)\) there. No compatibility between different \(g_i\) is needed, since \(Z\) is an ordinary fine bond field in the original coordinates. In particular this argument does not require a logarithm of \(W_0\) around a noncontractible loop.

Here is the bound on this field. Write \(\omega (i) = \theta_f (1 + \text{dist} (i_-, o))^{p_f}\) for the fine direction width. Let \(C \geqslant 1\) be the fixed local gauge response constant, \(M \geqslant 1\) the weighted tree-response constant just specified, and put

\(\displaystyle H_X = (2 + \text{breadth} (X))^{np_c}, \qquad A_X = 2 MC + \frac{(c_n Q + M) s^2 H_X}{\theta_f} .\)

The first derivative of \(b_i\) obeys the coarse weighted direction bound with cost \(C\); the linear response estimate and \(\theta_c \leqslant 2 \theta_f\), \(p_c \leqslant p_f\), therefore give \(2 MC \omega (i)\). For derivatives of order \(j \geqslant 2\), the local chart bound gives

\(\displaystyle \|D^j b_i (0)\| \leqslant s^j (1 + \text{dist} (\text{parent} (i_-), \text{parent} (o)))^{jp_c} \leqslant s^2 H_X .\)

The last inequality uses \(s \leqslant 1\), \(j \leqslant n\), and the diameter bound for a tested bond relative to \(o \in X\). The linear response on this bounded field costs at most \(Ms^2 H_X\).

The relative display on two copies of its inputs vanishes at zero; its derivative there is the linear response of their difference, up to \(Q \theta_c\). Its first derivative away from zero differs by at most a constant times \(Qs\), and derivatives of higher order are bounded by \(Q\). The partition chain rule, exactly as for (9.32), bounds the remaining terms at order \(j \geqslant 1\) by \(c_n Qs^2 H_X\). Adjoint return preserves the norm. Since \(\omega (i) \geqslant \theta_f\), these estimates prove

\(\displaystyle \|D^j Z_i (0)\| \leqslant A_X \omega (i), \qquad 1 \leqslant j \leqslant n.\)

The cutoff and the local health estimate make \(W_0\) healthy. Apply the ordinary fine regularity bound to \(f (\exp (Z) W_0)\) at \(Z = 0\). For each partition its argument fields have the displayed fine widths, so that the bound is at most \(C_n \tau A_X^n\) after summing partitions. The zeroth derivative is bounded by \(\tau\). This proves a general-support bound whose leading constant \(C_n (2 MC)^n\) is chosen before \(L\). Every dependence on the later chart constant \(Q\) is accompanied by \(s^2 / \theta_f\), which can be made small after \(L\) is fixed. There is no sum of separate insertion derivatives over bonds: the entire field \(Z\) is inserted into the same regularity estimate.

9.5.5Integrating the density derivatives

Use the same finite Gaussian coordinates for all derivatives, with its positive covariance and the frozen fine field fixed. The moment and density differentiation arguments of Sections 8.26 and 8.27 apply to this reference as well: its precision floor, inverse jets and local affine source were proved in Subsection 9.4. The cutoff is held fixed in these coordinates. Only on its support is the unmasked regular insertion evaluated; the health conclusion in the preceding paragraph applies there.

We detail the dependence of the bound on the support. Let \(q\) be its fine cover number and let \(H = (r_0 + 48) (1 + B)\), where \(r_0\) is the fixed allocation radius. Differentiating a Gaussian density inserts sums of products of inverse-covariance entries and at most quadratic coordinate factors. At order at most \(n\) the result is a polynomial in the Gaussian coordinates of degree at most \(2 n\). \(\displaystyle C_0 \tau L^{- d - 1} \{1 + \rho^{2 n} (1 +\|z\|)^{4 n} \} .\) (9.35)

Here \(C_0\) depends on the pre-\(L\) graded-field cost and the regularity conversion constants, while

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \rho \leqslant K_L (\theta_f + g / \theta_f) \{H (1 + q)\}^{a_n} .\)

To check this factor, insert \(w = gz\) into (9.33). The nonlinear remainder \(s^2 / \theta_f\) is bounded by \(\theta_f\) times a polynomial in \(B\) because the two thresholds have bounded ratio. The noise contributes \((g / \theta_f) \|z\|\). Every positive-order density or covariance variation has at least one coarse direction factor, which is at most \(\theta_c\) times such a polynomial; enlarge \(K_L\) and \(a_n\) to include it. Separate the fixed term \(M\) from these errors, and use \((a + b)^{2 n} \leqslant (2 a)^{2 n} (1 + b^{2 n})\) for \(a \geqslant 1\), \(b \geqslant 0\). This gives (9.35). For a general support, use the separate-gauge bound just proved, with its leading constant \(C_n (2 MC)^n\) and error \((c_n Q + M) s^2 H_X / \theta_f\). Combining it with the same density factors gives \(C_1 \tau\) in place of \(C_0 \tau L^{- d - 1}\), with possibly larger fixed exponents. Its pre-\(L\) constants are the ordinary local regularity and linear-response constants.

The Gaussian moment of \((1 +\|z\|)^{4 n}\) is bounded by \(K'_L \{H (1 + q)\}^{b_n}\). This follows by expanding the norm bound into coordinate moments and using the covariance upper bound, exactly as in Subsection 8.27. Choose \(h_n = 2 na_n + b_n\). For each fixed \(L\), increasing the entrance coupling makes

\(\displaystyle [K_L (\theta_f + g / \theta_f)]^{2 n} K'_L H^{h_n} \leqslant 1.\)

Indeed \(\theta_f\) times any fixed power of \(B\) tends to zero, and, uniformly over the incoming beta window, \(g / \theta_f \leqslant 2 / \text{monitor} (\beta_f)\). The monitor grows faster than every fixed power of \(B\). This argument does not assign a negative power exponent to \(g / \theta_f\). The resulting integrated derivative bounds are

\(\displaystyle \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} C \tau L^{- d - 1} (1 + q)^{h_n}, & q^3 < L,\\ C' \tau (1 + q)^{h'_n}, & q^3 \geqslant L. \end{array} \right.\) (9.36)

All differentiation under the integral is justified by the same polynomial Gaussian majorants on a common parameter neighborhood.

9.6Nonlinear singleton inheritance

A surviving singleton contributes an exponential of an old regular activity. Its first order part must be retained as a Gaussian reference observable. The error after this subtraction is smaller for two distinct reasons: the exponential remainder is quadratic, and the actual Gaussian law is close to the reference law. We prove both estimates on the same coordinates and then sum them over the inherited occurrences.

9.6.1The nonlinear comparison on fixed coordinates

Use the notation and quantitative hypotheses of Subsection 9.3. In particular, \(R_6, R_9\) are density ratios with the same compensated Jacobian, \(\gamma_{C_0}\) is the fixed reference probability measure, and \(w (z)\) is the original masked activity. Thus \(w\) is measurable and \(|w (z) | \leqslant \tau\). Assume

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \tau \leqslant K \leqslant 1.\)

Write \(c (y, z)\) and \(v (y, z)\) for the actual and reference cutoffs, respectively. They take values in \([0, 1]\) and have the common compact support and local plateau properties specified there. The exact integrand for the singleton plus its reference observable is

\(\displaystyle T (y, z) = R_6 (y, z) \{e^{- w (z) c (y, z)} - 1\} + R_9 (y, z) v (y, z) w (z) .\) (9.38)

The plus sign in the second term cancels the negative linear term of the first. The reference guard ensures that, wherever it is nonzero, the actual cutoff is one and the frozen field is healthy on the insertion support. Consequently the same \(w\) occurs in both terms, by the compensated locality identity. No derivative of \(w\) is needed.

Let \(P_0, P_1, P_2\) denote fixed positive polynomials in the local coordinate count and jet bounds, enlarged below when necessary. The density comparison already proved gives

\(\displaystyle \|R_6 (\cdot, z)\|_{J^n_0} \leqslant P_0, \qquad \|R_9 (\cdot, z) - R_6 (\cdot, z) \|_{J^n_0} \leqslant \delta P_1 .\)

Here and below the jet norm includes derivatives of every order from zero through \(n\). The polynomial constants depend on \(n\), not on the number of occurrences or the torus volume. If \(Q_c \geqslant 1\) bounds the cutoff jets, impose also \(KQ_c \leqslant 1\). For a nonempty derivative set \(I\), the derivative of \(e^{- wc}\) is

\(\displaystyle e^{- wc} \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{P} (I)} \prod_{A \in \pi} (- wD_A c) .\)

Since \(|w| Q_c \leqslant 1\), every product has size at most \(\tau Q_c\), up to the fixed number of partitions. For the zero-order term, \(|e^{- wc} - 1| \leqslant e \tau\). Product differentiation in (9.38) therefore gives the global bound \(\|T (\cdot, z)\|_{J^n_0} \leqslant \tau P_2\) on the compact read ball.

On the smaller ball where both cutoffs have germ one at \(y = 0\), the exact identity is

\(\displaystyle T = R_6 (e^{- w} - 1 + w) + (R_9 - R_6) w.\)

For real \(|w| \leqslant 1\), the exponential series gives

\(\displaystyle |e^{- w} - 1 + w| \leqslant |w|^2 \sum_{k \geqslant 2} \frac{1}{k!} \leqslant |w|^2 \leqslant \tau K.\)

The two factors involving \(w\) are constant in the differentiation parameter. Hence on this ball

\(\displaystyle \|T (\cdot, z)\|_{J^n_0} \leqslant \tau (KP_0 + \delta P_1) .\) (9.39)

Let its radius be \(v_0\) in the coordinate maximum norm and suppose \(C_0 \leqslant MI\). The coordinate Gaussian tail bound used in the principal comparison gives

\(\displaystyle \gamma_{C_0} \{\|z\|_{\infty} \geqslant v_0 \} \leqslant 2 N \exp (- v_0 + M / 2) .\)

The same compact-coordinate domination justifies differentiating (9.38) under the integral: on a common parameter neighborhood the smooth factors and their finitely many derivatives are bounded on the fixed compact ball, and \(w\) is bounded. Splitting that integral into the good ball and its complement proves

\(\displaystyle \left. \left| \int T \right( \cdot, z) \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_{C_0} (z) \right|_{J^n_0} \leqslant \tau \{KP_0 + \delta P_1 + 2 Ne^{- v_0 + M / 2} P_2 \} .\) (9.40)

This estimate includes the entire near-weight on the bad set; it does not use cancellation outside the common plateau.

In the renormalization step, \(K\) is the incoming regular budget, of upper exponent \(- 11 / 10\). The local covariance and mean comparison of Subsection 9.4 gives \(\delta\) of upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\): its two terms are the covariance discrepancy and the unscaled mean discrepancy multiplied by the inverse noise scale. The local coordinate counts, cutoff bounds, and polynomials above have upper exponent zero. The plateau radius is a fixed positive multiple of the fine monitor and its Gaussian tail has every negative upper exponent. Thus the braces in (9.40) have upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). In particular, all the smallness conditions in the density comparison and \(KQ_c \leqslant 1\) hold after increasing the entrance coupling, with the block size already fixed. Denote a common nonnegative bound for the braces by \(b (x)\).

9.6.2The retained reference term

These principal and remainder bounds feed the regular local return of Subsection 10.7. We spell out the summation cost of the reference term, since a per-occurrence gain alone does not give the required coarse budget. Let \(X_i\) be the fine support of occurrence \(i\), \(a_i\) its fine anchor, \(Y_i\) its allocated coarse support, and \(\bar{a}_i\) the parent of \(a_i\). Put \(q_i = \text{cov}_f (X_i)\) and \(q_i' = \text{cov}_c (Y_i)\) for their cover numbers. Suppose the incoming amplitudes satisfy

\(\displaystyle \sup_a \sum_{i : a_i = a} e^{Aq_i} \tau_i \leqslant E_{\text{in}}, \qquad A > 0.\) (9.41)

All sums use the given occurrence enumeration, including repeated supports. Fix a purchased derivative order \(n \geqslant 6\), spatial weight order \(p\), and allocation radius \(r_0\). The bounds through any smaller requested order follow by restriction. In particular, the degree-four attenuation used below requires the insertion regularity and moment cancellation of Subsection 9.5 through that degree. Here that cancellation follows from the defining incoming regular-family conditions; it is not an extra moment assumption. We give the argument on a nonwrapping support \(X\) anchored at \(o\), on a fine torus of side at least four. Write \(f\) for one inherited entry. It is local and gauge invariant, satisfies \(f (1) = 0\), is \(C^n\) in logarithmic coordinates, and its Hessian at the identity vanishes on pairs of affine link fields. These are the regular-family conditions preserved by the reset.

Put \(F = f \circ \exp\). Its first derivative at zero vanishes: constant gauge transformations make its restriction to each link an invariant linear functional on the simple Lie algebra, and hence zero on the Lie algebra, which is spanned by commutators. Use the germ \(\Psi (q, J)\) and the filtered spaces \(\mathcal{K}_a, \mathcal{M}_a\) of Subsection 7.11, with \(d = 4\). By locality and gauge invariance, \(F \circ \Psi\) is independent of \(q\). Consequently a mixed derivative vanishes if any argument has weight one: such an argument has no radial coefficient, since those coefficients start at weight two.

If every argument has weight at least two and their total weight is at most four, there are at most two arguments. With zero arguments the value is \(f (1) = 0\). With one argument the derivative is zero by \(DF (0) = 0\). With two arguments both weights equal two, and the second-order chain rule gives

\(\displaystyle D^2 (F \circ \Psi) (0) [u, v] = D^2 F (0) [D \Psi (0) u, D \Psi (0) v],\)

because the term containing \(DF (0)\) vanishes. The two image directions lie in \(\mathcal{M}_2\) by (7.75); they agree on \(X\) with affine link fields. Locality and the incoming affine-Hessian condition kill this term too. Thus every derivative of \(F \circ \Psi\) of total weight at most four vanishes.

To transfer this assertion back to \(F\), lift each polynomial direction of weight \(a_i\) through the surjection (7.74) and induct on the number of arguments. In the partition form of the chain rule, the singleton partition is the desired derivative of \(F\). Every other partition has fewer blocks; its block directions lie in the spaces with weights equal to the sums of the corresponding \(a_i\), by (7.75). The induction hypothesis kills those terms. The composite derivative was just shown to vanish, so the singleton term vanishes. Finally locality allows the directions to be screened to \(X\), by differentiating \(F = F \circ \text{screen}_X\). This proves precisely (7.72) through degree four for the inherited entry. Wrapping supports use the long-support estimate below and do not require this cancellation. The fine side-size condition follows in the application from the coarse-side bound and the positive block ratio. Let \(t \geqslant 0\) be the full requested coarse support exponent, including any reserve needed later.

The reference attenuation proof in Subsection 9.5 applies to each forest and tag. In its notation the pointwise derivative bound is

\(\displaystyle B_0 \tau_i d_L (q_i) \{1 + \rho_i^{2 n} (1 +\|z\|)^{4 n} \}, \qquad d_L (q) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} L^{- 5}, & q^3 < L,\\ 1, & q^3 \geqslant L. \end{array} \right.\)

Here \(B_0\) is fixed before \(L\). The same local Gaussian moment estimate bounds the integral of the polynomial factor. More precisely, write \(H = (r_0 + 48) (1 + B)\), where \(B\) is the scale radius. There are fixed integers \(a, v\) such that

\(\displaystyle \rho_i \leqslant K_L (\theta_f + g / \theta_f) [H (1 + q_i)]^a, \quad \int (1 +\|z\|)^{4 n} \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma \leqslant M_L [H (1 + q_i)]^v .\)

The fine and coarse thresholds here have the bounded ratio required by that proof. With \(h = 2 na + v\), impose the explicit post-\(L\) condition

\(\displaystyle [K_L (\theta_f + g / \theta_f)]^{2 n} M_L H^h \leqslant 1.\)

It is available by the threshold and monitor argument proved there; no negative power rate is assigned to \(g / \theta_f\). Integration yields \(B_0 \tau_i d_L (q_i) \{1 + (1 + q_i)^h \}\). Since

\(\displaystyle d_L (q) \leqslant L^{- 5} (1 + q)^{15},\)

including when \(q^3 \geqslant L\), the reference bound is at most \(2 B_0 \tau_i L^{- 5} (1 + q_i)^{15 + h}\).

The support compression estimate gives, provided \(20 t \hspace{0.17em} t_{r_0} \leqslant LA\),

\(\displaystyle tq_i' \leqslant t \hspace{0.17em} t_{r_0} + \tfrac{1}{2} Aq_i .\)

Choose \(R\) so that \((1 + q)^{15 + h} \leqslant Re^{Aq / 2}\) for \(q \geqslant 0\). Such an \(R\) exists by polynomial versus exponential growth and depends only on the displayed pre-\(L\) data. Thus for \(C' = 1 + 2 B_0 Re^{tt_{r_0}}\), the weighted reference bound is at most

\(\displaystyle e^{tq_i'} s_i \leqslant C' L^{- 5} e^{Aq_i} \tau_i .\) (9.42)

A finite average over forests and tags whose sum of absolute weights is at most one preserves this estimate. This is the actual fixed average used in the singleton construction; no new Gaussian marginal or smaller-period replacement is introduced.

Each coarse site has exactly \(L^4\) fine sites above it in four dimensions. For a coarse site \(\bar{a}\), summing (9.42) first over each fine anchor above \(\bar{a}\) and then over those \(L^4\) anchors proves

\(\displaystyle \sup_{\bar{a}} \sum_{i : \bar{a}_i = \bar{a}} e^{tq_i'} s_i \leqslant (C' / L) E_{\text{in}} .\) (9.43)

The factor \(L^4\) is essential. It is paid by the fifth power in the per-occurrence attenuation.

9.7Extra decay from the degree of a cluster

After extracting the singleton reference, many terms can be paid without a further subtraction. 9.7.1Grading the elementary activities

Write \(\beta_+ (x) = \beta_F (G, x, L)\) for the shifted fine coupling and set

\(\displaystyle D (x) = \beta_+ (x)^{3 / 8} .\)

Eventually \(D \geqslant 1\). Give the six inherited logarithmic species the following additional grades:

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c|rrrrrr} \text{species} & \text{regular} & \text{cubic} & \text{quartic} & \text{quadratic loop} & \text{fine tail} & \text{coarse tail}\\ \hline h & 1 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 & 2. \end{array}\)

These are extra grades; a further factor \(D\) per occurrence will be inserted at the connected-component stage.

Subsection 8.24 supplies the required full-support site bounds. Fix \(- 1 / 2 < t < - 3 / 8\), and choose \(s\) strictly between \(- 1 / 2\) and \(t\). The ungraded regular, quartic and loop bounds can each be bounded by \(\beta_+^{- 15 / 16}\) eventually. Multiplication by \(D\) changes this exponent to \(- 9 / 16 < s\). The cubic bound has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\) and is bounded by \(\beta_+^s\). Both true path tails can be bounded by \(\beta_+^{- 2}\); multiplication by \(D^2\) changes this to \(- 5 / 4 < s\). The six bounds therefore sum to at most \(6 \beta_+^s \leqslant \beta_+^t\) eventually. These inequalities preserve the original supports and all length charges. The forced inherited family retains every negative upper exponent and receives no extra grade.

The same argument applies to the local derivatives. Hold the original insertion fixed in the compensated coordinates as in Subsection 8.24. Its transported cutoff has jet scale bounded by an upper-exponent-zero function times the full cover plus length charge. The coarse tail has an additional varying coarse-field term; its own jet bound has upper exponent \(- 2\), and so the factor \(D^2\) again leaves exponent \(- 5 / 4\). Adding this term to its old bound preserves the common bound \(\beta_+^t\) by first taking the intermediate exponent \(s\). The cutoff product rule only changes the upper-exponent-zero jet scale.

For the other optional letters, assign grade zero to the quadratic local term and grade two to the remaining small correction family. Their ungraded bounds are, respectively, of upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\) and at most \(- 2\). The density and exponential jet estimates of Subsection 8.28 therefore preserve a common upper exponent \(t\) after the grades are inserted. More explicitly, if \(a_i\) is a nonnegative logarithmic amplitude and its jet scale is \(b_i\), the full-support site bound implies \(a_i \leqslant 1\) eventually: choose a root in its nonempty support, bound this single nonnegative summand by the site sum, and drop its weights, all at least one. Since \(a_i^q \leqslant a_i\), this bounds every jet through order \(n\) by \(C_n a_i \max (1, b_i)^n\). The scale \(b_i\) retains its full cover and length charges; only its scalar prefactor has upper exponent zero. The polynomial charge factor is paid by the reserve in the next paragraph. Give warning letters grade two and forced letters grade zero. The joint warning probability can be purchased with any fixed negative upper exponent per warning. For example, choose upper exponent \(- 5\); its square root, multiplied by \(D^2 = \beta_+^{3 / 4}\), has upper exponent \(- 7 / 4 < t\). Cauchy–Schwarz then retains one such purchased bound for each warning, without requiring independence of their events. Thus the grading is compatible with the actual Gaussian derivative majorants, not only their zeroth-order values.

The passage from graded letter bounds to differentiated component integrals uses the common random field. For a component \(P\), take \(I\) to be the full set of fine bonds read by its enlarged union support, and let \(J\) be its joint warning event. For the Gaussian coordinates \(z_{b, c}\) indexed by bond and colour, define

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{F}_I (z) = 1 + |I| + |I \times \mathcal{C}| + \sum_{(b, c) \in I \times \mathcal{C}} |z_{b, c} |,\)

where \(\mathcal{C}\) is the finite colour index set. The product-rule bound has the form

\(\displaystyle A_P \boldsymbol{1}_J \mathcal{F}_I^{kj}, \qquad A_P = \prod_{i \in P} \alpha_i \hspace{0.17em} V (x) (1 + S (P))^j,\)

where \(\alpha_i\) are the nonnegative elementary packet and letter bounds, \(V \geqslant 0\) has upper exponent zero, and \(S (P)\) is the full footprint charge. 9.7.2Recovering the footprint weight in the first expansion

We state the needed positive-weight calculation explicitly. For an elementary label \(i\), let \(X_i\) be its support, \(u_i \geqslant 0\) its bound, and \(e_i \geqslant 0\) its additional length charge. For a finite connected set \(P\) put

\(\displaystyle C (P) = c \left( \bigcup_{i \in P} X_i \right), \qquad S (P) = \sum_{i \in P} (c (X_i) + e_i) .\)

Here \(c\) is the connected cover number at the current scale. The cover subadditivity along a spanning tree gives \(C (P) \leqslant \sum_i c (X_i)\). Fix \(a \geqslant 0\), \(\epsilon > 0\) and integer \(k \geqslant 0\). If the elementary site estimate is available with exponent \(a + 2 \epsilon\) on cover and \(2 \epsilon\) on the length charge, the first tree estimate yields its rooted connected sum. It also pays the stronger summand

\(\displaystyle \boldsymbol{1}_{\{z \in \bigcup X_i \}} \prod_{i \in P} u_i \hspace{0.17em} e^{aC (P)} (1 + S (P))^k e^{\epsilon S (P)} .\)

Indeed

\(\displaystyle (1 + S)^k \leqslant e^{\epsilon} k! \epsilon^{- k} e^{\epsilon S}, \qquad S \geqslant 0,\)

by the exponential series at \(\epsilon (1 + S)\). Together with cover subadditivity this bounds the displayed summand by \(e^{\epsilon} k! \epsilon^{- k}\) times the summand already paid by the elementary site estimate. The same \(\epsilon\) therefore pays both the retained full-footprint exponential and the polynomial moment; we have used two copies of its reserve, not discarded either cost.

Apply this calculation to the mixed elementary alphabet of Subsection 8.2. Its mandatory packet weights already include their seed payment, while its optional letters have the grades above. For a component \(P\), let \(h (P)\) be the sum of its extra grades, \(|P|\) its number of elementary occurrences, \(m (P)\) its number of mandatory fine-cell, coarse-bond and flagged-site seeds, and \(S (P)\) its full footprint charge. The seed count includes all three types; cluster multiplicities are retained in the next expansion. The strengthened component multiplier is

\(\displaystyle D^{h (P) + |P|} \exp \{Mm (P) + \epsilon S (P)\},\) (9.45)

where \(M \geqslant 0\) is the seed mark. This factor is the product of the elementary factors \(D^{1 + h_i} e^{Mm_i}\) and the retained footprint factor. Multiplying every elementary bound by \(D\) multiplies its site budget by \(D\). Consequently the first expansion is valid if

\(\displaystyle \frac{4 b_B Dr_0 (x)}{\delta_0} \leqslant \frac{1}{2},\) (9.46)

where \(r_0\) and \(\delta_0 > 0\) are the original elementary root bound and its reserve, and \(b_B = (2 B + 1)^4\). The selected root bound \(r_0\) has upper exponent \(t < - 3 / 8\), as fixed above; hence \(Dr_0\) has negative upper exponent and this inequality holds eventually. Its component root bound is \(D \omega (x)\), where \(\omega\) is the original component bound including the footprint polynomial just proved. All these statements require the graded elementary site bound on the same incoming representation.

9.7.3The second expansion and its degree gain

Let \(j\) be the field derivative order, and let \(B_1 (x) \geqslant 0\) be the upper-exponent-zero constant from the component derivative estimates. A convenient second-expansion root bound is

\(\displaystyle \sigma (x) = (1 + 2^j B_1 (x)) \omega (x) .\)

Choose a target support exponent \(a \geqslant 0\) and a reserve \(\eta > 0\) with \(a + 2 \eta\) no larger than the available component exponent. In addition to (9.46), impose

\(\displaystyle \frac{4 b_B D \sigma (x)}{\eta} \leqslant \frac{1}{2} .\) (9.47)

The cluster estimate of Subsection 8.1 now applies to the strengthened component weights, with root bound \(D \sigma\) and tail ratio \(1 / 2\).

For a cluster \(\Gamma = (P_1, \ldots, P_q)\), counted with its actual occurrence multiplicities, define

\(\displaystyle d (\Gamma) = \sum_{r = 1}^q \{h (P_r) + |P_r |\}, \quad m (\Gamma) = \sum_r m (P_r), \quad s (\Gamma) = \sum_r S (P_r) .\)

Its strengthened weight is its old positive cluster majorant times

\(\displaystyle D^{d (\Gamma)} e^{Mm (\Gamma) + \epsilon s (\Gamma)} .\)

This identity follows by multiplying (9.45) over the cluster occurrences; repeated labels are counted at each occurrence. Suppose

\(\displaystyle D^3 \leqslant e^M, \qquad D^3 \leqslant e^{\epsilon T}, \qquad T \geqslant 0.\)

For any cluster satisfying at least one of

\(\displaystyle d (\Gamma) \geqslant 3, \qquad m (\Gamma) \geqslant 1, \qquad s (\Gamma) \geqslant T,\) (9.48)

the strengthened multiplier is at least \(D^3\). Every other factor is at least one. Its unstrengthened bound is therefore bounded by \(D^{- 3}\) times the strengthened bound.

Spatially weighted derivatives of order at most \(j\), with spatial power \(p\), cost \([\ell (x) (1 + C (\Gamma))]^{pj}\), where \(\ell (x) = 4 (2 B (x) + 1)\). The same exponential-series inequality pays this polynomial from the reserve \(\eta\), at cost

\(\displaystyle H_{\eta} (x) = \ell (x)^{pj} e^{\eta} (pj) ! \eta^{- pj} .\)

Using the cluster root bound at exponent \(a + \eta\), then removing this reserve, proves the site estimate at exponent \(a\)

\(\displaystyle \sup_z \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} \Gamma : z \in \text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} \Gamma\\ \Gamma \text{ satisfies } \textrm{(\hyperref[eq:degree-large-class]{9.48})} \end{array}$}}} \| \Phi_{\Gamma} \|_{j, p} e^{aC (\Gamma)} \leqslant D^{- 3} H_{\eta} (x) \frac{D \sigma (x)}{1 - 1 / 2} .\) (9.49)

Here \(\Phi_{\Gamma}\) denotes the cluster contribution and the norm is its local spatially weighted jet bound. Restricting a nonnegative sum to the displayed class is legitimate without analyticity of a field-dependent classification: the class depends only on labels, supports and charges. Uniform forest averaging preserves the bound.

9.7.4Paying an arbitrary fraction of the forecast

We check the exponents and the order of choices. Suppose the unstrengthened site bound is

\(\displaystyle H_i (x) \frac{\sigma (x)}{1 - 1 / 2} \leqslant \beta_+ (x)^{\varphi}, \qquad \varphi < - 3 / 8,\) (9.50)

for some \(i > 0\) with \(a + 2 i \leqslant E\), where \(E\) is the available component exponent. This receipt follows from the unstrengthened component construction when its upper exponent \(q\) satisfies \(q < \varphi\): \(B_1\) and \(H_i\) have upper exponent zero, \(\omega\) has upper exponent \(q\), and therefore the entire left side has upper exponent \(q\). Taking the reserve \(i = (E - a) / 2\) also makes its ordinary degree bound without the factor \(D\) at most \(1 / 2\) eventually, since \(q < 0\). The same graded local construction must supply the actual component majorants; the scalar exponent calculation alone does not construct them. Put \(\eta = (E - a) / 2\). Then \(0 < i \leqslant \eta\) and, writing \(r = pj\), we have

\(\displaystyle \eta^{- r} r! \leqslant i^{- r} r!, \qquad H_{\eta} (x) \leqslant e^{\eta} H_i (x) .\)

Also \(H_i (x) \geqslant \eta^{- r} r! > 0\), since \(\ell \geqslant 1\) and \(e^i \geqslant 1\). Thus (9.50) bounds \(\sigma\) by a fixed constant times \(\beta_+^{\varphi}\). Since \(b_B\) has upper exponent zero and \(3 / 8 + \varphi < 0\), it implies (9.47) at this fixed reserve for all large \(x\). The first condition follows in exactly the same way from the strictly smaller than \(- 3 / 8\) exponent of \(r_0\).

The seed mark and the large-footprint threshold obey \(e^{- M}\) and \(e^{- \epsilon T}\) estimates with every negative upper exponent, so the two inequalities \(D^3 \leqslant e^M, e^{\epsilon T}\) hold eventually as well. Substituting (9.50) into (9.49) gives

\(\displaystyle e^{\eta} D^{- 2} \beta_+^{\varphi} = e^{\eta} \beta_+^{\varphi - 3 / 4} .\)

The incoming regular forecast is \(\delta_E (x) = x^{- 11 / 10}\). Since

\(\displaystyle \varphi - \frac{3}{4} + \frac{11}{10} = \varphi + \frac{7}{20} < 0,\)

the last bound is at most \(c \delta_E (x)\) for any prescribed \(c > 0\) after increasing the entrance coupling. The comparison uses \(\beta_+ (x) \geqslant x\) and the negative exponent of \(\delta_E\).

For each stage only finitely many transitions and fixed derivative orders occur. First take the maximum of their block-size requirements; then take the maximum of their coupling thresholds and the previous entrance value. This yields one increasing collection of entrance requirements. Enlarging them preserves all the inequalities above. It does not require a uniform threshold over infinitely many derivative orders. The estimates for the remaining degree-one and degree-two classes, and the identities which attach their subtractions to the renormalized path, are separate steps.

9.8Comparing the local factors at neighboring couplings

We now prove the compensated comparison used in the fixed-volume continuity argument. The important simplification is to preserve the physical fine field while changing the Gaussian coordinate. Thus the incoming measurable activity is evaluated at the same point in the two representations. Only its value difference, and not a derivative of that activity, is needed.

Fix a derivative order \(j \in \mathbb{N}\) and a fine torus. For a scalar function of the coarse variation \(y\), write

\(\displaystyle \|f\|_{j, y_0} = \max_{0 \leqslant r \leqslant j} \|D^r f (y_0)\| .\)

All derivatives below are taken on a common admissible local chart, with its fixed normalized directions. In particular, the assertions include existence and continuity of these derivatives near \(y_0\). We use the product estimate

\(\displaystyle \|fh\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant 3^j \|f\|_{j, y_0} \|h\|_{j, y_0} .\) (9.51)

The Leibniz formula has \(2^r\) terms at order \(r\), so the slightly larger factor \(3^j\) is valid also for \(j = 0\).

The base representation supplies summable elementary bounds \(u_i \geqslant 0\), with \(B = \sum_i u_i < \infty\), for its compensated factors \(w_i\). All logarithms here are real. Assume explicitly a common base value bound \(| \ell_i (y_0) | \leqslant M\) for their real exponents; in the construction this follows from the summable incoming logarithmic bounds and the held-field estimate. A bound on \(e^{\ell_i} - 1\) alone would not control a large negative \(\ell_i\). We also use the following local estimates already required by the compensated construction: uniform jets of its enforcement cutoffs; a bound \(As\) for the packet action jets on every packet of size \(s\) whose cutoff is not identically zero near \(y_0\); and, for its density compensation \(K_I\) and quadratic exponent \(q_I\), for every subset \(I\) of read bonds,

\(\displaystyle \|K\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_0 F_0 (z)^N, \qquad \|q\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_0 F_0 (z)^2 .\) (9.52)

Here \(N \in \mathbb{N}\), \(C_0 \geqslant 0\), and \(F_0 (z) = 1 + d + \sum_{r = 1}^d |z_r | \geqslant 1\) uses the full finite Gaussian coordinate space. The constants \(N, C_0\) are common to every such \(I\); we suppress that index in the formulas. These estimates must hold for the same base representation, forests and compensation maps. Their uniform purchase in the scale construction is a separate requirement; the comparison below uses their actual values at the fixed stage.

Let \(R'\) be a neighboring representation with the same dictionaries, paths and masks as \(R\), and let \(t > 0\) bound its input differences as in the fixed-volume continuity argument. Put \(\lambda = g (\beta) / g (\beta')\). The identity \(g (\beta') \lambda = g (\beta)\) implies that the fine field at \((R', \lambda z)\) is precisely the fine field at \((R, z)\). It also gives \(\beta \lambda^2 = \beta'\) and makes the compensated noise course for \(R'\) equal to \(\lambda\) times the course for \(R\).

9.8.1Elementary exponential comparisons

Two elementary facts explain the logarithmic and packet estimates. First, suppose \(\|e^f - 1\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant u\), \(u \leqslant B\), and \(|f (y_0) | \leqslant M\). Then

\(\displaystyle \|f\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_{j, M, B} u.\) (9.53)

Indeed \(f = \log (1 + (e^f - 1))\). The derivatives of \(v \mapsto \log (1 + v)\) are bounded on \([e^{- M} - 1, e^M - 1]\). In each positive-order chain-rule term there is at least one derivative of \(e^f - 1\). A product of \(r\) such derivatives is bounded by \(u^r \leqslant u \max (1, B)^{r - 1}\). At order zero, the mean value theorem between \(0\) and \(e^{f (y_0)} - 1\) gives the same linear bound. The chain rule is used on the open interval \(v > - 1\), so no assertion of a fixed neighborhood contained in that compact interval is needed.

Second, if \(\|e^f - 1\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant B\) and \(\|h\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant v \leqslant D\), then

\(\displaystyle \|e^{f + h} - e^f \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_{j, B, D} v.\) (9.54)

Use \(e^{f + h} - e^f = e^f (e^h - 1)\). The zero-order estimate for the second factor is \(|e^{h (y_0)} - 1| \leqslant e^D |h (y_0) |\). For positive orders, the partition formula for derivatives of \(e^h\) gives finitely many products of derivatives of \(h\), each containing at least one factor. Thus \(\|e^h - 1\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_{j, D} v\), by \(v^r \leqslant v \max (1, D)^{r - 1}\). Apply (9.51) and \(\|e^f \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant B + 1\).

9.8.2The four elementary kinds of factor

We compare every neighboring factor after the noise rescaling. For a packet \(s\), write its base compensated factor as \(w_s\). The exact ratio identity is

\(\displaystyle w_s' - w_s = w_s (e^{H_s} - 1), \qquad H_s = - (\beta' - \beta) E_s - n_s \{\log m (\beta') - \log m (\beta)\} .\)

Here \(E_s\) is its compensated action including the fixed far-field term, and \(n_s\) is the number of chart normalizers in the packet. The restrictions deciding whether the packet occurs are the same in both representations. If its enforcement cutoff vanishes on a neighborhood of \(y_0\), both germs vanish. Otherwise the packet action estimate applies. Let \(s_{\ast}\) be the maximum packet size and let \(m_{\ast}\) be the total number of fine bonds. Both are finite on this torus. Then, with \(Y = 1 + As_{\ast} + m_{\ast}\),

\(\displaystyle \|H_s \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant tY, \qquad \|w_s \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant B.\)

Choose \(t \leqslant Y^{- 1}\). The exponential comparison and product rule give \(\|w_s' - w_s \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_{\text{p}} t\), uniformly over the finite packet catalogue and all its admissible auxiliary choices.

For a forced incoming factor, the held fine field is the same and the enforcement cutoff is the same. Its value difference is at most \(b_i\), and its cutoff has a common jet bound \(C_{\text{e}}\). Hence its factor difference has jet bound \(C_{\text{e}} b_i\). This differentiates only the cutoff, not the incoming forced activity.

For logarithmic factors write \(w_i = e^{\ell_i} - 1\). The bounded base logarithm and the summable base factor bounds give \(\| \ell_i \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant Cu_i\) by (9.53). The actual logarithmic catalogue has two cases. An inherited regular activity has logarithm minus its held value times the common enforcement cutoff; its logarithm difference therefore has jet bound \(C_{\text{e}} a_i\). Every other catalogue entry is either unchanged by the rescaling, or is multiplied by \(\lambda^2\). This follows term by term from the quadratic noise terms and \(\beta \lambda^2 = \beta'\); the path and loop terms without that quadratic scaling remain unchanged. Thus

\(\displaystyle \| \ell_i' - \ell_i \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C \{ \tilde{a}_i + tu_i \},\)

where \(\tilde{a}_i\) places the regular difference amplitudes at their inherited logarithmic addresses and is zero elsewhere. The address embedding is injective, so \(\sum_i \tilde{a}_i \leqslant t\). Since \(t \leqslant 1\) and \(u_i \leqslant B\), the right side is at most \(C (1 + B)\). Applying (9.54) gives

\(\displaystyle \|w_i' - w_i \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_{\text{l}} (\tilde{a}_i + tu_i) .\)

Finally, warning factors are exactly unchanged: their charge sets, forced supports and physical field agree. Their difference bound is zero.

There are finitely many packet labels, say \(n_{\ast}\). Combining the four cases gives nonnegative difference bounds \(v_i\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \|w_i' - w_i \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant v_i, \qquad \sum_i v_i \leqslant tK_{\ast}, \quad K_{\ast} = n_{\ast} C_{\text{p}} + C_{\text{e}} + C_{\text{l}} (1 + B) .\) (9.55)

The constants are uniform over the auxiliary choices at this fixed torus. This is stronger than continuity of each individual factor: the whole catalogue has a summable difference bound.

9.8.3Products over components

Set \(a_i = u_i + v_i / t\). Then \(\sum_i a_i \leqslant B + K_{\ast}\), \(\|w_i \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant a_i\), and \(\|w_i' - w_i \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant ta_i\). For every finite set of labels \(P\), put

\(\displaystyle p_P = \prod_{i \in P} 3 \hspace{0.17em} 3^j a_i .\)

For \(0 < t \leqslant 1\), induction on \(|P|\) yields

\(\displaystyle \left| \prod_{i \in P} w_i' \right|_{j, y_0} \leqslant p_P, \qquad \left| \prod_{i \in P} w_i' - \prod_{i \in P} w_i \right|_{j, y_0} \leqslant tp_P .\) (9.56)

For the induction step use \(\|w_i' \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant 2 a_i\) and

\(\displaystyle w_i' G' - w_i G = (w_i' - w_i) G' + w_i (G' - G) .\)

Each product costs at most \(3^j\); the chosen factor \(3 \hspace{0.17em} 3^j a_i\) pays both terms and the bound for the new product. The empty product has value one and difference zero.

Summing nonnegative products over finite subsets gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_P p_P \leqslant \exp \{3 \hspace{0.17em} 3^j (B + K_{\ast})\} .\) (9.57)

For a finite catalogue this is \(\prod_i (1 + 3 \hspace{0.17em} 3^j a_i)\) and \(1 + x \leqslant e^x\). Exhaustion and monotone convergence prove the countable case. Restricting to admissible molecules only decreases the sum. All supports and selection conditions agree under the common address identification.

We must identify these products with the actual component integrands. Assume both representations satisfy the incoming room and activity bounds for this same sector. The protected compensation map holds the physical fine field fixed on a neighborhood of \(y_0\). On that neighborhood, the definition of each component therefore equals the product of the elementary compensated factors with its fixed charge set. The neighboring compensation has the same protected patch: its noise course is multiplied by \(\lambda\), and its coupling cancels that factor in the physical field. Applying the same identity to the neighboring representation proves equality of its component germ with the primed product. Intersect the two neighborhoods. Health of the union of the assigned supports implies health of every factor support, because each is contained in that union. Thus all the bounds above apply to these actual germs, and their derivatives are the derivatives of the displayed finite products.

9.8.4The density compensation and the Gaussian ratio

The exact compensation identity has the anchored form

\(\displaystyle K' (y, \lambda z) = K (y, z) \exp \{(1 - \lambda^2) (q (y, z) - q (y_0, z))\} .\)

The subtraction at \(y_0\) is essential. Put \(h (y) = (1 - \lambda^2) (q (y, z) - q (y_0, z))\). Then \(h (y_0) = 0\), and every positive-order derivative through \(j\) is bounded by \(tCF_0 (z)^2\). In the partition formula for a derivative of \(e^h\) at \(y_0\), a term with \(r \geqslant 1\) blocks is bounded by \(t^r C^r F_0 (z)^{2 r}\). Since \(t \leqslant 1\) and \(F_0 \geqslant 1\), it is at most \(tC^r F_0^{2 j}\). The zero-order difference \(e^{h (y_0)} - 1\) is zero. Consequently, also when \(j = 0\),

\(\displaystyle \|e^h - 1\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant tC_j F_0 (z)^{2 j}, \qquad \|K' - K\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant tC_1 F_0 (z)^{N + 2 j} .\)

No exponential of an uncontrolled positive polynomial in \(z\) is introduced here.

Write \(g_P, g_P'\) for the component products in (9.56). With the fixed Gaussian density ratio \(J_{\lambda} (z)\), which is independent of \(y\), use the identity

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} J_{\lambda} K' g_P' - Kg_P = & J_{\lambda} (K' - K) g_P' + (J_{\lambda} - 1) Kg_P' + K (g_P' - g_P) . \end{array}\)

The preceding estimates and the product rule give

\(\displaystyle \|J_{\lambda} K' g_P' - Kg_P \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant tC_2 p_P F_0 (z)^{N + 2 j} \{J_{\lambda} + |J_{\lambda} - 1| / t + 1\} .\) (9.58)

Here \(C_2\) is fixed and \(\sum_P C_2 p_P < \infty\) by (9.57). This is the required local compensated comparison. The Gaussian estimates then integrate it with a common integrable dominator and preserve all jets through order \(j\).

9.8.5Packet values outside the healthy jet domain

For the forced output one also needs value estimates for packets outside the healthy domain used above. An upper bound on the packet energy itself is neither available nor needed. The actual packet has the form

\(\displaystyle P_{\beta} = m_0 \exp \{- \beta u + v_{\beta} \}, \qquad 0 \leqslant m_0 \leqslant M_0, \quad u \geqslant - d_0, \quad v_{\beta} \leqslant A_0,\)

with \(d_0, M_0 \geqslant 0\) fixed on this torus. The packet energy lower bound supplies \(d_0\); bounded cutoff and charge factors supply \(M_0\). The unchanged condensation and chart terms, and the finite number of normalizers, give \(|v_{\beta'} - v_{\beta} | \leqslant n_0 t\) for a finite \(n_0 \geqslant 0\). Assume \(| \beta' - \beta | \leqslant t \leqslant \min (1, \beta / 2)\) and put

\(\displaystyle c = \beta / 2 > 0, \qquad D = A_0 + n_0 + (\beta + 1) d_0 .\)

If \(- d_0 \leqslant u \leqslant 0\), both exponential arguments are at most \(D\), and their difference is at most \(t (d_0 + n_0)\). The mean value theorem gives a bound \(tm_0 e^D (d_0 + n_0)\). If \(u > 0\), both arguments are at most \(D - cu\) and their difference is at most \(t (u + n_0)\). Thus

\(\displaystyle |P_{\beta'} - P_{\beta} | \leqslant tm_0 e^D (u + n_0) e^{- cu} \leqslant tm_0 e^D (c^{- 1} + n_0),\)

using \(cu \leqslant e^{cu}\) and \(e^{- cu} \leqslant 1\). Combining both cases yields

\(\displaystyle |P_{\beta'} - P_{\beta} | \leqslant tM_0 e^D (d_0 + 2 n_0 + c^{- 1}) .\) (9.59)

Zero packet masks remain zero under the common data and require no estimate. This proves the packet value comparison uniformly over the unconstrained positive-energy tail. The corresponding forced product and Gaussian integration arguments use value bounds; they do not assert derivative estimates on that tail.

For completeness, the other unrestricted value factors have the same summable comparison. Let \(r_i \geqslant 0\) bound the base logarithms and put \(R_0 = \sum_i r_i < \infty\). Their differences are bounded by \(d_i = tr_i + \tilde{a}_i\), so \(\sum_i d_i \leqslant t (R_0 + 1)\). Both the base and new logarithms are at most \(2 R_0 + 1\) when \(t \leqslant 1\). The mean value theorem gives the exponential-factor difference bound \(e^{2 R_0 + 1} d_i\). Forced incoming factors have difference bound \(b_i\), since their common cutoff lies in \([0, 1]\); warnings cancel. Together with (9.59), the finite packet catalogue therefore has an overall elementary difference budget \(tC_3\).

The corresponding base value bounds are summable as well. Packets and warnings have finite catalogues. The logarithmic catalogue has its summable base bound, while the forced incoming bounds are bounded by their rooted weighted bounds. Every forced support is nonempty: it contains the parent of an inspected incoming inventory site. Summing the rooted bound over the finitely many torus sites therefore bounds the unrooted sum of forced bounds. The weights removed in this argument are at least one.

Apply the product argument above with \(j = 0\) to these value bounds. There are summable \(p_P^{\text{v}} \geqslant 0\) such that \(|G_P' | \leqslant p_P^{\text{v}}\) and \(|G_P' - G_P | \leqslant tp_P^{\text{v}}\) for the rescaled full component values. Before integration use

\(\displaystyle |J_{\lambda} G_P' - G_P | \leqslant |J_{\lambda} - 1| p_P^{\text{v}} + tp_P^{\text{v}} .\)

The Gaussian ratio estimate at moment order zero bounds the integral by \(tC_4 p_P^{\text{v}}\). Haar variables keep their normalized measure. The common support and charge data also identify all eligibility conditions. A normalized finite tag average preserves this bound; if a component is ineligible, both terms are zero. This proves the forced value comparison needed for the exponential expansion of the output array.

9.9Continuity of the nonlinear output on a fixed torus

The nonlinear step must vary continuously with its incoming coupling and activity array. This assertion concerns the actual array in a fixed support dictionary. Equality of the evaluated gas would be insufficient. The constants in this section may depend on the fixed torus; uniform small bounds for the scale construction are established separately.

9.9.1Which data are held fixed

Consider two incoming representations \(R, R'\) on the same fine torus. They have the same regular and forced addresses, thresholds, clipping convention and path data. Write \(\beta, \beta' > 0\) for their couplings. Their regular and forced activity differences have nonnegative amplitudes \(a_i, b_i\) with

\(\displaystyle \sum_i a_i \leqslant t, \qquad \sum_i b_i \leqslant t.\)

The regular differences here are differences of the actual masked occurrences. Their supports and healthy masks agree exactly. Let \(g (\beta) = \beta^{- 1 / 2}\) and put \(\lambda = g (\beta) / g (\beta') > 0\). The remaining comparison conditions are

\(\displaystyle | \lambda^2 - 1| \leqslant t, \quad | \beta' - \beta | \leqslant t, \quad | \log m (\beta') - \log m (\beta) | \leqslant t, \qquad 0 < t \leqslant \min (1 / 4, \beta / 2),\) (9.60)

where \(m (\beta) > 0\) is the fixed local Haar-chart normalizing factor. All forests, tags, component labels and output address identifications are retained. In particular, their compatibility and eligibility conditions agree under the identity on elementary labels.

For a continuous incoming curve these conditions hold locally for arbitrarily small \(t\). The activity conditions follow from its array continuity; multiplication by the fixed healthy mask preserves the value difference and makes both occurrences zero off that mask. The scalar conditions follow from continuity of the positive coupling, its inverse square root, and the logarithm of \(m\). This observation uses the same path throughout the curve.

9.9.2A Gaussian rescaling estimate

We explain why changing the coupling does not require differentiating the incoming measurable activities. On one fixed Gaussian space of dimension \(d\), with positive precision matrix \(Q\), put

\(\displaystyle p (z) = c_Q e^{- z^T Qz / 2}, \qquad J_{\lambda} (z) = \lambda^d \frac{p (\lambda z)}{p (z)} .\)

Thus, if \(C\) denotes the Gaussian covariance, \(Q = C^{- 1}\); the two matrices are kept distinct throughout this comparison. Then for every integrable function \(F\),

\(\displaystyle \int F (z) \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_Q (z) = \int J_{\lambda} (z) F (\lambda z) \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_Q (z) .\) (9.61)

This is the ordinary change of variables \(w = \lambda z\). The Haar factor, when present, is left unchanged. Since \(g (\beta') \lambda = g (\beta)\), the rescaled incoming field has the same physical noise; the corresponding compensation map keeps the original insertion values fixed.

For \(| \lambda^2 - 1| \leqslant t \leqslant 1 / 4\), we have \(\lambda \geqslant 1 / 2\) and \(| \log \lambda | \leqslant t\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle | \log J_{\lambda} (z) | \leqslant t \{d + \tfrac{1}{2} \|Q\| \hspace{0.17em} |z|^2 \} .\)

Let \(F_0 (z) = 1 + d + \sum_{r = 1}^d |z_r |\) and choose a finite constant \(C_Q\) such that the braces are at most \(C_Q F_0 (z)^2\). The inequality \(|u - 1| \leqslant (1 + u) | \log u|\) for \(u > 0\) follows by integrating \(e^s\) between \(0\) and \(\log u\). Thus

\(\displaystyle |J_{\lambda} - 1| \leqslant tC_Q (1 + J_{\lambda}) F_0^2 .\)

All polynomial moments of \(F_0\) are finite, by the finite Gaussian moment argument. If \(M_k = \int F_0^k \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_Q\), then (9.61) and \(F_0 (\lambda^{- 1} z) \leqslant 2 F_0 (z)\) give

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rll} \int J_{\lambda} F_0^k \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_Q & \leqslant 2^k M_k, \int |J_{\lambda} - 1| F_0^k \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_Q & \leqslant tC_Q (1 + 2^{k + 2}) M_{k + 2} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(9.62)} \end{array}\)

The same estimates hold for the full finite collection of Gaussian read coordinates and the product with normalized Haar measure. No smaller marginal is substituted when the parameter changes.

Here is the precise pointwise input needed to integrate local jet differences. Fix integers \(j, k \geqslant 0\). For every component \(P\), suppose its compensated integrands \(f_P, f_P'\) have, on each admissible local variation, the bound

\(\displaystyle \|J_{\lambda} f_P' (\cdot, \lambda z) - f_P (\cdot, z) \|_{J^j} \leqslant tc_P F_0 (z)^k \{J_{\lambda} (z) + t^{- 1} |J_{\lambda} (z) - 1| + 1\}, \qquad \sum_P c_P \leqslant C.\) (9.63)

Here \(c_P \geqslant 0\). The constants and \(k\) are common to the neighboring representation and to the forests and tags on this fixed torus. This is the pointwise compensated comparison estimate, a stronger input than individual boundedness of the two integrands. Its role is explicit: (9.62) gives uniform integral bounds. For a pointwise dominator, use

\(\displaystyle J_{\lambda} (z) \leqslant (5 / 4)^{d / 2} \exp (z^T Qz / 8) .\)

Together with \(t^{- 1} |J_{\lambda} - 1| \leqslant C_Q (1 + J_{\lambda}) F_0^2\), this bounds the integrand after division by \(t\) by a fixed polynomial times \(1 + \exp (z^T Qz / 8)\). Multiplying by the Gaussian density leaves a decaying exponential \(\exp (- 3 z^T Qz / 8)\), so this dominator is integrable. Integration and the derivative identities of Subsection 8.26 give

\(\displaystyle \|F_P' - F_P \|_{J^j} \leqslant tC_1 c_P, \qquad \sum_P C_1 c_P \leqslant C_1 C.\) (9.64)

For example one may take \(C_1 = (1 + 2^k) M_k + C_Q (1 + 2^{k + 2}) M_{k + 2}\). The same bound survives normalized forest and tag averaging and the common local extension, since their germs agree on the healthy domain. The construction of (9.63) from the individual logarithmic, packet and compensation factors remains a separate local estimate; it is not inferred merely from continuity of the scalar coupling.

9.9.3A summable difference through the cluster logarithm

We prove the summation step rather than interchange an infinite series with a limit without a common bound. Write \(u_P \geqslant 0\) for component jet bounds. Fix a target support exponent \(a \geqslant 0\); the cluster comparison itself also holds for arbitrary real \(a\). Suppose the ordinary cluster criterion holds for \(2^j u_P\), with reserve \(\delta > 0\), root bound \(\gamma > 0\) and ratio \(1 / 2\). Suppose component differences have bounds \(td_P\), where \(d_P \geqslant 0\) and \(\sum_P d_P \leqslant C\). On the fixed torus let

\(\displaystyle K_0 = \sum_{Y \subseteq \Lambda} e^{(a + \delta) c (Y)}, \qquad \kappa = \frac{\gamma}{2 K_0 2^j (1 + C)} > 0.\)

The finite sum includes every possible support. For every root, the additional vertex budget from \(2^j \kappa d_P\) is at most \(K_0 2^j \kappa C \leqslant \gamma / 2\). The augmented bounds \(2^j (u_P + \kappa d_P)\) therefore satisfy the cluster criterion with root bound \(3 \gamma / 2\) and ratio \(3 / 4\): the degree quantity is linear in the root bound. This chooses one neighborhood size \(\kappa\) after the torus and old criterion are fixed.

For \(0 \leqslant t \leqslant \kappa\), set \(\theta = t / \kappa \leqslant 1\). The finite product identity gives, in the joint jet bounds,

\(\displaystyle \left| \prod_{r = 1}^q F_{P_r}' - \prod_{r = 1}^q F_{P_r} \right|_{J^j} \leqslant \theta (1 + q)^j \left\{ \prod_{r = 1}^q (u_{P_r} + \kappa d_{P_r}) - \prod_{r = 1}^q u_{P_r} \right\} .\) (9.65)

To verify it, expand each \(F_P' = F_P + (F_P' - F_P)\). Every nonempty subset of difference factors contains at least one factor \(\theta\); its higher powers are bounded by \(\theta\). At derivative order \(r\) there are \(q^r\) assignments to the factors, bounded by \((1 + q)^j\). Summing the nonempty subsets gives exactly the difference of the positive products on the right. Repeated component labels are still distinct positions in this calculation.

Since \(1 + q \leqslant 2^q\) for \(q \geqslant 1\), the derivative factor is paid by \(2^j\) per component. Multiply by the absolute Ursell coefficient and its factorial normalization, then use the augmented cluster criterion. Summing rooted bounds over all \(| \Lambda |\) sites bounds the unrooted sum, because every nonzero cluster has nonempty support. Thus cluster differences have nonnegative bounds \(te_{\Gamma}\) with

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\Gamma} e_{\Gamma} \leqslant \kappa^{- 1} | \Lambda | \frac{3 \gamma / 2}{1 - 3 / 4} .\)

This estimate uses the original cluster criterion with a paid perturbation margin; no new uniform small-bound theorem for the neighboring representation has been assumed.

9.9.4Forced products and the output array

The same product calculation applies to the forced exponential expansion. If its component values have summable bounds \(h_i\) and differences bounded by \(td_i\), with \(0 \leqslant t \leqslant 1\), then a finite multiplicity vector \(\nu\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \left| \prod_i \frac{(F_i')^{\nu_i}}{\nu_i !} - \prod_i \frac{F_i^{\nu_i}}{\nu_i !} \right| \leqslant t \prod_i \frac{(h_i + d_i)^{\nu_i}}{\nu_i !} .\)

Indeed apply the same difference-of-products argument to repeated factors and divide by their positive factorials. Summing over all finite multiplicity vectors yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\nu} \prod_i \frac{(h_i + d_i)^{\nu_i}}{\nu_i !} = \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( \sum_i (h_i + d_i) \right),\)

by finite products of the exponential series followed by monotone convergence. Compatibility or eligibility conditions only remove terms; they agree under the fixed address identification. This proves a summable forced difference bound.

The remaining fixed path terms are affine in the coupling. If \(S_i (\beta)\) is such a term, then \(S_i (\beta) - S_i (\beta') = (\beta - \beta') \{S_i (1) - S_i (0)\}\). Their two summable derivative bounds therefore bound the difference by \(t\) times a summable array. A finite symmetry average preserves summability after its explicit fixed derivative factor. Any support weight \(e^{Ac (Y)}\) on this fixed torus is at most \(e^{A | \Lambda |}\), and moving an anchor costs at most a fixed power of \(1 + \text{diam} \hspace{0.17em} \Lambda\). Hence all these operations convert the preceding sums into the prescribed regular array budget in the jet norm and forced array budget in the value supremum norm, bounded by \(Kt\) for a finite \(K\) depending on the base representation and torus.

This also supplies the common majorant needed along a subsequence. If input parameters \(x_n\) tend to \(x\), select an increasing subsequence \(n_k\) for which the output differences from \(x\) have summable nonnegative bounds \(d_{k, i}\) with \(\sum_i d_{k, i} \leqslant 2^{- k}\). If \(h_i\) majorizes the output at \(x\), put

\(\displaystyle H_i = h_i + \sum_k d_{k, i} .\)

Tonelli's theorem gives \(\sum_i H_i < \infty\), and the triangle inequality shows that \(H_i\) bounds both the output at \(x\) and all outputs on this subsequence, component by component in the required jet or value norm. The regular and forced arrays are treated separately in this construction. It proves the required continuity and subsequential domination in the common dictionary, conditional on the local compensated difference estimate (9.63) and its forced counterpart.

9.10Supplying the common comparison estimates

The preceding comparison arguments use one collection of local bounds for the base representation. We show how the local estimates already proved provide that collection, and how its parameters are chosen before a running representation is inserted. Constants in a fixed-volume continuity estimate may depend on that volume. The radius of the allowed coarse variations and the large-coupling threshold used for the construction have a stronger uniformity.

Fix the branch data, a positive target support reserve, the derivative order \(j\), the cutoff convention, and positive width and packet-energy parameters. The scale factor is taken above the common floor in the local activity and Gaussian estimates. Fix a coarse variation scale \(v (x) \geqslant 0\) of upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). All estimates in this section refer to the same fine representation, sector, tags and held-field compensation.

9.10.1Elementary factors and conditional packet actions

The construction in Subsection 8.28 provides bounds for the elementary compensated factors together with their support-dependent derivative scales. At the fixed derivative order, choose a positive spare support and extra-cost reserve \(\delta\). If \(s\) denotes the support-cover plus length charge, then \((1 + s)^j \leqslant C_{j, \delta} e^{\delta s}\) for \(s \geqslant 0\): the continuous function \((1 + s)^j e^{- \delta s}\) is bounded on \([0, \infty)\). Apply the weighted estimate with this reserve before removing the weights. This pays the factor \(r (x)^j (1 + s)^j\) from the derivative scale and gives scale-one jet bounds at every order through \(j\). The warning catalogue is finite on the fixed torus and its finite jet bounds can also be summed. To pass from a rooted weighted sum to its unweighted sum on a fixed torus, sum the rooted inequality over all its sites. Every nonzero factor has nonempty assigned support and is counted at least once; all removed support and extra-cost weights are at least one. The same argument applies to the base logarithmic bounds. It gives both the finite total elementary bound and the finite common bound on the absolute values of the real logarithms used in Subsection 9.8. The bounds need not be small after this volume-dependent summation.

There is a separate condition on the packet action: its differentiated bound is needed whenever the enforcement cutoff is not a zero germ. Write that cutoff as a finite product of its bond cutoffs. If any bond cutoff were zero on a neighborhood of the base point, the whole product would be zero there. Thus a nonzero packet germ makes every relevant bond cutoff a nonzero germ. The compensated cutoff estimates then bound the corresponding increments and their derivatives by

\(\displaystyle s (x) + 2 a \tau (x),\)

where \(s\) has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\) and \(a > 0\) is the fixed compensation radius parameter. The mean coordinates have the same upper exponent, by Subsection 8.21. Enlarge these bounds to one nonnegative function of upper exponent zero.

The last subsection of Subsection 8.21 applies to an arbitrary held fine field. Each local packet-action summand is a held fine action, a varying coarse action, or an extracted affine square. Its derivatives are bounded by the corresponding local constants there. Let \(s\) count all seeds of the packet: its fine plaquettes, coarse bonds and flagged coarse sites. The number of such energy addresses is at most

\(\displaystyle s \hspace{0.17em} b (200 B + 1) \hspace{0.17em} n_L,\)

where \(b (r)\) is the finite lattice-ball count at radius \(r\) and \(n_L\) is the fixed number of fine energy addresses per coarse block. Summing the local jet bounds, including the far-field term, gives \(A (x) s\) with one finite \(A (x) \geqslant 0\). The same bound holds on every admissible chart and for every held fine field. In particular this supplies the conditional packet-action estimate, without requiring smoothness of the fine input or an upper bound on its uncut energy.

9.10.2Every read subset has the same polynomial bound

Let \(I\) be any subset of fine bonds, including the empty subset. The projected covariance contains all colours of those bonds. Write \(C_I (y)\) for this covariance and \(w_I (y, z)\) for the compensated Gaussian coordinate. Its inverse exists by the common positive covariance estimate. The quadratic exponent is

\(\displaystyle q_I (y, z) = \tfrac{1}{2} w_I (y, z)^T C_I (y)^{- 1} w_I (y, z) .\)

The inverse-matrix estimates and weighted matrix product estimates of Sections 8.19 and 8.26 apply to every principal read subset with the same row constants. This is where the projected covariance bounds, rather than merely positivity of the full covariance, are used.

For clarity, the finite-dimensional multiplication giving the quadratic bound can be seen directly. Suppose the coordinate jets of \(w_I\) are at most \(W\), and the jets of the inverse matrix have row sums at most \(B_r\) at order \(r \leqslant j\). A derivative of \(w_I^T C_I^{- 1} w_I\) distributes its ordered directions among three factors, giving at most \(3^j\) terms. Sum the middle matrix row first. Each term is at most \(|I| \dim \mathfrak{g}\) times \(W^2 \max_{r \leqslant j} B_r\). The factor \(1 / 2\) can be discarded in an upper bound. On the full torus the compensated-coordinate estimates give

\(\displaystyle W \leqslant [1 + g (\beta)^{- 1} \{s (x) + 2 A_{\text{mean}} (x)\}] F_0 (z) = V_0 F_0 (z),\)

where \(F_0\) uses every fine bond and colour and \(V_0 \geqslant 1\) is finite. Since \(|I| \dim \mathfrak{g}\) is bounded by the full Gaussian dimension, we obtain

\(\displaystyle \|q_I \|_{j, y_0} \leqslant C_{\ast} F_0 (z)^2 \quad \text{for every } I.\) (9.66)

All constants on the right are independent of the chosen subset. For \(I = \varnothing\), the quadratic form is zero.

The relative density compensation \(K_I\) is the Jacobian from Subsection 8.20 times the Gaussian density ratio from Subsection 8.26. At the base point its value is one. The finite-order determinant and inverse estimates there give, for some integer \(k\) and nonnegative finite \(U (x)\), a derivative scale bounded by \(U (x) F_I (z)^k\), where

\(\displaystyle F_I (z) = 1 + |I| \dim \mathfrak{g}+ \sum_{(e, a) \in I \times \mathfrak{g}} |z_{e, a} | .\)

In this expression the colour index means a fixed orthonormal basis. The bound uses only the prescribed derivative order, the local row bounds and the compensation Jacobian bounds. Its polynomial degree is fixed before the subset is selected. Since \(1 \leqslant F_I \leqslant F_0\),

\(\displaystyle \max_{0 \leqslant r \leqslant j} \{U (x) F_I (z)^k \}^r \leqslant (1 + U (x))^j F_0 (z)^{kj} .\)

Together with (9.66), this supplies (9.52) simultaneously for all \(I\), with \(N = kj\) and \(C_0 = (1 + U (x))^j + C_{\ast}\). The quadratic constant \(C_{\ast}\) is allowed to depend on the torus and base coupling; no uniform volume bound is asserted for it.

The same compensated map provides the held-field and protected-patch identities. The open-neighborhood envelope in Subsection 8.26 justifies differentiation of its integrals. Thus the algebraic pointwise comparison uses the actual integrands to which that differentiation theorem applies.

9.10.3Unrestricted packet values

The lower packet-energy bound in the seed estimates applies on the support of the packet mask. Adding the nonnegative far-field energy preserves this lower bound. At a fixed stage it gives \(u \geqslant - E (x) s \geqslant - E (x) s_{\ast}\) in the notation of (9.59). The product of profile, charge and enforcement factors lies in \([0, 1]\). Its remaining ticket factor is bounded by \(\exp (|c_{\text{ticket}} |s_{\ast})\), so this number can be taken as \(M_0\).

Each condensation value has a finite base bound, and each local Haar-chart correction has a finite absolute bound. There are at most as many terms as fine bonds. The remaining normalization term is \(- n_s \log m (\beta)\), again with \(n_s\) no larger than that finite bond count. Their sum gives a finite \(A_0\) bounding \(v_{\beta}\) from above. Only this last normalization term changes between the compared representations; hence \(|v_{\beta'} - v_{\beta} | \leqslant n_{\ast} t\). These are exactly the hypotheses of the damping estimate already proved. Positive unbounded energies are controlled by \(\beta' \geqslant \beta / 2\) and require no further cutoff.

9.10.4Choosing the common variation scale and thresholds

We spell out the quantifier order needed for a running history. The common scale-factor floor is chosen from the elementary-activity and Gaussian local estimates before the derivative order, cutoff convention and positive width parameters are supplied. For a scale factor above that floor and fixed branch data, the compensation chart has a fixed positive radius. Its protected-field requirement is eventually satisfied because \(\tau (x) \to 0\).

When a protected comparison chart is obtained from the stationary construction, restrict it to the coarse horizon and retain the fine zone of Subsection 10.2. If its stationary representative is a critical point of the same tested action in that zone, uniqueness there identifies it with the graph \(\rho\) used in the stationary estimates, for every tag. This identification uses the same block, path and clipping data; shrinking a chart alone does not identify stationary points of different actions. The comparison may then use a still smaller common variation neighborhood inside the protected chart. The fixed-volume continuity constants may depend on that restriction.

The elementary activity estimate is applied at the prescribed scale \(v (x)\) and the compensated-increment scale \(s (x)\), both of upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). The packet-action, Jacobian, density and quadratic estimates just established have eventual validity at those same scales. Take the intersection of their finitely many eventual conditions. This fixes one threshold in \(x\) before the finite tori and the incoming representation are supplied. The associated constants and summable bounds are then instantiated at that actual representation. In particular, a later fixed-volume comparison neighborhood may depend on its total bounds and covariance, as the continuity proof permits.

To combine this with the common integrated derivative estimate, choose a fixed \(Z > 0\) large enough for the normalized exponential coarse variations at derivative order \(j\). Set

\(\displaystyle t_0 (x) = 2 \tau (x), \qquad v (x) = (j + 1) Zt_0 (x) .\)

This function is eventually positive and has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). The elementary inequality \(jZt_0 \leqslant v\) pays the ordered variation directions. Apply both the local comparison construction and the integrated Gaussian estimate at this same \(v\). The latter supplies the ordinary component bound and derivative-scale data. The cluster budget construction, with the strictly positive target support reserve, then supplies the ordinary cluster criterion with its strict spare margin. All are evaluated at the same incoming data; no alternate representation is substituted between these steps.

Finally, a schedule has only finitely many transitions at each fixed stage. Take the maximum of their scale-factor floors at that stage. After those factors are fixed, take a common upper threshold for the finitely many transition conditions, enlarge the stage entry coupling to that threshold, and preserve any earlier lower bound. Increasing entry couplings further preserves these conclusions. Consequently the comparison data and cluster margin are available uniformly for each already supplied transition satisfying the underlying sector, shell-access, protected-chart and incoming activity hypotheses. This selection does not construct those incoming data.

For an application to a running curve one must also retain its renewed estimates, the geometric bounds on the downward map, the admissible initial action, and the inequality \(\text{preExponent} / B \leqslant \text{width} / 2\). The curve has the same clipping and path data and the same regular and forced dictionaries. Its displayed output must equal the constructed nonlinear output under the chosen dictionary identifications. With these hypotheses, the fixed-volume theorem applies to that actual output, giving regular jet and forced value control and its subsequential summable majorants. The construction of an entire history satisfying all these hypotheses remains a separate step.

9.11The remaining small-field budget inequalities

The nonlinear step uses several estimates at different small powers of the coupling. We verify their numerical compatibility before choosing the stage thresholds. Throughout this section the block factor and stage data are fixed. Write \(z = \beta_F (x)\) for the shifted fine coupling, \(B = B (x)\) for the block radius, and \(\tau = \tau (x)\) for the fluctuation threshold. Thus \(z \to \infty\), \(B\) has upper exponent zero, and \(\tau\) has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). We also use the stronger growth facts \(B / \log z \to \infty\) and \(P_F (x)^2 / \log z \to \infty\) for the actual radius and fine monitor. They follow from their prescribed logarithmic growth laws; upper exponent zero alone would not suffice for the exponential estimates. All constants may depend on the fixed derivative order, spatial weights, path bounds and positive support-width reserves.

9.11.1The low-degree action and its support tail

The explicit low-degree correction has four contributions: the quadratic reset term, cubic and quartic bare-action terms, and a quadratic loop term. Their rooted jet bounds, with the fixed support weights already included, have the form

\(\displaystyle u \leqslant C_2 | \alpha | \tau^2 + C_3 | \beta | \tau^3 + C_4 | \beta | \tau^4 + C_{\text{loop}} \Lambda \tau^2 .\) (9.67)

The constants include the finite coordinate and orientation costs, and the anchored support bounds for the corresponding degrees. This is the direct sum of the quadratic and homogeneous action bounds: a degree-\(r\) term tested in directions of size \(\tau\) contributes \(\tau^r\), while its coefficient contributes the displayed \(| \alpha |\), \(| \beta |\), or \(\Lambda\). The stage fixes the derivative order, spatial power and support exponent, so these constants are unchanged when its actual threshold varies.

Assume \(| \alpha | \leqslant A\), \(| \beta | \leqslant 2 z\), and \(0 \leqslant \Lambda \leqslant \Lambda_0 (x)\), where \(A\) is fixed and \(\Lambda_0\) has upper exponent zero. The four terms in (9.67) have upper exponents respectively \(- 1, - 1 / 2, - 1, - 1\). Hence one common nonnegative majorant for \(u\) has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\). Since \(- 1 / 2 < - 7 / 16\), eventually

\(\displaystyle u \leqslant z^{- 7 / 16} .\) (9.68)

If \(\sigma > 0\) is the fixed support width, then \(e^{- \sigma B / 4}\) has every negative upper exponent. Multiplying the same majorant by this tail gives, after increasing the threshold,

\(\displaystyle e^{- \sigma B / 4} u \leqslant z^{- 23 / 20} .\) (9.69)

Also \((a + 3) / B \leqslant \sigma / 4\) eventually for any fixed target support exponent \(a\). The thresholds here are uniform over the stated ranges of \(\alpha, \beta, \Lambda\) and over the actual finite torus.

The reset bound itself follows from the existing coupling estimate. If the running reset satisfies \(| \alpha + c_G \log L| \leqslant A_0\), then \(| \alpha | \leqslant A_0 + |c_G \log L|\). Enlarging the right side by one makes a fixed positive bound available for this chosen block factor. It does not require selecting a different reset at each application.

9.11.2The strengthened cluster remainder

Let \(D = z^{3 / 8}\) be the extra-degree weight, so \(D \geqslant 1\) eventually. Write \(\mathcal{P}_{\eta} (x) = C_j (x) e^{\eta} \eta^{- pj}\) for the derivative bound at positive reserve \(\eta\), where \(j, p \in \mathbb{N}\) are the fixed derivative order and spatial power. The factor \(C_j (x)\) includes \((pj)\)! and the lattice scale factor; it is common to all reserves but may vary with the block radius. It has upper exponent zero. We suppress \(x\) in these bounds below. Suppose the ordinary component/cluster budget at reserve \(i > 0\) has already given

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{P}_i \hspace{0.17em} S (1 - 1 / 2)^{- 1} \leqslant z^q, \qquad q \leqslant - 7 / 16.\)

Here \(S = (1 + 2^j B_1) \omega \geqslant 0\) is the unstrengthened root bound for the second expansion, in the notation of Subsection 9.7; it includes the component derivative factor. The reserve \(i\) lies below the fixed available reserve \(y > 0\), and let \(a \geqslant 0\) be the target support exponent. Retain the graded elementary bound and the two smallness conditions from Subsection 9.7, namely

\(\displaystyle \frac{4 (2 B + 1)^4 Dr_0}{\delta_0} \leqslant \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \frac{4 (2 B + 1)^4 DS}{y} \leqslant \frac{1}{2},\)

with the available support exponent covering the reserve \(a + 2 y\). Here \(r_0 \geqslant 0\) and \(\delta_0 > 0\) are the first expansion's root bound and reserve. Also retain its seed-mark and footprint purchases, for \(M \geqslant 0\), \(\epsilon > 0\) and \(T \geqslant 0\), \(D^3 \leqslant e^M\) and \(D^3 \leqslant e^{\epsilon T}\), in the notation of that section. These conditions concern the same incoming representation and graded component bounds. The extra-degree argument of Subsection 9.7 gives a residual bound

\(\displaystyle D^{- 3} \mathcal{P}_y \hspace{0.17em} DS (1 - 1 / 2)^{- 1} .\)

Since \(i \leqslant y\), the inverse powers satisfy \(y^{- pj} \leqslant i^{- pj}\), while \(e^i \geqslant 1\). Consequently \(\mathcal{P}_y \leqslant e^y \mathcal{P}_i\). The displayed remainder is at most

\(\displaystyle e^y D^{- 2} z^q = e^y z^{q - 3 / 4} .\)

Now \(q - 3 / 4 \leqslant - 19 / 16 < - 23 / 20\). The strict gap absorbs the fixed factor \(e^y\) and proves the common residual budget \(z^{- 23 / 20}\). This calculation consumes the unstrengthened bound \(S\) built from the original component bound and its derivative factor; it does not assume an independent bound for the strengthened expansion.

9.11.3Polynomial localization costs and the incoming allowance

The auxiliary periods in the five-period construction have side lengths bounded by a fixed polynomial in \(B\). Their volumes, local ball counts and ordered-derivative transport costs therefore all have upper exponent zero. Multiplication by any of them preserves the upper exponent of a bound.

In particular, localizing the ordinary error of size \(z^{- 7 / 16}\) produces a total auxiliary error of upper exponent \(- 7 / 16\). A further fixed support conversion, including the constant exponential of the support-cover bound, still has that exponent. Since \(- 7 / 16 < - 1 / 8\), its final bound is eventually at most \(z^{- 1 / 8}\). Similarly, localizing a bound \(z^{- 23 / 20}\), including the factor \(j! 3^j\) from Subsection 10.6, gives a bound of upper exponent \(- 23 / 20\).

Let \(G (x)\) be the sum of the latter localized bound and the finitely many unlocalized \(z^{- 23 / 20}\) remainders, each with its fixed coefficient. Then \(G\) also has upper exponent \(- 23 / 20\). If \(c > 0\) is the fixed regular allowance constant, the strict inequality \(- 23 / 20 < - 11 / 10\) gives

\(\displaystyle G (x) \leqslant \tfrac{c}{2} z^{- 11 / 10} \leqslant \tfrac{c}{2} x^{- 11 / 10}\)

eventually; the second inequality uses \(z \geqslant x > 0\). Thus these remainders use at most half of the available regular allowance. All polynomial localization factors are paid before this final comparison.

9.11.4Absorbing the forced correction at its prescribed bound

A second exponential absorption must retain its prescribed positive strong forced bound \(v\). Let \(a, b \geqslant 0\) be its hard and soft input bounds, with

\(\displaystyle a \leqslant v / 8, \qquad b \leqslant z^{- 1 / 8} .\)

Put \(p = z^{1 / 16}\). The constructed strong bound has the form \(v = C \exp [- r (\tau / g (\beta))^2]\) with fixed \(C, r > 0\), where \(g (\beta) = \beta^{- 1 / 2}\). It therefore has the monitor estimate \(v \leqslant C \exp (- cP_F (x)^2)\) uniformly for the allowed coupling band, with fixed \(C, c > 0\). Thus \(vp \leqslant 1\) eventually. Also, uniformly for \(b\) in the stated range,

\(\displaystyle b \leqslant 1, \qquad pb \leqslant 1 / 8, \qquad (2 B + 1)^4 \{(8 p)^{- 1} + 2 b\} \leqslant 1\)

eventually: the two powers in the last braces are strictly negative and the prefactor has upper exponent zero.

Choose

\(\displaystyle \kappa = - \log (vp) \geqslant 0, \qquad I = e^{\kappa} a + 2 b.\)

Then \(I \leqslant (8 p)^{- 1} + 2 b\), so \((2 B + 1)^4 I \leqslant 1\) and in particular \(0 \leqslant I \leqslant 1\). Using \(e^I - 1 \leqslant 2 I\) gives

\(\displaystyle e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant 2 vp \{(8 p)^{- 1} + 2 b\} = v (1 / 4 + 4 pb) \leqslant 3 v / 4 \leqslant v.\)

These are the exact smallness and output inequalities required by the forced-gas absorption, at the original allowance \(v\). Its uniform monitor estimate follows from the coupling-band comparison for the gap. Indeed \(\tau = z^{- 1 / 2} P_F (x)\) and \(\beta \geqslant z / 4\) give \(\beta \tau^2 \geqslant P_F (x)^2 / 4\). The fixed positive gap and normalization constants are absorbed into \(c\). All constants and the threshold are chosen before the particular coupling in the band.

Each assertion above is eventually valid for a fixed transition. Take the finite maximum of its required thresholds at each stage, then enlarge the entry coupling accordingly. Auxiliary periods are also chosen above their fixed geometric lower bounds, and the volume growth exponent is increased to at least one. These choices are preserved by any later increase of the delays. They supply the numerical budgets; the actual local action identities, stationary comparison and funded incoming representations are still required where each budget is used.
