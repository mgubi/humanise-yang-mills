\section{The same stress laws in every character phase}
\label{sec:phase-ward-inheritance}

The character construction evaluates an operator for a whole word. This
allows us to transfer a Ward identity with spectators, even though the
identity includes an integral and is not a product of individual character
values. We prove the required operator identity before evaluating it.

Retain the unselected hierarchy $S$, its Euclidean Hilbert space
$\mathcal H_E$, ground subspace $\mathcal H_0$, orthogonal projection $P_0$,
and the commutative compression algebra $\mathcal A$ of
Section~\ref{ex-phases}. Write $A_D(w)$ for its bounded insertion operator
in a time slab of width $D>0$. The norm smoothness and derivative identities
are those of Section~\ref{ex-slab-regularity}; the whole-word character
hierarchies are those of Section~\ref{ex-phase-regularity}. Retain the same
physical field spaces, derivative maps $\partial_b$, rotation generators
$\mathcal R(M)$ and normalized stress tensor $J_{ab}$ constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:physical-angular-actions}. In this section its unselected
translation and rotation Ward identities are hypotheses already supplied
by that construction. No new normalization of $J$ is made in a phase.

Fix a distinguished insertion $B$ at $x_0$, spectator fields
$B_1,\ldots,B_n$ at distinct points $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, and a smooth real
cutoff $\chi$ of compact support. Require that $\chi=1$ on a neighborhood
of $x_0$ and that $x_j\notin\operatorname{supp}\chi$ for $j>0$.
All $n+1$ points are distinct. Put
\[
 S_\xi(B;x_0\mid\boldsymbol B,\boldsymbol x)
   =S_\xi(B,B_1,\ldots,B_n;x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_n).
\]
Here $\xi\in\Xi$ is an arbitrary character. The corresponding notation
with an extra field before $B$ includes its extra position before $x_0$.

\begin{proposition}[Inheritance of the shell identities]
\label{prop:phase-ward-inheritance}
For every character $\xi$, every coordinate $b$, and every real
skew-symmetric matrix $M$, one has
\begin{align}
 S_\xi(\partial_bB;x_0\mid\boldsymbol B,\boldsymbol x)
 &=-\sum_a\int_{\mathbb R^4}\partial_a\chi(y)
     S_\xi(J_{ab},B;y,x_0\mid\boldsymbol B,\boldsymbol x)\,dy,
 \label{eq:phase-translation-flux}\\
 S_\xi(\mathcal R(M)B;x_0\mid\boldsymbol B,\boldsymbol x)
 &=-\sum_{a,b}\int_{\mathbb R^4}\partial_a\chi(y)
      [M(y-x_0)]_b
     S_\xi(J_{ab},B;y,x_0\mid\boldsymbol B,\boldsymbol x)\,dy.
 \label{eq:phase-rotation-flux}
\end{align}
Both integrals are absolutely convergent. The same cutoff and the same
field maps work for every character, including the selected one.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{A continuous evaluator on the uncompressed operators}

A character is continuous and complex linear on $\mathcal A$. By the
complex Hahn--Banach theorem, extend it to a bounded complex-linear
functional $\widetilde\xi$ on $\mathcal B(\mathcal H_0)$, and set
\[
 \Phi_\xi(T)=\widetilde\xi(P_0T|_{\mathcal H_0}),
               \qquad T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H_E).
\]
Compression is bounded linear, so $\Phi_\xi$ is bounded linear. We use
no positivity or multiplicativity of its extension outside $\mathcal A$.
For a separated word $w$ placed strictly inside a slab by a translation
$s$, the whole-word definition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-fixed-evaluator}
             S_\xi(w)=\Phi_\xi(A_D(w+s)).
\end{equation}
The evaluator is fixed while positions, labels, and the slab placement
vary. In particular it may be passed through a norm-convergent integral.

Choose $s$ and $D$ so that both the finite configuration and the
\emph{whole} compact support of $\chi$, after translation by $s$, lie
strictly between time $0$ and time $D$. To see that this is possible,
bound their time coordinates between $\alpha$ and $\beta$, translate
by $(1-\alpha)e_0$, and take $D>\beta-\alpha+1$.
This leaves a positive margin at each slab boundary. For a field tuple
$v$ and a configuration $z$ in this fixed slab, write
\[
                         K_v(z)=A_D(v;z+s).
\]
Representatives in the original label spaces can be fixed once for the
finitely many fields involved. The slab identities and equality of the
phase null spaces ensure that the resulting correlation values are the
ones on the physical field spaces. Norm regularity gives continuity of
$K_v$ on separated configurations in this slab.

\subsection{Integrating the shell before taking a character}

Let $u:\mathbb R^4\to\mathbb C$ be continuous; the cases needed are
$u=1$ and $u(y)=[M(y-x_0)]_b$. Put
\[
             w_a(y)=\partial_a\chi(y)u(y).
\]
Its support is compact and contained in $\operatorname{supp}\chi$.
Moreover it misses a neighborhood of every $x_j$: at $x_0$ the cutoff
is locally constant, and at the spectator points it is locally zero.
Consequently the operator-valued function
\[
 F_{ab,u}(y)=w_a(y)
  K_{(J_{ab},B,\boldsymbol B)}(y,x_0,\boldsymbol x)
\]
is continuous and compactly supported when defined to be zero in the
neighborhoods where $w_a$ vanishes. On its support the inserted point
is separated from all fixed points and remains strictly inside the
fixed slab, so norm continuity follows from that of $K$.
At a point outside the support of $w_a$, the entire product is locally
zero, even if the unweighted correlation there would have a collision.
This proves continuity on all of $\mathbb R^4$.

A continuous compactly supported Banach-valued function has bounded
norm, separable range on its compact support, and integrable norm.
Thus the Bochner integral
\[
                   U_{ab}(u)=\int_{\mathbb R^4}F_{ab,u}(y)\,dy
                \quad\hbox{in }\mathcal B(\mathcal H_E)
\]
exists. Every bounded linear functional $\Lambda$ commutes with this
integral. One may verify this first for simple functions and then use
norm-integrable approximation and the bound
$|\Lambda(T)|\le\|\Lambda\|\,\|T\|$. Applied to
\eqref{eq:phase-fixed-evaluator}, it gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-shell-evaluation}
 \Phi_\xi(U_{ab}(u))=
 \int w_a(y)S_\xi(J_{ab},B;y,x_0\mid\boldsymbol B,\boldsymbol x)\,dy.
\end{equation}
It also proves absolute integrability of the scalar integrand. Values
at collisions have no bearing on this equality, since the weight is
zero in a neighborhood of each possible collision.

\subsection{Testing the operator identity against exterior words}

Let $l,r$ be arbitrary positive-time word vectors in $\mathcal H_E$.
The slab matrix-element identity expresses
$\langle[l],K_v(z)[r]\rangle$ as an unselected correlation containing
$v$ at $z$, together with a reflected copy of $l$ below the slab and
a translated copy of $r$ above it. Translate all points back by $-s$.
Permutation symmetry puts the inserted fields first; translation
invariance then gives precisely this correlation in the original
coordinates. The two extra word configurations are internally
separated and lie on opposite sides of the slab. They therefore
introduce neither collisions with one another nor collisions with
its interior insertions.

Crucially, these extra spectators lie outside the entire support of
$\chi$, since that support was placed strictly inside the slab.
Thus $\chi$ is still an admissible cutoff for the unselected Ward
identity with these additional spectators. Commuting the continuous
matrix-element functional with each Bochner integral, that Ward
identity proves
\begin{align*}
 \left\langle[l],-\sum_aU_{ab}(1)[r]\right\rangle
 &=\langle[l],K_{(\partial_bB,\boldsymbol B)}
                       (x_0,\boldsymbol x)[r]\rangle,\\
 \left\langle[l],-\sum_{a,b}U_{ab}([M(\,\cdot-x_0)]_b)[r]\right\rangle
 &=\langle[l],K_{(\mathcal R(M)B,\boldsymbol B)}
                       (x_0,\boldsymbol x)[r]\rangle.
\end{align*}
Positive-time word vectors span a dense subspace. If a bounded operator
has zero matrix elements on this span, fix its right argument there
and use density on the left to see that its value is zero. Continuity
then extends the vanishing to every right argument. Hence the two
matrix-element equalities are equalities of bounded operators.

Apply $\Phi_\xi$ to these operator equalities. Its linearity, the
whole-word evaluation formula, and \eqref{eq:phase-shell-evaluation}
give \eqref{eq:phase-translation-flux} and
\eqref{eq:phase-rotation-flux}. This proves the proposition. Notice
that no limiting argument over characters and no almost-everywhere
exceptional set occurred.

\subsection{The mixed and selected continuations}

There are two applications of the continuation construction, with the
same field labels and calibrated rotation action. For the mixed table
use its original Hilbert space, bounded slabs and slot identities.
For the selected character use the Hilbert space and slab operators
of Corollary~\ref{ex-phase-dynamics} and
Proposition~\ref{ex-phase-slab-bounds}. Their norm bounds, derivative
identities and field inclusions have already been verified; equality
of null spaces is supplied by Section~\ref{ex-phase-fields}.
Reflection transport supplies the same orthogonal field covariance.
The common label action of Section~\ref{sec:label-descent} consequently
applies to both tables.

Apply Sections~\ref{sec:complex-heat}--\ref{sec:analytic-boundary}
separately with these two sets of Hilbert-space data. The continued
functions are built from each table's own slab matrix elements, so
their imaginary-time restrictions recover that table, and their
tempered boundary distributions are its own boundary values. The
Fourier-support, Lorentz-covariance, full-tube and locality arguments
use these same functions in each application. None of those arguments
uses one-dimensionality of the time-invariant subspace. The word-domain
construction and heat-vector isometry likewise apply to each table.

Only for the selected character do we invoke
Theorem~\ref{ex-pure-ground}: its invariant Euclidean subspace is its
vacuum line. The isometric embedding and translation intertwining
then give vacuum uniqueness in its reconstructed theory, as proved
in Section~\ref{sec:spectral-measure}. No such uniqueness is asserted
for the mixed theory. This is why constructing both continuations
requires no change of the selected phase and no second field quotient.
