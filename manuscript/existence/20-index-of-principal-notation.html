20Index of principal notation

The entries refer to the conventions at the indicated places. Symbols used only in a local calculation retain the meaning declared there. In particular, a change of type, scale, normalization, or limiting order requires the stated bridge; repeated letters alone do not identify mathematical objects.

\(G\), \(r_G\), \(\mathfrak{g}\)

The fixed compact matrix group in its specified representation of complex dimension \(r_G\), and its real Lie algebra (Section 1).

\(x = (\boldsymbol{x}, t)\), \(\bar{V}_+\)

Euclidean coordinates with time last, and the closed forward cone for the Minkowski form \(t^2 - |\boldsymbol{x}|^2\) (Section 1).

\(P_p\), \(D_N\), \(a_N\)

Physical period, lattice denominator, and mesh, with \(a_N = D_N^{- 1}\). The site count per axis is \(P_p D_N\) (Section 1).

\(\Lambda_{N, p}\), \(U\), \(U_{ij} (x)\)

Periodic sites, a group-valued link configuration, and its oriented plaquette holonomy (Subsection 3.1).

\(S_{\ell}\), \(\beta\), \(\mu_{\ell, \beta}\)

The finite plaquette action, its bare coefficient, and the normalized Wilson probability. Each pair of axes is counted once and the trace is divided by \(r_G\) (Subsection 3.1). This action is distinct from the correlation table \(S\) below.

\(O_{N, p, v}\), \(M_{N, p}\), \(W_{N, p}\)

A token insertion, its finite moment, and the smeared moment. The latter includes \(a_N^{4 n}\) at arity \(n\) (1.5).

\(\text{Conf}_n\), \(\text{Gen}\), \(\text{Cyc}_p\), \(\text{NoHit}_p\)

Distinct-point configurations, their generic subset with separated coordinate projections, and the two period-dependent configuration domains defined in Subsection 4.9; the coordinatewise conditions are not interchangeable.

\(f_{\text{mesh}}\), \(f_{\text{per}}\)

The two increasing sequences of an internal extraction order. The ordered tail has a period threshold outside a mesh threshold that may depend on the period (Definition 4.18).

\(S\), \(S^{\chi}\), \(S^{\text{mix}}\)

The limiting Euclidean table, a character component, and its mixed correlation. The mixture is the integral of the character correlations in (1.6); it is the mixed correlation that appears in the Wilson limit.

\(V_d\), \(\mathcal{N}_d\), \(\mathcal{F}_d\), \([v]_d\)

Classical jet polynomials of weighted degree at most \(d\), the all-spectator null space, and its quotient \(\mathcal{F}_d = V_d /\mathcal{N}_d\). The grade is part of the label: equal polynomials at different grades need not represent the same field (Subsection 1.1). A grade is not automatically a scaling dimension (Section 1).

\(L_d\), \(R_{d, N, p}\), \(I^{(d)}_{N, p}\)

The jet-normalized local section, its reference-scale readout and the renormalized lattice insertion in (1.2). The grade-\(d\) unit is \([a_{r_d}^d \boldsymbol{1}]_d\); the readout is inverted before insertion.

\(\textsf{D}^{\text{gr}}_{d, L}\)

The diagonal dilation of grade-\(d\) coefficient data at block size \(L\) (Subsection 2.1). The map \(\textsf{D}_m\) in Subsection 11.3 denotes the logarithm of a product of link exponentials.

\(X\), \(\nu\), \(\chi_{\ast}\)

In the public target, a compact phase space, its full-support probability, and a selected character. The symbols are local to that discussion; the construction uses \(\Xi\) and \(\mu\) in Proposition 5.14.

\(\mathcal{H}\), \(\Omega\), \(\mathcal{D}\), \(\Phi\)

A reconstructed character's Hilbert space, vacuum, common invariant domain, and fields (Definition 1.3). The domain is distinct from any locally named contact-device or matrix object.

\(A\), \(M\), \(P_{\mathcal{H}}\) in the sector calculation

The completed precision \(A = M^{\ast} M + P_{\mathcal{H}^c}\), selected row map, and projection onto active bonds (8.93). The Gaussian covariance, when defined, is the inverse precision; the completion is identity on inactive coordinates.

\(D_{v, a}\) and \(\mathscr{D}_{v, a}\) in the free-kernel calculation

The directional filter and level response, respectively. They have different formulas and cannot be substituted for each other (Sections 3.6 and 3.5).

\(A\), \(A_0\), \(C\) in Gaussian reassembly

The completed sector precision, flat reference precision, and localized positive finite-range covariance. The correction \(C^{- 1} - A\) is retained; \(C\) is not simply identified with \(A^{- 1}\) (Subsection 8.29).

\(a_m, b_m, c_m\) in background paths

The quadratic-loop, cubic, and quartic coefficient arrays at a fixed scale, in the prescribed real coefficient spaces (Subsection 12.1). This \(a_m\) is local and differs from the physical mesh.

\(\varepsilon\) in the healthy-field comparison

The field error and per-step row/covariance error are separate quantities in the inputs to the comparison. The stated conclusion uses a common bound for both (Subsection 14.17).

\(\lambda\) in the sector calculation

The normalization \(q^{- 1 / 2}\) for \(q\) signed channels, so its square gives the orientation average (Subsection 8.12). Other local uses of \(\lambda\) are introduced separately.

\(\lambda_G\), \(b_0 (G)\), \(s_G\)

The annulus coefficient, canonical running coefficient, and shooting-line slope, respectively. The two Casimir definitions use the same color inner product, giving \(\lambda_G = 2 b_0 (G)\). The shooting slope is the same constant, \(s_G = \lambda_G\) (12.86); it also appears as \(c_G\) and \(\kappa\).

\(E_d\), \(\mathcal{F}_d\), \(\pi_d\), \(\tau_{I, D}\) in the annulus

Reference label space, selected-table field space, quotient map, and spectator tester (Subsection 17.5). The finite-volume labels and their quotient fields have distinct domains.

\(\textsf{P}_r (M ; C)\) in the annular estimate

A bound on the matrix path and its first two derivatives with weights \(r_i^j\) for \(j = 0, 1, 2\) (Subsection 11.12). Here \(r_i\) is a local bound scale, not automatically an annulus or short-distance radius.

\(T_{\mu \nu}\), \(\Theta\)

The Ward-normalized physical stress tensor and its trace centered in the selected phase, as in Theorem 18.53. Centering refers to that phase, not to the mixed state.

\(T (r)\)

The selected two-point trace quantity in the final short-distance requirement (19.32); this is a separate condition from the operator-product estimate in (19.31).
