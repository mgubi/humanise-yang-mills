19The common scale choice and final assembly

19.1Purchasing the nonlinear estimates on one scale sequence

The nonlinear estimates above are uniform in the finite torus once the block size, derivative orders and stage parameters have been fixed. We now choose coupling thresholds that pay them together. In particular, the marked and unmarked constructions can use the same transition. The two logarithmic corrections absorbed into the forced gas require two successive payments; both are included.

19.1.1An elementary exponential payment

Let \(\beta_f (x) = x + c_L\), with fixed \(c_L > 0\), let \(B (x) = \lceil (\log x)^2 \rceil\), and let \(P_f (x)\) be the shifted fine monitor. For \(C > 0\) put

\(\displaystyle \tau_C (x) = \exp \{- CP_f (x)^2 / 2\} .\)

Fix \(D_0 > 0\) and a function \(f\) of negative upper exponent. For all sufficiently large \(x\), the following assertion holds simultaneously for every pair \(a, b \geqslant 0\) such that

\(\displaystyle a \leqslant D_0 e^{- CP_f (x)^2}, \qquad b \leqslant f (x) .\)

There is \(\kappa \geqslant 0\) with

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} b \leqslant 1, \qquad (2 B + 1)^4 I \leqslant 1, \qquad e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant 2 \tau_C (x),\\ I = e^{\kappa} a + 2 b. \hspace{3cm} \text{(19.1)} \end{array}\)

Take \(\kappa = CP_f (x)^2 / 2\). Then \(e^{\kappa} a \leqslant D_0 \tau_C (x)\), so

\(\displaystyle (2 B + 1)^4 I \leqslant (2 B + 1)^4 (D_0 \tau_C + 2 f) .\)

The right side tends to zero. Indeed the geometric factor has upper exponent zero, \(f\) has negative upper exponent, and \(\tau_C\) has every negative upper exponent by the monitor estimate. The same choice of threshold gives \(b \leqslant 1\) and the middle inequality of (19.1). Since \((2 B + 1)^4 \geqslant 1\), also \(0 \leqslant I \leqslant 1\). Convexity of the exponential on \([0, 1]\) gives \(e^I - 1 \leqslant (e - 1) I \leqslant 2 I \leqslant 2\). Multiplication by \(e^{- \kappa} = \tau_C\) proves the last inequality. These are exactly the smallness and output-bound conditions for the forced-gas absorption in Subsection 8.32. The threshold depends on \(C, D_0, f, L\), but not on \(a, b\).

19.1.2The two soft correction bounds

We verify the negative power needed in the second payment. Fix a stage, including its derivative order, spatial weight, support exponent, path widths and path bounds. Let \(t \in (0, 1]\) be its threshold and \(B\) its radius. Let \(\alpha\) be the coupling reset, with \(| \alpha | \leqslant A\), where \(A \geqslant 0\) is fixed after the block length has been chosen. If the regular remainder allowance is \(0 \leqslant H \leqslant t^2\), the reset's boundary-hit coefficient is

\(\displaystyle h_{\text{hit}} = 2 t (| \alpha | + H / t^2) + \tfrac{1}{2} HC_{\text{pair}} .\)

Here \(C_{\text{pair}} \geqslant 0\) depends only on the fixed derivative orders. Since \(H \leqslant t^2 \leqslant t\),

\(\displaystyle h_{\text{hit}} \leqslant [2 (A + 1) + C_{\text{pair}} / 2] t.\)

There are two correction families, with the support reserve used for the first absorption and the larger reserve used for the second. For either family let \(R_0, K_0 \geqslant 0\) be respectively its reset and path bounds computed at radius one. The rooted footprint sum gives

\(\displaystyle R (B, H) \leqslant R_0 (1 + B)^4 H, \qquad K (B) \leqslant K_0 (1 + B)^4 .\)

To see the radius dependence explicitly, its footprint population is a fixed nonnegative multiple of either \((2 B + 1)^4\) or \((8 B + 5)^4\). The inequalities \(2 B + 1 \leqslant 3 (1 + B)\) and \(8 B + 5 \leqslant 13 (1 + B)\) compare them with their radius-one values. All remaining factors are fixed: exponential support charges, inverse positive width powers and the convergent geometric series for the unused width reserve. The same calculation works with the larger reserve, using its smaller positive width. Thus the total soft bound satisfies

\(\displaystyle R (B, H) + h_{\text{hit}} K (B) \leqslant C_A (1 + B)^4 t, \qquad C_A = R_0 + [2 (A + 1) + C_{\text{pair}} / 2] K_0 .\) (19.2)

These constants are selected separately for the two families.

At coupling \(x\), take

\(\displaystyle t (x) = x^{- 1 / 2} \exp ((\log \log x)^2), \qquad H (x) = c_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10}, \qquad 0 < c_{\text{st}} \leqslant 1 / 64.\)

For all sufficiently large \(x\), we have \(t (x) \leqslant 1\). For \(x > 1\) we also have \(H \leqslant x^{- 1} \leqslant t^2\), so the preceding calculation applies eventually. Moreover, for any fixed constant \(C_A\),

\(\displaystyle C_A (1 + B (x))^4 t (x) \leqslant \beta_f (x)^{- 1 / 4}\)

eventually. Divide the left side by \(x^{- 1 / 4}\) and take logarithms: the variable terms are \(4 \log (1 + \lceil (\log x)^2 \rceil) + (\log \log x)^2 - \tfrac{1}{4} \log x\), which tend to minus infinity. Finally \(\beta_f (x) / x \to 1\), so the same assertion holds with the shifted scale. This proves the required soft bound uniformly for \(| \alpha | \leqslant A\), both derivative budgets and both finite-volume families.

The first hard bound of the nonlinear expansion is at most \(\exp (- \mu P_f^2 / 4)\), with \(\mu > 0\). Its accompanying soft bound is at most \(\beta_f^{- 1 / 8}\). In the multiple-insertion version it is at most \(C_{\text{cov}} \beta_f^{- 1 / 8}\), where the positive constant \(C_{\text{cov}}\) depends only on the fixed enlargement depth. Both have negative upper exponent. Apply (19.1) with \(C = \mu / 4\), \(D_0 = 1\) to obtain the first output bound

\(\displaystyle 2 e^{- \mu P_f^2 / 8} .\)

Use this as the hard bound for the second correction, whose soft bound is at most (19.2). A second application with \(C = \mu / 8\), \(D_0 = 2\) gives

\(\displaystyle 2 e^{- \mu P_f^2 / 8} \quad \longmapsto \quad 2 e^{- \mu P_f^2 / 16} .\) (19.3)

These are the two successive forced allowances used in the nonlinear transition, including the larger-reserve correction. All inequalities use the same value of \(x\).

19.1.3Choosing thresholds without changing the transition

The marked construction first chooses a sufficiently large block size to pay its footprint conversion. For that fixed block and fixed stage orders, Sections 8.34 and 9.1 give a threshold beyond which the local marked derivatives, the two connected expansions and their tail estimates hold. The chart transport radius is chosen before this threshold; the microscopic fluctuation threshold tends to zero and eventually fits inside it. The Gaussian and all-species estimates are uniform in the finite torus and in the insertion, so these choices do not depend on either of them.

There are finitely many permitted target stages at each current stage \(j\). For each such pair \((j, i)\), choose the block-size floor given by the marked construction and take their finite maximum. Choose the actual block size \(L_j\) above that floor. Only after \(L_j\) is fixed do we choose the coupling thresholds needed for (19.1), (19.2) and the fixed-order marked estimates. This order permits their constants to depend on the actual block length and reset bound.

More explicitly, let \(d_j\) be a previously chosen entry threshold, and let \(r_{j, i}\) be the threshold for all the requirements at transition \((j, i)\). Replace it by

\(\displaystyle d'_j = \max \left( d_j, \max_{i \text{permitted at} \hspace{0.17em} j} r_{j, i} \right) .\) (19.4)

For any later thresholds \(d''_j \geqslant d'_j\) and every \(x \geqslant d''_j\), all the requirements still hold. This is immediate from \(x \geqslant d'_j \geqslant r_{j, i}\); the other selected data, including the block sizes and the transition maps, are unchanged. Applying (19.4) successively to the ordinary and the larger-reserve multiple-insertion requirements gives one set of thresholds satisfying both. No uniform maximum over all orders or all stages is needed.

Consequently the numerical arrival conditions previously required by constructions with one or several insertions and by the centered construction can be imposed simultaneously on the same selected scale sequence. This is a purchase of their actual displayed estimates. The construction of the underlying funded Gaussian data and the subsequent coefficient and normalization identities remain the respective analytic and algebraic steps already separated in those arguments.

19.2One scale sequence for all the estimates

The estimates are indexed by finite stages, while the final theory requires arbitrarily high orders. We choose a slowly advancing stage map so that every fixed stage eventually appears, with enough time to satisfy its comparison estimates. The same choice also pays the numerical requests made after the block lengths have been selected.

19.2.1A slowly advancing sequence with long constant intervals

Let \(b_j, c_j\) be arbitrary nonnegative integers. Define recursively

\(\displaystyle A_0 = 0, \qquad k_j = c_j + 2 A_j + 1, \qquad A_{j + 1} = A_j + b_{j + 1} + 2 k_j + 1.\)

The \(A_j\) are strictly increasing and tend to infinity. Define \(J_0 (m) = j\) when \(A_j \leqslant m < A_{j + 1}\). These half-open intervals partition the nonnegative integers. Then \(J_0\) is nondecreasing, \(J_0 (0) = J_0 (1) = 0\), and

\(\displaystyle J_0 (m + 1) \leqslant J_0 (m) + 1, \qquad J_0 (m) \longrightarrow \infty .\)

The increment bound holds because an integer step can cross at most one of the distinct integer endpoints. The divergence follows from \(m \geqslant A_j \Rightarrow J_0 (m) \geqslant j\).

If \(J_0 (m + 1) = j > 0\), then \(A_j \leqslant m + 1\) and \(A_j \geqslant b_j + 1\), so \(b_j \leqslant m\). Also \(k_j \geqslant c_j\), and the whole interval

\(\displaystyle k_j - \lfloor k_j / 2 \rfloor \leqslant m \leqslant k_j + \lfloor k_j / 2 \rfloor\) (19.5)

lies in \([A_j, A_{j + 1})\): its lower endpoint is at least \(A_j\) because \(k_j \geqslant 2 A_j + 1\), and its upper endpoint is at most \(2 k_j < A_{j + 1}\). Thus \(J_0 = j\) throughout this interval. This explicit recursion simultaneously postpones entry to every stage and gives that stage a prescribed long comparison interval.

19.2.2Physical scales, entry requirements and periods

Let \(L_j \geqslant 3\) be the selected nondecreasing odd block lengths, and let \(j_{\ast}\) be the first stage. Put

\(\displaystyle J (m) = j_{\ast} + J_0 (m), \qquad s_0 = 0, \qquad s_{m + 1} = s_m + \log L_{J (m + 1)}, \qquad a_m = e^{- s_m} .\)

Induction and monotonicity give

\(\displaystyle m \log 3 \leqslant s_m \leqslant m \log L_{J (m)} .\) (19.6)

In particular \(a_m \downarrow 0\), and \(e^{s_m}\) is the integer product of the first \(m\) block lengths.

In this construction \(d_j\) denotes the final entry threshold, including all the finite-stage enlargements described below. Choose an initial coupling \(\beta_{\ast} > 1\) with \(\beta_{\ast} \geqslant d_{j_{\ast}}\), large enough that \(t (x) \leqslant 1\) for \(x \geqslant \beta_{\ast}\). This is possible since \(t (x) = x^{- 1 / 2} \exp ((\log \log x)^2) \to 0\). Set

\(\displaystyle \hat{\beta}_m = \beta_{\ast} + s_G s_m,\)

where \(s_G = \lambda_G = 2 b_0 (G) > 0\) is the slope constant (12.86) of Subsection 12.13; the intercept \(\beta_{\ast}\) is the \(\bar{\beta}\) of that section. For each fixed stage \(j > j_{\ast}\) there are only finitely many entry and comparison bounds. Require, at sufficiently large \(s\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} d_j \leqslant \beta_{\ast} + s_G s, \qquad e_j (\beta_{\ast}) \leqslant s, \qquad s > 0,\\ \log L_j \leqslant s / j, \qquad V_j (s) \leqslant e^s . \hspace{3cm} \text{(19.7)} \end{array}\)

Here \(d_j\) is the coupling entry, \(e_j\) the scale entry, and the finite-volume requirement is

\(\displaystyle V_j (s) = h_j (1 + B (s) + B_j^+ (s))^{r_j} + 100 (B (s) + 1) + 100 ((j + 3) L_j + 1),\)

with nonnegative integers \(h_j, r_j\) and

\(\displaystyle B (s) = \lceil \log^2 (\beta_{\ast} + s_G s) \rceil, \qquad B_j^+ (s) = \lceil \log^2 (\beta_{\ast} + s_G (s + \log L_j)) \rceil .\)

All inequalities in (19.7) hold eventually. The first four follow from positive linear growth in \(s\); for the last, both ceiling functions are \(O (s)\), so \(V_j (s)\) is bounded by a fixed polynomial and hence is \(o (e^s)\). Select \(b_{j - j_{\ast}}\) so that they hold whenever \(s \geqslant b_{j - j_{\ast}} \log 3\). The entry property of \(J_0\) and the lower bound in (19.6) now give all these requirements at every step with \(J (m + 1) > j_{\ast}\). On the first plateau, \(J (m + 1) = j_{\ast}\) and \(\hat{\beta}_m \geqslant \beta_{\ast} \geqslant d_{j_{\ast}}\). Consequently the coupling entrance \(d_{J (m + 1)} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) holds at every step, including \(m = 0\). The scale-entry and positive-scale requirements in (19.7) are only imposed after that first plateau; the finite first-plateau volume requirements are paid by the period choice below.

For the comparison interval at stage \(j\), suppose its required window \(w_j (s)\) is nonnegative and integer valued and \(w_j (s) / s \to 0\), and its minimum scale is \(r_j^0\). Choose \(c_{j - j_{\ast}}\) so large that

\(\displaystyle r_j^0 \leqslant s, \qquad w_j (s) \leqslant \lfloor k / 2 \rfloor\)

whenever \(k \geqslant c_{j - j_{\ast}}\) and \(k \log 3 \leqslant s \leqslant k \log L_j\). Such a choice exists because eventually \(w_j (s) / s \leqslant (2 \log L_j)^{- 1}\). At the center \(k = k_{j - j_{\ast}}\) of (19.5), (19.6) gives precisely these scale bounds. Thus the whole requested window around \(k\) stays at stage \(j\).

Only finitely many mesh steps remain in the first plateau. Choose an integer \(M\) at least their finitely many values \(V_{j_{\ast}} (s_m)\) and at least the required minimum physical period. Use periods

\(\displaystyle P_p = 4 (M + p + 1) .\)

The lattice side at level \(m\) is \(P_p e^{s_m}\). On the first plateau it pays \(V_{j_{\ast}} (s_m)\) by the choice of \(M\). At every later step it pays \(V_{J (m + 1)} (s_m)\) because that quantity is at most \(e^{s_m}\). The three summands of \(V_j\) pay respectively the specified volume, coarse-cover and fine-patch requirements.

The periods increase strictly and have arbitrarily late multiples of every positive integer \(c\). Indeed, given \(z\), take \(p = c (M + z + 1) - (M + 1)\). Then \(p \geqslant z\) and \(P_p = 4 c (M + z + 1)\). Finally

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \frac{\log L_{J (m + 1)}}{s_m} \leqslant \frac{1}{J (m + 1)} \longrightarrow 0\)

after the first plateau, by (19.7). Thus the relative logarithmic mesh increment tends to zero as required. The stage profiles, block lengths and kernel have all been retained; the new choices are their scheduling and periods.

19.2.3Countably many requests on that same sequence

Let \(F_{n, i} \in \mathbb{R}\) be a countable family of numerical requests selected before constructing the sequence. Define

\(\displaystyle E_j = 1 + \sum_{n = 0}^j \sum_{i = 0}^j |F_{n, i} | .\)

Include \(\exp (\exp E_j)\) in \(d_j\) before choosing \(\beta_{\ast}\) and applying the preceding construction. Since \(d_{J (m + 1)} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\), monotonicity of the logarithm twice gives

\(\displaystyle E_{J (m + 1)} \leqslant \log \log \hat{\beta}_m .\)

For fixed \(n\), the divergence of \(J\) supplies a first index after which \(n \leqslant J (m + 1)\). At every such \(m\) and every \(i \leqslant J (m + 1)\),

\(\displaystyle |F_{n, i} | \leqslant E_{J (m + 1)} \leqslant 1 + \log \log \hat{\beta}_m .\) (19.8)

The first index depends on \(n\). We have not required that a finite stage pay infinitely many independent requests.

There is also an all-stage form. For a prescribed real sequence \(H_j\), include \(H_j\) in that same final maximum for \(d_j\), again before choosing \(\beta_{\ast}\). Then

\(\displaystyle H_{J (m + 1)} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) (19.9)

for every \(m\), including zero. Both conclusions persist on every realization paying enlarged entry thresholds, because their proofs use only a lower bound on the threshold actually paid. Combining finitely many such purchases before choosing the realization therefore retains every earlier conclusion.

19.2.4Selecting the lengths before their numerical costs

The ordering of choices is essential. First fix the stage profiles, the original kernel, the row-bound families and their secondary widths. Their analytic suppliers give integer block-size floors. Take the pointwise maximum of the original floor, the flat and dual support floors, the curvature floor, the comparison floors, and the treasury and period floors. Two further families of floors are named explicitly, because later sections consume them as schedule conditions.

  1. Fixed-section thresholds. The family of fixed marked sections, its sections and its regradings are fixed before the profiles' bound maps and the floors (Subsection 13.4). For that family of fixed marked sections, Subsection 13.6 produces stage thresholds \(n^{\text{mk}}_j\) on the block length. Include \(n^{\text{mk}}_j\) in the floor of stage \(j\). Then every block side used at stage \(j\) is at least \(n^{\text{mk}}_j\); this is the comparison of thresholds with floor lengths required by the caller in Subsection 13.6.

  2. Drift and logarithmic-size ratios. For \(j \geqslant j_{\ast}\) let \(\varepsilon_j \geqslant 0\) be the drift allowance of the stage profile \(\pi_j\) and \(\lambda_j \geqslant 0\) the logarithm of one plus the sum of its path allowance, inverse width, column count, clip relaxation and support exponent. Both are fixed with the profile. Require

    \(\displaystyle \frac{\varepsilon_j}{\log L_j} \leqslant \min \left\{ \frac{s_G}{4}, \frac{1}{j^2} \right\}, \qquad \frac{\lambda_j}{\log L_j} \leqslant \frac{1}{j^2} \qquad (j \geqslant j_{\ast}) .\) (19.10)

    Since \(\varepsilon_j, \lambda_j\) do not depend on the length and \(c / \log n \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\) for each fixed \(c \geqslant 0\), there is an integer \(u_j\) such that (19.10) holds for every \(L_j \geqslant u_j\); include \(u_j\) in the floor of stage \(j\).

A nondecreasing odd sequence \(L_j\) above these floors exists: choose each \(L_j\) above the current finite maximum and above \(L_{j - 1}\). These choices precede any numerical request that depends on \(L_j\).

At step \(m\) the block side is \(L_{J (m + 1)}\) and the incoming stage is \(J (m + 1) \geqslant j_{\ast}\). Thus (19.10) at \(j = J (m + 1)\) reads

\(\displaystyle \frac{\varepsilon_{J (m + 1)}}{\log L_{J (m + 1)}} \leqslant \min \left\{ \frac{s_G}{4}, \frac{1}{J (m + 1)^2} \right\},\)

which, together with \(J (m) \to \infty\), is the drift condition (12.89) and the second inequality of (12.87) on the selected schedule, in the notation of Subsection 12.13 (there \(\varepsilon_{i + 1}\) is the allowance of the stage \(J (i + 1)\) and \(L_i = L_{J (i + 1)}\)).

With the lengths now fixed, form the actual post-length numerical costs. If two constructions require \(H_j^{(1)}\) and \(H_j^{(2)}\), pay \(H_j = \max (H_j^{(1)}, H_j^{(2)})\) by (19.9). Their countable refinements, as well as the previously selected inverse-width requests, are paid by (19.8). Add the nonlinear entry requirements of Subsection 19.1 and every other selected finite-stage threshold before constructing \(J\) and \(\hat{\beta}\). Every realization paying these enlarged thresholds preserves all the earlier purchases by the preceding monotonicity argument. Explicitly, the final entry threshold \(d_j\) includes the following stage thresholds, each of which is a finite maximum at stage \(j\) selected before \(\beta_{\ast}\):

  1. the terminal fee threshold \(k_j\) and the entrance delay of the family of fixed marked sections, both from Subsection 13.4; the latter is the delay condition of Subsection 13.6 for the adjacent stage pairs, including its profile-cost, credit and tail clauses;

  2. the startup and reset thresholds and the remaining entry delays listed at the end of Subsection 13.1, selected after the protection parameters there;

  3. the loop-capacity threshold \(\Delta_j\) of Lemma 19.1 below, for the side-dependent thresholds \(W_j (L_j)\) of Subsection 11.21.

Since \(d_{J (m + 1)} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) at every step, every one of these thresholds is paid at stage \(J (m + 1)\) by the coupling \(\hat{\beta}_m\). In particular \(k_{J (m + 1)} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\), which is the fee condition used in Proposition 13.15.

Lemma 19.1. (Loop capacities) Let \(\theta_j > 0\) be nonincreasing protection exponents, fixed after the block lengths, and put

\(\displaystyle P (x) = \exp ((\log \log x)^2), \qquad \text{loopCap}_m = 2 P (\hat{\beta}_m)^{\theta_{J (m)}} .\)

For every real sequence \(W_j\) there are thresholds \(\Delta_j\) such that, whenever \(\Delta_j \leqslant d_j\) for all \(j\), the selected schedule satisfies at every \(m \geqslant 0\), with \(j = J (m + 1)\),

\(\displaystyle W_j \leqslant \text{loopCap}_m, \qquad \tfrac{1}{2} \text{loopCap}_{m + 1} \leqslant \text{loopCap}_m, \qquad 1 \leqslant \text{loopCap}_m .\) (19.11)

Proof. Fix \(j\) and put \(v_j = s_G \log L_j \geqslant 0\). As \(x \to \infty\), \(2 P (x)^{\theta_j} \to \infty\), so eventually \(W_j \leqslant 2 P (x)^{\theta_j}\). For the second requirement let \(\ell = \log x\) and \(\ell' = \log (x + v_j)\). For \(x > 1\), \(\ell \leqslant \ell' \leqslant \ell + v_j / x\), since \(\log (1 + v_j / x) \leqslant v_j / x\). Using \(\log t \leqslant t - 1\),

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \log \ell' - \log \ell \leqslant \frac{\ell'}{\ell} - 1 \leqslant \frac{v_j}{x \ell} .\)

Once \(\ell \geqslant 1\) and \(x \geqslant v_j\), also \(0 \leqslant \log \ell \leqslant \ell\) and \(\log \ell' \leqslant \ell' - 1 \leqslant \ell + v_j / x - 1 \leqslant \ell + 1\), so

\(\displaystyle (\log \ell')^2 - (\log \ell)^2 \leqslant (\log \ell' + \log \ell) \frac{v_j}{x \ell} \leqslant \frac{(2 \ell + 1) v_j}{x \ell} \leqslant \frac{3 v_j}{x} .\)

Hence \(P (x + v_j)^{\theta_j} \leqslant e^{3 v_j \theta_j / x} P (x)^{\theta_j} \leqslant 2 P (x)^{\theta_j}\) as soon as \(3 v_j \theta_j / x \leqslant \log 2\). Let \(\Delta_j\) be a common threshold beyond which both properties hold, and \(x \geqslant \max \{e, v_j \}\).

Now let \(m \geqslant 0\), \(j = J (m + 1)\) and \(x = \hat{\beta}_m \geqslant d_j \geqslant \Delta_j\). Since \(P \geqslant 1\) and \(\theta_j \leqslant \theta_{J (m)}\) (because \(J (m) \leqslant j\) and the exponents are nonincreasing), \(P (x)^{\theta_j} \leqslant P (x)^{\theta_{J (m)}}\). The first property gives \(W_j \leqslant \text{loopCap}_m\). Next \(\hat{\beta}_{m + 1} = x + s_G \log L_{J (m + 1)} = x + v_j\) and \(J (m + 1) = j\), so

\(\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2} \text{loopCap}_{m + 1} = P (x + v_j)^{\theta_j} \leqslant 2 P (x)^{\theta_j} \leqslant 2 P (x)^{\theta_{J (m)}} = \text{loopCap}_m .\)

Finally \(P (x) \geqslant 1\) and \(\theta_{J (m)} > 0\) give \(\text{loopCap}_m \geqslant 2\).\(\Box\)

These are the three loop-capacity inequalities used in Subsection 11.21, with \(W_j\) its side-dependent threshold at stage \(j\) evaluated at \(L_j\); the threshold is indexed by the incoming stage and the capacity by the output level, as there.

Every step then uses the original length \(L_{J (m + 1)}\), so its actual finite patch is exactly the patch for which the post-length cost was computed. In particular a bound of the form \(e^{H_j} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) yields \(H_j \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_m\) on that patch; no estimate for a different block can be substituted. A maximum floor gives each of its component floors, and a maximum post-length cost gives each component cost. Applying the native analytic suppliers to these inequalities therefore supplies their conclusions on the same original levels and quadratic horizon.

This proves the scheduling and numerical selection step. The analytic hypotheses of those suppliers, and the sublinear window bounds used above, are still the specific estimates established in their own sections. Numerical selection does not replace them.

19.3Additional scale estimates for the operator-product expansion

The operator-product construction uses the same block lengths and hatted couplings as the centered estimates. Its additional numerical requirements can all be imposed after the block lengths are chosen and before the scale sequence is realized. We verify them explicitly.

For the final construction choose the profile order and spatial power at stage \(j\) to be at least \(6 + j\) and \(2 + j\), respectively, and choose the support exponent to be exactly \(100\) at every stage. Constant support exponents satisfy the monotonicity requirement. Increasing the order and spatial power does not require increasing this exponent: the finite profile bounds are selected afresh at the chosen orders, and their later floors and delays are allowed to depend on them. Thus the common funding and observable purchases apply with this choice. The constant exponent will also be retained when intermediate clusters are regrouped.

19.3.1The halo and block-length inequalities

At a transition write \(L\) for the already selected integer block side, \(x\) for the hatted coupling and \(y = x + s_G \log L\) for the next hatted coupling, where \(s_G > 0\) is the slope constant of Subsection 19.2. Set \(B (x) = \lceil (\log x)^2 \rceil\) and define

\(\displaystyle H_L (x, y) = (1 + L (B (x) + B (y) + 1)^2)^4 .\)

For fixed \(L\) and any fixed \(A \geqslant 0\), there is an entrance threshold such that

\(\displaystyle AH_L (x, y) \leqslant \sqrt{x} \) (19.12)

above that threshold. Indeed \(y / x \to 1\), so eventually \(B (y) \leqslant 2 B (x)\). Hence \(B (x) + B (y) + 1 \leqslant 4 (1 + B (x))\) and \(H_L (x, y) = O ((1 + \log x)^{16})\). The logarithm of \((1 + \log x)^{16} / \sqrt{x}\) tends to \(- \infty\), proving (19.12). Apply this with \(A = 101\), the fine support exponent plus one. The power four is the spatial dimension; the factor \(L\) is needed because two child anchors which first meet after blocking can be separated by a block side as well as the two cover radii.

The stronger block-length condition

\(\displaystyle L \leqslant \log x \) (19.13)

is obtained by requiring \(x \geqslant e^L\). It is a condition at a fixed profile stage, not a uniform bound over unchosen block lengths. Let \(C_i\) denote the column count at stage \(i\). At a transition with fine stage \(j\) the coarse stage is either \(j\) or \(j - 1\). Imposing

\(\displaystyle x \geqslant \max (L_j, C_j, C_{j - 1}) \) (19.14)

therefore gives both adjacent column bounds and the block bound required by the logarithmic estimates. For \(j = 0\) use \(C_0\) in place of \(C_{j - 1}\).

19.3.2Uniform logarithmic bounds for the fixed-stage data

Write \(h_i\) for the path bound at stage \(i\), \(\sigma_i > 0\) for its width, \(\lambda_i > 0\) for its linear clipping parameter, \(r_i\) for its relaxation parameter, and \(d_i\) for its direct clipping parameter. These are fixed finite nonnegative numbers, with the stated strictly positive denominators. Let \(D_G = \dim_{\mathbb{R}} M_{r_G} (\mathbb{C})\), and let \(n_{\square}\) be the number of oriented plaquette-axis types used in the action. The elementary vacuum bound has the form

\(\displaystyle V_i (x) = 2 (1 + (4 h_i + 1)) + 1 + n_{\square} (d_i + h_i^2) + 4 D_G (\log 16 + \tfrac{1}{2} \log x) .\)

Thus \(V_i (x) + n_{\square} = B_i + 2 D_G \log x\) for a fixed constant \(B_i\). At each fixed stage we can require simultaneously

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} L_j \leqslant \log x, \qquad 10 / \sigma_i \leqslant \log x, \qquad 10 / \lambda_i \leqslant \log x, \hspace{3cm} \text{(19.15)} \\ V_i (x) + n_{\square} \leqslant (\log x)^2, \hspace{3cm} \text{(19.16)} \\ 10^5 (i + 1) \leqslant \log x, \qquad 10^5 (1 + h_i + 100 + r_i + \sigma_i + C_i + d_i + \lambda_i) \leqslant \log x. \hspace{3cm} \text{(19.17)} \end{array}\)

For (19.16), take \(z = \log x \geqslant \max (1, B_i, 2 D_G + 1)\); then \(B_i + 2 D_G z \leqslant z + (z - 1) z = z^2\). The remaining inequalities hold because \(\log x \to \infty\). To allow the stage indexing at both ends of a transition, purchase (19.15)–(19.17) for every \(i \leqslant j\) with the actual block side \(L_j\). This is a finite list at each \(j\).

Take the maximum of these thresholds and those for (19.12)–(19.14), and include these thresholds in the final entry thresholds \(d_j\) of Subsection 19.2 before \(\beta_{\ast}\) is chosen. Every estimate holds for all larger \(x\), so this preserves earlier purchases. At level \(m\) take \(j = J (m + 1)\) and \(i = J (m)\). The common scale construction ensures the entrance condition at this \(j\), and the identities for the actual clock give

\(\displaystyle L = L_{J (m + 1)}, \qquad x = \hat{\beta}_m, \qquad y = \hat{\beta}_{m + 1} .\)

Thus (19.12)–(19.17) hold on the selected family, at all steps and periods. They require no subsequent change of its history.

19.3.3Two consequences used in choosing intermediate scales

The physical scale satisfies \(\hat{\beta}_m = \beta_{\ast} + s_G \log a_m^{- 1}\), with \(s_G > 0\). For every fixed integer \(q \geqslant 0\),

\(\displaystyle a_m \hat{\beta}_m^q \longrightarrow 0. \) (19.18)

Indeed, setting \(s = \log a_m^{- 1} \to \infty\) gives an exponential \(e^{- s}\) times a polynomial of degree \(q\). Also, for every \(c, \epsilon > 0\) and integer \(q \geqslant 0\), eventually

\(\displaystyle c (\log x)^q \leqslant \epsilon \exp \{(\log \log x)^2 \} . \) (19.19)

The logarithm of the ratio of the left side to the right side is \(\log (c / \epsilon) + qu - u^2\) with \(u = \log \log x \to \infty\), so the ratio tends to zero.

Finally, if \(r > 1\) and \(ra_i < 1\), there is \(j < i\) such that

\(\displaystyle a_{j + 1} \leqslant ra_i < a_j . \) (19.20)

Choose the first index \(k\) with \(a_k \leqslant ra_i\). It exists since \(i\) qualifies; it is positive since \(a_0 = 1 > ra_i\). Set \(j = k - 1\). Minimality gives the strict upper inequality and \(k \leqslant i\) gives \(j < i\). These are the intermediate-scale choices used with the common clock. The analytic OPE estimates remain those of the preceding local construction; (19.12)–(19.20) supply its additional scale inequalities.

19.4The operator-product estimates on the selected history

The local projection and the several-insertion expansion must use the same adjacent transition. We establish this compatibility on the family of Subsection 13.10. Throughout this section the profiles have support exponent \(100\), as selected in Subsection 19.3. The orders and spatial powers still increase. No background representation is chosen again.

19.4.1Finite elementary representations of transferred fields

Fix a finite torus, a representation \(R\) and an anchor \(o\). For a support \(X\) and a nonnegative bound \(h\), write

\(\displaystyle m_R (X, h) = e^{bC_B (X)} h,\)

where \(b \geqslant 0\) and \(B\) are its support exponent and covering radius. The reserved regular slot on each nonempty support and the reserved forced slot at each admissible support–inventory pair are retained from the marked construction. A finite elementary representation of an ordinary part \(g\) and a forced profile \(K\) means identities

\(\displaystyle g = p + \sum_{j \in I} z_j \text{ord} (M_j), \qquad K = \sum_{j \in I} z_j \text{flag} (M_j), \qquad \sum_{j \in I} |z_j | \text{cost} (M_j) \leqslant A.\) (19.21)

Here \(I\) is finite, all insertion supports contain \(o\), and \(p \in \mathbb{C}\) is the separately recorded constant. In particular \(K\) is the actual profile, not merely an array with the same integrated density.

Suppose first that \(g = \sum_i f_i\), where the sum is countable, each \(f_i\) is measurable and gauge invariant, depends only on its support \(X_i\) and the flags there, vanishes unless \(X_i\) is healthy, and \(o \in X_i\). Assume

\(\displaystyle |f_i (U) | \leqslant h_i, \qquad \sum_i m_R (X_i, h_i) \leqslant A.\)

Since \(b \geqslant 0\), \(\sum_i h_i < \infty\). Group by the finitely many supports of the torus and put

\(\displaystyle f_X = \sum_{i : X_i = X} f_i, \qquad h_X = \sum_{i : X_i = X} h_i .\)

These series converge uniformly and absolutely. Their sums preserve measurability, gauge invariance, local dependence and the healthy-set condition. The real and imaginary parts of \(f_X\) can be placed in the reserved regular slot of \(X\), each with bound \(h_X\). Their coefficients are \(1\) and \(i\). The resulting elementary representation has ordinary part \(f_X\), zero forced profile and bound at most \(2 m_R (X, h_X)\). Zero functions may be omitted. Summing over the finite support set gives (19.21) with \(p = 0\), \(K = 0\) and bound \(2 A\). The equality of the bound sums follows by grouping an absolutely convergent nonnegative series; its support weight is constant within each group.

The same argument applies to a dominated forced array. Group its coefficients by their support–inventory pair \((X, J)\). Suppose the grouped coefficient vanishes unless the flags in \(X\) are exactly \(J\), where \(J\) is nonempty, and every nonzero group has \(o \in X\). Use the reserved forced slot at \((X, J)\) and split the grouped function into real and imaginary parts. An array with total weighted majorant \(A\) therefore has a finite elementary representation of its profile, with ordinary part zero and bound at most \(2 A\). The exact inventory condition is preserved under grouping. This construction uses only finitely many pairs even when the original array has infinitely many serial addresses.

We apply this compression to an elementary insertion \(M\) at a fine scale, with mass \(m = \text{cost} (M) \geqslant 0\). Denote its block transfer by \(\mathcal{T}\) and the forced derivative fixed in Subsection 9.2 by \(\mathcal{D}M\). The exact differentiated expansion gives an ordinary sum and this forced derivative. Its nonprincipal ordinary mass is at most \(p_M\); its possible principal term has mass at most \(E_{\ast} m\); and its forced mass is at most \(q_M\). The local derivative and support estimates give

\(\displaystyle 2 p_M \leqslant m, \qquad 2 q_M \leqslant m, \qquad E_{\ast} \leqslant L.\) (19.22)

Here \(E_{\ast}\) is the fixed exponential charge for enlarging the inherited support. The last inequality is the geometric block-length floor in Subsection 9.2. More precisely, \(p_M\) is the nonprincipal regular bound of that construction, and \(q_M\) is its forced derivative bound before division by the clipping gain. Put \(\kappa = \sqrt{\text{coarse forced allowance}}\) and let \(H_0\) be the degree-zero splitting factor. Its explicit form is

\(\displaystyle H_0 = 1 + 2 |v_0 | (1 + C_{\text{orb}} + |f_0 |) \geqslant 1, \qquad c_0 = L^{- 2 / 5} / H_0 \leqslant 1,\)

where \(v_0, f_0\) are the fixed finite local-splitting constants and \(C_{\text{orb}} \geqslant 0\) is its motion bound. The two degree-zero payments are exactly

\(\displaystyle 2 p_M \leqslant c_0 m, \qquad 2 \kappa^{- 1} q_M \leqslant c_0 m, \qquad 0 < \kappa \leqslant 1.\)

They imply the first two inequalities in (19.22). This uses the same unscaled forced derivative as the profile identity. The principal contribution is absent for a forced input. For an ordinary input its masked value is at most its original height: the single-insertion, order-zero cluster has no additional insertion factor. Enlarging its support therefore costs precisely \(E_{\ast}\) times its original mass. All remaining clusters are charged to \(p_M\).

The compression just proved gives a finite elementary representation of the transferred ordinary part and the profile of \(\mathcal{D}M\) with bound at most

\(\displaystyle 2 (p_M + E_{\ast} m) + 2 q_M \leqslant (2 + 2 L) m \leqslant 6 Lm,\)

where \(L \geqslant 1\). Its inserted density is exactly \(\mathcal{T}\) applied to the density of \(M\), by the differentiated block identity. Thus the profile as well as the density is retained.

This conclusion extends to every finite representation (19.21). Apply it to each \(M_j\) and multiply its output coefficients by \(z_j\). Concatenating the finite lists gives total bound at most \(6 LA\). The constant term remains \(p\) because \(\mathcal{T} \rho_f = \rho_c\). Finite sums commute with integration: each elementary density is integrable under the lawful representation, and the transition is the already constructed kernel. The output profile is exactly \(\sum_j z_j \mathcal{P} (\mathcal{D}M_j)\). This proves the transport bound needed when a grouped observable is carried through intermediate scales.

19.4.2Several insertions and their common allowances

At adjacent levels \(m + 1, m\) set

\(\displaystyle x = \hat{\beta}_m, \quad y = \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}, \quad L = L_{J (m + 1)}, \quad N = N_{J (m + 1)}, \quad \delta_m = y^{- 2}, \quad \sigma_m = y^{- 1} .\)

Here \(N_j \geqslant 1\) is the nondecreasing integer occupancy schedule fixed in Subsection 13.4, so \(N\) is the purchased occupancy bound at the stage \(J (m + 1)\) of the incoming level. Let \(\chi_m\) be the forced-gas bound from the two successive absorptions, with its actual positive suppression constant and this block length. It is the bound denoted by \(t\) in Subsection 3.16. The single-insertion and arbitrary finite-menu estimates of that section use this same \(\mathcal{D}\), with input support exponent \(96\), ordinary output exponent \(100 + 1 / 2\), and forced output exponent \(101\). Their output allowances are respectively \(1\) and \(\chi_m\).

To apply them to several slots in each replica cell, take the finite marker set to be the product \(V \times S\) of the cell set \(V\) and its slot set \(S\), with a fine site \(o (v, \varsigma)\) for every marker. The compiler of the finite-menu construction accepts any finite marker set, but its estimates are conditional on the hypotheses of Subsection 3.16, and these must now hold for the whole product set:

  1. every marker weight \(r (v, \varsigma)\) is positive;

  2. the parent-site occupancy is at most \(N\): for every coarse site \(y\), at most \(N\) markers \((v, \varsigma) \in V \times S\) have \(\text{parent} (o (v, \varsigma)) = y\);

  3. the list of insertions is a menu offer for \(V \times S\), with the positions \(o\) and the weights \(r\): the named sites of each insertion lie in its fine crest, and the offer bound holds at exponent \(b_f - 4\) and allowance \(u\).

Condition (2) counts every pair (cell, slot), so it does not follow from an occupancy bound for the cells alone, and enlarging the slot sets can violate it at fixed \(N\). Nothing in the regrouping argument below supplies (2) or (3); they are hypotheses on the marker set and its offer, checked by the caller with the purchased value \(N = N_{J (m + 1)}\) and the exponents just stated. The ordinary coefficient is indexed by individual markers and the forced coefficients by their subsets. Grouping by the cell coordinate is exactly the substitution used in Subsection 7.6. The quiet coefficient stays the actual background profile. Before grouping, the singleton coefficient for each individual marker (cell, slot) is the sum of the derivatives of its original insertions through \(\mathcal{D}\). After grouping, the coefficient of a singleton cell is the old coefficient at that cell's full slot set, by the definition of the regrouping map in Subsection 7.6. For example, with two slots, the coefficient of \(e_{\{1, 2\}}\) in \(\exp (z_1 e_{\{1\}} + z_2 e_{\{2\}})\) is \(z_1 z_2\); this is the resulting singleton-cell coefficient. The motion identities follow by applying the same permutation to the cell coordinate and leaving the slot coordinate fixed; equal weights on corresponding slots preserve every weighted estimate. Thus increasing the number of slots does not change the background transition or its insertion transport.

Write \(B = B_m\) and \(\alpha = 100 + 1 / 2\). The enlarged-support bound of Subsection 7.5 gives the local bound

\(\displaystyle s_m = 2 (1 + N) \delta_m (Ne^{\alpha} + 1) + (1 + N) \chi_m .\) (19.23)

The two copies in its first term are the healthy and unhealthy pieces of the ordinary grouped entry. The last term is the forced entry. The purchases in (9.15) give, simultaneously,

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} 0 < \delta_m \leqslant 1, \qquad \chi_m \leqslant 1, \qquad (2 B_m + 1)^4 s_m \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad e^{s_m} - 1 \leqslant 1, \hspace{3cm} \text{(19.24)} \\ \alpha (2048 B_m + 1)^4 \leqslant \sqrt{\hat{\beta}_{m + 1}} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(19.25)} \end{array}\)

The allowance for the elementary degree-\(d\) projection is \(X_m = \exp (\sqrt{\hat{\beta}_{m + 1}}) \geqslant 1\), by (9.14). Both that projection and the \(6 L\) elementary transport use the same \(\mathcal{D}\) just identified.

19.4.3Uniformity above the reference level

If \(m \geqslant t_d\), monotonicity gives \(d \leqslant J (m)\). Proposition 19.3. (Boundary barycenter) Use one mixed table and its character tables from Subsection 5.5, with their common field union and the slab bounds of Proposition 5.24. Lemma 19.4. (Identifying the full mesh limit) Fix a test and its insertion labels. Suppose that at every suitable period \(p\) the full sequence \(W_{N, p}\) converges to some \(\ell_p\).
