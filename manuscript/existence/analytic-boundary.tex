\section{Analytic time continuation and joint boundary values}
\label{sec:analytic-boundary}
We next construct the tempered boundary distribution used in the field
reconstruction argument. The crucial bound is a power of the smallest imaginary-time
gap. No bound uniform in the number of insertions is needed in this section.

\subsection{Centered insertions and continuation}
Work in the selected Euclidean Hilbert space with heat contractions $E_t$,
spatial translations $U(x)$, and vacuum $\Omega_E$. In this section we use the
complex heat extension $E(z)$ on $\operatorname{Re}z>0$: it is holomorphic in
operator norm, satisfies the semigroup law and $\|E(z)\|\leq1$, agrees with
$E_t$ for positive real $z$, and commutes with $U(x)$. These properties were established in
Proposition~\ref{prop:complex-heat}.

For a finite-level label $v$, define $B_p(v)$, $p>0$, to be the slab operator
of width $2p$ with its single insertion at spatial position zero and time $p$.
All exterior matrix elements are precisely the corresponding Euclidean
correlations. Multilinearity of those correlations, tested against the dense
span of Euclidean words on both sides, proves linearity in $v$. The same
argument, using compatibility of the finite-level inclusions in every
correlation, proves that these maps agree under inclusion. They therefore
define one linear map $B_p:E\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H_E)$ on the union of the
field spaces. This use of all exterior words is what makes the union map
well-defined.

The single-insertion case of the all-copy slab bound gives, for each fixed
$v\in E$, constants $C_v>0$ and an integer $a_v\geq0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bar-power}
 \|B_p(v)\|\leq C_vp^{-a_v},\qquad 0<p<1.
\end{equation}
Indeed, choose a finite level representing $v$ and a bound for the norm of its
chosen raw representative. The centered slab has both boundary clearances
$p$, so the estimate with one insertion applies with buffer $p$.
The padding identities for slab operators give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bar-padding}
 B_{p+u}(v)=E_u B_p(v)E_u\qquad(p>0,\ u\geq0).
\end{equation}
Both statements retain the constants of the all-copy estimate; no uniform
bound over all labels in the union is asserted.

For times $z_i=t_i+iy_i$ with $y_1<\cdots<y_n$, choose $0<p<1$ with
$4p<y_j-y_i$ whenever $i<j$. Write
\[
 R_p(z,x)=E(-iz-2p)U(x).
\]
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continued-matrix}
 F_n(z,x;v)=\langle\Omega_E,
 B_p(v_1)R_p(z_2-z_1,x_2-x_1)B_p(v_2)\cdots
 R_p(z_n-z_{n-1},x_n-x_{n-1})B_p(v_n)\Omega_E\rangle.
\end{equation}
For $n=1$ the intervening factors are absent; for $n=0$ the value is one.
All arguments of $E$ have positive real part.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:continued-matrix}
The value \eqref{eq:continued-matrix} is independent of the admissible width
$p$. It is multilinear in the labels, jointly continuous in $(z,x)$, and
holomorphic in the complex time variables on the strictly ordered tube.
At purely imaginary times it equals the Euclidean correlation of the
insertions at $(x_i,y_i)$. For each fixed label tuple there are $C\geq0$ and
$m\in\mathbb N$ such that, for $0<k\leq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-power}
 y_j-y_i\geq k\ (i<j)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |F_n(t+iy,x;v)|\leq Ck^{-m}
\end{equation}
uniformly in all real times $t$ and spatial positions $x$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Compare widths $p$ and $q=p+u$. For $u>0$, the semigroup law and spatial
commutation imply
\[
 R_p(z,x)=E_uR_q(z,x)E_u.
\]
Substituting \eqref{eq:bar-padding} for every $B_q$ in the product at width
$q$ merges the two adjacent $E_u$ factors around each $R_q$ into $R_p$.
The exterior $E_u$ factors disappear against the invariant vacuum. This
proves equality; $u=0$ is immediate, and any two widths can be ordered.
The admissibility inequalities are strict, so the same fixed width works on
a neighborhood of every point of the tube. There the product is holomorphic
in all time variables. It is jointly continuous in the spatial variables as
a vector matrix element: strong continuity of $U(x)$, local norm continuity
of the other factors, and their local uniform bounds prove this successively
from the rightmost vector. No operator-norm continuity of spatial
translations is required. Multilinearity follows from that of each $B_p$.

At imaginary times each intervening factor is the ordinary positive heat
translation through the unused gap. Starting with the rightmost insertion,
the slab insertion rule and the time and space translation rules show that
the suffix train after a preceding reference $(X,Y)$ is the Euclidean
word vector with suffix positions $(x_i-X,y_i-Y-p)$. For the first suffix
point its preceding heat duration is $y_i-Y-2p>0$: the centered slab
places that insertion at height $p$, and translation through this duration
places it at $y_i-Y-p$. The remaining suffix points follow the same
translation, proving the induction invariant. Pairing the resulting train
with the initial centered insertion gives the complete word shifted by
$(-x_1,-y_1+p)$. Pairing with the vacuum and using translation invariance
removes this last shift and gives the asserted Euclidean correlation.

All intervening factors have norm at most one. Thus the absolute value is
at most $\prod_i\|B_p(v_i)\|$, since $\|\Omega_E\|=1$. When the gaps are
at least $k$, choose $p=k/8$. Equation~\eqref{eq:bar-power} gives
\[
 m=\sum_i a_{v_i},\qquad C=\prod_i(C_{v_i}8^{a_{v_i}}),
\]
and proves \eqref{eq:gap-power}. The empty product and sum also cover $n=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Differentiating tested functions}
Fix the arity and labels, and write $X=(t_1,x_1,\ldots,t_n,x_n)$ for real
positions. For strictly ordered heights $y$ and a joint Schwartz test $f$, put
\[
 \psi_y(f)=\int F_n(t+iy,x;v)f(X)\,dX.
\]
For a real height vector $a$, let $\partial_a$ denote differentiation of the
real time coordinates in direction $a$, and put $D_a=-i\partial_a$ on tests.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tested-cr}
The integral defining $\psi_y$ is absolutely convergent, depends continuously
on $y$ in the open ordered cone, and satisfies
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\psi_{y+sa}(f)=\psi_{y+sa}(D_af)
\]
whenever the heights remain strictly ordered.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Near any strictly ordered $y$, all gaps have a common positive lower bound.
Equation~\eqref{eq:gap-power} therefore supplies a bound uniform in $X$ and
in the nearby heights. Multiplication by the integrable function $|f|$
proves integrability and, by dominated convergence, height continuity.
For the derivative, restrict the holomorphic function to the complex line
which changes all complex time coordinates by $\zeta a$. A fixed disc about
zero stays in the tube for every real configuration $X$ and every height in
a sufficiently small neighborhood. The same gap bound holds throughout that
disc. Cauchy's formula therefore bounds the first complex derivative by a
constant independent of $X$. The Cauchy--Riemann equations give the pointwise
height derivative $i\partial_aF$. This uniform bound times $|f|$ justifies
differentiation under the integral.

To move the derivative onto the test, use smooth compactly supported cutoffs
$\chi_R(X)=\chi(X/R)$ equal to one on the radius-$R$ ball and supported on
the radius-$2R$ ball. Integration by parts for the real time direction gives
\[
 \int i(\partial_aF)f\chi_R
 =-\int iF(\partial_af)\chi_R-\int iFf\partial_a\chi_R.
\]
The last integral has magnitude at most a fixed constant times
$R^{-1}\|f\|_{L^1}$, hence tends to zero. The other two integrals converge
by the uniform bounds for $F$ and $\partial_aF$ and the integrability of
$f,\partial_af$. This proves the identity, including its sign.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact Taylor formula at the edge}
Let $r=(0,1,\ldots,n-1)$ and $D=D_r$. If $y$ is only weakly ordered, then
$y+sr$ has all distinct-index gaps at least $s$ for $s>0$; for arities zero
and one the conditions on pairs are empty. Choose $m,C$ from
\eqref{eq:gap-power} for the fixed labels. Define
\begin{align}
 \widetilde\psi_y(f)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{(-1)^j}{j!}\psi_{y+r}(D^jf)
   -\int_0^1\frac{(-s)^m}{m!}
                    \psi_{y+sr}(D^{m+1}f)\,ds.
                    \label{eq:edge-taylor}
\end{align}
This formula uses values strictly inside the tube even when some heights
of $y$ coincide.

\begin{theorem}[Joint tempered boundary]\label{thm:joint-boundary}
Formula~\eqref{eq:edge-taylor} defines a continuous linear functional of $f$
on the joint Schwartz space, continuously in every weakly ordered $y$.
It agrees with $\psi_y(f)$ when $y$ is strictly ordered. In particular
\[
 W_n(v)(f):=\widetilde\psi_0(f)
\]
is a tempered distribution and
\[
 \psi_y(f)\longrightarrow W_n(v)(f)
 \quad\text{as }y\to0\text{ through all strictly ordered heights}.
\]
The limit is multilinear in the labels. No restriction on the ratios of
height gaps is imposed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First take strictly ordered $y$ and set
\[
 P(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{(-s)^j}{j!}\psi_{y+sr}(D^jf).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:tested-cr}, differentiating the finite sum cancels
successive terms, leaving
\[
 P'(s)=\frac{(-s)^m}{m!}\psi_{y+sr}(D^{m+1}f).
\]
Integrating from zero to one and using $P(0)=\psi_y(f)$ proves the asserted
identity with \eqref{eq:edge-taylor}.

For weakly ordered $y$ and $0<s\leq1$, the gap estimate gives
\[
 \left|\frac{(-s)^m}{m!}\psi_{y+sr}(D^{m+1}f)\right|
 \leq\frac{C}{m!}\|D^{m+1}f\|_{L^1}.
\]
Thus the remainder is absolutely integrable uniformly in $y$. For each
$s>0$ its integrand is continuous in $y$ on the closed ordered cone, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tested-cr}. Dominated convergence proves continuity of the
remainder, and the finite sum in \eqref{eq:edge-taylor} is continuous for
the same reason. The formula also gives the uniform estimate
\[
 |\widetilde\psi_y(f)|\leq
 C\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\|D^jf\|_{L^1}}{j!}
   +\frac{C}{m!}\|D^{m+1}f\|_{L^1}.
\]
These are continuous seminorms of $f$ in the Schwartz topology. Explicitly,
in real dimension $d=4n$, each $L^1$ norm is bounded by
$\int(1+|X|)^{-d-1}dX$ times the corresponding weighted supremum norm;
$D^j$ is a finite sum of ordinary derivatives. This proves continuity as a
test functional.

The cone of strictly ordered heights accumulates at every weakly ordered
height, by the path $y+sr$. Hence its limits determine the functional
uniquely. Linearity in $f$ and multilinearity in every label follow by
passing those identities for the interior integrals to the common boundary
limit. This also shows that the answer is independent of the chosen power
bound $m,C$, although those bounds may depend on the label tuple. Finally,
continuity of \eqref{eq:edge-taylor} at zero and its agreement with the
interior integrals give the full asserted limit.
\end{proof}

The theorem constructs boundary values for joint tests on all real positions,
not just factorized tests and not just pointwise limits. To obtain the heat
vectors needed for positivity, one must also identify their Gram matrix with
these same continued functions at mixed positive and negative heights. That
identification is the next step.
