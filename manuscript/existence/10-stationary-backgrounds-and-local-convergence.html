10Stationary backgrounds and local convergence

10.1Local action bounds for the stationary construction

We verify the localized derivative input of Subsection 10.2 for the actual unmasked block action. The action is a sum over sites. Its positive-order coordinate derivatives have uniform local bounds, although its scalar value need not have a volume-independent bound.

Fix the block side \(L\), the patch, the direct clip, and the kernel parameters, including its positive frame and predictor cutoffs and positive penalty coefficient. Fix also an order \(n \geqslant 3\) and an exponential path width \(\mu > 0\). The incoming path data have row, cubic and quartic budgets at most \(C_0\) at width \(\mu\); the loop budget is at most \(\Lambda\). These budgets are the sums of the absolute coefficients with their specified exponential length weights. All constants below may depend on this fixed data and on the group, but not on the auxiliary torus, its forest, or the tag choices.

10.1.1From a finite read set to a localized derivative

A function based at a site \(v\) has read radius \(R\) if changing coordinates anchored farther than \(R\) from \(v\) leaves the function unchanged. A derivative containing any one such coordinate is zero: restrict to a line varying that coordinate, differentiate the constant function, and commute derivatives. If derivatives through order \(n\) have operator norm at most \(A\), then, for \(q \leqslant n\),

\(\displaystyle | \partial_{i_1} \cdots \partial_{i_q} u| \leqslant Ae^{anR} \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( - a \sum_{h = 1}^q \text{dist} (v, a_{i_h}) \right) .\) (10.1)

If every tested coordinate lies in the read set, the sum of their distances is at most \(nR\); otherwise the left side vanishes. This also holds for vector-valued expressions with norms in place of absolute values.

For a path atom of total length \(t\), the fine forest chart and a signed motion enlarge its read radius to at most \(2 t + 1 + L\). The path itself travels at most \(2 t\) in fine units; reversing an oriented bond can move its initial point by one, and the coarse replica coordinate is anchored at most \(L\) from the fine bond. This estimate remains valid for winding paths: periodic distance is bounded by lifted path length.

Choose

\(\displaystyle a = \frac{\mu}{4 (n + 1) (1 + L)} .\)

The elementary inequality

\(\displaystyle e^{\mu t / 2} e^{an (2 t + 1 + L)} \leqslant e^{\mu t} e^{\mu}\) (10.2)

follows by comparing exponents: the second exponent is at most \(\mu t / 2 + \mu\). The holomorphic path bounds at width \(\mu / 2\) give a derivative bound \(A_n e^{\mu t / 2}\) for a slot, and \(A_{n, k} \text{magnitude} (q) e^{\mu t / 2}\) for a degree-\(k\) atom \(q\). To recall why the constants are uniform, each matrix transport is a finite product; on the fixed complex neighborhood its norm is bounded exponentially in path length. The Cauchy estimate on a fixed smaller neighborhood bounds derivatives by that exponential times a constant depending only on \(n\) and the neighborhood. Composing with the fine exponential chart uses its uniform link derivatives and the finite partition formula. The fixed color projections and multilinear atom contractions contribute only the constants \(A_n, A_{n, k}\). Combining this estimate with (10.1) and (10.2) pays the localized derivative by the original width-\(\mu\) coefficient budget.

For an infinite row or atom family, the paid coefficient sequence is summable. It bounds the values and derivatives through order \(n\) on the common coordinate ball. Uniform convergence and the fundamental theorem of calculus along coordinate segments justify termwise differentiation, inductively in the derivative order. The underlying holomorphic series converges normally on a smaller complex neighborhood, so it is smooth to all orders there. Consequently a row based at \(v\) has localized derivative cost \(C_0 A_n\), and a cubic or quartic family has cost \(C_0 A_{n, 3}\) or \(C_0 A_{n, 4}\). A loop family has cost \(\Lambda A_{n, 2}\). The latter dependence is linear in \(\Lambda\).

10.1.2Summing sites without a volume factor

Suppose a site term \(u_v\) has derivatives bounded by

\(\displaystyle | \partial_{i_1} \cdots \partial_{i_q} u_v | \leqslant B \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( - a \sum_h \text{dist} (v, a_{i_h}) \right) .\)

Fix a tested coordinate \(i_t\) and set \(s = \sum_h \text{dist} (v, a_{i_h})\). Then \(\text{dist} (v, a_{i_t}) \leqslant s\) and

\(\displaystyle \sum_h \text{dist} (a_{i_t}, a_{i_h}) \leqslant (q + 1) s \leqslant (n + 1) s.\)

Splitting the exponent in two gives

\(\displaystyle e^{- as} \leqslant e^{- (a / 2) \text{dist} (v, a_{i_t})} \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( - \frac{a}{2 (n + 1)} \sum_h \text{dist} (a_{i_t}, a_{i_h}) \right) .\)

Summing \(v\) costs only the distance-shell sum at width \(a / 2\). If at most \(h\) terms have the same base site, multiply this cost by \(h\). We have obtained the bound (10.3), with width \(a / [2 (n + 1)]\), for every positive derivative of the site sum. The argument needs a tested coordinate as root, so it makes no assertion at order zero. Averaging over signed motions preserves the cost: each motion permutes sites and the averaging weights sum to one.

The classical site density has three types of terms. The direct plaquette term has a fixed read radius and derivative bounds from its four unitary factors and the smooth group penalty. The squared-row term is \(\| \ell (U)\|^2 / 2\). Its row value and derivatives are bounded by the row budget just proved; the finite product rule bounds its derivatives by a constant depending on \(C_0\). Its localized bounds are preserved by that product rule, since the exponential factors for the split derivative slots multiply to the factor for all slots. Finally the cubic and quartic terms have the atom bounds above. Adding these terms and summing sites proves the required localized derivative estimate for the fine classical action. The classical bound depends on \(C_0\) and the direct clip, and does not depend on the loop budget \(\Lambda\).

10.1.3The prediction penalty on the same ball

The minimizing frame and predictor of Subsection 8.8 are smooth near the identity. The finite-read transplantation argument of Subsection 8.21 makes their fixed-order neighborhood and derivative bounds uniform over periods. We must also verify that all tags select that same branch there.

On a coordinate ball of radius \(r\), each fine bond chord is at most \(C_1 r\), by integrating its first derivative from the identity. Every path used by a frame is of bounded length for the fixed patch, so its transport chord is at most \(C_2 r\). Shrink \(r\) until the strict frame test holds. The uniqueness of the normalized minimizing frame identifies all frame tags there. Its coordinates and first derivatives are uniformly bounded on a smaller ball. A connector contains the frame at each of its two endpoints and a straight fine path. Its chord is therefore at most \(C_3 r\), with a common constant \(C_3 > 0\).

Let \(Y_i\) be these connectors and \(w_i \geqslant 0\) their weights, \(\sum_i w_i = 1\), and write \(\Phi (g)\) for half the squared chord from \(g\) to the identity. Evaluating the prediction objective at the identity gives

\(\displaystyle \min_u \sum_i w_i \Phi (Y_i u^{- 1}) \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2} (C_3 r)^2 .\)

Choose \(r < \sqrt{\zeta} / C_3\), where \(\zeta > 0\) is the predictor cutoff. This inequality is then strictly below the cutoff. Also

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_i w_i \text{mat} (Y_i) - 1 \right| \leqslant c_G C_3 r,\)

where \(c_G\) converts chord to matrix norm. Shrink \(r\) further so this lies in the fixed nearest-point tube. The selected predictor is the smooth nearest-point projection of this weighted matrix average. Hence every predictor tag agrees with this same smooth expression throughout the ball. Product and composition rules bound its derivatives uniformly, using the same actual endpoint frames in both occurrences.

For each coarse bond, the smoothing penalty is the group bound of the coarse field times the inverse predicted field. Its value and its derivatives through order \(n\) have a common bound on this ball. Its read set is the finite union of the patch paths at the two endpoints and the coarse bond coordinate. Apply (10.1) with that fixed radius, then the site-summation argument. The embedded coarse sites are distinct; there are four coarse bond directions at each such site. Thus this sum has the same localized derivative bound, with multiplicity four, multiplied by the fixed kernel penalty coefficient.

The full tested action is the sum of this smoothing penalty and the fine classical action. Taking the smaller common radius and adding their costs proves (10.3) for the actual action, with all tags equal there. At the identity the curvature slots, plaquette cost and prediction discrepancy vanish; their first derivatives in the full action vanish as well, since the direct penalty is stationary and every other term has degree at least two in these vanishing slots. Thus \(f (0) = 0\) and \(D_y f (0) = 0\). Together with the tree-gauge Hessian gap, this supplies all inputs to the uniform stationary construction. The argument fixes the block and path budgets first; it does not assert a new estimate for the evolution of those budgets along a history.

10.2A uniform stationary chart and its auxiliary-volume derivatives

We construct the stationary branch on the real coordinate neighborhood used by the nonlinear step. The neighborhood must be uniform in the auxiliary torus; the subsequent derivative bounds may pay a fixed polynomial in its volume. These are different requirements.

10.2.1Local derivative data and the Hessian gap

Let \(I\) and \(J\) be the coarse and free fine real-coordinate sets, and write \(w = (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^I \times \mathbb{R}^J\). Both coordinate spaces initially carry the supremum norm. Give every coordinate an anchor \(a_i\) on the fine torus, with at most \(K\) coordinates at each site. Put

\(\displaystyle S_a = K \sum_{r \geqslant 0} (2 r + 1)^4 e^{- ar} \qquad (a > 0) .\)

The distance-shell bound implies \(\sum_i e^{- a \text{dist} (v, a_i)} \leqslant S_a\), uniformly in the torus and in \(v\).

Fix an order \(N \geqslant 3\). The local action estimates provide a smooth real function \(f (x, y)\) on \(\|w\|_{\infty} < r_0\) and constants \(a, C, m, r_0 > 0\) such that \(f (0) = 0\), \(D_y f (0) = 0\), and

\(\displaystyle | \partial_{i_1} \cdots \partial_{i_q} f (w) | \leqslant C \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( - a \sum_{h = 1}^q \text{dist} (a_{i_t}, a_{i_h}) \right) \quad (1 \leqslant q \leqslant N, 1 \leqslant t \leqslant q) .\) (10.3)

The vertical Hessian \(H (0) = D_y^2 f (0)\) satisfies \(H (0) \geqslant mI\) in the Euclidean quadratic-form sense. The constants and coordinate multiplicity are independent of the auxiliary torus after the block data have been fixed. The Hessian gap is the tree-gauge energy comparison in Subsubsection 11.21.2; the derivative estimate is the additional quantitative local-action input. Its application here uses the larger one-step data of that comparison: the coarse side exceeds one and the fine side is at least \(100 R_{\text{macro}}\), where \(R_{\text{macro}} = (j_{\text{ph}} + 7) \ell\), the blocking side \(\ell \geqslant 3\) is odd, the phase \(j_{\text{ph}} \geqslant 4\), and the kernel's branch-compatibility hypothesis holds.

For two points \(v, w\) in the original coordinate ball, differentiate a Hessian entry along their joining segment. Applying (10.3) with the first Hessian coordinate as root, and summing the varied coordinate, gives

\(\displaystyle |H (v)_{ij} - H (w)_{ij} | \leqslant CS_a \|v - w\|_{\infty} e^{- a \text{dist} (a_i, a_j)} .\)

Both row and column sums of the difference are therefore at most \(CS_a^2 \|v - w\|_{\infty}\). Its Euclidean operator norm and its supremum operator norm have this same bound. Choose

\(\displaystyle r_H = \min \left\{ r_0 / 2, \frac{m}{2 (1 + CS_a^2)} \right\} .\)

Then \(H (w) \geqslant (m / 2) I\) on \(\|w\|_{\infty} \leqslant r_H\). The second-derivative case of (10.3) also gives uniformly bounded exponentially weighted row and column sums of \(H (w)\), after reducing \(a\) to \(a / 2\). The inverse argument of Subsection 8.13, applied with lower bound \(\min (1, m / 2)\), supplies an exponential bound on \(H (w)^{- 1}\). Summing that bound gives a fixed constant \(J_0 \geqslant 1\) with

\(\displaystyle \|H (w)^{- 1} \|_{\infty \to \infty} \leqslant J_0 .\) (10.4)

The Euclidean bound \(\|H (w)^{- 1} \|_{2 \to 2} \leqslant 2 / m\) follows directly from the positive gap. No conversion between the two vector norms is used to obtain either uniform bound.

10.2.2Contraction on a common coordinate ball

Let \(F (x, y) = D_y f (x, y)\), viewed as its array of real coordinates. Choose

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} r_V & = \min \{r_H, [4 J_0 (1 + CS_a^2)]^{- 1} \},\\ r_X & = \min \{r_V, r_V / [4 J_0 (1 + CS_a)]\} . \end{array}\)

For \(\|x\|_{\infty} < r_X\), define on the closed ball \(\|y\|_{\infty} \leqslant r_V\)

\(\displaystyle T_x (y) = y - H (0)^{- 1} F (x, y) .\)

The Hessian difference estimate gives

\(\displaystyle \|D_y T_x (y)\|_{\infty \to \infty} \leqslant J_0 CS_a^2 r_V \leqslant \tfrac{1}{4} .\)

The second-derivative bound, summed with its vertical coordinate as root, gives

\(\displaystyle \|F (x, 0) - F (0, 0)\|_{\infty} \leqslant CS_a \|x\|_{\infty} .\)

Consequently \(\|T_x (0)\|_{\infty} \leqslant r_V / 4\) and

\(\displaystyle \|T_x (y)\|_{\infty} \leqslant \tfrac{1}{4} \|y\|_{\infty} + r_V / 4 \leqslant r_V / 2.\)

Thus \(T_x\) maps the complete closed ball into itself and contracts by \(1 / 4\). Its iterates from zero form a Cauchy sequence: consecutive differences are bounded by a geometric sequence of ratio \(1 / 4\). Their limit \(\rho (x)\) is a fixed point by continuity. If two fixed points existed, their distance would be at most one quarter of itself, so they would coincide. We have proved

\(\displaystyle \| \rho (x)\|_{\infty} \leqslant r_V / 2, \qquad F (x, \rho (x)) = 0, \qquad \rho (0) = 0,\)

with uniqueness among all stationary points in the closed \(r_V\)-ball.

At each \((x, \rho (x))\) the vertical derivative of \(F\) is the invertible Hessian \(H (x, \rho (x))\). The finite-dimensional smooth implicit-function theorem therefore supplies a local smooth stationary graph. It remains inside the open \(r_V\)-ball after restricting its neighborhood, and uniqueness identifies it with the graph just constructed. These local identifications prove smoothness of \(\rho\) throughout \(\|x\|_{\infty} < r_X\). If several local tags give the same function on the original coordinate ball, the constructed graph is stationary for every tag and is the same graph for all of them. No extension outside the common ball is asserted.

Differentiating \(F (x, \rho (x)) = 0\) once gives

\(\displaystyle D \rho (x) = - H (x, \rho (x))^{- 1} D_x F (x, \rho (x)) .\)

The rooted row sum bounds \(\|D_x F\|_{\infty \to \infty}\) by \(CS_a\). Hence \(\|D \rho \|_{\infty \to \infty} \leqslant J_0 CS_a\). Integration along a coarse line segment proves

\(\displaystyle \| \rho (x) - \rho (x')\|_{\infty} \leqslant C_{\rho} \|x - x' \|_{\infty}, \qquad C_{\rho} = 1 + J_0 CS_a .\) (10.5)

In particular the constant used to spread fine-field slack is independent of the auxiliary volume.

10.2.3Fixed-order derivatives with polynomial volume cost

Let \(W \geqslant 1\) bound \(|I| + |J|\). We now allow polynomial costs in \(W\). A scalar \(q\)-linear form with coordinate entries bounded by \(C\) has supremum operator norm at most \(CW^q\): expand its arguments in coordinate basis vectors and bound the resulting at most \(W^q\) terms. For a vector-valued form with supremum output norm the same bound holds coordinate by coordinate. Passing that output to its Euclidean norm costs at most \(\sqrt{W} \leqslant W\).

These elementary conversions turn (10.3) into polynomial bounds for the ordinary derivatives of \(f\) through order \(N\). Its value has the same kind of bound on the fixed ball, because \(f (0) = 0\) and the first derivative can be integrated radially. They also give polynomial bounds for every derivative of \(\rho\) through order \(N - 1\). For completeness, differentiate \(F \circ \Gamma = 0\), where \(\Gamma (x) = (x, \rho (x))\). At order \(k \geqslant 2\), the partition formula reads

\(\displaystyle 0 = H (\Gamma (x)) D^k \rho (x) + \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} \pi \text{ a partition of } \{1, \ldots, k\}\\ | \pi | \geqslant 2 \end{array}$}}} D^{| \pi |} F (\Gamma (x)) [D^{|B|} \Gamma (x) : B \in \pi] .\)

Every block in the sum has size less than \(k\); the single-block term is exactly the isolated Hessian term, since the coarse component of \(D^k \Gamma\) vanishes. The first derivative was already bounded in (10.5). Inductively, if the lower derivatives have polynomial bounds \(P_h (W)\), the sum is bounded by

\(\displaystyle C \sum_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} \pi \vdash \{1, \ldots, k\}\\ | \pi | \geqslant 2 \end{array}$}}} W^{| \pi |} \prod_{B \in \pi} (1 + P_{|B|} (W)) .\)

Multiplying by \(J_0\) bounds \(D^k \rho\). This is a fixed polynomial in \(W\) at every fixed \(k\), and uses derivatives of \(f\) only through order \(k + 1\). The Euclidean output version follows by the preceding factor \(W\). Thus the coordinate conversion does not affect the uniform radius or the uniform first-derivative bound.

Put \(M (x) = H (\Gamma (x))\). The chain rule gives polynomial bounds for its entry derivatives through order \(N - 2\). A matrix with entries bounded by \(B\) has Euclidean operator norm at most \(WB\), so these are polynomial operator bounds as well. The inverse identity gives, for each nonempty set of derivative slots \(A\),

\(\displaystyle \partial_A M^{- 1} = - M^{- 1} \sum_{\varnothing \ne B \subseteq A} (\partial_B M) (\partial_{A \setminus B} M^{- 1}) .\)

All inverse derivatives on the right have lower order. Together with \(\|M^{- 1} \|_{2 \to 2} \leqslant 2 / m\), this proves polynomial bounds by induction. Finally

\(\displaystyle D \log \det M [h] = \text{tr} (M^{- 1} DM [h]) .\)

Repeated product differentiation and \(| \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} A| \leqslant W \|A\|_{2 \to 2}\) give polynomial bounds for every positive derivative of \(\log \det M\) through order \(N - 2\). Its centered value is bounded by integrating the first derivative. Its uncentered value is bounded too: the eigenvalues lie between \(m / 2\) and a fixed upper row-sum bound, so the sum of their logarithms has absolute value at most a fixed constant times \(W\). In particular all determinants used here are positive.

10.2.4The loop exponent and the Gaussian comparison data

The remaining exponent is

\(\displaystyle \psi (x, y) = \ell (\mathcal{D}_x (y)) - \sum_{i \in J_{\text{bond}}} \log J_H (y_i),\)

where \(J_{\text{bond}}\) denotes the free bonds and \(J_H\) the single-bond Haar Jacobian. The notation separates the bond set from the earlier real-coordinate set \(J\). On the fixed combined coordinate unit ball, the actual loop coefficient budget bounds each coordinate derivative through the requested order by a fixed constant times \(\Lambda\), summed over at most \(W\) sites. Its exponentially summable coefficient series converges normally there, which justifies differentiation before summation. Conversion to operator derivatives costs at most a further power of \(W\). On the half-radius Haar chart, Subsection 8.18 bounds the derivatives of \(\log J_H\). Summing over at most \(W\) free bonds and composing with the fixed Lie-coordinate maps has polynomial cost in \(W\). Thus the \(\psi\) bounds have the form

\(\displaystyle P_0 (W) + P_1 (W) \Lambda\)

for fixed nonnegative polynomials \(P_0, P_1\).

Apply the preceding construction at order \(N = n + 3\). It supplies bounds through order \(n + 2\) for \(\rho\), through order \(n + 3\) for \(f\), and through order \(n + 1\) for the relative log determinant. The loop and Haar estimates are purchased through order \(n + 4\). Take coarse radius \(u\) and fluctuation parameter radius \(v\) with

\(\displaystyle (1 + C_{\rho}) u + vD < \min (r_H, 1), \qquad u \leqslant r_X, \quad c_{\text{Lie}} (C_{\rho} u + vD) \leqslant r_H^{\text{Haar}} / 2.\)

Here \(c_{\text{Lie}}\) is the fixed norm of the Lie-coordinate map and \(r_H^{\text{Haar}} > 0\) is the Haar-chart radius. On every segment \((x, \rho (x) + tbz)\), with \(\|x\|_{\infty} < u\), \(|b| < v\), \(0 \leqslant t \leqslant 1\) and \(\|z\|_2 \leqslant D\), these inequalities verify the needed action, coordinate and Haar domains. The stationary Hessian is positive with inverse covariance bound \(2 / m\), and stationarity removes the linear vertical Taylor term. These are exactly the data required in Subsection 10.3. For its final estimate through order \(j\) with the even second-order gain, choose \(n \geqslant j + 2\); the two additional parameter derivatives must be included when fixing the stationary-chart order.

For the selected auxiliary volumes, \(W\) is bounded by a polynomial in the auxiliary size parameter. All the bounds obtained here are therefore polynomial in that parameter, except for the single linear loop factor. With \(\Lambda \leqslant 2 P_F\) their common bound can be written \(p_0 + p_1 P_F\), with polynomially bounded \(p_0, p_1\). This verifies the precise bound dependence used in Subsection 10.4. The remaining quantitative inputs are the local-action estimate (10.3), the initial Hessian gap, and the loop coefficient and Haar-chart budgets. The construction never changes the selected finite-volume action or its tags.

10.3The logarithm of a truncated stationary Gaussian integral

The local small-field comparison uses a normalized integral near the actual stationary point. Its first correction has order \(\beta^{- 1}\). Two details matter: the cutoff is retained when the coupling parameter vanishes, and the stationary Hessian varies with the coarse field. We prove the corresponding parameter estimate with both features.

10.3.1The normalized integral

Let \(E\) be the finite-dimensional space of coarse logarithmic coordinates and let \(\mathbb{R}^d\) be the fine integration space, with its Euclidean norm. Let \(\varphi (x, y)\) be the real action, \(\psi (x, y)\) the additional real exponent, and \(\rho (x)\) the stationary point. Assume

\(\displaystyle D_y \varphi (x, \rho (x)) = 0, \qquad D_y^2 \varphi (x, \rho (x)) = M (x) > 0.\)

On the parameter domains under consideration assume smoothness of \(\rho\), \(\varphi\), \(\psi\) and \(\tfrac{1}{2} \log \det M\), with derivatives through the orders used below. More precisely, a derivative estimate through order \(k\) for the integral uses orders \(k + 2\) for \(\varphi\) and \(\rho\), and order \(k\) for \(\psi\) and the relative log determinant, on every segment \((x, \rho (x) + tbz)\) with \(0 \leqslant t \leqslant 1\) and \(|z| \leqslant D\). Let their common bounds be \(p\). These are bounds on the actual moving stationary branch and its Hessian, not on a separately chosen quadratic approximation.

Define the smooth divided difference

\(\displaystyle V (b, x, z) = \int_0^1 (1 - t) D_y^2 \varphi (x, \rho (x) + tbz) [z, z], dt.\)

Taylor's formula and stationarity give

\(\displaystyle V (b, x, z) = \frac{\varphi (x, \rho (x) + bz) - \varphi (x, \rho (x))}{b^2} \quad (b \ne 0), \qquad V (0, x, z) = \tfrac{1}{2} z^T M (x) z.\)

The integral formula supplies its derivatives also at \(b = 0\). Put \(s (x) = \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det M (x)\) and

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} A (b, x, z) = & s (x) - s (0) + V (0, 0, z) - V (b, x, z)\\ & + \psi (x, \rho (x)) - \psi (x, \rho (x) + bz) . \end{array}\)

With \(\gamma_0\) the Gaussian of covariance \(M (0)^{- 1}\), set

\(\displaystyle R (b, x) = \int_{|z| \leqslant D} e^{A (b, x, z)} \hspace{0.17em} d \gamma_0 (z), \qquad L (b, x) = \log R (b, x) .\) (10.6)

The normalization is fixed at the base point in the measure; the varying determinant is explicitly present in \(A\).

At \(b = 0\) the density identity gives exactly

\(\displaystyle R (0, x) = \gamma_x \{|z| \leqslant D\}, \qquad \gamma_x = N (0, M (x)^{- 1}) .\) (10.7)

Indeed the factor \(e^{s (x) - s (0)}\) converts the determinant prefactor, and \(V (0, 0, z) - V (0, x, z)\) converts the quadratic exponent. The two \(\psi\) terms cancel. The result is the truncated Gaussian mass, which need not equal one.

There is also an exact parity identity

\(\displaystyle R (- b, x) = R (b, x) .\) (10.8)

Changing \(z\) to \(- z\) preserves the ball and the centered Gaussian. The segment \(\rho (x) + t (- b) (- z)\) equals the original segment, and the two appearances of \(z\) in the quadratic evaluation cancel their signs. Thus \(A (- b, x, - z) = A (b, x, z)\), proving the identity before taking any expansion.

10.3.2Uniform derivatives on the truncated ball

The chain rule in the integral defining \(V\) gives a polynomial bound \(P_k (p, D)\) for all derivatives of \(A\) through order \(k\), uniformly for \(|z| \leqslant D\). To see the polynomial nature, the map \((b, x) \mapsto (x, \rho (x) + tbz)\) has derivatives bounded by fixed polynomials in \(1 + p + D\); each chain-rule term has at most \(k\) factors. Evaluating the two additional slots at \(z\) costs at most \(D^2\). Integration against \(1 - t\) adds no large constant. The \(\psi\) and log-determinant terms have the same type of bound.

Purchase \(k \geqslant 1\) (replace a requested order zero by order one here). Suppose \(|b| < v \leqslant 1\), \(\|x\| < u \leqslant 1\), and \(P_k (p, D) \max (u, v) \leqslant 1\), enlarging \(P_k\) to include its first order bound. Since \(A (0, 0, z) = 0\), integration along the parameter segment from zero gives \(|A (b, x, z) | \leqslant 1\). Derivatives of \(e^A\) through order \(k\) are consequently bounded by another fixed polynomial in \(P_k\), times \(e\). The cutoff ball is fixed in these parameters, and the reference Gaussian has mass one. This bound is integrable and uniform on the open parameter domain, so differentiation under the integral is justified at every order through \(k\).

If \(\gamma_0 \{|z| \leqslant D\} \geqslant 1 / 2\), then

\(\displaystyle e^{- 1} / 2 \leqslant R (b, x) \leqslant e.\) (10.9)

It follows by integrating \(e^{- 1} \leqslant e^A \leqslant e\) on the ball. Derivatives of \(\log\) are bounded on this fixed positive interval, so the chain rule gives a common bound for all derivatives of \(L\) through order \(k\). If \(p, D\) have upper exponent zero, this bound also has upper exponent zero. The smallness condition on \(u, v\) holds eventually whenever both have a fixed negative upper exponent.

10.3.3The cutoff error when the dimension grows

The auxiliary tori have dimension at most \(Q (x)\), where \(Q\) is bounded by a fixed polynomial in \(1 + (\log \beta_F (x))^2\). Suppose \(M (y)^{- 1} \leqslant cI\) uniformly on the coarse-field ball, with \(c > 0\) fixed. Let \(P_F (x)\) be the fine monitor and take \(D = d_0 P_F (x)\), where \(d_0 > 0\) is fixed. For the actual fine monitor, \(P_F (x) / \log z \to \infty\), and every fixed polynomial in \(\log z\) is eventually bounded by \(P_F (x)^{\epsilon}\) for every \(\epsilon > 0\), where \(z = \beta_F (x)\). These stronger growth properties are used in the following tail estimate.

Here is a dimension-explicit tail estimate. If a centered Gaussian has covariance at most \(cI\) in dimension at most \(P \geqslant 1\), then for \(r, s \geqslant 0\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{P} \{|Z| > Pr\} \leqslant 2 P \exp (- sr + cs^2 / 2) .\)

If every coordinate has absolute value less than \(r\), then \(|Z|^2 \leqslant Pr^2 \leqslant P^2 r^2\). A union bound and the one-coordinate exponential-moment estimate therefore prove the assertion. For \(P = 1 + Q (x)\), the monitor growth gives \(P \leqslant \sqrt{P_F (x)}\) eventually. Choose \(r = d_0 \sqrt{P_F (x)}\) and \(s = r / c\). Then \(Pr \leqslant D\) and

\(\displaystyle 1 - \gamma_y \{|z| \leqslant D\} \leqslant 2 (1 + Q (x)) \exp \{- d_0^2 P_F (x) / (2 c)\} .\) (10.10)

The right side has every negative upper exponent. In particular it is at most \(1 / 2\) eventually, proving the mass condition above.

If \(0 \leqslant \epsilon \leqslant 1 / 2\) and \(1 - \epsilon \leqslant r \leqslant 1\), integration of \(1 / t\) on \([r, 1]\) gives \(| \log r| \leqslant 2 \epsilon\). Applying this to (10.7) shows that \(L (0, y)\) has a value smaller than every fixed negative power of \(\beta_F\), uniformly on the allowed coarse ball. Derivatives require an additional argument; a small value by itself is insufficient.

10.3.4Turning a small value into small jets

We give the finite-order interpolation used for that purpose. Fix \(j \in \mathbb{N}\), \(a > 0\), and real \(d, m\). There is an exponent \(B\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(z\), the following implication holds in any finite-dimensional normed spaces. If on \(\bar{B} (y, z^{- a})\) a function \(F\) has derivatives through order \(j + 1\) bounded by \(z^d\), and its value is bounded by \(z^B\), then its jets through order \(j\) at \(y\) are bounded by \(z^m\).

The elementary one-step estimate is

\(\displaystyle \|D^{r + 1} F (y)\| \leqslant 2 v / s + Cs\)

whenever \(\|D^r F\| \leqslant v\) and \(\|D^{r + 2} F\| \leqslant C\) on the radius-\(s\) ball. Subtract the tangent map of \(D^r F\) at \(y\), integrate its derivative along a segment of length \(s\), and use the second-derivative bound to control the tangent-map error. This gives the estimate in every unit direction, hence in operator norm, without a dimension factor.

Induct on \(j\). The case \(j = 0\) takes \(B = m\). In the induction step choose

\(\displaystyle c = a + \max (0, d - m) + 1, \qquad b = m - 1 - c, \qquad s = z^{- c} .\)

Eventually \(2 s \leqslant z^{- a}\). Apply the induction hypothesis with target exponent \(b\) on every radius-\(s\) ball whose center lies within \(s\) of \(y\); those balls lie in the original domain. A sufficiently negative common value exponent \(B\) then bounds jets through order \(j - 1\) by \(z^b\) on that inner ball. The one-step estimate bounds the order-\(j\) derivative at \(y\) by

\(\displaystyle 2 z^{b + c} + z^{d - c} .\)

Both exponents are strictly less than \(m\), and \(b < m\) also bounds all lower derivatives. Increasing the lower threshold on \(z\) proves the assertion. This is a finite induction, with all exponents chosen before the function or dimension is supplied.

Apply it to \(F (y) = L (0, y)\) on a microscopic ball of radius \(z^{- 1}\) inside the coarse-field domain. The derivative bound of upper exponent zero is at most \(z\) eventually. The tail estimate provides any value exponent \(B\) the induction requests. Hence, for every fixed real \(e\),

\(\displaystyle \|L (0, \cdot)\|_{J^j, y} \leqslant z^e\) (10.11)

eventually, with the stated microscopic margin.

10.3.5The even second-order gain

Purchase two additional parameter derivatives in the earlier bounds. For every \(r \leqslant j\), the function \(b \mapsto D_y^r L (b, y)\) is even by (10.8). Its first derivative at zero vanishes. If its second derivative is bounded by \(C (x)\) for \(|b| \leqslant \epsilon\), Taylor's formula gives

\(\displaystyle \|D_y^r L (\epsilon, y) - D_y^r L (0, y)\| \leqslant C (x) \epsilon^2 .\)

The derivative constant \(C\) has upper exponent zero. Combining with (10.11), assuming \(\beta > 0\) and \(\epsilon = \beta^{- 1 / 2} < v\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle \|L (\beta^{- 1 / 2}, \cdot)\|_{J^j, y} \leqslant C (x) \beta^{- 1} + z^e .\) (10.12)

This is the required first-order inverse-coupling bound, uniform for the prescribed auxiliary volumes and coupling band, with the closed microscopic ball \(\bar{B} (y, z^{- 1})\) contained in the coarse-field parameter domain, provided the actual stationary branch, segment derivative and Hessian bounds stated above hold. Identifying this truncated integral with the full nonlinear push additionally requires its separate exterior estimate; that estimate is not part of the parity argument.

10.4The retained stationary region and its exterior

The Gaussian comparison must describe a region of the actual fine configuration space. In particular, the fine-field cutoff must be one throughout that region. We establish this using the funded expansion to find a configuration with slack, then propagating the slack by a uniform Lipschitz estimate.

10.4.1Coordinates and the normalized integral

Fix an auxiliary finite torus, a tree gauge, and the actual coarse field \(V (x)\) in logarithmic coordinates \(x \in X\). Let \(Y\) be the Euclidean space of free fine logarithmic coordinates, \(d = \dim Y\), and \(N\) the number of free fine bonds. Its coordinate map is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{D}_x (y)_i = \exp (y_i) \hspace{0.17em} V (x)^{\text{rep}}_i .\)

Here \(y_i\) includes the fixed orthonormal identification with the Lie algebra, and tree bonds are restored by the gauge fixing. The space \(X\) uses the supremum norm on real color coordinates. The space \(Y\) uses its Euclidean norm for Gaussian integration; write \(\|y\|_{\infty}\) for the supremum norm after forgetting that Euclidean norm. Thus \(\|y\|_{\infty} \leqslant \|y\|_2\). The combined chart norm is \(\max (\|x\|_{\infty}, \|y\|_{\infty})\). Write \(\rho (x)\) for the actual stationary branch, \(b = \beta^{- 1 / 2}\), and

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{S}_x =\mathcal{D}_x (\rho (x) + b \bar{B}_Y (0, D)) .\)

This is a compact measurable subset of the free configuration space. All Haar measures in this section are probability measures.

The local Haar density of Subsection 8.18 is \(c_H J (y_i)\) on each free bond. Set

\(\displaystyle \psi (x, y) = \ell (\mathcal{D}_x (y)) - \sum_{i = 1}^N \log J (y_i), \qquad M (x) = D_y^2 \phi (x, \rho (x)),\)

where \(\phi\) is the fine classical action plus the smoothing penalty, and \(\ell\) is the fine loop correction. Assume that the stationary chart estimates hold on the region in question, with \(M (x) > 0\). Introduce

\(\displaystyle \Phi (x) = \beta \phi (x, \rho (x)) + \psi (x, \rho (x)) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det M (x), \qquad \Omega = \pi_{\beta} (\sqrt{2 \pi} \hspace{0.17em} b)^d c_H^N,\)

where \(\pi_{\beta} > 0\) is the smoothing-kernel prefactor. The tagged integrand of the pure push is

\(\displaystyle U_x (U) = \sum_k \pi_{\beta} \lambda_k (U) e^{- \beta [a (U) + n_k (U, V (x))] - \ell (U)} \boldsymbol{1}_{\{ \text{fine field healthy} \}} .\) (10.13)

The nonnegative tag weights satisfy \(\sum_k \lambda_k (U) = 1\); a dead tag has zero weight. Tree-gauge integration identifies the pure push with \(Z (x) = \int U_x \hspace{0.17em} dU\). The incoming density and kernel have integrable envelopes, so all restricted integrals below exist.

Suppose every point of \(\mathcal{S}_x\) is fine healthy, the whole logarithmic ball is inside the Haar chart, and all live tag actions there equal \(\phi\). Then the product chart formula and the affine change \(y = \rho (x) + bz\) give

\(\displaystyle \int_{\mathcal{S}_x} U_x \hspace{0.17em} dU = \Omega e^{- \Phi (x)} R (b, x),\) (10.14)

with exactly the truncated integral \(R\) of Subsection 10.3. Indeed the Jacobian is \(b^d c_H^N \prod_i J (y_i)\), the tag sum is one, and \(\beta b^2 = 1\). The quadratic Gaussian factor contributes \((2 \pi)^{d / 2} (\det M (0))^{- 1 / 2}\); its determinant and the relative determinant in the exponent defining \(R\) combine to \((\det M (x))^{- 1 / 2}\). The remaining exponent is precisely \(- \Phi (x)\). No tag-count factor occurs. Injectivity of the Haar chart ensures that the inverse image of \(\mathcal{S}_x\) within that chart is the chosen closed ball, so this change of variables also handles its boundary.

10.4.2The energy cost outside the retained region

Let \(\gamma > 0\) be the stationary chart gap and put \(\Delta = \gamma D^2 / 4\). On the common safe chart assume the fine Hessian satisfies \(D_y^2 \phi \geqslant (\gamma / 2) I\). Stationarity and Taylor's integral formula then imply

\(\displaystyle \phi (x, y) - \phi (x, \rho (x)) \geqslant \frac{\gamma}{4} \|y - \rho (x)\|^2 .\) (10.15)

Here the safe chart is the inverse image of the closed combined coordinate supremum ball, while the displayed quadratic lower bound uses the Euclidean norm on \(Y\). Both endpoints lie in that chart, and its convexity contains their entire joining segment. Consequently any point of that chart outside \(\mathcal{S}_x\) pays at least \(\Delta\) after multiplication by \(\beta\).

We also need to recognize chart points without presupposing that the fine field is in the chart. The tree-gauge geometric estimate supplies a constant \(C_0\) such that an alive tag, fine plaquette distances at most \(\delta\), and prediction-wire distances at most \(\delta\) imply

\(\displaystyle \text{dist} (U_i, V (x)^{\text{rep}}_i) \leqslant (C_0 s + 1) \delta\)

for every free bond. Here \(s\) bounds the number of plaquettes in the fixed local comparison box. The estimate follows by comparing that bond with its tree-gauge replica: the local filling contributes at most \(s \delta\), its geometric comparison costs \(C_0\), and the prediction wire contributes the final \(\delta\). Choose logarithm-chart constants \(r_0, C_{\log} > 0\), so that distances at most \(r_0\) have logarithms bounded by \(C_{\log}\) times the distance. For a prescribed safe coordinate radius \(r_s > 0\), choose

\(\displaystyle \delta \leqslant \min \left\{ \frac{r_0}{C_0 s + 1}, \frac{r_s}{C_{\log} (C_0 s + 1)} \right\} .\)

Taking the logarithm of each relative free bond now yields \(U =\mathcal{D}_x (y)\) with \(\|y\|_{\infty} \leqslant r_s\). The coarse coordinates are already inside the safe radius. This argument uses the geometric comparison for alive tags only.

Write \(\tau\) for the fine health threshold, and suppose \(\tau \leqslant \delta\). Let \(W \geqslant 1\) bound all relevant site and coordinate counts. The path budgets on a healthy field give

\(\displaystyle | \ell (U) | \leqslant W \Lambda \tau^2, \qquad a (U) \geqslant - WC (\tau^3 + \tau^4) .\)

The second bound uses the nonnegative quadratic part of the action and the absolute cubic and quartic budgets. Define

\(\displaystyle B_{\text{loop}} = \beta C (\tau^3 + \tau^4) + \Lambda \tau^2 .\)

Let \(m_W\) bound \(| \phi (x, \rho (x)) | / \|x\|\) on the coarse chart, and let \(p + t_W\) bound \(| \Phi (x) - \beta \phi (x, \rho (x)) |\). The latter consists of the loop/Haar bound \(p\) and a log-determinant bound \(t_W\). If a prediction wire exceeds \(\delta\), its nonnegative smoothing penalty is at least \(\kappa \delta^2 / 2\), where \(\kappa > 0\) is the kernel penalty constant. Assume

\(\displaystyle \beta m_W \|x\|+ \Delta \leqslant \beta \kappa \delta^2 / 2.\) (10.16)

For a healthy point outside \(\mathcal{S}_x\), there are now two cases. If all prediction wires are small, the preceding chart-recognition argument and (10.15) apply. Otherwise the large-wire penalty and (10.16) apply. In both cases the exponent in each live tag satisfies

\(\displaystyle - \beta [a (U) + n_k (U, V (x))] - \ell (U) \leqslant - \Phi (x) - \Delta + WB_{\text{loop}} + p + t_W .\)

Unhealthy fields and dead tags contribute zero. Summing the tag weights therefore proves a pointwise exterior bound.

To express it in stationary units, put \(m_H = c_H (\sqrt{2 \pi} \hspace{0.17em} b)^{\dim \mathfrak{g}}\), so \(\Omega = \pi_{\beta} m_H^N\), and define

\(\displaystyle Y_{\ast} = - N \log m_H - \Delta + WB_{\text{loop}} + p + t_W .\) (10.17)

Then

\(\displaystyle U_x (U) \leqslant \Omega e^{- \Phi (x)} e^{Y_{\ast}} \quad (U \not\in \mathcal{S}_x) .\) (10.18)

Since the ambient Haar measure has mass one, the same bound holds for the exterior integral.

10.4.3Finding one configuration with slack

Let \(Z_{\text{sl}} (x)\) denote the portion of the pure push allocated to the empty coarse and fine defect sector. The sector allocation has values between zero and one. Its nonzero support requires all fine plaquette distances to be at most \(\tau / 2\). The exact funded expansion of this sector is

\(\displaystyle Z_{\text{sl}} (x) =\mathcal{O} (x) \sum_I e_I (x), \qquad e_{\varnothing} (x) = 1,\)

where \(\mathcal{O} (x) > 0\) is the same sector overhead as in the block integral, and the expansion gives \(\sum_I |e_I (x) | \leqslant e^{\varepsilon}\). These are the actual funded sector identities, with its fixed tree and allocation. They imply

\(\displaystyle Z_{\text{sl}} (x) \geqslant \mathcal{O} (x) (2 - e^{\varepsilon}) .\) (10.19)

To see the factor two, separate the empty term: the absolute sum of the remaining terms is at most \(e^{\varepsilon} - 1\), so their sum is at least \(1 - e^{\varepsilon}\). The expansion's absolute convergence justifies this separation. On the auxiliary volume, its rooted budget gives \(\varepsilon = | \Lambda_c | \hspace{0.17em} o (x)\), with a negative upper exponent for \(o\). Since \(| \Lambda_c |\) has upper exponent zero, \(e^{\varepsilon} \leqslant 3 / 2\) eventually.

We compare \(\mathcal{O}\) to the stationary normalization before using (10.19). On a healthy coarse field its exact formula is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{O}= \Omega \exp \{- \beta a_{\text{coarse}, 2} (x) - \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det A_0 \} .\)

Here \(a_{\text{coarse}, 2}\) is the coarse quadratic action and \(A_0\) is the empty-sector covariance precision matrix. The funded matrix bound gives \(\log \det A_0 \leqslant Wa_{\ast}\). Classical matching through degree four, with fifth-derivative cost \(K_5\), and the coarse cubic and quartic budgets give

\(\displaystyle | \phi (x, \rho (x)) - a_{\text{coarse}} (x) | \leqslant WK_5 \|x\|^5, \quad |a_{\text{coarse}} (x) - a_{\text{coarse}, 2} (x) | \leqslant WC' (\tau_c^3 + \tau_c^4) .\)

Combining these inequalities with the bound \(p + t_W\) yields

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{O} \geqslant \Omega e^{- \Phi - e_{\ast}}, \qquad e_{\ast} = \beta W [K_5 \|x\|^5 + C' (\tau_c^3 + \tau_c^4)] + p + t_W + Wa_{\ast} / 2.\) (10.20)

If

\(\displaystyle Y_{\ast} + e_{\ast} + 2 \leqslant - \Delta / 2, \qquad Y_{\ast} \leqslant - \Delta / 2,\) (10.21)

then the exterior upper bound is strictly less than \(\tfrac{1}{2} \mathcal{O} \leqslant Z_{\text{sl}}\), since \(e^{- 2} < 1 / 2\). If \(\mathcal{S}_x\) contained no point with all fine plaquettes at most \(\tau / 2\), the allocated integrand would vanish throughout \(\mathcal{S}_x\). Outside it, that integrand is bounded by \(U_x\). Its integral would consequently be bounded by the exterior upper bound, a contradiction. Thus there exists

\(\displaystyle U_0 =\mathcal{D}_x (\rho (x) + bz_0), \qquad \|z_0 \| \leqslant D, \qquad \text{toll}_i (U_0) \leqslant \tau / 2 \quad \text{for all } i.\)

This existence argument does not differentiate the fine cutoff.

10.4.4Spreading the slack and choosing the parameters

The fine plaquette tolls are uniformly Lipschitz, with a constant \(L_0 > 0\) independent of the auxiliary volume, on the combined coordinate supremum unit ball, with Lipschitz distance measured in that same norm. Indeed each plaquette contains four links; the uniform first-derivative estimate for a link, the product rule, and unitarity bound the derivative of its matrix holonomy. The reverse triangle inequality for the matrix norm then bounds the difference of tolls. Integrating that derivative along the joining segment proves the asserted Lipschitz estimate.

Let \(C_{\rho}\) be the supremum-coordinate Lipschitz bound for the stationary branch, with \(\rho (0) = 0\). For \(\|y - x\| \leqslant r\) and \(\|z\| \leqslant D\), the supremum distance between the combined coordinates of \(\mathcal{D}_y (\rho (y) + bz)\) and \(U_0\) is at most

\(\displaystyle (1 + C_{\rho}) r + 2 bD.\)

We used \(\|z - z_0 \|_{\infty} \leqslant \|z - z_0 \|_2 \leqslant 2 D\). Thus every such field is fine healthy provided

\(\displaystyle L_0 [(1 + C_{\rho}) r + 2 bD] \leqslant \tau / 2.\) (10.22)

The two configurations lie in the coordinate unit ball under the radius conditions below. The same conditions place the entire region inside the Haar chart and inside the region where all tag actions coincide.

Here are the simultaneous purchases used in the construction. The action constants \(C, C'\) are fixed and nonnegative, while \(\kappa, \gamma\) are fixed and positive. The constant \(C_{\rho}\) is fixed at this stage independently of the auxiliary volume. The loop budget obeys \(0 \leqslant \Lambda \leqslant 2 P_F\). Write \(z = \beta_F\), \(P_F\) for the fine monitor, \(u = z^{- 7 / 16}\) and \(r = z^{- 1}\), and take

\(\displaystyle \beta \in [z / 2, 2 z], \qquad \tau = z^{- 1 / 2} P_F, \qquad D = d_0 P_F, \qquad d_0 = (16 L_0)^{- 1} .\)

Every fixed polynomial in the auxiliary size parameter is \(o (P_F^{\epsilon})\) for every \(\epsilon > 0\); \(P_F\) itself has upper exponent zero. These are the monitor's two distinct growth properties. Since \(b \leqslant 2 z^{- 1 / 2}\), \(2 L_0 bD \leqslant \tau / 4\), and \(L_0 (1 + C_{\rho}) r \leqslant \tau / 4\) eventually. This proves (10.22). Moreover \((1 + C_{\rho}) u + uD\) tends to zero. It is eventually smaller than the fixed safe radii, the action-coincidence radius and one, while its fine-coordinate component times the Lie-coordinate norm cost is at most half the Haar-chart radius. Also \(b < u\) and \(\tau \leqslant \delta\) eventually.

The coefficients \(m_W, t_W, W, a_{\ast}, K_5\) are bounded by fixed polynomials in the auxiliary size parameter. The stationary jet bound has the more precise form

\(\displaystyle p = p_0 + p_1 P_F,\)

with both \(p_0, p_1\) bounded by such polynomials: only the loop bound contributes the linear monitor factor. Also \(- N \log m_H\) is bounded above by a polynomial in that parameter, including its logarithmic coupling cost. The two action remainders \(WB_{\text{loop}}\) and \(\beta W [K_5 u^5 + C' \tau_c^3 + C' \tau_c^4]\) tend to zero, for \(\tau_c \leqslant 2 \tau\). It follows that every positive term in \(Y_{\ast} + \Delta + e_{\ast} + 2\) is \(o (P_F^2)\). Since \(\Delta = \gamma d_0^2 P_F^2 / 4\), both inequalities in (10.21) hold eventually. Finally \(m_W u \to 0\) and \(\Delta / \beta \to 0\), proving (10.16) uniformly for \(\|x\| < u\). All these thresholds can be enlarged together at a fixed stage.

We can now use (10.14): slack propagation has verified its cutoff condition on the entire retained region. Split the positive full integral into that region and its complement, and use (10.18) and (10.21). The result is

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant Z (y) - \Omega e^{- \Phi (y)} R (b, y) \leqslant \Omega e^{- \Phi (y)} \exp (- \gamma D^2 / 8)\)

for every \(\|y\| < u\) with \(\|y - x\| \leqslant r\), where the center \(x\) is coarse healthy. This is the exterior estimate required in Subsection 10.5, on its full microscopic comparison ball. Its hypotheses retain the actual funded sector expansion, the stationary chart and Hessian estimates, and the classical matching for the same coefficient data.

10.5The small-field remainder of the pure nonlinear push

We combine the truncated stationary integral with the full positive push and the classical matching conditions. The full logarithm and the stationary logarithm are kept distinct until their difference has been estimated.

10.5.1From an exterior bound to a logarithmic error

Let \(Z (x)\) be the actual pure nonlinear push in coarse logarithmic coordinates, and let \(\Omega > 0\) be its parameter-independent normalizing factor. The stationary construction supplies a real normalizing exponent \(\Phi (x)\) and the positive truncated integral \(R (\beta^{- 1 / 2}, x)\) of Subsection 10.3. Suppose its exterior estimate is

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant Z (x) - \Omega e^{- \Phi (x)} R (\beta^{- 1 / 2}, x) \leqslant \Omega e^{- \Phi (x)} \eta .\) (10.23)

The integral over the retained stationary region is exactly the middle term, with the same moving Hessian and Haar-chart factor. The complementary integral is nonnegative. Its upper bound in (10.23) is a substantive exterior estimate, not a consequence of positivity.

Assume \(R \geqslant a_0 > 0\). Then \(Z > 0\) and, for

\(\displaystyle E (x) = \log Z (x) - \log \Omega + \Phi (x) - \log R (\beta^{- 1 / 2}, x),\)

we have

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant E (x) \leqslant \eta / a_0 .\) (10.24)

Indeed the ratio \(Z / (\Omega e^{- \Phi} R)\) lies between \(1\) and \(1 + \eta / R\), and \(0 \leqslant \log (1 + s) \leqslant s\) for \(s \geqslant 0\). In the construction \(a_0 = e^{- 1} / 2\) and \(\eta = \exp (- cP_F^2)\) with a fixed \(c > 0\).

10.5.2Polynomial derivative bounds for the full logarithm

The coarse field enters the positive integral through the smoothing kernel. At a fixed fine configuration and fixed tag its contribution has the form

\(\displaystyle U (x, \omega) = a (\omega) e^{p (x, \omega)}, \qquad a (\omega) \geqslant 0.\)

The fine density and tag likelihood are held in \(a\). The exponent \(p\) is minus \(\beta\) times the finite sum of kernel penalties between the varied coarse bonds and the fixed predicted bonds. On a fixed logarithmic ball, smoothness of the group exponential and the penalty gives a common derivative bound

\(\displaystyle \|p (\cdot, \omega)\|_{J^k, x} \leqslant C_k | \beta | \hspace{0.17em} n_b,\)

where \(n_b\) bounds the number of coarse bonds. It is uniform in the held fine field because the group representation is unitary and that field enters only through fixed group multiplication.

Subtract \(p (x, \omega)\) before differentiating the exponential. The finite partition formula gives

\(\displaystyle \|U (\cdot, \omega)\|_{J^k, x} \leqslant K_k U (x, \omega),\)

where \(K_k\) is a fixed polynomial in \(1 + | \beta | n_b\). Finite tag summation preserves this relative bound. Differentiation under the Haar integral is justified by the bounded fine density and kernel on the compact configuration space, together with the uniform derivative bound on a neighborhood. Consequently

\(\displaystyle \|Z\|_{J^k, x} \leqslant K_k Z (x) .\) (10.25)

At a point where \(Z (x) > 0\), normalize by that one value: \(f (y) = Z (y) / Z (x)\). Then \(f (x) = 1\) and its jets are bounded by \(K_k\). The logarithm chain rule at one bounds the jets of \(\log Z (y) - \log Z (x)\) by a fixed polynomial in \(K_k\). This argument never divides an absolute derivative bound by an uncontrolled small partition function; the factor \(Z (x)\) has already canceled in (10.25).

The other terms defining \(E\) have polynomial derivative bounds: \(\Phi\) consists of the stationary action multiplied by \(\beta\), the stationary extra exponent and the relative log determinant; the truncated logarithm has the bounds proved in the preceding section. For auxiliary volumes polynomial in the block radius and \(\beta\) in its allowed band, these give a bound \(z^d\) through any fixed positive order, for some fixed \(d\), where \(z = \beta_F (x_{\text{scale}})\). The order-zero bound for \(E\) follows directly from (10.24). The exponentially small value bound (10.24) holds on a microscopic ball of radius \(z^{- 1}\) about each tested coarse point. Applying the interpolation lemma of the preceding section yields, for every prescribed \(e\),

\(\displaystyle \|E\|_{J^j} \leqslant z^e .\) (10.26)

This uses the exterior estimate throughout that ball. An estimate only at its center would not suffice.

10.5.3The exact decomposition and the two cancellations

Write \(a_{\text{fine}} (x)\) for the fine action evaluated on the actual stationary branch, \(a_{\text{coarse}} (x)\) for the coarse classical action, and \(d_{\text{coarse}} (x)\) for the prescribed coarse quantum and reset contribution. Define

\(\displaystyle C (x) = a_{\text{coarse}} (x) - a_{\text{fine}} (x), \qquad W (x) = d_{\text{coarse}} (x) - \psi (x, \rho (x)) - \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det M (x) .\)

Thus the normalizing exponent is \(\Phi = \beta a_{\text{fine}} + \psi (x, \rho (x)) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det M (x)\). The actual corrected logarithm

\(\displaystyle G (x) = \log Z (x) + \beta a_{\text{coarse}} (x) + d_{\text{coarse}} (x)\)

satisfies the exact identity

\(\displaystyle G = \log \Omega + L + E + \beta C + W, \qquad L (x) = \log R (\beta^{- 1 / 2}, x) .\) (10.27)

Every function in this identity uses the same stationary branch, coefficient data and finite torus.

Classical matching through degree four says \(D^r C (0) = 0\) for \(0 \leqslant r \leqslant 4\). Suppose its derivatives of orders \(1\) through \(n \geqslant 5\) on the ball of radius \(h \leqslant 1\) are bounded by \(K_C\). Taylor's formula, applied also to each derivative of order less than five, gives

\(\displaystyle C \in \mathcal{B}_n (K_C h^5, h^{- 1}) .\) (10.28)

For orders at least five the original derivative bound suffices, because \(h^r \leqslant h^5\); for lower orders integrate the fifth derivative along the radial segment, using the zero jets at its starting point.

There is a distinct cancellation for the linear part of \(W\). The actual corrected logarithm \(G\) is invariant under the prescribed lattice translations and signed coordinate symmetries. For each coordinate basis vector supported on the bond \((b, \mu)\), central inversion followed by translation by \(2 b + e_{\mu}\) fixes that unoriented bond and reverses its orientation. It therefore sends the single-coordinate logarithmic vector \(e_i\) to \(- e_i\). Differentiating invariance at zero gives \(DG (0) e_i = - DG (0) e_i\), hence \(DG (0) = 0\). Since \(DC (0) = 0\), (10.27) implies

\(\displaystyle DW (0) = - DL (0) - DE (0) .\)

In particular, if the relevant jets of \(L, E\) are bounded by \(a, v\), then \(\|DW (0)\| \leqslant a + v\). This cancellation uses the symmetry of the full logarithm, rather than asserting that its separate stationary terms are individually even in every coarse coordinate.

The finite-volume quadratic matching estimate supplies a further bound \(\|D^2 W (0)\| \leqslant q\). Its operator norm includes the conversion from the tested momentum waves to the actual coordinate space. If the number of coordinates is at most \(M\), that conversion has a fixed polynomial cost in \(M\). Thus the exponentially small finite-volume bound has the form

\(\displaystyle q \leqslant P (M) \Theta \Lambda e^{- \nu \ell}, \qquad \Theta, \nu > 0,\)

where \(\ell\) is the auxiliary torus side and \(P\) is a fixed nonnegative polynomial including the coordinate norm conversion. This quadratic matching is a separate input from classical matching through degree four.

If derivatives of \(W\) of orders three through \(n\) are bounded by \(K_W\) on the same ball, Taylor's formula now gives

\(\displaystyle W - W (0) \in \mathcal{B}_n (K_W h^3 + 3\{(a + v) h + qh^2 \}, h^{- 1}) .\) (10.29)

For orders zero, one and two, integrate the third derivative and retain the displayed first and second jets. For higher orders, \(h^r \leqslant h^3\) pays the original bound. The factor three is a common upper bound for all these finite Taylor coefficients.

10.5.4The final small-field bound

Take \(h = z^{- 7 / 16}\) and assume \(\beta \in [z / 2, 2 z]\). The stationary estimate gives \(a = C (x_{\text{scale}}) \beta^{- 1} + z^{- 2}\) with \(C\) of upper exponent zero; (10.26) gives \(v = z^{- 2}\). On the radial segment to the tested point, the centered stationary logarithm has bound \(ah\) at scale \(h^{- 1}\), by integrating its first derivative; the higher derivative bounds give the same bound since \(h \leqslant 1\). The centered error has bound \(2 v\). Using (10.27)–(10.29) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle G - G (0) \in \mathcal{B}_j (ah + 2 v + \beta K_C h^5 + K_W h^3 + 3\{(a + v) h + qh^2 \}, h^{- 1}) \qquad (1 \leqslant j \leqslant n) .\) (10.30)

All constants refer to the same original data.

The coefficients \(K_C, K_W\), the coordinate bound \(M\), and the loop bound \(\Lambda\) have upper exponent zero; \(\Theta, \nu\) are fixed positive constants. The auxiliary side is at least the prescribed logarithmic-square scale, so its exponential finite-volume factor pays every fixed inverse power; in particular \(q\) may be bounded by a function of upper exponent \(- 1\). The terms in the displayed amplitude have upper exponents at most

\(\displaystyle - 23 / 16, \quad - 2, \quad - 19 / 16, \quad - 21 / 16, \quad - 23 / 16, \quad - 15 / 8.\)

Their maximum is \(- 19 / 16 < - 23 / 20\). After one common increase of the stage threshold, the amplitude is at most \(z^{- 23 / 20}\). The estimate holds at every healthy coarse point with the two microscopic margins needed for the comparison ball and the radial segment. It supplies the centered chart estimate used in Subsection 10.6. Its remaining hypotheses are the actual stationary-branch bounds, classical and quadratic matching, and the exterior estimate (10.23); none is replaced by a comparison with a different finite-volume model.

10.6Extending small-field derivative estimates to the boundary

A small-field configuration may have a plaquette exactly at the health threshold. Identities first proved where every plaquette is strictly below that threshold must therefore be extended to this boundary. We follow the actual radial argument. It uses the smallness of the logarithmic field and a second-derivative estimate; it does not assume that the healthy set is convex.

10.6.1A radial retreat lemma

Let \(X : [0, 1] \to E\) be a twice continuously differentiable curve in a finite-dimensional normed space, with

\(\displaystyle \sup_{0 \leqslant s \leqslant 1} \|X'' (s)\| \leqslant K < t, \qquad \|X (1) - X (0)\| \leqslant t.\)

Then \(\|X (s) - X (0)\| < t\) for all \(s < 1\) sufficiently close to one. If the endpoint inequality is strict, this is continuity. In the remaining case put \(D = X (1) - X (0)\), so \(\|D\| = t\), and \(g (s) = X (s) - sD\). The fundamental theorem of calculus gives

\(\displaystyle \|X' (1) - D\| = \left| \int_0^1 \{X' (1) - X' (r)\} \hspace{0.17em} dr \right| \leqslant K < t.\)

Since \(g' (1) = X' (1) - D\), the one-sided difference quotient satisfies \(\|g (s) - g (1)\| < (1 - s) t\) near one. For \(0 \leqslant s < 1\) in that neighborhood,

\(\displaystyle X (s) - X (0) = sD + g (s) - g (1), \qquad \|X (s) - X (0)\| < st + (1 - s) t = t.\)

The inequality is strict even when the original endpoint lies exactly on the boundary.

Let \(W\) be the actual logarithmic coordinate of a coarse field, and write \(U_s = \text{actual} (sW)\) for the field obtained by exponentiating its bond coordinates. For one plaquette let \(X (s) = \rho (\text{Hol}_{\square} \hspace{0.17em} U_s)\), where \(\rho\) is the fixed unitary representation. Then \(X (0) = 1\) and

\(\displaystyle \text{chord} (\text{Hol}_{\square} \hspace{0.17em} U_s) = \|X (s) - 1\| / r_G,\)

with the fixed representation normalization \(r_G > 0\). On a fixed small logarithmic ball the exponential has bounded first and second derivatives. If the coordinate norm is at most \(w \leqslant 1\), its bond derivative scale is at most \(Cw\). The product rule over the four plaquette bonds gives

\(\displaystyle \sup_{s \in [0, 1]} \|X'' (s)\| \leqslant r_G (4 r_G Cw)^2 .\)

Thus, if \((4 r_G Cw)^2 < \tau\) and all endpoint plaquette chords are at most \(\tau\), the retreat lemma with \(t = r_G \tau\) makes every plaquette strictly healthy for \(s < 1\) close enough to one. There are finitely many plaquettes, so one neighborhood works for all of them.

In the construction the logarithmic radius is \(h = \beta_F^{- 7 / 16}\), while the health threshold has scale \(\tau = \beta_F^{- 1 / 2}\) times its prescribed slowly varying factor. The coordinate norm conversion has a fixed constant. Since \(h^2\) has exponent \(- 7 / 8 < - 1 / 2\), the displayed strict curvature inequality holds eventually, uniformly over every field in that logarithmic ball. This is the needed strict radial approximation.

10.6.2Transferring chart jets to ordered group derivatives

We record the normalization used with this approximation. Let \(j\) be fixed. Compactness on a small Lie-algebra chart supplies constants \(J, d > 0\) for derivatives through order \(j\) of the local logarithm. Shrink the chart so that the logarithm inverts the exponential there. Let \(U = \text{actual} (W)\), with \(\|W\| \leqslant h\), and suppose

\(\displaystyle f \circ \text{actual} \in \mathcal{B}_j (T, h^{- 1} ; W) .\)

Consider an admissible group-valued variation \(V (y)\) through \(U\), whose link derivative scale is \(C\). The local logarithm and the inverse linear coordinate map produce a chart variation \(a (y)\) with \(a (y_0) = W\) and \(V = \text{actual} \circ a\) near \(y_0\). The logarithm chain rule bounds its positive-order derivatives by

\(\displaystyle j!J \{(1 + r_G) C\}^r \qquad (1 \leqslant r \leqslant j) .\)

Assume

\(\displaystyle (1 + r_G) C \leqslant 1, \qquad j!J (1 + r_G) C \leqslant h.\)

Then every derivative of \(a\) through order \(j\), including its value, is at most \(h\). In the chain rule for \(f \circ \text{actual} \circ a\), an outer derivative of order \(m\) costs \(Th^{- m}\) and its \(m\) inner factors cost at most \(h^m\). They cancel. Bounding the finite partition count conservatively gives

\(\displaystyle \|f \circ V\|_{j, y_0} \leqslant j! 3^j T.\) (10.31)

The same estimate applies to ordered left derivatives along any list of at most \(j\) directions of norm at most \(\tau\). Indeed use the corresponding ordered products of exponentials as the variation; the compact exponential bounds give a link scale at most \(C_j j \tau\). Choose \(C\) to dominate that value. In the present construction \(C\) has upper exponent \(- 1 / 2\), strictly below the radius exponent \(- 7 / 16\), so both normalization inequalities hold eventually. The construction also proves the required local differentiability of \(f\) in the group chart.

10.6.3Subtracting a summable activity array

Let \(u_i\) be regular activities with nonempty assigned supports and nonnegative jet bounds \(k_i\). Suppose their rooted support-weighted bound is at most \(b\) per torus site and their derivatives in directions bounded by \(\tau\) are at most \(k_i\). Then

\(\displaystyle \sum_i k_i \leqslant | \Lambda | b.\)

This follows by summing over the roots and removing weights at least one. On the open set \(\mathcal{S}\) of strictly healthy configurations, every \(u_i\) is differentiable to order \(j\). Their series can be differentiated term by term there. To check the needed domination for any fixed list of directions, scale every direction by a common positive number so that its norm is at most \(\tau\). Multilinearity of the ordered derivative then multiplies the summable majorant \(k_i\) by only one fixed power of that number. The common majorant is uniform on \(\mathcal{S}\). The local differentiation theorem for normally convergent derivative series therefore applies, order by order. In particular, for \(U \in \mathcal{S}\) and normalized directions,

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_i u_i (U) \right| \leqslant | \Lambda | b, \qquad \left| D_{\ell} \sum_i u_i (U) \right| \leqslant | \Lambda | b \quad (| \ell | \leqslant j) .\) (10.32)

Suppose the local action identity on \(\mathcal{S}\) is

\(\displaystyle f (U) = z - H (U) - \sum_i u_i (U),\)

where \(z\) is constant. For any function \(q\) write \(\tilde{q} (U) = q (U) - q (1)\). The identity field belongs to \(\mathcal{S}\), because \(\tau > 0\). Subtracting its value yields

\(\displaystyle \tilde{H} (U) = \sum_i u_i (1) - \sum_i u_i (U) - \tilde{f} (U) .\)

Assume now that the chart bound with amplitude \(T\) and scale \(h^{- 1}\) holds for \(\tilde{f} \circ \text{actual}\) at every \(W'\) such that \(\|W' \| + 2 d < h\) and \(\text{actual} (W') \in \mathcal{S}\). This is a bound on the centered function itself. Apply (10.31) to \(\tilde{f}\). Combining it with (10.32) gives

\(\displaystyle \|D_{\ell} \tilde{H} (U)\| \leqslant j! 3^j T + 2 | \Lambda | b.\) (10.33)

The extra factor two covers the centered value at derivative order zero; for positive orders the identity-field constant differentiates to zero.

Now let \(U = \text{actual} (W)\) have no flagged plaquettes, allowing equality at the threshold, and assume \(\|W\| + 2 d < h\) with \(d > 0\). For \(0 \leqslant s \leqslant 1\), \(\|sW\| + 2 d < h\) as well. The radial retreat places \(U_s\) in \(\mathcal{S}\) for \(s < 1\) sufficiently close to one, so the same interior estimate applies there. Assume \(H\) is regular on the full healthy domain, as supplied by the local action construction. Its ordered derivatives through order \(j\) are continuous at \(U\) in the local group chart. Since \(U_s \to U\), passing to the limit in (10.33) gives the same bound at \(U\). Only \(H\) needs this boundary regularity: no boundary smoothness is inferred for the original masked series or for \(f\) from its interior identity.

10.7The principal regular insertion after one step

We combine the reference attenuation with the comparison to the actual principal cluster. This supplies the remaining ordinary principal term in the zero-coefficient transition of Subsection 7.12. The forced and nonprincipal terms retain the bounds proved in Subsection 9.2.

Let \(f\) be a real regular fine insertion on \(X\), of bound \(\tau \geqslant 0\), and let \(Y\) be its selected coarse enlargement. Write \(q = C_{B_f} (X)\), \(q' = C_{B_c} (Y)\), and \(m_f = e^{a_f q} \tau\) for its fine mass. Its polynomial moments through degree \(d\), in the sense of (7.72), are assumed zero whenever \(X\) is nonwrapping. For the selected transition, assume \(X\) has an anchor, \(B_f \leqslant 2 B_c\), and the coarse period side \(c\) satisfies \(100 (1 + B_c) \leqslant c\). These are the radius and room conditions used in the short-support geometry. The order and weight requirements are

\(\displaystyle \max \{2, d + 1\} \leqslant n_c, \qquad d \leqslant p_f, \qquad d + 2 \leqslant J_f,\)

where \(J_f\) is the fine kernel's available derivative level. Together with the local regularity required in the preceding sections, these permit the strip derivatives and the extra insertion derivative used in the Taylor argument. Let \(e = a_c + 2\) be the exponent charged by the coarse reserved mass, and put

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{m}_c (h) = e^{eq'} h, \qquad \gamma_L = \frac{L^{- 2 / 5}}{h_d}, \qquad w_L = \frac{\gamma_L}{2 L^d},\)

where \(h_d \geqslant 1\) is the fixed projection and frame cost. We prove a coarse derivative bound of reserved mass at most \(w_L m_f\) for the actual principal real insertion.

The constants in the short and long reference estimates, say \(A_d\) and \(A'_d\), are chosen before \(L\). Choose \(L\) so that the support compression inequality holds and

\(\displaystyle A_d L^{- d - 1} \leqslant w_L / 2, \qquad A'_d L^{- d - 1} \leqslant w_L / 2.\)

This is possible since each reduces to \(4 h_d A_d \leqslant L^{3 / 5}\) or the analogous inequality for \(A'_d\). The strip-to-polynomial bound is chosen before these two constants; it is uniform in all admissible periods. Only after these choices do we increase the entrance coupling to meet the reference smallness conditions.

Denote the actual principal insertion by \(F\) and the reference Gaussian integral by \(H\). For \(q' \leqslant B_c\), Subsection 9.3 gives, uniformly over the forests and replica choices,

\(\displaystyle |D^j (F - H) | \leqslant \tau z_{\beta}, \qquad 0 \leqslant j \leqslant n_c,\)

for the admissible coarse ordered directions. Here \(z_{\beta} \to 0\) after \(L\) is fixed. Averaging over the forests and replica choices preserves this bound: the norm of a finite average of differences is at most their common bound. The enlargement estimate gives

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{m}_c (\tau z_{\beta}) \leqslant e^{et_r} z_{\beta} m_f,\)

where \(t_r\) is the fixed allocation cost. Increase the entrance coupling so that \(e^{et_r} z_{\beta} \leqslant w_L / 2\).

If \(q^3 < L\), the stated radius, room and anchor conditions make \(X\) nonwrapping by the short-support geometry, so its assumed moments vanish. The short reference estimate then gives

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{m}_c (\text{bound of } H) \leqslant A_d L^{- d - 1} m_f .\)

If \(q^3 \geqslant L\), use the separate-gauge reference bound. Multiplying it by \([(1 + q)^3 / L]^{d + 1} \geqslant 1\) inserts \(L^{- d - 1}\); the extra support power is absorbed by the unused exponential fine mass, as proved in Subsection 9.5. Thus its reserved mass is at most \(A'_d L^{- d - 1} m_f\). In either case the reference bound and comparison bound add to at most \(w_L m_f\).

It remains to consider \(q' > B_c\). Here the principal reserve from Subsection 9.2 suffices directly. The large-support derivative bound has a fixed polynomial cost in \(q'\) multiplied by an exponential with at least one spare unit of support exponent. For a fixed integer \(k\),

\(\displaystyle (1 + q')^k e^{- q'} \leqslant [\sup_{t \geqslant 0} (1 + t)^k e^{- t / 2}] e^{- B_c / 2} .\)

The supremum is finite: differentiation of \(k \log (1 + t) - t / 2\) puts its maximum at \(t = \max \{0, 2 k - 1\}\). The remaining multiplier also contains the covariance and chart bound divided by the Gaussian scale: denote it by \(E_L (\beta)\). It has finite upper power exponent in the incoming coupling, as proved in Subsection 9.2; it is not a fixed constant. The principal reserved mass is at most \(E_L (\beta) e^{- B_c / 2} m_f\). For some fixed \(a, C\), eventually \(E_L (\beta) \leqslant C \beta^a\). Since \(B_c\) grows quadratically in \(\log \beta_c\), and \(\beta_f = \beta_c +\) a fixed shift after \(L\) is chosen, \(\log (C \beta^a e^{- B_c / 2}) \to - \infty\). Increase the coupling until \(E_L (\beta) e^{- B_c / 2} \leqslant w_L\). This estimate applies without a logarithmic chart on the whole support.

All cases give the asserted principal bound. For a complex insertion, apply the result to its real and imaginary parts. Each has the same admissible fine bound, and combining their two coarse bounds costs \(2 w_L m_f\). Physical normalization multiplies by \(L^d\), giving

\(\displaystyle L^d (2 w_L m_f) = \gamma_L m_f .\)

This is the principal share used by the ordinary projection estimate. It is produced for the same insertion, support, forest average and transition as the other terms; no replacement of the transition operator is involved. The case of a primary coefficient of positive grade uses moments only through one less than that grade and is handled separately below.

10.8Calibration of the primary coefficient transition

The primary transition must do more than bound a regular insertion. Its coefficient must approach the prescribed linear transport on the finite coefficient frame. We prove this by the same three comparisons used in the construction: the actual principal term to its Gaussian reference, the Gaussian reference to the linear predicted field, and that field to the finite polynomial probe calculation.

Fix a frame of total degree \(d\) and a nonconstant coordinate \(i\). Write \(g_i \in \{1, \ldots, d\}\) for its spatial grade and \(\ell_i\) for its order of vanishing in the field amplitude. These are different gradings. The primary insertion \(h_i\) is the local section at fine threshold \(\theta_f\), normalized by \(\theta_f^{- \ell_i}\) as in Subsection 7.10. Its regularity bound is at most the fixed fine-frame constant \(T_f\). All constants in this section may depend on the chosen frame and the block factor \(L\). Periods and the incoming coupling remain uniform. Both adjacent kernel levels are at least four, and the fine level is at least \(d\).

10.8.1Removing the Gaussian fluctuation at the flat field

Let \(h\) be one real component of \(h_i\). Denote by \(H (V)\) its reference Gaussian integral and by

\(\displaystyle h_0 (V) = h \left( \exp \left( \mathcal{A} \hspace{0.17em} \text{crop}_R (\log V) \right) \right)\)

the linear prediction, with the installed response \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(R = 45 (1 + B_c)\). This formula is used as a germ at \(V = 1\); its crop is in the identity gauge. Let \(k\) be the derivative order of a frame probe. Purchase regularity through order \(n \geqslant k + 1\), as well as \(n \geqslant 2\). The additional derivative will control the change of the insertion's base point.

Use the fixed Gaussian measure at \(V = 1\) and let \(R_V (z)\) be the normalized density ratio. Put \(A_V (z) = h (W (V, z))\), where \(W\) is the actual received reference configuration. With the cutoff \(\Gamma\) of the preceding section, the exact difference of integrands is

\(\displaystyle \Gamma R_V A_V - h_0 (V) = \Gamma R_V (A_V - h_0 (V)) + \Gamma (R_V - 1) h_0 (V) + (\Gamma - 1) h_0 (V) .\) (10.34)

Thus no derivative of the varying density is discarded.

Write \(P (z) \geqslant \max \{1, \| \text{tape} (z)\|\}\) for the fixed Gaussian coordinate norm augmented by one. On the cutoff support, the display comparison of Subsection 9.5 gives, for every jet through order \(n\), an error of fine direction bound

\(\displaystyle p (z) = C_n \frac{Qs^2 + g \| \text{tape} (z)\|}{\theta_f} .\)

The linear predictor's argument jets have bounded fine direction bound \(W \geqslant 1\). The nonlinear jets have bound at most \(W + p (z)\). For a partition with \(r\) arguments, subtract the two chain-rule terms by first changing their base point and then their arguments one at a time. Changing the base point costs at most \(C \tau p (z) (W + p (z))^r\): integrate the \((r + 1)\)st derivative of the insertion along the healthy radial segment proved earlier. Changing the arguments costs at most \(Cr \tau p (z) (W + p (z))^{r - 1}\). All these terms use \(r + 1 \leqslant n\). Summing the finitely many partitions proves

\(\displaystyle |D^j (A_V (z) - h_0 (V)) |_{V = 1} \leqslant C_n \tau p (z) (W + p (z))^n, \qquad j \leqslant k.\)

The same argument without subtraction gives \(|D^j h_0 (1) | \leqslant C_n \tau\).

The density-ratio calculation in Subsection 8.26 gives a small factor \(\theta_f\) times a polynomial in the read radius and \(P (z)^2\) in every positive-order jet of \(R_V - 1\) at \(1\). Its value there is zero since \(R_1 = 1\). More explicitly, differentiate \(\tfrac{1}{2} \log \det C (V) - \tfrac{1}{2} \langle z, C (V) z \rangle\) with the reference precision \(C\). Each positive-order term contains at least one admissible coarse direction, and the inverse and precision jets bound it by \(K_L \theta_f (1 + B_c)^a P (z)^2\). The partition formula for its exponential multiplies such factors, retaining at least one.

The cutoff error is controlled even outside its support. It is one on the plateau \(g \| \text{tape} (z)_a \| \leqslant \varepsilon \theta_f / 8\) for every coordinate \(a\). If a coordinate fails this test, then \((g / \theta_f) P (z)^2 \geqslant \varepsilon / 8\), while \(| \Gamma - 1| \leqslant 2\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle | \Gamma (z) - 1| \leqslant \frac{16}{\varepsilon} \frac{g}{\theta_f} P (z)^2 .\)

This bound does not require evaluating \(A_V\) off the cutoff support: there the first two terms of (10.34) vanish identically.

Collect the displayed small factors into \(J_{\beta} \geqslant 0\) so that

\(\displaystyle J_{\beta} \leqslant K_L (\theta_f + g / \theta_f) (1 + B_c)^a, \qquad p (z) \leqslant J_{\beta} P (z)^2 .\)

The bounded threshold ratio supplies \(s^2 / \theta_f \leqslant K_L \theta_f (1 + B_c)^a\). Increase the coupling until \(J_{\beta} \leqslant 1\). Leibniz' rule in (10.34) and the partition bounds then give

\(\displaystyle |D^j (\Gamma R_V A_V - h_0 (V)) |_{V = 1} \leqslant C_n \tau J_{\beta} P (z)^{4 (n + 1)}, \qquad j \leqslant k.\)

All factors from higher-order products are absorbed by the displayed power, since \(P \geqslant 1\) and \(J_{\beta} \leqslant 1\). The fixed Gaussian moments bound its integral by \(K'_L (1 + B_c)^b\). Compact cutoff support and the common local smoothness justify differentiation under the integral; the polynomial bound also dominates the subtracted constant term. The monitor estimate from the preceding section implies

\(\displaystyle \max_{j \leqslant k} |D^j H (1) - D^j h_0 (1) | \longrightarrow 0 \quad (\beta \to \infty) .\) (10.35)

The bound is uniform in every forest, replica choice and allowed period. Averaging therefore preserves it. Combining it with the actual-to-reference comparison gives the same convergence for the actual principal insertion.

10.8.2Normalized stencil functions and their jets

We give the finite-dimensional estimate needed to sample \(h_0\) on the frame probes. Let \(H_i\) be the analytic unnormalized stencil function of coordinate \(i\), on the finite-dimensional space of its link logs. It satisfies \(D^j H_i (0) = 0\) for \(j < \ell_i\). For \(0 < t \leqslant 1\) set

\(\displaystyle H_{i, t} (v) = t^{- \ell_i} H_i (tv) .\)

On any fixed ball \(\|tv\| \leqslant R_0\), Taylor's formula and compactness give, for every prescribed order \(j\),

\(\displaystyle \|D^j H_{i, t} (v)\| \leqslant C (1 +\|v\|)^{\ell_i} .\) (10.36)

Indeed let \(a = \max \{\ell_i - j, 0\}\). The vanishing derivatives imply \(\|D^j H_i (tv)\| \leqslant C \|tv\|^a\) by integrating \(a\) times from zero. For \(a = 0\), use the maximum of the continuous derivative on the ball. The scaling factor is \(t^{j - \ell_i + a} \leqslant 1\), with nonnegative exponent; and \(\|v\|^a \leqslant (1 +\|v\|)^{\ell_i}\). Taking a maximum over the finitely many required orders proves the bound with one \(C\).

Suppose two \(C^k\) curves \(v, q\) have jets at zero bounded by \(U + e, U\), respectively, and their jet differences through order \(k\) are at most \(e \geqslant 0\). Suppose also their values, multiplied by \(t\), lie in the fixed ball. Along the segment joining their values, use (10.36) through order \(k + 1\). For a partition with \(r \leqslant j \leqslant k\) blocks, the difference of the outer \(r\)th derivatives costs at most \(C (1 + U + e)^{\ell_i} e\). Replacing its \(r\) arguments one at a time costs at most \(Cr (1 + U + e)^{\ell_i + r} e\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \max_{j \leqslant k} \|D^j (H_{i, t} \circ v) (0) - D^j (H_{i, t} \circ q) (0)\| \leqslant C_{k, i, U} (1 + e)^{\ell_i + k} e.\) (10.37)

This estimate is uniform as \(t \downarrow 0\). The same partition calculation without subtraction bounds the jets of \(H_{i, t} \circ q\) uniformly. The constant is a finite sum over partitions, not a limit at the singular normalization \(t = 0\).

10.8.3The cropped torus probe and the full polynomial forecast

A probe consists of a fixed polynomial potential and a derivative order \(k \leqslant d\). Its test-link curve is the holonomy curve defined in Subsection 7.10. At derivative order \(j \leqslant k\), its link-log jet on the covering lattice is a polynomial field of degree at most \(dj\). This follows by expanding the exponential, product and logarithm to order \(j\): every term is a product of at most \(j\) potential factors, each of degree at most \(d\). Integration along a unit bond and finite differences do not increase that bound. The Taylor coefficients are fixed by the probe.

Normalize the coarse probe by its threshold \(y = \theta_c\) and the fine link field by \(t = \theta_f\), with \(y / t \leqslant 2\) and \(y \leqslant 1\). Apply the installed response to the cropped torus link-log curve and restrict to the fixed fine stencil; call the resulting normalized curve \(v_R\). Let \(q\) be the full covering-lattice prediction obtained by applying the same response to the polynomial link-log jets. Only its \(k\)-jet is used; it is equivalently obtained by the finite polynomial jet algebra of the probe. Both curves vanish at zero. The exact correspondence between periodic and covering convolution holds because the retained kernel is periodized from its absolutely summable covering kernel.

On covering sites of distance at most \(R\), the crop and the full probe have identical jets. The room inequality \(2 R < c\) ensures that these sites have their canonical lifts, so there is no seam error there. Outside this region, the probe jets have polynomial growth and the cropped jets have norm at most \(K (1 + 4 R)^{dk}\). The response strip bound gives

\(\displaystyle \max_{j \leqslant k} \|D^j v_R (0) - D^j q (0)\| \leqslant C \{1 + K (1 + 4 R)^{dk} \} e^{- \sigma R / 2} = : e_R .\) (10.38)

To verify the tail estimate, take half the exponential weight to bound the polynomial factor uniformly, and sum the remaining weighted kernel. The finite stencil offsets and four directions supply only a fixed multiplier. The torus sum is the same sum regrouped by residue class; absolute convergence permits this regrouping. This is the cropped-versus-full comparison, including all derivative orders, for the actual retained response.

The full predicted jets have a bound \(U\) independent of the period and \(R\). Indeed convolution of a degree-\(dj\) polynomial costs its fixed weighted kernel moment. In a nonzero chain-rule term with \(r \geqslant 1\) blocks, the threshold factor is \(y^r / t \leqslant y / t \leqslant 2\). The finitely many probe partitions therefore have a common bound. Apply (10.37) with \(t = \theta_f\) and \(R_0 = 1\): both input values are zero and hence lie in this fixed positive-radius ball. Since \(e_R \to 0\), the resulting normalized stencil jet difference tends to zero. Multiplication by the fixed physical factor \(L^d\) preserves that convergence.

To identify the prescribed coefficient transport explicitly, let \(\mathscr{S}_t\) be diagonal multiplication by \(t^{\ell_i}\), let \(\mathscr{D}_L\) be diagonal multiplication by \(L^{g_i}\), and let \(\mathscr{V}_L\) be diagonal multiplication by \(L^{d - g_i}\). If \(\mathscr{R}\) is coefficient recovery of the full polynomial forecast, the grade-mixing factor is the matrix

\(\displaystyle \mathscr{S}_{\theta_c} \hspace{0.17em} \mathscr{R} \hspace{0.17em} \mathscr{D}_L \hspace{0.17em} \mathscr{S}_{\theta_f^{- 1}}, \qquad x_i = \mathscr{S}_{\theta_c} \mathscr{\operatorname{RD}}_L \mathscr{S}_{\theta_f^{- 1}} \mathscr{V}_L e_i .\)

On column \(i\), the last three diagonal factors contribute \(L^{g_i} \theta_f^{- \ell_i} L^{d - g_i} = L^d \theta_f^{- \ell_i}\). The remaining coarse scaling substitutes \(\theta_c\) times the probe potential into its polynomial jet. Consequently, for every frame probe \(p\),

\(\displaystyle \text{probe}_p (x_i) = L^d D^{k_p} (H_{i, \theta_f} \circ q_p) (0) .\)

This identity is an equality of the finite jet calculation: evaluate the polynomial transport on the probe, substitute the response's polynomial moments, and use the local section's exact coefficient recovery. The normalization on each side is \(L^d \theta_f^{- \ell_i}\), with the same coarse threshold in the probe. Thus (10.38) identifies the linear prediction's probes with those of \(x_i\), to an error tending to zero. It does not replace the retained response by another continuum kernel.

10.8.4From probes to the paid primary coefficient

Let \(F_i\) be the actual complex principal insertion, already multiplied by \(L^d\), and let \(b_i\) be its extracted coefficient vector. The nonwrapping coarse stencil allows coefficient recovery by the fixed finite probe family. Ordered derivative convergence in (10.35) implies probe convergence: each probe derivative is a finite combination of ordered derivatives along its fixed test-link curve. The coefficients are uniformly bounded by its finite local bound. The last subsection and the triangle inequality therefore give

\(\displaystyle \max_p | \text{probe}_p (b_i - x_i) | \longrightarrow 0.\)

A finite separating family of linear probes controls the coefficient norm: on its unit sphere their maximum has a positive minimum by compactness. Equivalently use the finite recovery matrix from Subsection 7.10. Denote its bound by \(T_{\text{test}}\). Then \(\|b_i - x_i \|_1 \leqslant T_{\text{test}} \delta_{\beta}\), where \(\delta_{\beta} \to 0\) is the maximum displayed probe error.

The remaining regular rows have weighted sum tending to zero after \(L\) is fixed, by the nonprincipal estimate of Subsection 9.1. The extraction map on them costs the fixed read constant \(T_{\text{read}}\). If their summed majorant is \(u_{\beta}\), the total extracted coefficient differs from \(x_i\) by at most

\(\displaystyle p_{\beta} = T_{\text{test}} \delta_{\beta} + T_{\text{read}} u_{\beta} \longrightarrow 0.\)

The equality splitting the sum into its principal row and the other rows is justified by their absolute weighted summability.

Move this coefficient error into the local-section remainder. The local splitting identity of Subsection 7.9 makes this an exact equality of densities and of the ordinary and flagged profiles. Its additional cost is at most \(C_{\text{sec}} p_{\beta}\), with \(C_{\text{sec}}\) the fixed section and symmetry cost. Choose the probe error to spend half the desired allowance \(L^{- 2 / 5}\) and the nonprincipal error to spend the other half. The probe family and frame are finite, so one entrance threshold meets all these demands. This proves the small primary coefficient error for the prescribed transport \(x_i\), on the actual selected transition.

For completeness, its principal remainder has the required graded bound as well. The local section for coordinate \(i\) has zero moments through degree \(g_i - 1\). Apply the short reference estimate with that degree to its fixed stencil; it gives \(C_i L^{- g_i}\) times the fine mass. After \(L\) is fixed, choose the comparison error so that its reserved mass is at most \(L^{- g_i}\) times that mass. Apply this to the real and imaginary parts and multiply by \(L^d\). The resulting principal bound is at most

\(\displaystyle 2 (C_i + 1) L^{d - g_i} m_i .\)

Here the constant \(C_i\) is purchased before the block factor using the weighted response derivatives through order \(g_i - 1\). The projection calculation already proved bounds the remaining cost by fixed multiples of this bound and \(m_i\). Since \(L^{d - g_i} \geqslant 1\) and the normalized local-section mass \(m_i\) is bounded by the fixed frame credibility constant, choose the primary ledger constant \(E_J\) larger than their finite sum over the frame. This gives the graded remainder budget \(E_J L^{d - g_i}\). It is the ordinary primary budget used in the calibrated coefficient-field recursion, together with the small coefficient error just proved.

10.9Polynomial transport and the invertible coefficient map

The primary comparison of Subsection 10.8 uses a particular coefficient map. We prove the two-sided bound needed to iterate it, and then combine the primary, regular and forced insertions on one transition. The coefficient norm throughout is the sum of the absolute values of the coordinates.

10.9.1Exact polynomial transport on the cover

Fix the positive blocking penalty \(k > 0\). Let \(L\) be the block side, \(u = L^{- 1}\), and let \(j, t \geqslant 4\) be the adjacent fine and coarse kernel levels. Write \(A_x^{ab} (z)\) for the real one-form transport row at the fine site \(x\), with output direction \(a\), input direction \(b\), and coarse displacement \(z\). The actual input site in this row is \(\lfloor x / L \rfloor + z\); the floor here means the centered block quotient. The strip estimate gives constants \(\sigma, C > 0\), selected before \(L\), such that

\(\displaystyle \sum_z |A_0^{ab} (z) | e^{\sigma |z|_1} \leqslant Cu.\)

The analogous rows at other residues are exponentially summable too. We first show that, for every vector-valued polynomial \(p\) of degree less than \(j\),

\(\displaystyle (Ap) (x)_a = \sum_b \sum_z A_0^{ab} (z) p_b (ux + z) .\) (10.39)

This is an exact identity, including for \(x\) outside the central block.

Here are the low-frequency details. Denote the analytic alias column of Subsection 3.7 by \(\mathcal{A}_l (q)\), in its rescaled one-form convention. Fourier reconstruction and \(x = L \lfloor x / L \rfloor + \text{rem} (x)\) give

\(\displaystyle e^{iq \cdot \lfloor x / L \rfloor} \hat{A}_x^{ab} (q) = u \sum_l e^{i (q + 2 \pi l) \cdot x / L} \mathcal{A}_l (q)_{ab} .\)

For a nonzero alias \(l\), both terms in the regular one-form formula have the factor

\(\displaystyle z_l (q)^j, \qquad z_l (q) = \text{area}_L (- (q + 2 \pi l) / L), \qquad z_l (0) = 0.\)

Indeed the transposed filter contains the \(j\)th power of this area factor, and the nonprincipal gain contains the same factor. Removing it leaves the analytic port, rail and gain factors from the component estimates. Their denominators are nonzero at the origin. Thus \(\mathcal{A}_l = z_l^j F_l\) with \(F_l\) analytic there. Every derivative of order less than \(j\) of this term, even after multiplication by the displayed exponential, vanishes at zero: in the product of \(j\) vanishing factors some factor remains undifferentiated. The principal column has \(\mathcal{A}_0 (0) = I\), obtained by the regular principal formula at zero. In particular

\(\displaystyle \sum_z A_0^{ab} (z) = u \delta_{ab} .\) (10.40)

Restrict \(q\) to \(s \xi\) for a real covector \(\xi\), and differentiate \(r < j\) times in \(s\). Exponential summability justifies differentiation of the Fourier series: its differentiated terms are bounded by a polynomial in \(|z|_1\) times the summable exponential. All nonprincipal aliases disappear. Comparing the resulting principal expression at \(x\) and at zero, after inserting \(e^{is \xi (ux)}\) at zero, gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_z A_x^{ab} (z) \xi (\lfloor x / L \rfloor + z)^r = \sum_z A_0^{ab} (z) \xi (ux + z)^r .\)

Polarization of the symmetric \(r\)-linear forms gives the same identity for products of \(r\) possibly different covectors. Such products span the homogeneous polynomials. Summing the homogeneous parts and the finitely many colour coordinates proves (10.39).

Let \(p\) now have homogeneous degree \(r < j\). Expanding \(p (ux + z)\) multilinearly, a term containing \(s\) copies of \(x\) has a factor \(u^s\). Its remaining \(r - s\) factors are bounded by a constant times \((1 + |z|_1)^{r - s}\). Consequently its coefficient tensor has norm at most

\(\displaystyle C_r \|p\| u^{s + 1}, \qquad 0 \leqslant s \leqslant r.\)

The constants are independent of \(L\): use \(\sup_{v \geqslant 0} (1 + v)^{r - s} e^{- \sigma v} < \infty\) and the row bound. Passing from diagonal polynomial values to symmetric tensor norms costs only a fixed finite-dimensional constant. This follows by compactness of the tensor unit sphere and injectivity of the diagonal map, or directly by polarization. The degree-\(r\) term is exactly \(u^{r + 1} p (x)\) by (10.40). Adding homogeneous parts proves that, if \(\deg p < a \leqslant j\), then

\(\displaystyle \deg (Ap) < a, \qquad \deg (Ap - u^a p) < a - 1,\) (10.41)

and the degree-\(s\) output tensor is bounded by \(C_a u^{s + 1}\) times the sum of the input tensor norms. Lower-degree input terms do not affect the asserted highest-degree identity.

10.9.2The two gradings of the primary matrix

Let \(e_i\) be the canonical coordinate basis of the degree-\(d\) frame, with \(e_0\) the unit, and write \((g_i, \ell_i)\) for spatial grade and field-amplitude grade. Both lie between zero and \(d\). Let \(R\) be the unscaled polynomial coefficient transport used in Subsection 10.8. We claim

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} R_{bi} & = u^{g_i} \delta_{bi} & & (g_b \leqslant g_i), \hspace{3cm} \text{(10.42)} \\ R_{bi} & = 0 & & (\ell_b < \ell_i), \qquad |R_{bi} | \leqslant Du^{g_i} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(10.43)} \end{array}\)

Here \(D \geqslant 1\) is chosen before \(L\), for the fixed frame and kernel levels, and we assume \(d \leqslant j\).

To verify the first identity, differentiate the local holonomy display in directions of engineering weights \(w_1, \ldots, w_n\). A block of a chain-rule partition has weight \(a = \sum_{r \text{ in block}} w_r\). Its unsmoothed potential is polynomial of degree less than \(a\). By (10.41), the smoothed potential is \(u^a\) times that potential plus a polynomial of degree less than \(a - 1\). Expand the outer multilinear derivative of the \(i\)th section in these sums. If the total weight is at most \(g_i\), every term using one lower-degree correction has weight strictly below \(g_i\) and vanishes by the primary section's lower-grade cancellation: its multilinear derivative is zero on polynomial tuples of total engineering weight less than \(g_i\). Indeed its local-section tests read the homogeneous label \(e_i\), so all smaller spatial grades vanish (Subsection 7.10); the filtered gauge-coordinate argument of Subsection 7.11 then gives exactly this lower-weight assertion. No vanishing of its grade-\(g_i\) test is claimed. The remaining term has factor \(u^{\sum_r w_r}\). The unsmoothed section reads exactly \(e_i\), by the defining local-section identity. Extracting its homogeneous part of output bidegree \((g_b, \ell_b)\) proves (10.42). This also covers order zero.

For the second identity, the section has ordinary derivatives zero through order \(\ell_i - 1\) at the flat field. In a derivative of total order less than \(\ell_i\) of its composition, the outer derivative has no more arguments than that total order. It therefore vanishes. This proves the amplitude-grade restriction, regardless of spatial grade.

For the uniform bound, use the homogeneous decomposition following (10.41) in every inner block of the chain rule. A term of polynomial degree \(s\) in that block has weight \(s + 1\) and coefficient norm at most \(Cu^{s + 1}\). Expand the outer multilinear derivative. Terms whose weights sum to less than \(g_i\) vanish; every other term has a factor at most \(u^{g_i}\), since \(0 < u \leqslant 1\). The fixed stencil, bounded derivative order and finite partition set give a constant for each polynomial test. A finite family of these tests controls the coefficient norm, as proved in Subsection 7.9. Taking the maximum over the finitely many columns and tests proves the last inequality in (10.43).

Let \(f, c \in (0, 1]\) be the fine and coarse small-field thresholds, with \(c / f \leqslant \pi\) and \(f / c \leqslant \pi\), where \(\pi \geqslant 1\). The actual coefficient map \(B\) has entries

\(\displaystyle B_{bi} = c^{\ell_b} f^{- \ell_i} L^{g_i} R_{bi} .\)

If \(\ell_b \geqslant \ell_i\), then \(c^{\ell_b} / f^{\ell_i} \leqslant (c / f)^{\ell_i} \leqslant \pi^d\); otherwise the entry is zero. Set \(a = \pi^d D\). We have

\(\displaystyle a \geqslant 1, \qquad |B_{bi} | \leqslant a, \qquad |B_{ii} | = (c / f)^{\ell_i} \geqslant a^{- 1},\)

and an off-diagonal nonzero entry requires \(g_i < g_b\). The unit is fixed. The actual polynomial transport commutes with signed coordinate motions and conjugation: change variables in its absolutely convergent real kernel sum, use the covariance of the local section, and then take its finite jet. The three diagonal scalings in \(B\) preserve those actions because the bidegrees do.

10.9.3A bound for the inverse without a perturbative assumption

Let \(n\) be the number of coordinates and put \(K = a + na^2 \geqslant 1\). For \(y = Bv\), induction on the grade proves

\(\displaystyle |v_i | \leqslant K^r \|y\|_1 \quad (g_i < r) .\)

The claim is empty for \(r = 0\). If \(g_i < r + 1\), the other nonzero entries in row \(i\) come from grades less than \(g_i\), hence less than \(r\). Isolating the diagonal entry and applying the induction hypothesis gives

\(\displaystyle |v_i | \leqslant a \left( |y_i | + \sum_{b \ne i} |B_{ib} v_b | \right) \leqslant a \|y\|_1 + na^2 K^r \|y\|_1 \leqslant K^{r + 1} \|y\|_1 .\)

Summing with \(r = d + 1\), and bounding the forward map row by row, yields

\(\displaystyle \|Bv\|_1 \leqslant C_B \|v\|_1, \qquad \|v\|_1 \leqslant C_B \|Bv\|_1, \qquad C_B = n (a + na^2)^{d + 1} .\) (10.44)

Thus \(B\) is injective and, in this finite-dimensional space, bijective. Substituting \(v = B^{- 1} w\) gives the same bound for its inverse. The unit column and grade-zero row show also that \(B\) commutes with deletion of the unit coordinate, denoted by \(\text{core}\). In particular this argument requires no smallness of the off-diagonal entries.

10.10.1Keeping the three exact identities

For an inserted density \(F\), the local splitting constructs a coefficient field \(Q\) and a response \(\psi\) such that

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} \text{dens} (Q) & = v\mathcal{T}F,\\ \alpha & = \sum_r z_r \text{ord} (M_r), \qquad \omega = \sum_r z_r \text{flag} (M_r),\\ \mathcal{P}_c (Q_{\text{forced}}) - \text{hook} (\psi) & = v \sum_r z_r \mathcal{P}_c (\mathcal{D}M_r) . \end{aligned}\) (10.45)

Here \(\alpha\) is the input exponential entry, \(\omega\) its input forced profile, \(M_r\) are real elementary insertions, and \(\mathcal{P}_c\) groups output coefficients with the same support and inventory. The ordinary and flagged entries are separate functions; the density combines them with the background density and gas. 10.11Stability in the common support dictionary

The fixed-scale contraction argument needs convergence of a one-step map on each fixed input. Its output is a countable list of local terms. Pointwise convergence of the terms and boundedness of their total bound would not be enough: bound could escape to later addresses. The needed extra property is uniform smallness of the tails. We prove the summation and local-subtraction argument with this property explicit. The supports, thresholds and address encodings are fixed throughout.

10.11.1Uniform tails and products

For nonnegative numbers \(a_{N, i}\), indexed by a countable set \(I\), say that their tails are uniformly small if

\(\displaystyle \sup_N \sum_{i \in I} a_{N, i} < \infty, \qquad \forall \epsilon > 0 \exists I_0 \subset I \text{ finite} : \quad \sup_N \sum_{i \not\in I_0} a_{N, i} < \epsilon .\)

The definition is unchanged if the tail sums are replaced by sums over arbitrary finite subsets of \(I \setminus I_0\). Indeed all summands are nonnegative. Domination, addition and multiplication by a fixed nonnegative constant preserve this property. Extension by zero through a fixed injection preserves it as well.

It also passes to finite-subset products. If \(\sum_i a_{N, i} \leqslant C\), set \(A_{N, S} = \prod_{i \in S} a_{N, i}\) for finite subsets \(S \subset I\), including the empty subset. For every finite \(J \subset I\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S \subset J} A_{N, S} = \prod_{i \in J} (1 + a_{N, i}) \leqslant \exp \left( \sum_{i \in J} a_{N, i} \right) \leqslant e^C .\)

This bounds the entire sum by monotone passage through finite \(J\). If \(S\) is not contained in \(I_0\), choose one of its elements outside \(I_0\) and overcount by all such choices. Removing the chosen element gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S \mathrel{\not\subset} I_0} A_{N, S} \leqslant \sum_{i \not\in I_0} a_{N, i} \sum_{T \subset I \setminus \{i\}} A_{N, T} \leqslant e^C \sum_{i \not\in I_0} a_{N, i} .\)

Thus the finite collection of subsets of \(I_0\) captures all but an arbitrarily small bound. Compatibility restrictions only remove nonnegative terms and preserve the conclusion. This is the tail estimate for the finite-subset products in the gas.

There is a corresponding assertion for connected clusters, where indices may repeat. Suppose the elementary bounds \(a_{N, i}\) have uniformly small tails and obey the rooted cluster criterion with one fixed margin \(\rho < 1\). The rooted tree bound controls the total bound of clusters of length at least \(k + 1\) by

\(\displaystyle V \gamma \frac{\rho^k}{1 - \rho},\)

where \(V\) is the number of sites. Every cluster has a nonempty support, so summing the rooted bounds over sites bounds the unrooted sum. Choose \(k\) making this bound small. At each of the finitely many remaining lengths \(r \leqslant k\), the absolute connected-graph coefficient divided by \(r\)! has a finite maximum: there are finitely many graphs on \(r\) labeled vertices. The remaining bound is at most a constant times a product of \(r\) elementary bounds. The total bound of ordered tuples with some entry outside \(I_0\) is at most

\(\displaystyle rC^{r - 1} \sum_{i \not\in I_0} a_{N, i}, \qquad C = \sup_N \sum_i a_{N, i} .\)

Choose \(I_0\) to make these finitely many bounds small. The finite set of words of length at most \(k\) in \(I_0\) then captures all but an arbitrarily small cluster bound. This proves uniform tails for the connected expansion.

For marked clusters the margin is obtained as follows. Suppose the unmarked criterion has margin \(1 / 2\) and rooted allowance \(\gamma > 0\), and the marked elementary bounds \(v_{N, i}\) have uniformly small tails and total bound \(B\). Let \(M\) bound the support weight in the rooted criterion on this finite torus. Take

\(\displaystyle \delta = \frac{\gamma}{2 (1 + MB)} > 0.\)

The additional rooted bound of \(\delta v\) is at most \(\delta MB \leqslant \gamma / 2\). Thus \(a + \delta v\) satisfies the criterion with allowance \(3 \gamma / 2\). The degree bound is linear in this rooted allowance: its original bound is \(\mathcal{B}_{\text{degree}} (\gamma) \leqslant 1 / 2\), and hence \(\mathcal{B}_{\text{degree}} (3 \gamma / 2) = (3 / 2) \mathcal{B}_{\text{degree}} (\gamma) \leqslant 3 / 4\). For each word, expansion of the product gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j = 1}^r v_{N, i_j} \prod_{h \ne j} a_{N, i_h} \leqslant \delta^{- 1} \prod_{j = 1}^r (a_{N, i_j} + \delta v_{N, i_j}) .\)

The right side includes all terms with exactly one marked factor and has nonnegative remaining terms. At derivative order \(j\), the product rule costs at most \((r + 1)^j \leqslant 2^{jr}\); absorb \(2^j\) into each elementary bound before this comparison. The paid derivative criterion supplies the enlarged unmarked bounds. Use the total bound for the enlarged marked bounds when choosing \(B\) and then \(\delta\) above; thus the choice may depend on the fixed order \(j\). An unmarked bound may be replaced by the smaller of its uniform-tail majorant and its rooted-criterion majorant, preserving both bounds. The cluster argument just proved then gives uniform tails for the differentiated logarithm, not only a bound on its total bound.

Let \(u_{N, i}, v_{N, i}\) take values in a Banach space, and suppose their norms have uniformly small tails. If \(u_{N, i} - v_{N, i} \to 0\) for every fixed \(i\), then

\(\displaystyle \sum_i u_{N, i} - \sum_i v_{N, i} \longrightarrow 0. \) (10.48)

To prove it, choose a common finite \(I_0\) making each norm tail less than \(\epsilon / 4\). The difference of the two sums over \(I_0\) tends to zero, and is eventually less than \(\epsilon / 2\). The triangle inequality proves the assertion. Absolute convergence justifies the subtraction. The same proof applies to supremum norms of bounded functions and to norms of their fixed-order derivatives.

One useful form of uniform convergence does not require a compact configuration space. If for every sequence of configurations \(U_N\) one has \(f_N (U_N) - g_N (U_N) \to 0\), then

\(\displaystyle \sup_U |f_N (U) - g_N (U) | \longrightarrow 0.\)

Otherwise some subsequence would have supremum at least \(\epsilon\); choose on it points with difference greater than \(\epsilon / 2\), and complete them to a sequence arbitrarily. This contradicts the hypothesis. The same selection argument applies to derivative evaluations along normalized local charts. It is this uniform form, rather than pointwise convergence at each fixed configuration, that is needed for moving field insertions.

10.11.2From entrywise convergence to a small remainder bound

At the fixed scale let \(X_i\) be the regular supports and \(Y_i\) the forced supports. Write \(M (X) t\) for the support bound of an amplitude \(t \geqslant 0\). Since the torus is finite, it has finitely many supports, and

\(\displaystyle M_{\ast} := \max_X M (X) < \infty .\)

The bound is linear in the amplitude. Suppose two regular lists \(f_{N, i}, g_{N, i}\) have derivative majorants with uniformly small tails, and for every fixed \(i\) the difference has arbitrarily small derivative majorant for sufficiently large \(N\). For any \(\epsilon > 0\), choose \(I_0\) so that the sum of the two tail majorants is below \(\epsilon / (2 M_{\ast})\). On \(I_0\) use the common eventual majorant

\(\displaystyle \frac{\epsilon}{2 M_{\ast} (|I_0 | + 1)} .\)

Outside \(I_0\) use the sum of the original two majorants. The resulting majorants bound every difference, and their total support bound is at most \(\epsilon\). No supremum over infinitely many convergence thresholds has been taken.

For forced lists use the identical argument with uniform value bounds. If their contribution to the remainder norm is divided by \(\kappa > 0\), make their total unnormalized support bound at most \(\kappa \epsilon / 2\), and the regular bound at most \(\epsilon / 2\). Their sum has remainder seminorm at most \(\epsilon\). This proves smallness in the full remainder space of Subsection 10.13. In particular it does not identify arrays merely because they have the same gas evaluation.

10.11.3Local subtraction preserves this convergence

Let \(f_{N, i}\) be a regular local term on \(X_i\). Its subtraction uses the fixed linear jet projection \(\pi_i\), the fixed local section \(\sigma_i\), and the enlarged support \(\hat{X}_i\):

\(\displaystyle p_{N, i} = \pi_i f_{N, i}, \qquad r_{N, i} = f_{N, i} - \sigma_i p_{N, i} .\)

The mask identity leaves the forced rebate

\(\displaystyle b_{N, i} = \chi_{X_i} f_{N, i} - \sigma_i p_{N, i} - \chi_{\hat{X}_i} r_{N, i} .\)

At this fixed scale the already established projection and local section estimates bound each of these three linear maps by a fixed constant times its input derivative majorant. Subtracting two inputs gives exactly the same maps of their difference:

\(\displaystyle \Delta p_i = \pi_i \Delta f_i, \quad \Delta r_i = \Delta f_i - \sigma_i \pi_i \Delta f_i, \quad \Delta b_i = \chi_{X_i} \Delta f_i - \sigma_i \pi_i \Delta f_i - \chi_{\hat{X}_i} \Delta r_i .\)

Thus the projected coefficients converge by (10.48), and the residual and rebate lists retain the required small-tail bounds and entrywise convergence. Masks may be discontinuous at the threshold. They are identical on the two sides and have modulus at most one, so they do not create a continuity assumption at that threshold.

The forced correction also contains products with the background activities. On a fixed support–inventory profile it is a sum of terms

\(\displaystyle b_{N, i} (U) \hspace{0.17em} 1_{\mathcal{E} (i, S, U)} \prod_{j \in S} K_{N, j} (U) .\)

Here \(\mathcal{E}\) is the fixed compatibility and flag condition. For two backgrounds its indicator is the same: the address dictionary, supports, inventory and threshold are the same. For a fixed finite \(S\), the identity

\(\displaystyle \prod_{j \in S} u_j - \prod_{j \in S} v_j = \sum_{j \in S} (u_j - v_j) \prod_{k < j} u_k \prod_{k > j} v_k\)

in any chosen ordering shows convergence of the product when each activity converges uniformly and is uniformly bounded. The product-tail estimate above and (10.48) justify summing over \(S\). Multiplying by the convergent rebate, summing the finitely many profiles, and using the fixed extension of each profile to its address therefore preserves convergence. The same argument applies to the raw forced output before the rebate is added.

Consequently local subtraction, barcode grouping, addition of the background hook, and placement in the common dictionary preserve convergence of coefficients and smallness of the full remainder difference. These are exact operations on the common representation. Changing its forced dictionary midway would invalidate the product and mask comparisons.

10.11.4Summing the fixed input columns

For one fixed input remainder, its regular and forced bounds are summable. The selected one-step expansion has one primary column and one column for each regular and forced input entry. The primary column has a single fixed bound; the other columns have bounds proportional to these summable input bounds, with the forced bound divided by its fine-scale \(\kappa\). Thus there is a summable sequence \(w_i\) bounding, uniformly in terminal cutoff, both the coefficient norm and remainder bound of output column \(i\).

Suppose corresponding individual output columns have the same regular supports, convergent coefficient differences, and remainder differences tending to zero in the preceding sense. Choose a finite set of columns outside which \(\sum_i 2 w_i\) is less than \(\epsilon / 2\). On that finite set take a common convergence threshold and sum the small coefficient and remainder bounds. Outside it use \(2 w_i\). This proves convergence after all columns have been merged. The unit column is constant and requires no limiting argument. For the nonunit primary column the fixed input function is its local-section basis element. For regular columns it is the fixed masked regular input; for forced columns it is the fixed profile of the input array. Hence each column comparison uses the same inserted function on the two histories, although its background changes.

Signed motions and complex conjugation are bounded linear or real-linear maps on these data, with the fixed motion bounds already proved. The finite motion average therefore preserves the convergence. Applying the fixed coefficient pencil and the common profile capture gives convergence of the actual one-step coefficient map and its recorded remainder on every fixed coefficient vector. The coefficient space is finite dimensional; comparison on its finite basis, followed by the triangle inequality, gives operator-norm convergence. This is the local stability input used by the fixed-scale graph argument.

To apply this result to the selected histories one still needs the individual marked-column comparisons, with uniformly small tails, for their ordinary and forced expansions. Their hypotheses are more specific than a uniform total estimate: convergence of the adjacent path and Gaussian kernels, convergence of the background activities and coupling, and the common marked input are all required. The next analytic comparison must supply these on the same selected histories. The preceding proof isolates the exact summation, subtraction and normalization steps after that comparison; it does not replace it by an unproved assertion of continuity of the infinite expansion.

10.12Comparing the marked local integrals

We now supply the local analytic comparison used in Subsection 10.11. Fix an adjacent pair of scales and one period. Compare two sequences of paid transitions on the same support dictionary. Assume their fine and coarse canonical path coefficients, retained Gaussian rows and couplings have common limits. The path sums have the strict-width bounds proved above. For the two backgrounds assume that each ordinary and forced activity difference tends uniformly to zero and that their activity majorants have uniformly small tails. In a marked comparison the inserted function and its address are the same on both sides and its paid size is bounded. An application must supply these assumptions for the actual pair of admissible background histories and their retained path data. The insertion's support and eligibility conditions must hold for each background. When a regular address is used as a separate marked slot, its background entry is zero in each history.

The claim is convergence of the difference of each local marked integral and of every paid derivative on its healthy support. It includes uniformity over moving coarse configurations and normalized local charts. The principal issue is that the Gaussian measures in the two integrals vary. We first put them on a common physical variable; this also keeps possibly discontinuous inserted functions at exactly the same argument.

10.12.1One common physical integration variable

Fix a forest, its finite coordinate choice and the support pattern of a local term. Its integral is over a compact gauge slice \(Y\) with probability Haar measure and a real Gaussian coordinate \(z \in \mathbb{R}^D\). The coordinate change in the local integral has the form

\(\displaystyle w = \mu_N (Y) + g_N z, \qquad z = r_N (w - \mu_N (Y)), \quad r_N = g_N^{- 1} .\)

Coordinate permutations belonging to the fixed support pattern are included in this notation; they have unit absolute Jacobian. The paid coupling window gives constants \(0 < u \leqslant v < \infty\) such that \(u \leqslant r_N \leqslant v\), and the local mean estimate gives \(\| \mu_N (Y)\| \leqslant B\) independently of \(N, Y\). If \(\rho_N\) is the centered Gaussian density, the transformed density is

\(\displaystyle d_N (Y, w) = r_N^D \rho_N (r_N (w - \mu_N (Y))) .\)

The actual covariance bounds give fixed \(C, \tau > 0\) with \(\rho_N (z) \leqslant Ce^{- \tau \|z\|^2}\). Since

\(\displaystyle \|w\|^2 \leqslant 2\|w - \mu_N (Y) \|^2 + 2 B^2,\)

we have

\(\displaystyle d_N (Y, w) \leqslant v^D Ce^{\tau u^2 B^2} e^{- \tau u^2 \|w\|^2 / 2} . \) (10.49)

Also

\(\displaystyle 1 + \|r_N (w - \mu_N (Y))\| \leqslant (1 + v + vB) (1 +\|w\|) .\)

Thus a local integrand with bound \(C_k (1 +\|z\|)^k\) becomes bounded, after multiplication by \(d_N\), by a constant times

\(\displaystyle e^{- \tau u^2 \|w\|^2 / 2} (1 +\|w\|)^k .\)

This is integrable on \(\mathbb{R}^D\). The constants may depend on the fixed scale, period, support and derivative order, but not on the terminal cutoff, the slice point, or the moving coarse configuration.

The local differentiation theorem of Subsection 8.26 writes each derivative as an integral of the differentiated compensated integrand against the Gaussian measure frozen at the base configuration. The bounds in Sections 8.25 and 8.34 give the polynomial bound just used. Therefore the change of variable is applied to that already justified derivative integral; it does not differentiate a moving measure without compensation.

10.12.2Why the transformed integrand differences vanish

First hold the finite support pattern and coordinate choice fixed. At a fixed physical point \((Y, w)\) the fine configuration read by an inserted activity is independent of the Gaussian covariance and of the coupling: the identity \(\mu_N + g_N r_N (w - \mu_N) = w\) cancels their dependence exactly. Hence a common bounded marked function is evaluated at the same fine configuration on both sides. No continuity of that function is needed. An unmarked background activity has the same property; its two values differ by the assumed uniform activity difference.

The other factors in the compensated integrand are smooth finite expressions in the local path and retained-kernel data. Their continuity can be checked directly. A fixed length cutoff leaves only finitely many canonical paths: their displacement vectors lie in a finite lattice box. Each path holonomy is a finite product of link matrices; all its fixed-order derivatives are finite sums of such products. Convergence of its coefficient therefore gives convergence of these derivatives along any sequence of bounded local chart jets. Infinite canonical path sums have the same conclusion by the strict-width summability proved in Subsection 10.13.

The local Gram matrices are finite sums and products of those rows. The common positive lower bound passes to their limits. Their inverses converge by

\(\displaystyle A^{- 1} - B^{- 1} = A^{- 1} (B - A) B^{- 1} .\)

Repeated differentiation of \(AA^{- 1} = I\) proves convergence of all required inverse jets by induction. Determinants are polynomials in the entries, and the determinant stays strictly positive. Their logarithms and inverse square roots, and their fixed-order derivatives, consequently converge. This covers the selected covariance, means, Gaussian densities and density ratios, including the projected covariance in the compensation. The denominator density at a fixed \(w\) has a strictly positive limit; its reciprocal therefore causes no loss of pointwise convergence. Uniform domination is supplied separately by (10.49) and the compensated derivative bounds.

Before differentiating these factors, retain the full protected support of each activity and of the marked insertion. The base coarse configuration is healthy on the enlarged union of these supports, and the normalized chart is based there. Equation (8.157) holds on every active protected bond wherever the broader cutoff is nonzero near the base point; inactive bonds stay fixed by definition. Indeed, a nonzero cutoff germ gives the base fluctuation bound \(4 at\); continuity gives the strict \(8 at\) bound on a neighborhood. The compensator identity then applies on all the finitely many protected bonds on one common neighborhood. Where a cutoff has zero germ, the complete factor has zero germ. Consequently an activity or mark \(h_N\) occurs, as a germ in the chart parameter \(y\), only as

\(\displaystyle h_N (U_N (Y, w)) \hspace{0.17em} \zeta_N (y ; Y, w),\)

with \(U_N (Y, w)\) independent of \(y\) and \(\zeta_N\) smooth. This is exactly the held-factor identity (8.252), applied to each relevant read support. All derivatives fall on \(\zeta_N\) and the remaining smooth factors. Uniform convergence of activity values is therefore sufficient; convergence of their uncompensated derivatives is not being inferred.

For completeness, the elementary factors in a local product fall into four mathematical cases. A cell-action factor is the exponential of a finite local action and fixed cutoff functions; its action jets converge by the preceding path argument. A forced-activity factor is its value at the common fine configuration times a smooth local cutoff. An ordinary logarithmic factor is the exponential minus one of an ordinary activity value together with the local quadratic compensation; the first value is compared uniformly and the second by the matrix argument. A warning factor is a fixed local mask or charge times a smooth cutoff, possibly minus its constant term. The flag and compatibility decisions have the same values on both sides, because their geometric support data, threshold and physical configuration are identical. Product and chain rules now prove that the differences of the fixed-order jets of every finite product tend to zero. With one marked factor, replace the corresponding ordinary factor by the common inserted function times its cutoff; the same proof applies.

Moving configurations do not require convergence along the whole sequence. For any subsequence, take a further subsequence on which their finitely many link matrices and the bounded chart jets converge. An order-\(r\) derivative evaluation can be pulled back to \(r\) real parameters, so these finitely many jets lie in one fixed finite-dimensional bounded set. Compactness of the gauge group and of the closure of that set gives the subsequence. The support and coordinate choices range over finite sets, so take them constant on a further subsequence. The calculations above make the difference of the two transformed integrands tend to zero there. A common bounded mark need not itself converge: it multiplies a smooth-factor difference tending to zero, and its uniform bound suffices. If a difference failed to tend to zero in the original sequence, a subsequence would stay a positive distance from zero and could not have such a further subsequence. Thus the differences tend to zero without any preliminary convergence assumption on the moving configurations. Shared discontinuous indicators may also vary: for their common value \(\boldsymbol{1}_{E_N}\) use \(|\boldsymbol{1}_{E_N} (A_N - B_N) | \leqslant |A_N - B_N |\). Only the finite structural support and coordinate choices are frozen; no simultaneous stabilization of indicators for every \((Y, w)\) is needed.

10.12.3Integration and local derivative norms

Let \(F_N (Y, z)\) and \(G_N (Y, z)\) be the two compensated derivative integrands. For fixed \(Y, w\), their density-weighted transforms have difference tending to zero. Each is dominated by an integrable function of \(w\) independent of \(Y, N\). Dominated convergence first in \(w\) proves convergence of the difference of their inner integrals for almost every \(Y\). The norms of these differences are bounded by the integral of the sum of the two majorants. A second application of dominated convergence against the probability Haar measure in \(Y\) gives convergence of the complete local integrals. This argument also justifies the two uses of Fubini, since absolute integrability was established before passing to the limit.

Apply this to every moving base configuration and normalized chart of each order up to the paid order. The selection argument of Subsection 10.11 turns it into uniform convergence of those derivative evaluations. On the fixed finite torus, the conversion from normalized chart derivatives to the local derivative bound has a finite constant: all site separations are bounded, and only finitely many derivative orders occur. Therefore each individual ordinary output entry has difference tending to zero in its required derivative bound. For the forced entry use order zero and arbitrary moving configurations; its uniform value difference tends to zero.

Finite averaging over forests and coordinate choices preserves both statements. The ordinary marked logarithm is a sum of finite products with one marked factor, and the forced marked polynomial is formed by finite-subset products, addition of the ordinary logarithm, and differentiation in its one source parameter. Each operation preserves the entrywise convergence just proved. The marked cluster and product estimates in Subsection 10.11 give their uniform tails. Local subtraction and the background hook then give the convergence of each fixed input column in the actual common dictionary. Summation and finite motion averaging give the adjacent-scale stability required in Subsection 10.13.

This comparison uses both backgrounds' common-limit hypotheses; it does not prove convergence of the background activity trajectory. It proves the needed continuous dependence of the selected ordinary and marked local transformations, with their masks, Gaussian measures, derivatives and countable output lists retained.

10.13Convergence at a fixed scale and normalization

The full-mesh argument in Subsection 4.14 compares the final observable with one at a fixed intermediate scale. We now prove the convergence mechanism at that scale. The period and the intermediate scale are fixed throughout; the terminal scale tends to infinity. In particular the configuration space in each convergence statement below is one fixed finite torus.

10.13.1Contraction of the remainder graph

At scale \(m\), keep every regular support and forced address in one fixed dictionary. Let \(E_m\) be its vector space of admissible remainder arrays, with the seminorm obtained by minimizing their total regular mass plus \(\kappa_m^{- 1}\) times their forced mass. The regular mass uses the prescribed derivative bounds. This seminorm retains the actual arrays; quotienting by equality of their gas evaluations is not part of its definition. The elementary sum and scaling estimates give its triangle inequality and absolute homogeneity. Evaluation of the masked ordinary sum has norm at most one, and evaluation of any forced support–inventory profile has norm at most \(\kappa_m\). For example,

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_a \chi_{X_a} r_a (U) \right| \leqslant \sum_a \|r_a \|_{X_a} \leqslant \|r\|_{E_m}, \qquad |\mathcal{P}(r_{\text{forced}}) (b, U) | \leqslant \kappa_m \|r\|_{E_m} .\)

To justify the last inequalities when the infimum defines the norm, first use any admissible majorant within \(\varepsilon\) of the infimum and let \(\varepsilon \downarrow 0\). The same estimates applied to a difference give uniform Lipschitz bounds on these evaluation maps. The absolutely summable majorants justify subtracting the series.

Represent a linear family of remainders by a map \(G : F_d \to E_m\) with

\(\displaystyle \|Gv\|_{E_m} \leqslant s_m \| \text{core} \hspace{0.17em} v\|_1, \qquad s_m \geqslant 1.\)

Two such maps are within \(a \geqslant 0\) if

\(\displaystyle \|(G - H) v\|_{E_m} \leqslant as_m \|v\|_1 \quad \text{for every } v.\)

This relation is symmetric, has the additive triangle property, and any two allowed graphs are within \(2\).

One step produces from \(G\) a coarse graph \(\Phi G\) and an invertible coefficient map \(I_G\). Before reparametrizing by the coarse coefficient, write its remainder map as \(J_G = (\Phi G) I_G\). The paid regular and forced estimates of Subsection 10.10, applied to differences of insertions on the same supports, give

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} \|(I_G - I_H) v\|_1 & \leqslant \eta as_{m + 1} C_{\text{orb}} \|v\|_1,\\ \|(J_G - J_H) v\|_{E_m} & \leqslant \eta as_{m + 1} C_{\text{orb}} \|v\|_1 \end{aligned}\) (10.50)

when \(G, H\) are within \(a\). Here the same raw supports are used in the two expansions. Subtraction cancels their primary insertion. The ordinary integral and the marked singleton derivative are linear in the remaining insertion; local subtraction uses the same linear section. Thus the difference has the bound of the difference of the inputs. If representatives are chosen through derivative majorants, the bound is obtained with those majorants and then their infimum. Finite motion averaging costs \(C_{\text{orb}}\), exactly as in the weighted sum in Subsection 4.13. This explains why one must retain the common slots and supports.

The same one-step estimate gives \(\|I_G^{- 1} v\|_1 \leqslant s_{m + 1} \|v\|_1\). Put \(\delta = \eta s_{m + 1} C_{\text{orb}}\) and let \(b = L^{- 3 / 8}\). The schedule's two relevant payments are

\(\displaystyle \delta \leqslant b, \qquad 4 s_{m + 1} b \leqslant 1.\)

The second follows from its bank inequality: if \(q = L^{1 / 8} \geqslant 1\), that inequality gives \(4 s_{m + 1} \leqslant q\), while \(b = q^{- 3}\). For a coarse vector \(x\), set \(y = I_G^{- 1} x\). Then

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \|(\Phi G - \Phi H) x\|_{E_m} & \leqslant \|(J_G - J_H) y\|_{E_m} + \| \Phi H (I_H y - x)\|_{E_m}\\ & \leqslant (1 + s_m) \delta a \|y\|_1\\ & \leqslant (1 + s_m) \delta as_{m + 1} \|x\|_1 \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2} as_m \|x\|_1 . \end{array}\)

Thus the coarse graphs are within \(a / 2\). This contraction acts on the complete regular and forced remainder, after reparametrizing by its actual coefficient map.

10.13.2Forgetting the terminal graph

Write \(G_{m, N}\) for the selected graph at scale \(m\) in the history with terminal index \(N\). For each fixed \(m\), let \(\Phi_{m, N}\) be its one-step map. Retain the following precise local stability input: for every fixed admissible graph \(H\), the graphs \(\Phi_{m, N} H\) are Cauchy in the preceding “within” relation, and their coefficient maps \(I_{m, N, H}\) are Cauchy in operator norm. Their dependence on \(H\) has the uniform bounds just proved. This is a statement about the selected adjacent-scale kernels and their marked derivatives; the contraction alone does not imply it.

First, if \(H_N\) is Cauchy, then \(\Phi_{m, N} H_N\) is Cauchy. For a prescribed small error, fix a sufficiently late \(N_0\). Compare \(\Phi_{m, N} H_N\) with \(\Phi_{m, N} H_{N_0}\) using contraction, compare the latter at two terminal indices using fixed-input stability, and compare back to \(\Phi_{m, M} H_M\). The two outside errors are half the corresponding input errors. The identical three-term comparison with (10.50) proves that \(I_{m, N, H_N}\) is Cauchy.

For a fixed integer \(h \geqslant 0\), replace \(G_{m + h, N}\) by one fixed admissible graph and propagate it downward through \(h\) steps. The resulting graph \(V_{m, N}^{(h)}\) is Cauchy by the preceding paragraph and finite induction. The exact history recursion and the bound \(2\) on any initial graph difference imply

\(\displaystyle G_{m, N} \text{ is within } 2^{1 - h} \text{ of } V_{m, N}^{(h)}\)

for all sufficiently large \(N\). Given \(\varepsilon > 0\), choose \(h\) so that \(2^{1 - h} < \varepsilon / 4\), and then use the Cauchy property of \(V^{(h)}\). The triangle inequality proves that \(G_{m, N}\) is Cauchy. Only a finite number of levels is used for each error tolerance; no infinite product of inverse maps occurs.

Now fix the label \(y\) at its reference scale \(r\). Its coefficient at that scale is exactly \(y\), because the terminal coefficient is normalized by the inverse of its readout at \(r\). For each fixed \(m \geqslant r\), let \(x_{m, N}\) be the resulting coefficient. The history relation is

\(\displaystyle x_{m, N} = I_{m, N, G_{m + 1, N}} x_{m + 1, N} .\)

Suppose \(x_N = S_N y_N\) is Cauchy, \(S_N\) is Cauchy in operator norm, and \(\|S_N^{- 1} \| \leqslant b\). Then \(y_N\) is bounded and

\(\displaystyle \|y_N - y_M \|_1 \leqslant b \left( \|x_N - x_M \|_1 +\|S_M - S_N \| \hspace{0.17em} \|y_M \|_1 \right) \longrightarrow 0.\)

Starting with the constant reference coefficient, finite induction therefore proves that \(x_{m, N}\) is Cauchy at every fixed \(m\). Moreover

\(\displaystyle G_{m, N} x_{m, N} - G_{m, M} x_{m, M} = G_{m, N} (x_{m, N} - x_{m, M}) + (G_{m, N} - G_{m, M}) x_{m, M}\)

is Cauchy in \(E_m\). This proves convergence of the coefficient and Cauchyness of the full remainder data in \(E_m\) at the fixed scale, conditional only on the specified adjacent-scale stability input and its paid history realization. Their coefficient norms are bounded for all terminal indices; finitely many indices before the eventual recursion can be included by enlarging the bound.

10.13.3Uniform convergence of the inserted functions

The primary exponential-entry map is linear in \(x_{m, N}\) and uniformly bounded on the fixed finite torus. Indeed each of its finitely many local-section columns is continuous on the compact gauge configuration space; multiplication by its fixed mask preserves its supremum bound. Expanding in the coefficient basis therefore bounds its difference by \(C \|x_{m, N} - x_{m, M} \|_1\). The ordinary remainder and each forced profile are uniformly Cauchy by their evaluation bounds in the first subsection. Adding them proves that the exponential entries and forced profiles of the inserted fields are uniformly Cauchy over all configurations. Their bounded scalar-valued function spaces are complete, so they have uniform limits; no completeness of \(E_m\) is needed. This proof permits discontinuous masks: their supports and thresholds are fixed at this scale. Translation, signed coordinate motion and conjugation preserve the conclusion. The fixed physical scaling factor does too.

For a finite set of inserted labels all these statements hold on one common tail. Their coefficient bounds have finite bounds \(M_i\). Given positive allotted bounds \(\zeta_i\), take \(w_i = \zeta_i / (|M_i | + 1) > 0\). Then \(w_i\) times the actual coefficient bound is at most \(\zeta_i\), uniformly in terminal index. This is the positive weight choice used in the fixed-rung vault of Subsection 4.14.

10.13.4Convergence of the background density

We spell out the background convergence needed for the same normalized moments. At the fixed scale retain common path stencils and activity addresses. Suppose the path coefficients converge in the weighted sums of their differences: for one \(v > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_a |c_{N, a} - c_a | e^{v \text{length} (a)} \longrightarrow 0\)

for each one-form row, and the analogous atom-budget sums tend to zero for the quadratic loop, cubic and quartic arrays. All paths have the common bounds of the construction. Suppose also that the couplings \(\beta_N\) are Cauchy, and the background activity arrays are Cauchy in their common norm of rooted ordinary derivative bounds and forced value bounds. These are the precise background inputs used here.

The path convergence gives uniform convergence of the action on the fixed configuration space. To see this directly, each transported curvature in a row has norm at most \(2\), so a row difference is at most twice its coefficient-difference budget. A degree-\(q\) atom difference, with the same stencil, is at most \(2^q\) times its coefficient budget. The masks are identical for the two terms and hence do not add an error. The common path bounds bound all row values uniformly; consequently \(|\|u\|^2 -\|w\|^2 | \leqslant (\|u\|+\|w\|) \|u - w\|\) treats the quadratic row action. There are finitely many sites, plaquette directions and signed motions. Summing their estimates, and using boundedness and convergence of \(\beta_N\), proves the assertion for the full symmetrized action.

For the activity arrays, sum a rooted budget over the finitely many sites. Every ordinary term has its designated site, and every forced term has a nonempty marked support, so each term is counted at least once. If \(V\) is the number of sites and \(b, k\) are the fixed two activity bounds, the resulting uniform evaluation bounds are \(Vb \|A\|\) for the ordinary sum and \(Vk \|A\|\) for a forced profile. Applied to differences, these give uniform convergence of the ordinary activity sum and every support–inventory profile. The fixed torus has finitely many such profiles. Its gas is a finite sum over compatible subsets of these profiles, each multiplied by the fixed flag indicator. It therefore converges uniformly as well. This finite profile description does not require the original activity list to be finite; its regrouping was justified by the absolute budgets.

Write the full density as \(\rho_N\) and its scalar term as \(s_N\). Its scalar-stripped density

\(\displaystyle \tilde{\rho}_N = e^{- s_N} \rho_N\)

is the product of the gas and the exponential of minus the path action and ordinary activity sum. All exponent arguments are uniformly bounded on a tail. Exponentiation is uniformly continuous on that bounded interval, so the stripped density is uniformly Cauchy. Its integral \(Z_N^0\) against the probability Haar prior is Cauchy, since

\(\displaystyle |Z_N^0 - Z_M^0 | \leqslant \sup_U | \tilde{\rho}_N (U) - \tilde{\rho}_M (U) | .\)

The weighted path convergence just used follows from coefficientwise convergence at a strictly larger exponential width. Here is the useful precise implication. If \(v < w\), the row coefficients at width \(w\) have a common budget \(B\), and each coefficient converges, then

\(\displaystyle |c_{N, a} - c_a | e^{v \text{length} (a)} \leqslant 2 Be^{- (w - v) \text{length} (a)} .\)

Here the canonical row index enumerates a plaquette direction and a vector \(x \in \mathbb{Z}^4\); its chosen coordinate path has length \(|x|_1 + 4\). Consequently, for every \(\delta > 0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_a e^{- \delta \text{length} (a)} = |\mathcal{A}| e^{- 4 \delta} \left( \frac{1 + e^{- \delta}}{1 - e^{- \delta}} \right)^4 < \infty,\)

where \(\mathcal{A}\) is the finite set of plaquette directions. This is a sum over the chosen coordinate paths, not over all walks. Dominated convergence for series gives convergence of the whole row difference budget.

For an atom of degree \(q = 2, 3, 4\), the canonical index enumerates \(q\) plaquette directions and \(q - 1\) displacement vectors. The total length is \(\ell = \sum_{j = 1}^{q - 1} |x_j |_1 + 4 q\). Its coefficients have the form \(e^{- w \ell} C_N (a, \nu)\), where \(\nu\) runs through the finite color multi-indices. The coefficient arrays \(C_N\) converge coordinatewise and have a common Hilbert norm bound \(B\); finite partial sums give the same bound for their limit \(C\). The atom difference budget at width \(v < w\) is exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a, \nu} e^{- (w - v) \ell (a)} |C_N (a, \nu) - C (a, \nu) | .\)

Each summand is at most \(2 Be^{- (w - v) \ell (a)}\). Summing this majorant gives a finite multiple of

\(\displaystyle e^{- 4 q (w - v)} \left( \frac{1 + e^{- (w - v)}}{1 - e^{- (w - v)}} \right)^{4 (q - 1)} .\)

A second application of dominated convergence proves the required atom-budget convergence. Choose \(v\) strictly between the paid width and the minimum of the larger row and three atom widths. All four conclusions then hold at this one width, on a common tail. It is essential that these are the same canonical stencils; convergence of an unrelated finite-volume matrix is insufficient.

10.13.5A positive limit of the partition functions

Positivity of each \(Z_N^0\) alone would not suffice. We need one strictly positive lower bound independent of \(N\). Let \(0 < \theta \leqslant 1\) be the fixed scale's small-field threshold, let \(H = 2 \hat{\beta}_m\) be the common upper bound on its coupling, and let \(F, T\) be the ordinary and loop allowances there. With \(A\) the path bound and \(\mathcal{C}\) its quadratic clipping data, set

\(\displaystyle K = V \{F +\mathcal{B}_{\text{loop}} (\theta, H, A, T) + H \text{baseBudget} (\mathcal{C}, A, \theta)\} .\)

These quantities are finite and fixed while \(N\) varies. The small-link-ball proof in Subsection 4.2 gives

\(\displaystyle Z_N^0 \geqslant a_m := \exp \{- K - |E_m^{\text{bond}} |d_G \log (16 / \theta)\} > 0.\) (10.51)

Here \(E_m^{\text{bond}}\) is the finite set of positive bonds, not the remainder space, and \(d_G\) is the ambient real matrix dimension. The bound follows by taking every link in the ball of chord radius \(\theta / 4\): all flags vanish, the action is bounded by \(K\), and the product Haar probability is at least \((\theta / 16)^{|E_m^{\text{bond}} | d_G}\). Nonnegativity of the full selected density permits restriction of its integral to this event. The scalar factor cancels in \(Z_N^0\). Replacing \(\beta_N\) by \(H\) only increases the nonnegative action bound. Thus the Cauchy limit \(Z^0\) satisfies \(Z^0 \geqslant a_m > 0\).

Finally let \(P_{N, i} (U)\) and \(F_{N, i, b} (U)\) be the inserted exponential entries and forced profiles already shown uniformly Cauchy. Fix a finite census algebra, whose nonconstant generators are nilpotent. The scalar-stripped restored polynomial is

\(\displaystyle e^{- \text{path action}_N - \text{ordinary sum}_N} \exp_{\text{nil}} \hspace{-0.17em} \left( \sum_i t_i P_{N, i} \right) \mathcal{G} \hspace{-0.17em} \left( \text{background profiles}_N + \sum_i t_i F_{N, i} \right) .\)

The nilpotent exponential and the grouped gas are finite polynomials in their entries. Hence every mode is uniformly Cauchy and bounded. Their measurability follows from that of the local sections, masks and coefficient arrays and from the absolutely convergent groupings. Their Haar integrals are Cauchy. Dividing by \(Z_N^0 \to Z^0 > 0\) proves convergence of each normalized fixed-scale mode. The scalar-stripping identity identifies this ratio with the actual restored history moment, since its numerator and partition function contain the identical factor \(e^{- s_N}\). This proves the fixed-rung convergence required by the full-mesh comparison, under the stated selected branch and background convergence inputs. Their production must still be established for the same funded history.
