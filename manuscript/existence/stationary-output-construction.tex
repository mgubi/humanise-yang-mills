\section{Constructing the stationary output action}
\label{sec:stationary-output-construction}
We now apply the curvature lift to the Taylor coefficients of the
stationary action. The construction has two separate parts. Cubic
and quartic atoms match the classical action through degree four.
The quadratic response determines one scalar change in the kinetic
coefficient and an invariant loop remainder with vanishing moments.
Both parts use the same coarse bed and the same sequence of tori.

\subsection{Recovering Ward data from finite-volume coefficients}
For an analytic real function $F$ of the link coordinates, write
$C_n(F)=D^nF(0)/n!$, extended complex multilinearly to complex link
fields. A unit Fourier wave has a specified momentum, coordinate
direction, and colour basis vector. A common coefficient picture
$P$ for functions $F_k$ on tori $\Lambda_k$ means that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output-record}
 \left|C_n(F_k)[v_1,\ldots,v_n]
 -\mathbf1_{\sum p_j=0}\,|\Lambda_k|\,
      P(p_1,\ldots,p_{n-1})\right|
       \le |\Lambda_k|\varepsilon_k
\end{equation}
for every such tuple of unit waves; the zeroth momentum in the
picture is minus the sum of the other momenta. Direction and
colour indices in $P$ are understood. We assume a common positive
strip width and finite component bound, and $\varepsilon_k\to0$.
The side lengths tend to infinity. Exact recording is the case
$\varepsilon_k=0$.

Suppose $F_k$ is invariant, as a germ at the identity, under gauge
transformations and under simultaneous adjoint colour rotations.
Assume its positive-degree derivatives below degree $n$ vanish.
Differentiate the gauge-invariance identity once in the gauge
parameter and $n-1$ times in the link variables. The term where
all link derivatives fall on $F_k$ gives its $n$th derivative
with one argument equal to the linear gauge variation $d_0h$.
Every other term contains a positive-degree derivative of $F_k$
of degree less than $n$ and is zero. Consequently
\[
             C_n(F_k)[v_1,\ldots,d_0h,\ldots,v_n]=0.
\]
On Fourier waves this is exactly the contraction by
$q(p_j)$ in factor $j$. It holds in every factor by symmetry
of the derivative. This is also the leading case of the finite
gauge-variation identity in
Section~\ref{sec:ex-soft-curvature-receipts}.

Approximate any tuple of real independent momenta by finite-torus
frequencies and choose the zeroth frequency to make their sum
zero. Representatives may differ by $2\pi$ multiples; both the
picture and the symbols $q$ are periodic. Divide
\eqref{eq:output-record} by $|\Lambda_k|$. The error tends to
zero, while continuity of the common picture and of $q$ permits
passage to the limit in the finite contraction sum. Thus the
picture has all continuous-momentum Ward identities. Conjugating
real Taylor coefficients and reversing every wave gives
$\overline{P(-p)}=P(p)$ by the same limit. Differentiating the
constant colour-rotation invariance and passing to the limit
makes the real and imaginary colour tensors invariant. These
are precisely the hypotheses of
Section~\ref{sec:multileg-curvature-lift}.

For $n=2$, only the first derivative of $F_k$ needs to vanish.
This follows from colour invariance when the gauge Lie algebra is
simple: an invariant linear functional kills every bracket, and
the brackets span the algebra. The constant value of $F_k$ need
not be zero.

\subsection{Matching a leading Taylor coefficient}
Suppose $n\in\{3,4\}$, the two cases used below, each $F_k$ is
invariant under signed coordinate permutations as a germ, $F_k$ vanishes to order $n$, and $P$ records its
$n$th coefficient exactly, at strip cost $A$. The curvature lift
produces invariant real atoms of degree $n$ with total exponential
budget $KA$, at a positive width chosen before $A$ and $P$.
Let $S$ be their translation sum and then their average over the
finite group of signed coordinate permutations.

Here is the normalization check. Each transported plaquette slot
has zero constant term and linear term equal to its discrete curl.
The leading coefficient of the product of $n$ slots is therefore
the product of these curls. The normalized coefficient $C_n(S)$
is the symmetrization of that ordered product, namely the average
over the $n!$ permutations of its arguments. On unit Fourier
waves, summing the anchor over the torus gives zero unless the
momenta sum to zero, and gives $|\Lambda_k|$ otherwise. The
remaining absolutely convergent displacement sum is exactly the
curvature symbol contracted in every factor. It is $P$ by the
lifting identity. Since $C_n(F_k)$ is symmetric, symmetrizing this
identity retains it. Averaging orientations also retains it,
because $F_k$ is invariant under those orientations. Reflections
may multiply a wave by a unit-modulus edge phase; these phases
are included when applying the same identity to the transformed
wave. Thus $C_n(S)=C_n(F_k)$ on every tuple of unit Fourier waves.

These waves span the complexified link space, by finite Fourier
inversion. Multilinearity gives equality on arbitrary arguments
and hence on real fields. Both functions are analytic, and each
atom vanishes to order $n$; the exponential budget permits
termwise differentiation on a common complex link ball as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:ex-atom-stream-picture}. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output-leading-removal}
                           F_k-S=O(|x|^{n+1})
\end{equation}
along every real ray. In the approximate version of
\eqref{eq:output-record}, permutation and orientation averages
are convex averages, so the same argument retains the coefficient
error $|\Lambda_k|\varepsilon_k$ on each tuple of unit waves.
It does not insert a factorial or a further volume factor.

\subsection{The cubic and quartic construction}
Fix the outgoing clip $q$, with direct coefficient
$q_{\rm direct}\leq Z$, and the coarse bed $C$, with strip bound
$(w,M_H)$ for its metric and root rows, with its floor in the clip $q$
at every torus side, and with the sworn condition at every coarse side
of the sequence. Let $E_k$ be the classical stationary action in the
chosen chart, that is, the tested action of the incoming clip and path
along the stationary branch. Let $R_C$ be the path whose row family is
the root row of $C$ and whose loop, cubic and quartic families are
zero, and let $J_k$ be the complete quadratic action of $q,R_C$ on the
$k$th coarse torus: the average over signed lattice transformations
$s$ of
\[
 \sum_f\Bigl[q_{\rm direct}\,\varpi\bigl(\operatorname{hol}_f
      (s^*\exp x)\bigr)
      +\tfrac12\bigl\|\ell_{R_C}(s^*\exp x;f)\bigr\|^2\Bigr],
\]
the sum being over plaquettes, with $\varpi$ the Wilson plaquette
price and $\ell_{R_C}$ the row field. It contains the direct Wilson
term of the clip as well as the squared row term. Both $E_k$ and $J_k$
are analytic, gauge invariant, and invariant under signed coordinate
permutations. Their values and first derivatives at zero are zero.
Their Hessians at zero agree, and equal the coarse Gaussian form
$\mathcal K_C(x,y)=\sum_f\langle(d_1x)_f,(M_Cd_1y)_f\rangle$ of the
metric of $C$. For $J_k$ this is
Lemma~\ref{lem:ex-soft-quadratic-hessian}, applied to $R_C$ at the
coarse side; $R_C$ is attached to $C$ by construction and satisfies
the path bounds by the root strip bound, and the lemma uses the floor
and the sworn condition of $C$ at that side. For $E_k$ it is the reduced
Hessian (heat) identity of the stationary branch for this same bed
$C$,
\[
 D^2E_k(0)=\mathcal K_C ,
\]
which is one of the chart receipts treated in
Section~\ref{subsec:path-stationary-chart}. It is an input here, as is
the Hessian gap $\eta$ common to all $k$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:stationary-output-path}: it
holds for the coarse bed matched to the fine bed and the installed
tower by the two-scale identities, not for an arbitrarily chosen
coarse bed. Thus
\[
                              F_k=E_k-J_k
\]
has order three. Suppose $E_k$ has exact common coefficient
pictures of degrees three and four with component bound $M\ge1$
at width $\sigma$. The row estimate of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ex-row-quadratic-price} for the squared row term,
and the Wilson pictures of Proposition~\ref{prop:ex-wilson-stream}
multiplied by $q_{\rm direct}\leq Z$ for the direct term, supply such
pictures for $J_k$ at positive widths and fixed bounds $P_3,P_4$,
which depend on $w,M_H,Z$.
Choose the minimum of the three widths. Both residual pictures
then have cost at most
\[
                             M(1+P_3+P_4).
\]
Apply \eqref{eq:output-leading-removal} at $n=3$ to obtain an
invariant cubic atom action $S_3$ of budget $K_3M$ and with
$F_k-S_3$ of order four.

Its fourth coefficient must also be subtracted. In general an
atom of degree three has nonzero higher Taylor terms. The uniform
atom-picture estimate gives an exact common degree-four picture
for $S_3$, of cost at most $K_3M B_4$ at a positive width. Subtract
it from the degree-four picture of $F_k$, and take the minimum
width. The resulting cost is at most a fixed multiple of $M$.
Apply the leading-removal construction at $n=4$ to $F_k-S_3$.
This produces an invariant quartic action $S_4$, again with budget
bounded by a fixed multiple of $M$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output-classical-five}
                     E_k-J_k-S_3-S_4=O(|x|^5).
\end{equation}
The two atom families and their common positive width are chosen
independently of $k$. They consequently match the action on every
torus in the sequence. Taylor expansion on rays followed by
polarization proves equality of the full derivatives of orders
zero through four. The chart's tag-independence is an equality
of germs, so the same derivatives agree for every allowed choice
of tag, without choosing a different path for each tag.

\subsection{Removing the unique quadratic kinetic coefficient}
We need one further consequence of the Ward and lattice symmetries.
Let $f_{ab}^{uv}(p)$ be an analytic quadratic coefficient picture,
where $a,b$ are coordinate indices and $u,v$ colour indices,
with momenta $(-p,p)$. Suppose it comes from the common recording
of analytic, gauge-invariant, signed-coordinate-invariant real
functions as above. It then has the exchange symmetry
\[
                  f_{ab}^{uv}(p)=f_{ba}^{vu}(-p).
\]
The leading homogeneous terms transform as ordinary coordinate
tensors under signed permutations: the reflected-edge phases have
constant term equal to the relevant coordinate sign. Once all
lower homogeneous terms vanish, the higher terms of these phases
do not contribute at the order being considered.

First $f(0)=0$. Indeed the first-order part of the Ward identity
for a constant tensor is $\sum_b p_b f_{ab}^{uv}(0)=0$ for all
$p$, and coordinate vectors detect every entry. For its linear
term write $K_{ab}^{uv}(p)$. The left and right Ward identities
give
\[
 \sum_b p_bK_{ab}^{uv}(p)=0,\qquad
 \sum_a p_aK_{ab}^{uv}(p)=0.
\]
They imply $K_{ab}^{uv}(e_b)=K_{ab}^{uv}(e_a)=0$ and
$K_{ab}^{uv}(e_j)+K_{aj}^{uv}(e_b)=0$. If $a,b,j$ are distinct,
the coordinate permutation exchanging $b$ and $j$ identifies the
two terms, so both vanish. When indices coincide the preceding
two identities already imply the same conclusion. The linear
term is zero and $f$ has order two.

Let $Q_{ab}^{uv}(p)$ be its quadratic homogeneous term. Fix
$a\ne b$ and one unit colour vector $e_u$. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output-one-test}
                      Q_{aa}^{uu}(e_b)=0
\end{equation}
forces $Q=0$. At fixed $a,p$, the colour form
$Q_{aa}^{uv}(p)$ is real, symmetric, and adjoint invariant.
Reality follows from momentum reversal and even homogeneity;
symmetry follows from the exchange identity. Such a form is a
scalar multiple of the invariant inner product. Indeed its
self-adjoint representing operator commutes with every adjoint
operator; each eigenspace is an ideal, so simplicity leaves only
one eigenvalue. Therefore \eqref{eq:output-one-test} kills that
whole colour form. Coordinate permutations transfer the conclusion
to $Q_{xx}^{uv}(e_y)$ for all $x\ne y$. The case $x=y$ follows
directly from the leading Ward identity.

Polarize $Q$, writing $T_{xy}^{uv}(e_k,e_l)$ for its symmetric
bilinear coefficient in momentum. Reflection in coordinate $r$
multiplies this entry by $(-1)^{\mathbf1_{r=k}+\mathbf1_{r=l}}$
on the momentum side and by
$(-1)^{\mathbf1_{r=x}+\mathbf1_{r=y}}$ on the tensor side.
Unless these parities agree for every $r$, the entry is zero.
For $x=y$ this eliminates every mixed momentum coefficient;
the diagonal coefficients were just shown to vanish. For $x\ne y$
it leaves only the mixed pair $\{k,l\}=\{x,y\}$. Finally insert
$p=e_x+e_y$ in the Ward identity with first tensor index $x$.
All other entries have vanished, so it kills this last mixed
coefficient as well. Thus $Q=0$ in every colour component.

The picture now has order three. Reversal of all coordinate signs
acts by $+1$ on its two tensor indices but by $-1$ on a homogeneous
cubic polynomial. Its cubic term is therefore zero. We have proved
that the single test \eqref{eq:output-one-test} gives order four.

Let $H(p)$ be the coarse metric row, with $H(0)=I$, and let $b_H$
be the exactly recorded quadratic picture of its quadratic path
action. Its quadratic homogeneous term is
\[
           (b_H)_{ab}^{uv,[2]}(p)
          =\tfrac12\bigl(|p|^2\delta_{ab}-p_ap_b\bigr)\delta_{uv}.
\]
This follows by replacing both discrete curls by their linear
terms $ip\wedge$ and using $H(0)=I$; the action has coefficient
$1/2$ and its two waves have opposite momenta. In particular the
transverse unit test is $1/2$. Fixing distinct axes $0,1$ and any
unit colour basis vector $e_u$, define
\[
                   \alpha=2\operatorname{Re}f_{11}^{uu,[2]}(e_0).
\]
Then $f-\alpha b_H$ passes the zero test. It inherits the Ward,
reality, exchange, and lattice symmetries by subtracting the
actual quadratic action, so it has order four. This establishes
the normalization of $\alpha$ before any curvature lift is chosen.

\subsection{The loop remainder and its real-wave estimate}
Let $U_k$ denote the local determinant and Jacobian response and
$V_k$ the inherited loop response. Suppose their common pictures
are $f$ and $g$, with strip bounds $\Phi$ and $R$ on width
$\sigma$, respectively. Assume $f$ records $U_k$ with error
$|\Lambda_k|\varepsilon_k$, while $g$ records $V_k$ exactly and
has order four. Assume also that $J_k$ has the exact picture
$b_H$, of bound $B_H$, at that width. All three functions have
the invariances used above. Then
\[
                h=f-\alpha b_H+g
\]
has order four, strip cost at most $\Phi+|\alpha|B_H+R$,
and records $U_k-\alpha J_k+V_k$ with the same error.
The two-factor lift, with retained order two, gives an invariant
loop atom family of budget
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output-loop-budget}
                          D(\Phi+|\alpha|B_H+R).
\end{equation}
It has zero total coefficient and zero first displacement moments.
Let $S_2$ be its orientation-averaged action. The normalization
argument above shows that the difference between
$C_2(S_2)$ and $C_2(U_k-\alpha J_k+V_k)$ on every pair of unit
complex Fourier waves is bounded by $|\Lambda_k|\varepsilon_k$.

Put $d=4\dim\mathfrak g$, the number of one-form direction and
colour coordinates at a site. A complex wave with all component
amplitudes bounded by one is a sum of $d$ unit basis waves, so
bilinearity bounds the coefficient difference by
$d^2|\Lambda_k|\varepsilon_k$. A real wave is half the sum of
that complex wave and the opposite-momentum wave with conjugate
amplitude. Expanding two real waves gives four terms, each with
factor $1/4$, and hence the same bound. Finally $D^2=2C_2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output-real-wave}
 \left|D^2(S_2-U_k+\alpha J_k-V_k)(0)[w_p,w_q]\right|
        \le 2d^2|\Lambda_k|\varepsilon_k.
\end{equation}
The momenta $p,q$ are arbitrary; no opposite-momentum restriction
has been added.

\subsection{One common output path}
Take the row family to be the coarse bed roots and take the loop,
cubic, and quartic families just constructed. Their exact row
attachment is unchanged by adding the other three families.
The metric identity follows from that root attachment and the
coarse bed identity. Invariance and the two loop moment identities
hold separately for the constructed atoms. At any positive width
below the common construction width, the row and cubic/quartic
budgets are satisfied whenever
\[
      A'\ge\max\{\operatorname{rowPrice}(w,M_H),KM\},
 \qquad
      \Lambda'\ge D(\Phi+|\alpha|B_H+R).
\]
Here $M_H$ is the root-strip bound and $M$ the classical Taylor
bound. Width reduction only decreases each weighted absolute sum.
Thus this is a path with all the required summability, metric,
invariance, and moment properties, not merely a list of formal jets.

Its classical part is $J_k+S_3+S_4$ and obeys
\eqref{eq:output-classical-five}. Its response part is
$\alpha J_k+S_2$. The stationary determinant identity identifies
the actual thermal response, as a germ, with $U_k+V_k$.
Consequently \eqref{eq:output-real-wave} is the required Hessian
comparison for the actual response. If
\[
                 2d^2\varepsilon_k
                    \le\Theta\Omega e^{-g\,\mathrm{side}(\Lambda_k)},
\]
its right side is at most
$|\Lambda_k|\Theta\Omega e^{-g\,\mathrm{side}(\Lambda_k)}$.
The stationary gap gives positivity of the stationary fluctuation
Hessian, so its determinant remains positive near zero; the analytic
chart gives the required smooth response germs.
These conclusions all concern the same output path. Production of
the common determinant picture, the inherited picture with its
fourth-order bound, and the displayed exponential error is still
required when this construction is used in a transition.

We record the result with its order of choices.

\begin{proposition}[The stationary output path]
\label{prop:stationary-output-path}
Let $G$ be simple and fix $\sigma>0$, $w>0$, $M_H\geq0$ and
$Z\geq0$. There are $s$ with $0<s\leq\min(\sigma,w)$ and
$K,D\geq0$, depending only on these data, with the following
property. Let coarse sides $c_k\to\infty$ and fine sides $f_k$ be
given, and for every $k$ a block step from the coarse to the fine
torus whose patch satisfies the branch compatibility of the kernel,
whose fine side is at least one hundred times the macro radius of the
patch and whose coarse side exceeds one, a forest, a choice $t_k$ of block representatives and a
map $\rho_k$ into the tree chart. Let $p,q$ be clips with
$q_{\rm direct}\leq Z$, let $B,C$ be beds, $U$ a path, $\mu>0$,
$\eta>0$, and fix a local Haar chart. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $U$ has path bounds in $p$ and is attached to $B$; the floor of
 $B,p$ and the sworn condition of $B$ hold at every $f_k$;
\item for every $k$, $\rho_k$ is a stationary branch in the sense of
 Section~\ref{sec:stationary-germ-symmetry} with Hessian gap $\eta$,
 and it satisfies the reduced Hessian identity for the bed $C$;
\item the metric and root rows of $C$ have strip bound $(w,M_H)$, the
 floor of $C,q$ holds at every torus side, and the sworn condition of
 $C$ holds at every $c_k$;
\item the classical stationary actions $E_k$, formed with $t_k$, have
 exactly recorded common pictures of degrees three and four, with
 components holomorphic on the strip of width $\sigma$ and bounded by
 $X\geq1$;
\item there are two-leg pictures $f$ and $g$ with components
 holomorphic on the strip of width $\sigma$ and bounded by $\Phi$ and
 $R$, such that $f$ records the completed determinant correction
 $\mathcal B_{t_k}$ along $\rho_k$, with regulator $\mu$, with error
 $|\Lambda_k|\varepsilon_k$, where $\varepsilon_k\geq0$ and
 $\varepsilon_k\to0$, and $g$ records the inherited loop of $U$ along
 $\rho_k$ exactly and has order four at zero momentum.
\end{enumerate}
Fix a color $u$ and put $\alpha=\alpha_u(f)$. Then there is a path $T$
such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the row family of $T$ is the root row of $C$, and $T$ is
 attached to $C$;
\item[(b)] $T$ has the path bounds $(s',A',\Lambda')$ in $q$ whenever
 $0<s'\leq s$,
 $A'\geq\max\{\operatorname{rowPrice}(w,M_H),KX\}$ and
 $\Lambda'\geq D\bigl(\Phi+|\alpha|
   \operatorname{bedCharge}(\min(\sigma,w),M_H)+R\bigr)$;
\item[(c)] for every $k$, every $j\leq4$ and every choice of block
 representatives, the tested action of $p,U$ along $\rho_k$ and the
 main term of $T$ in the clip $q$ on the $k$th coarse torus are
 analytic at zero and have the same $j$th derivative there;
\item[(d)] for every $k$ and all $\Theta,\gamma,\Omega$ with
 $2d^2\varepsilon_k\leq\Theta\Omega e^{-\gamma c_k}$: for every choice
 of block representatives the determinant of the tree-chart Hessian of
 the tested action $\mathcal T$ is positive along $\rho_k$ near zero,
 the thermal function
 $\vartheta(x)=\mathcal L_U(x,\rho_k(x))
   +\frac12\log\det D_z^2\mathcal T(x,\rho_k(x))
   -\log\prod_bJ(\rho_k(x)_b)$,
 formed with the loop term $\mathcal L_U$ of $U$ and the density $J$
 of the Haar chart, and the function
 $\alpha\mathcal K_{2,T}+\mathcal L_T$, formed from the quadratic term
 and the loop term of $T$ in $q$, are analytic at zero, and their
 second derivatives at zero on any two real plane waves with complex
 amplitudes of modulus at most one differ by at most
 $|\Lambda_k|\Theta\Omega e^{-\gamma c_k}$.
\end{enumerate}
The path $T$ does not depend on the choices of representatives in
\textup{(c)} and \textup{(d)}, nor on $\Theta,\gamma,\Omega$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The preceding subsections prove this. The cubic and quartic families
are those of \eqref{eq:output-classical-five}, constructed from the
pictures in~(4) and from the pictures of $J_k$, which exist by~(3);
the equality of Hessians used there is the lemma cited for $J_k$,
which uses~(3), and the reduced Hessian identity in~(2). The symmetry
and gauge properties of $E_k$, of the determinant correction and of
the inherited loop are those of
Section~\ref{sec:stationary-germ-symmetry}, whose standing hypotheses
are~(1) and~(2). The loop family is the one constructed from
$h=f-\alpha b_H+g$, with budget \eqref{eq:output-loop-budget}. The
path is assembled in the present subsection, which gives (a) and (b).
Assertion (c) for the representatives $t_k$ is
\eqref{eq:output-classical-five} with polarization; for other
representatives it follows from the equality of germs proved in
Section~\ref{sec:stationary-germ-symmetry}. For (d), the moving Haar
identity \eqref{eq:ex-thermal-determinant} identifies the thermal
function, as a germ, with the inherited loop plus the completed
determinant correction, up to the choice of representatives, which
again does not change the germ; \eqref{eq:output-real-wave} is then
the stated comparison.
\end{proof}

The stationary data in~(2) and the pictures in~(4) and~(5) are inputs
to this proposition. The hypotheses are extracted from the standing data of the one-step
construction, in which the incoming path $U$ is attached to the fine
bed $B$ and the output path to the coarse bed $C$. In the one-step
construction the pictures
in~(4) are those of Proposition~\ref{prop:ex-classical-stationary-pictures},
the inherited picture $g$ is that of
Proposition~\ref{prop:inherited-loop-picture}, and the determinant
picture $f$ with its exponentially small error is the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:ex-stationary-determinant-prices}.
