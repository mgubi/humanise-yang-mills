% The common finite Gaussian model underlying a full reader family.
\section{One joint Gaussian model for the whole reader family}
\label{sec:ex-lattice-all-arity-reader-gaussian-model}

The leading Gaussian construction assigns a local reference model to an
entire finite family of readers at once. It is important that this is one
joint law: replacing it by a separate Gaussian sample for each reader would
lose the cross-covariances used in the principal comparison. The model is an
auxiliary leading law for a fixed block, pose, forest, and tag. It is not a
claim that the complete interacting block measure is Gaussian.

\subsection{Fixed data and the common sample space}

Fix adjacent coarse and fine levels, a block and its point set, a coarse
configuration $V$, a store of flags, a forest $F$, and one channel tag $c$.
Fix also the positive scale parameter $x$, the channel mask parameter $q$
and width $d$, the signed transport
$s$, its coarse anchor $t$, the fine anchor $o$, and a pose at that anchor.
Transport the store, forest, tag, and
coarse field together by this same signed map. Denote the transported data
by $A^\sharp,F^\sharp,c^\sharp,V^\sharp$. In particular, the transformed
tag is the image of $c$ under the signed channel action; it is not chosen
again independently.

Let $I=\operatorname{In}(x,A,F)$ be the finite set of active fine-link Lie
coordinates for the fixed store $A$ and forest $F$. Let $\Pi$ be the
centered covariance prescribed by the channel on these input coordinates.
The sample space is
\begin{equation}
 \Omega=\mathcal H_{\mathrm{frozen}}\times\mathbb R^I,
 \qquad
 d\mu(H,Z)=d\pi_{\mathrm{Haar}}(H)\,d\gamma_{\Pi}(Z).
 \label{eq:ex-lattice-reader-space}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{frozen}}$ is the product of the compact gauge
groups on the bonds frozen by the store, with normalized product Haar
measure, and $\gamma_\Pi$ is the centered Gaussian measure with covariance
matrix $\Pi$. The two coordinates in \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-space} are independent because the law
is a product. The channel covariance and all its tags and masks remain fixed
throughout the construction.

For each sample $(H,Z)$, form the channel's local drift $a(H)$ from the
frozen links, using the fixed tube, tag, mask parameter, and width. Convert
$a(H)+gZ$ to the gauge group by the fixed
exponential-coordinate chart, then apply the finite-volume local link
update to $V$ while retaining $H$ on the frozen bonds. Denote this
specified update map by $\mathsf U_{A,F,V}$. Define the fine configuration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex-lattice-reader-fine-field}
 V_*(H,Z)=\mathsf U_{A,F,V}\!\left(H,a(H)+gZ\right),
 \qquad g=\beta^{-1/2}>0.
\end{equation}
The crucial point is that $H,Z$, the channel, and the coarse configuration
are shared by every reader. The different readers enter only through their
read sets and their local masks.

The chosen pose is a single geometric input at $o$: on its room the coarse
field agrees with one represented direction, after the fixed signed
transport, and the room is healthy and clear of the assigned flags. The
pose supplies the gauge frame used below. It is selected before the reader
index is chosen and is not allowed to vary from one read coordinate to the
next. For clarity, the quantitative receipts used by this handoff are these:
the channel covariance is positive semidefinite and transforms covariantly
under the fixed signed transport; for every admissible pair of incidence
coordinates, there are nonnegative sight and common-history errors
$\varepsilon_{\rm sight},\varepsilon_{\rm hist}$ with sum at most
$\varepsilon$; for each reader $a$, its measurable mask has values in
$[0,1]$ and integral loss at most $\varepsilon^2$; and, whenever that mask
is positive, the same pose room and local comparison provide one gauge
element for the whole reader patch with coordinate error at most
$\varepsilon$. The covariance split, Markov bound, and common-sample
notation below are proved here. Production of these paid sight, mask, and
field-comparison receipts is the separate input supplied by the selected
local construction; it is not inferred from positivity of the covariance.

When the surrounding construction averages over its finite list of tag
atoms, apply this construction to each atom with its own fixed tag and
covariance. Those outer weights are nonnegative and sum to one. The theorem
below is uniform over the allowed atoms; it does not identify distinct
tagged Gaussian measures with one another.

\subsection{Exact joint law of the transverse coordinates}

Let $\mathcal I$ be the fine incidence-coordinate set, and let
\[
 R:\mathbb R^I\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}
\]
be the row matrix that reads the transported fine curvature coordinates
and restricts them to these incidences. Write $Z^\sharp$ for the Gaussian
coordinate after the fixed signed transport, the channel's forest
reorganization, and the one pose gauge change. Define the centered reader
vector by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex-lattice-reader-transverse}
 \eta=RZ^\sharp.
\end{equation}
The exact transported channel covariance on the coordinates before the row
restriction is denoted $\Pi^\sharp$. The measure-preserving transport laws
give
\begin{equation}
 Z^\sharp\sim\gamma_{\Pi^\sharp},\qquad
 \eta\sim\gamma_C,\qquad
 C=R\Pi^\sharp R^{\mathsf T}.
 \label{eq:ex-lattice-reader-covariance}
\end{equation}
This includes every cross-entry: for all $u,v\in\mathcal I$,
\[
 \mathbb E_\mu[\eta_u\eta_v]
   =\sum_{r,r'\in I}R_{ur}\Pi^\sharp_{rr'}R_{vr'}=C_{uv}.
\]
The law statement in \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-covariance}, not just its covariance identity, follows because
the first transport pushes the product sample law to the transported
Gaussian channel and the remaining maps are real-linear. Thus any finite
subcollection of readers has the corresponding principal submatrix of
$C$ as its exact joint covariance. No independence among reader
coordinates is asserted. The covariance $C$ may be positive semidefinite
and singular; the Gaussian pushforward statement remains valid in that
case.

The local estimate compares this matrix with the short reference
covariance. Let $C^{\mathrm{short}}$ be that covariance on the same
incidence coordinates. For every pair $u,v$ in the support of the chosen
reader family, the paid sight estimate compares $C_{uv}$ with the tracked
finite-volume covariance $C^{\mathrm{track}}_{uv}$. The common-history
telescoping identity compares the latter with $C^{\mathrm{short}}_{uv}$.
Adding these two differences gives the exact bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex-lattice-reader-covariance-error}
 |C_{uv}-C^{\mathrm{short}}_{uv}|
 \le |C_{uv}-C^{\mathrm{track}}_{uv}|
       +|C^{\mathrm{track}}_{uv}-C^{\mathrm{short}}_{uv}|
 \le \varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The two terms are bounded by $\varepsilon_{\rm sight}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\rm hist}$, respectively, and their sum is at most
$\varepsilon$. The common-history identity and room-separation conditions
are required for both coordinates. In particular, \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-covariance-error} holds for every pair
of admissible read coordinates in the full family, not just for its
diagonal entries.

\subsection{All-reader field comparison on the same sample}

For a fine reader patch $a$, let $J_a$ be the chamber-coordinate set read
by that patch inside the prescribed room. A patch is admissible when its
reader support is contained in the coarse block tag. Its mask
$\Gamma_a:\Omega\to[0,1]$ depends on the same frozen field and channel as
\eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-space}. The construction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex-lattice-reader-guard}
 \int_\Omega(1-\Gamma_a)\,d\mu\le\varepsilon^2.
\end{equation}
By Markov's inequality,
\[
 \mu\{\Gamma_a=0\}\le\varepsilon^2,
 \qquad
 \mu\{\Gamma_a\le1-\delta\}\le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\delta}
 \quad(0<\delta\le1).
\]
The statement is per reader; it does not take a union over the reader
family. When $\Gamma_a(H,Z)>0$,
the local guard places the sample in the healthy room where its field
coordinates can be compared.

Write $\operatorname{Basis}_a(W)$ for the local chamber basis at patch $a$,
and define the complex gauged read by
\[
 \operatorname{Read}_{a,j}(W;q)
 :=\left[\operatorname{rigid}\!\left(
       \operatorname{coloring}\bigl(q,\operatorname{Basis}_a(W)\bigr)
      \right)\right]_j.
\]
Thus the read is a complex coordinate after applying the patch basis and one
gauge frame. Let $\mathsf B_{\rm parent}(U)$ denote the coarse chamber basis
constructed once from the fixed parent manner, coarse configuration $U$,
and reference tag. It is common to all fine patches; each set $J_a$ only
restricts its coordinates. Let $\mathsf N_{H_i}$ be the normal-read map in
the fixed reference chamber frame $H_i$. The center is the actual linear
forecast
\[
 m_{a,j}:=\operatorname{Re}\left[
   \mathsf N_{H_i}\bigl(\operatorname{rigid}(\mathsf B_{\rm parent}(U))\bigr)
 \right]_j,
 \qquad j\in J_a.
\]
It is the real part of the normal read in the reference frame, not an
untransformed coordinate of $U$. On every guarded sample there is one gauge
element $q_a(H,Z)$ for the whole patch such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex-lattice-reader-coordinate}
 \left|
  \operatorname{Read}_{a,j}\bigl(V_*(H,Z);q_a(H,Z)\bigr)
       -\bigl(m_{a,j}+\eta_j(H,Z)\bigr)
 \right|\le\varepsilon,
 \qquad j\in J_a.
\end{equation}
The frame may depend on the reader and on the sample, but within one
reader it is shared by all of that reader's coordinates. The same random
vector $\eta$ from \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-covariance} occurs for every patch, and the center $m_{a,j}$
is the actual coarse reader value. There is no separate sampling or
re-centering for each $j$.

The geometry behind \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-coordinate} is as follows. The fine read region is first
rewritten in the common coarse frame; the block room condition places its
whole stencil inside the pose room. The paid local guard then supplies the
healthy-region condition. Applying the field comparison on that room
produces the one gauge element $q_a(H,Z)$ and the coordinate estimate for
each $j$ in the patch. The reader's mask is what localizes this statement:
outside its support no coordinate comparison is claimed. This is exactly
the scope needed for bounded chamber-local insertions.

The law \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-space}, the covariance statements
\eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-covariance}--\eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-covariance-error},
the guard estimate \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-guard}, and the coordinate
comparison \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-reader-coordinate} are the joint reader model. They are the
finite-dimensional input used by the subsequent Gaussian density
comparison, with a fixed measurable masked insertion. That comparison is
proved in Section~\ref{sec:principal-gaussian-comparison}; it derives the
polynomial density-jet bound and never differentiates the insertion. The
Gaussian reassembly in Section~\ref{sec:gaussian-reassembly} separately
proves that the absolutely summable activity expansion restores the
original block integral. This auxiliary reader model alone does not assert
that identity. Finally, the response billing in
Section~\ref{sec:ex-lattice-trace-eve-producer} uses one uniform derivative
and tail envelope over all allowed leaves and reader directions. The
present section supplies the common-law and same-insertion identification
required to apply those comparison and reassembly results; their primitive
mean, covariance-jet, cutoff, and packet estimates remain those stated in
the cited sections.
