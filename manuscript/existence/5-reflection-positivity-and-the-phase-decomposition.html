5Reflection positivity and the phase decomposition

5.1Reflection positivity and the Euclidean Hilbert space

The reflected inner product follows the Osterwalder–Schrader construction [OS73, §3]. For the present separated correlation tables, the large-separation estimates used in proving contractivity come from the slab bounds.

Reflection positivity is the mechanism that turns Euclidean correlations into an inner product. A second ingredient is needed to make positive-time translation contractive: a bound on correlations as the two halves move apart. We give the complete argument, keeping this bound explicit.

A word \(w = ((x_1, v_1), \ldots, (x_n, v_n))\) records finitely many distinct insertion points and their labels. Write \(S (w)\) for its correlation and \(\varnothing\) for the empty word. Fix a coordinate axis and call its coordinate \(t\). Let \(\mathcal{W}_+\) consist of words whose points have strictly positive \(t\), including the empty word. Reflection \(\theta\) reverses the order of a word, reflects the points across \(t = 0\), and applies the reflected conjugate to each label. Assume \(\theta^2 = 1\). For \(v, w \in \mathcal{W}_+\), the concatenation \((\theta v) w\) has no coincident points: its two parts lie in opposite open half-spaces.

The inputs used in this section are normalization \(S (\varnothing) = 1\), translation invariance, the reflection relation \(S (\theta w) = \overline{S (w)}\), permutation symmetry on distinct points, and the following two conditions:

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} & \sum_{i, j = 1}^m \overline{c_i} c_j S ((\theta w_i) w_j) \geqslant 0 \quad (w_i \in \mathcal{W}_+, c_i \in \mathbb{C}), \hspace{3cm} \text{(5.1)} \\ & x \longmapsto S ((\theta v) (w + x)) \text{ is continuous and bounded on } \{x : t (x) \geqslant 0\} \quad (v, w \in \mathcal{W}_+) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(5.2)} \end{array}\)

The positivity in (5.1) means that the displayed complex number is real and nonnegative.

Lemma 5.1. (From smeared to point reflection positivity) Suppose the correlations are continuous at every configuration of distinct points. Assume reflection positivity for finite sums of smooth compactly supported tests on positive-time configurations, with arbitrary fixed label tuples for the separate summands. Then (5.1) holds.

Proof. Fix positive words \(w_1, \ldots, w_m\) and coefficients \(c_1, \ldots, c_m\). For each nonempty word choose a small configuration neighborhood in which its points stay distinct and strictly in the positive half-space. Place a nonnegative smooth bump of integral one in that neighborhood, centered at the word's positions, with support radius \(\epsilon\). Such bumps are obtained by translation and rescaling of \(\exp (- 1 / (1 - |x|^2))\) on the unit ball, zero outside, followed by division by its positive integral. The empty word has a zero-dimensional configuration space; use the constant test one there and counting measure of total mass one.

For each pair \(i, j\), the concatenation of a reflected configuration near \(w_i\) with a configuration near \(w_j\) has distinct points. Within either half this follows from the choice of neighborhood; across halves it follows from the strict time separation. Thus its correlation kernel is continuous near the pair of centers, even if some spatial coordinates agree. Write \(K_{ij}^{\epsilon}\) for its integral against the two bumps. Nonnegativity and unit integrals give

\(\displaystyle |K_{ij}^{\epsilon} - S ((\theta w_i) w_j) | \leqslant \sup | K_{ij} (x, y) - K_{ij} (x_i, y_j) | \longrightarrow 0,\)

where the supremum is over the shrinking product support. The kernels are bounded on these compact supports, so the integrals are well-defined. Smeared reflection positivity says \(\sum_{i, j} \overline{c_i} c_j K_{ij}^{\epsilon} \geqslant 0\). There are finitely many terms, and the nonnegative real half-line is closed in \(\mathbb{C}\). Passing to the limit proves (5.1).\(\Box\)

This argument needs continuity at all distinct-point configurations. Continuity only where every coordinate is pairwise different would not suffice: a word and its reflection share spatial coordinates, including in the diagonal entries of the reflection matrix.

Lemma 5.2. (A sufficient separated-correlation bound) Suppose the correlations are smooth at distinct points and, for each fixed finite list of labels, there are \(C \geqslant 0\) and \(A \geqslant 0\) such that

\(\displaystyle |S_{\boldsymbol{v}} (\boldsymbol{x}) | \leqslant C \rho^{- A} \quad \text{whenever } 0 < \rho \leqslant 1 \text{ and } |x_i - x_j |_{\infty} \geqslant \rho (i \ne j) .\)

Then (5.2) holds.

Proof. Fix \(v, w\). The distances between points within either word are unchanged by translation. For a reflected point from \(v\) and a point of \(w + x\), their time separation is the sum of their original positive times plus \(t (x)\). It is bounded below by a positive number independent of \(x\) when \(t (x) \geqslant 0\). There are only finitely many pairs. Choose \(\rho \leqslant 1\) smaller than all their positive lower bounds; if there are no pairs, take \(\rho = 1\). The assumed estimate gives the required uniform bound. The configuration depends continuously on \(x\) and always remains in the distinct-point set, so smoothness gives continuity.\(\Box\)

Proposition 5.3. (Hilbert space and vacuum) There is a Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}_E\) with vectors \([w]\), \(w \in \mathcal{W}_+\), whose linear span is dense and such that

\(\displaystyle \langle [v], [w] \rangle = S ((\theta v) w) .\)

The vector \(\Omega_E = [\varnothing]\) has norm one.

Proof. On the free complex vector space of finitely supported coefficients on \(\mathcal{W}_+\), define

\(\displaystyle B (c, d) = \sum_{v, w} \overline{c_v} d_w S ((\theta v) w) .\)

Reflection, \(\theta^2 = 1\), and permutation symmetry show \(B (d, c) = \overline{B (c, d)}\); positivity follows from (5.1). For completeness, Cauchy–Schwarz follows by applying positivity to \(c + \lambda d\): if \(B (d, d) > 0\), minimize the resulting quadratic expression in \(\lambda\); if \(B (d, d) = 0\), varying the magnitude and phase of \(\lambda\) forces \(B (c, d) = 0\). Thus \(|B (c, d) |^2 \leqslant B (c, c) B (d, d)\). Consequently the null vectors \(\mathcal{N}= \{c : B (c, c) = 0\}\) are orthogonal to every vector and form a subspace. The quotient by \(\mathcal{N}\) is an inner-product space. Complete it and map each word to the class of its basis vector. This gives the stated inner product and density. Finally \(\| \Omega_E \|^2 = S (\varnothing) = 1\).\(\Box\)

Let \(\tau_s w\) translate every point of \(w\) by \(s\) in the positive time direction, where \(s \geqslant 0\). Define for a finite formal sum \(c\) the vector \(T_s c = \sum_w c_w [\tau_s w]\). At this point \(T_s\) is defined on formal sums; descent to the null quotient will follow from its norm bound. Translation invariance gives

\(\displaystyle \langle T_s c, T_t d \rangle = \langle T_0 c, T_{s + t} d \rangle .\) (5.3)

Indeed, reflecting the translation by \(s\) shifts the first word by \(- s\); translating the whole concatenation by \(s\) gives the right side. Sesquilinearity extends the identity from words to finite sums.

Lemma 5.4. (The contraction estimate) For every finite sum \(c\) and every \(s \geqslant 0\), \(\|T_s c\| \leqslant \|T_0 c\|\).

Proof. For a single word, (5.3) gives \(\|[\tau_s w]\|^2 = S ((\theta w) (\tau_{2 s} w))\). Condition (5.2) bounds this expression uniformly in \(s\). The triangle inequality therefore bounds \(\|T_s c\|\) uniformly for every finite sum \(c\). Put \(M = \sup_{s \geqslant 0} \|T_s c\| < \infty\) and \(a = \|T_0 c\|\). By (5.3) and Cauchy–Schwarz,

\(\displaystyle \|T_s c\|^2 = | \langle T_0 c, T_{2 s} c \rangle | \leqslant aM.\)

Taking the supremum gives \(M^2 \leqslant aM\). Since \(M \geqslant 0\), either \(M = 0\), or division by \(M\) gives \(M \leqslant a\). Both cases prove the result.\(\Box\)

Theorem 5.5. (Euclidean translations) Positive time translations extend uniquely to a strongly continuous semigroup \(E_s\), \(s \geqslant 0\), of self-adjoint contractions on \(\mathcal{H}_E\). Spatial translations extend to a strongly continuous unitary representation \(U_y\), \(y \in \mathbb{R}^3\), commuting with \(E_s\). Both leave \(\Omega_E\) fixed.

Proof. The contraction estimate sends null vectors to null vectors. It therefore defines a contraction on the quotient, and then uniquely on its completion. On word vectors, \(E_0 = 1\) and \(E_s E_t = E_{s + t}\); continuity of the bounded operators extends these identities from their dense span to the whole Hilbert space. Equation (5.3) with one time equal to zero says that \(E_s\) is symmetric. A bounded symmetric operator defined everywhere is self-adjoint: its adjoint has the same matrix elements against all vectors, hence is the same operator.

For a spatial shift \(y\), reflection commutes with the shift. Translation invariance gives \(\langle [v + y], [w + y] \rangle = \langle [v], [w] \rangle\). Thus the shift descends to an isometry on the quotient and its completion. Its inverse is the shift by \(- y\), so it is unitary. The group law and commutation with \(E_s\) follow on words from commutation of ordinary translations, and extend by density. The empty word is unchanged by any translation, proving the vacuum assertions.

It remains to prove strong continuity. For a word \(w\), the square of \(\|E_s w - E_t w\|\), with word brackets understood, is the sum of the two diagonal inner products minus twice the real part of the cross term. By (5.3) these are values of the continuous function \(u \mapsto S ((\theta w) (\tau_u w))\) at \(2 s, 2 t, s + t\). Their combination tends to zero as \(s \to t\). The identical norm-square argument and (5.2) give continuity of spatial translations on word vectors. Finite linear combinations remain continuous. For an arbitrary \(\psi\), choose a finite word sum \(v\) with \(\| \psi - v\| < \varepsilon / 3\). For either family \(V_z\), its norm bound gives

\(\displaystyle \|V_z \psi - V_{z_0} \psi \| \leqslant 2\| \psi - v\|+ \|V_z v - V_{z_0} v\| .\)

The last term is smaller than \(\varepsilon / 3\) near \(z_0\), proving strong continuity.\(\Box\)

This theorem establishes Euclidean Hilbert-space dynamics from the displayed correlation hypotheses. Relativistic reconstruction also requires field operators, rotations, locality, boundary distributions and a selected phase with a unique invariant vacuum. These do not follow merely by naming the Hilbert space. The reconstruction in this monograph is given directly by the complex heat and joint boundary constructions, the word-field and word-motion propositions, and the covariance, locality and spectral arguments below; Theorem 5.19 supplies vacuum uniqueness. The corrected theorem of Osterwalder–Schrader [OS75, §IV.1] is an alternative literature route. We do not invoke it here: its linear growth and other hypotheses have not been matched to this construction.

5.2The algebra of time-invariant observables

The Hilbert space constructed from an unselected correlation table need not have a one-dimensional vacuum space. We first identify the whole subspace left fixed by time evolution. Compressed slab observables on this subspace will then distinguish its phases. Throughout this section the hypotheses of Theorem 5.5 remain in force. We write \(\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_E\) and \(\Omega = \Omega_E\). In this section \(e_0\) denotes the chosen time axis and \(x_0\) its coordinate; this local notation does not change the earlier coordinate ordering.

Proposition 5.6. (The large-time projection) The subspace

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{H}_0 = \{v \in \mathcal{H}: E_t v = v \text{ for every } t \geqslant 0\}\)

is closed. If \(P_0\) denotes its orthogonal projection, then \(E_t v \to P_0 v\) in norm as \(t \to \infty\), for every \(v \in \mathcal{H}\).

Proof. Put \(A = E_1\). This is a positive contraction since \(E_1 = E_{1 / 2}^2\). If \(Av = v\), contractivity gives \(\|v\| \leqslant \|E_s v\| \leqslant \|v\|\) for \(0 < s < 1\), using \(E_1 = E_{1 - s} E_s\). Since \(E_s\) is positive, \(I - E_s^2 \geqslant 0\) and equality of norms implies \(E_s^2 v = v\), hence \(E_s v = v\) by the positive square root. The semigroup law then shows \(\ker (I - A) =\mathcal{H}_0\), which is closed. By the bounded Borel functional calculus [RS80, Theorem VII.2], the powers \(A^n\) converge strongly to the spectral projection at \(1\), by dominated convergence on \([0, 1]\). This projection is \(P_0\). For \(t = n + s\) with integer \(n\) and \(0 \leqslant s < 1\), contractivity on \(A^n v - P_0 v\) gives the same convergence for \(E_t\).\(\Box\)

We next explain the analytic estimate needed to insert an entire word as a bounded operator. A word \(w\) lies in a slab of width \(d > 0\) if all its points are distinct and have time coordinates in \((0, d)\). For a positive word \(v\), put

\(\displaystyle I_w v = w \hspace{0.17em} \tau_d v, \qquad w^{\sharp} = \tau_d \theta w.\)

Both are positive words, and \(w^{\sharp}\) lies in the same slab. Let \(I_w\) also denote the linear map on finite formal word sums. Translation invariance and reflection give

\(\displaystyle \langle [I_w c], [b] \rangle = \langle [c], [I_{w^{\sharp}} b] \rangle .\) (5.4)

Indeed, translating the configuration \(\theta (w \tau_d v) \hspace{0.17em} u = (\tau_{- d} \theta v) (\theta w) u\) by \(d\) gives \((\theta v) (\tau_d \theta w) (\tau_d u)\), the configuration in the right-hand pairing. This proves the identity on words and then on finite sums.

Lemma 5.7. (From repeated insertion to a bounded operator) Fix a slab word \(w\) and \(D > 0\). Suppose that for every positive word \(v\) there is \(B_v \geqslant 0\) such that

\(\displaystyle | [(I_{w^{\sharp}} I_w)^n v] |^2 \leqslant B_v D^{4 n} \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots) .\) (5.5)

There is a unique bounded operator \(A_d (w)\) on \(\mathcal{H}\) with \(A_d (w) [v] = [w \tau_d v]\); it satisfies \(\|A_d (w)\| \leqslant D\).

Proof. Write \(T = I_{w^{\sharp}} I_w\) on formal sums and \(Xc = [c]\). The triangle inequality and (5.5) imply, for \(c = \sum_v c_v v\),

\(\displaystyle \|XT^n c\| \leqslant C_c D^{2 n}, \qquad C_c = \sum_v |c_v | \sqrt{B_v} < \infty .\)

Equation (5.4) shows that \(T\) is symmetric for the possibly degenerate form induced by \(X\). Repeatedly moving \(T\) across the inner product therefore gives \(\|XT^n c\|^2 = \langle Xc, XT^{2 n} c \rangle\). Put \(b_n = D^{- 2 n} \|XT^n c\|\) and \(M = \sup_{n \geqslant 0} b_n < \infty\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(b_n^2 \leqslant \|Xc\| b_{2 n} \leqslant \|Xc\| M\). Taking the supremum yields \(M^2 \leqslant \|Xc\| M\), hence \(M \leqslant \|Xc\|\). Finally,

\(\displaystyle \|XI_w c\|^2 = | \langle Xc, XTc \rangle | \leqslant \|Xc\| \|XTc\| \leqslant D^2 \|Xc\|^2 .\)

Thus insertion respects the null space and induces a bounded operator on the quotient. Its unique extension to the completion has the asserted norm and action. Uniqueness follows from density of the word span.\(\Box\)

The estimate in (5.5) concerns arbitrarily long repeated words; the constants \(B_v\) may depend on the exterior word. Bounds for each fixed correlation order alone do not supply this estimate. Its production from the lattice construction remains an input at this stage. Assume henceforth that it holds, with some \(D\), for every slab word. The resulting operators obey

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} A_d (\varnothing) & = E_d, \hspace{3cm} \text{(5.6)} \\ A_{d + e} (w \tau_d v) & = A_d (w) A_e (v),\\ A_d (w)^{\ast} & = A_d (w^{\sharp}),\\ A_{d + r} (w) & = A_d (w) E_r, \qquad A_{r + d} (\tau_r w) = E_r A_d (w) . \end{array}\)

Here each word lies in its indicated slab and \(r \geqslant 0\). The first, second and last two identities follow by applying both sides to a positive word and concatenating and translating its entries. The adjoint identity follows from (5.4). All extend from the dense word span by boundedness. Spatial translation also gives \(U_y A_d (w) = A_d (w + y) U_y\), by the same calculation on words.

5.2.1Spatial invariance of the ground space

Assume now full orthogonal covariance of the correlation table: there is an action on quotient field labels such that, for every \(K \in O (4)\) and every distinct-point word \(w\), the jointly rotated word \(Kw\) satisfies \(S (Kw) = S (w)\). Rotation includes this label action in what follows. To evaluate a rotated word, project each raw label to its field class, rotate that class, and choose a raw representative. Correlations are independent of that choice. No group action on the chosen raw representatives is assumed. This is stronger than covariance under signed coordinate permutations.

Lemma 5.8. Every \(v \in \mathcal{H}_0\) is fixed by all spatial translations.

Proof. For two distinct-point words \(p, q\), set \(F_{p, q} (x) = S (p (q + x))\) whenever the two clusters are disjoint. Translate \(\theta p\) and \(q\) separately far enough in positive time to obtain positive words \(v, w\). If the translation amounts are \(r, s\), then, whenever \(x_0 \geqslant r + s\), translation invariance gives

\(\displaystyle F_{p, q} (x) = \langle [v], U_{x_{\perp}} E_{x_0 - r - s} [w] \rangle, \qquad x_{\perp} = x - x_0 e_0 .\) (5.7)

The time separation also ensures that the two clusters are disjoint. For any fixed \(h \in \mathbb{R}\) and any path \(x (t)\) with \(x_0 (t) \to \infty\), it follows that

\(\displaystyle F_{p, q} (x (t) + he_0) - F_{p, q} (x (t)) \longrightarrow 0.\) (5.8)

Indeed, the difference of the two heat-evolved vectors in (5.7) tends to zero by Proposition 5.6; the spatial unitary has norm one, even when \(x_{\perp} (t)\) varies without bound.

If \(b_0 > 0\), choose an orthogonal map \(K\) taking \(b\) to \(|b| e_0\). Orthogonality implies \((Ke_0)_0 = b_0 / |b| > 0\). Rotate both clusters and apply (5.8) along the path \(tKe_0\), whose time coordinate tends to infinity. Covariance gives

\(\displaystyle F_{p, q} (te_0 + b) - F_{p, q} (te_0) \longrightarrow 0.\)

For a spatial \(y\), take \(b = y + e_0\) and subtract (5.8) along \(x (t) = te_0 + y\), with \(h = 1\). We obtain \(F_{p, q} (te_0 + y) - F_{p, q} (te_0) \to 0\). Apply this with \(p = \theta v, q = w\) for positive words. The two correlations are \(\langle [v], U_y E_t [w] \rangle\) and \(\langle [v], E_t [w] \rangle\). Their limits show \(\langle [v], (U_y - I) P_0 [w] \rangle = 0\). Density of word vectors first gives \((U_y - I) P_0 [w] = 0\) and then \((U_y - I) P_0 = 0\). Since \(P_0\) is the identity on \(\mathcal{H}_0\), the conclusion follows.\(\Box\)

5.2.2Compression and separation of clusters

For a slab word define \(M (w) = P_0 A_d (w) |_{\mathcal{H}_0}\). The width is immaterial: increasing it appends an \(E_r\), which is the identity on \(\mathcal{H}_0\); two widths can be compared with their sum. Likewise a positive time shift inserts \(E_r\) on the left, and \(P_0 E_r = P_0\). Spatial shifts give the same operator by Lemma 5.8 and spatial covariance. For a negative time shift, reverse the comparison. Every finite distinct-point word can be translated into a slab. Thus \(M (w)\) is well-defined for every such word, independent of both the placement and width, and

\(\displaystyle M (w + b) = M (w), \qquad \langle \Omega, M (w) \Omega \rangle = S (w) .\) (5.9)

The latter follows from \(A_d (w) \Omega = [w]\) after a slab placement, \(P_0 \Omega = \Omega\), and translation invariance.

For a positive word also put \(X (w) = A_d (w) |_{\mathcal{H}_0}\), as a map into \(\mathcal{H}\). Width independence follows as above, and

\(\displaystyle X (w) \Omega = [w], \qquad M (w) \Omega = P_0 [w], \qquad M ((\theta v) w) = X (v)^{\ast} X (w) .\)

For the last identity, place the reflected word into the slab of width \(d\), append the word \(w\) in a subsequent slab of width \(e\), and use the concatenation and adjoint identities in (5.6). Compression then gives exactly the displayed product. In particular \(M (\theta v) = M (v)^{\ast}\). This also shows that the generators are closed under adjoints, after translation into slabs.

Lemma 5.9. (Separated-cluster limit) Let \(w_1, \ldots, w_m\) be words with distinct points. Suppose \(b_1 (n), \ldots, b_m (n) \in \mathbb{R}^4\) satisfy \((b_{j + 1} (n) - b_j (n))_0 \to \infty\) for \(j < m\). Then

\(\displaystyle S ((w_1 + b_1 (n)) \cdots (w_m + b_m (n))) \longrightarrow \langle \Omega, M (w_1) \cdots M (w_m) \Omega \rangle .\)

The concatenated words have distinct points for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Proof. Fixed translations of individual words, compensated in their \(b_j\), preserve the assertion and the gap condition. Make all words positive. We prove a stronger vector limit by induction from the last cluster: after translating the concatenation so that its first cluster has its original position, its vector tends to \(X (w_1) M (w_2) \cdots M (w_m) \Omega\). For one cluster this is \(X (w_1) \Omega = [w_1]\).

Choose a slab width \(d\) for \(w_1\). Write the displacement of the second cluster relative to the first as \(g_n e_0 + y_n\), with \(y_n\) spatial. Eventually \(g_n > d\). If \(z_n\) is the positive word vector of the remaining clusters relative to the second, the whole vector is \(A_d (w_1) U_{y_n} E_{g_n - d} z_n\). By induction \(z_n \to z = X (w_2) M (w_3) \cdots M (w_m) \Omega\). The estimates

\(\displaystyle \|U_{y_n} E_{g_n - d} z_n - P_0 z\| \leqslant \|z_n - z\| + \|E_{g_n - d} z - P_0 z\| \longrightarrow 0\)

use contraction, unitarity and \(U_{y_n} P_0 z = P_0 z\). Boundedness of \(A_d (w_1)\) proves the vector limit. The same induction shows positivity and distinctness eventually, since the successive time gaps tend to infinity. Pairing the vector limit with \(\Omega\), and using translation invariance and \(P_0 \Omega = \Omega\), proves the scalar assertion. The empty list has correlation one and needs no induction.\(\Box\)

Proposition 5.10. (Commutativity) The norm-closed unital star algebra \(\mathcal{A} \subset B (\mathcal{H}_0)\) generated by the \(M (w)\) is commutative, and \(\Omega\) is cyclic and separating for \(\mathcal{A}\).

Proof. Write \(F (w_1, \ldots, w_m) = \langle \Omega, \prod_j M (w_j) \Omega \rangle\), in the indicated order. If \(b_0 > 0\) and \((Kb)_0 > 0\), place the clusters at \(jnb\), rotate the configuration by \(K\), and use Lemma 5.9 before and after rotation. Covariance gives \(F (w_1, \ldots, w_m) = F (Kw_1, \ldots, Kw_m)\).

To exchange the middle two of four words, use one spatial axis \(e_1\) and displace the four clusters by

\(\displaystyle 0, \quad n (e_0 + 2 e_1), \quad n (2 e_0 + e_1), \quad 3 n (e_0 + e_1) .\)

They are ordered by time as written. Interchange \(e_0, e_1\) by an orthogonal map \(K\). The resulting time order is first, third, second, fourth. Permutation symmetry of distinct-point correlations and the cluster limit give \(F (x, y, w, z) = F (Kx, Kw, Ky, Kz)\). Use the preceding rotation identity with \(b = e_0 + e_1\), fixed by \(K\), to conclude \(F (x, y, w, z) = F (x, w, y, z)\).

The vectors \(M (v) \Omega = P_0 [v]\), for positive words \(v\), have dense linear span in \(\mathcal{H}_0\): approximate any vector in \(\mathcal{H}_0\) by word sums in \(\mathcal{H}\) and apply \(P_0\). For two generators, their commutator has zero matrix elements between these dense vectors, since

\(\displaystyle \langle M (u) \Omega, [M (w), M (z)] M (v) \Omega \rangle = F (\theta u, w, z, v) - F (\theta u, z, w, v) = 0.\)

The commutator is bounded, hence is zero. The generators and their adjoints therefore commute; polynomial approximation and continuity of multiplication show that their norm closure is commutative. The same density proves cyclicity. If \(A \Omega = 0\), then \(AB \Omega = BA \Omega = 0\) for every \(B \in \mathcal{A}\). Cyclicity and boundedness give \(A = 0\), which is separation.\(\Box\)

This proves commutativity from the displayed slab and covariance inputs. The next construction must establish norm separability of \(\mathcal{A}\), represent the vacuum state on its compact character space, and show that each resulting whole-word correlation table retains the required regularity, null spaces and field identities. Those conclusions are not part of Proposition 5.10.

5.3Uniform bounds for arbitrarily many slab copies

The centered estimates give more than bounds for a fixed number of fields. They also control arbitrarily long strings of reflected copies with one constant per insertion. This distinction is needed to construct bounded operators on the reflection-positive Hilbert space. We prove that implication on the same selected family and the same order of limits.

Write \(a_k\) for its decreasing mesh scales, with \(a_0 = 1\) and \(a_k \to 0\). We use the scale inequality

\(\displaystyle a_{k + 1} \geqslant c_{\ast} a_k^2 \qquad (c_{\ast} > 0),\) (5.10)

supplied by the selected mesh. A word is a finite ordered list of distinct points with raw field labels; its length is denoted by \(n\). Reflection reverses the word, reflects the points in the time-zero plane, and applies the corresponding reflected, conjugated labels. Fix a grade bound \(D\) and a bound \(R\) for the norms of the raw labels. All constants below may depend on \(D, R\) and the selected family. They will not depend on \(n\), the slab width, its separation buffer, or the number of copies.

The two inputs from the selected construction are the centered service of Subsection 13.10, with its uniformity over bounded label sets, and the smooth point limits of that very family. We retain its normalization, permutation and translation symmetries, and reflection positivity, together with the positive-word Hilbert space and contractive heat semigroup constructed earlier. We use the expanding order: first the fine mesh limit at a suitable fixed period, then the cofinal period limit. The centered estimates and point limits must use this same order. No bound at every fixed period is needed for the argument below.

5.3.1One bound for the entire reflected family

For every \(D, R\) there are \(F \in \mathbb{N}\), \(J > 0\), and \(P = D + 1\) such that, for \(k \geqslant F\), every label of grade at most \(D\) and norm at most \(R\) has centered root bound at most

\(\displaystyle Ja_k^{- P} .\) (5.11)

Here the bound includes every signed-coordinate transform and adjoint of the label, as well as the chamber interiority and stencil fit needed to apply the planted inequality.

To see the uniformity, first bound all those transformed labels by a single \(Q > 0\). There are only finitely many grades and signed-coordinate transformations. On each finite-dimensional grade space each transformation has finite operator norm, and coefficientwise conjugation preserves the raw norm. The adjoint of a label is the reflected conjugate \(R_{\rho} \bar{v}\). By the commutation of conjugation with signed-coordinate transformations in Subsection 5.7, \(R_{\rho} \overline{R_s v} = R_{\rho s} \bar{v}\). This label belongs to the same finite family and is covered by the same bound. We do not assume that the signed-coordinate action is isometric. Apply the centered service in each grade \(d \leqslant D\), with norm bound \(Q\) and exponent slack \(1\). In the expanding formulation an arbitrarily small additive error is allowed; taking that error to be \(1\) gives \(A_d a_k^{- (d + 1)} + 1\). Since \(a_k \leqslant 1\), this is at most \((A_d + 1) a_k^{- (d + 1)}\). Take the maximum of the finitely many grade thresholds and constants and use \(d + 1 \leqslant P\). Fixed stencils fit eventually at every such reference scale. For a fixed word only finitely many labels and orientations occur, so their eventual mesh and period requirements have a common tail. The bound \(J\) itself was selected before that word.

5.3.2Two scales for the main word and the exterior

We first record how to combine different centered bounds. At finite cutoff let \(T\) be a link-cell tiling, and let \(Y\) be an aligned subdivision of it. Suppose main insertions lie in distinct \(T\)-cells, and no exterior insertion lies in those cells. The exterior insertions may share a \(T\)-cell, but lie in distinct \(Y\)-cells. Suppose all required margins and stencil fits hold. If the centered bound of each main insertion is \(A\), and that of each exterior insertion is \(X \geqslant 0\), then

\(\displaystyle \left| \mathbb{E} \left[ \prod_{i \in I} f_i \prod_{j \in E} g_j \right] \right| \leqslant A^{|I|} X^{|E|} .\) (5.12)

These are moments of the original observables; no absolute values have been inserted inside the expectation.

Here is the extra point when several exterior fields share a coarse cell. Move that cell to the home cell by its signed reflection, transforming all its labels accordingly, and let \(G\) be the product of its \(h\) exterior factors. Its coarse root is obtained by copying the entire product to all \(M_T\) coarse cells. The resulting \(M_T h\) factors lie in distinct fine cells: equality of their fine cells first forces equality of their coarse cells, by alignment, and then equality of the original factors, by their fine-scale separation. Reflections preserve the fine margins and separation. The planted inequality in \(Y\), together with the one-field root bounds, therefore gives

\(\displaystyle |\mathbb{E}[T \text{-full-copy} (G)] | \leqslant X^{M_T h} .\)

Taking the \(M_T\)th root bounds the coarse root of \(G\) by \(X^h\). The empty product is covered by the same argument. The chamber-interior property is preserved under finite products. For the main insertions the one-field coarse root bound is the bracketing and copied-orbit argument of Subsection 4.1. Apply the coarse planted inequality to these main insertions and the grouped exterior products. Their coarse cells are distinct. Multiplying their bounds proves (5.12).

We also need to place all those cells without paying for the number of main replicas. Suppose the main points have only \(N_0\) residue patterns modulo \(a_m \mathbb{Z}^4\), and choose levels \(m \leqslant k \leqslant l \leqslant b\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle N_0 (10 a_k) < a_m, \qquad |E| (6 a_b) < a_l .\) (5.13)

Assume main–main and main–exterior sup distances exceed \(a_m\), and exterior–exterior distances exceed \(a_l\). In each coordinate, the \(N_0\) patterns forbid at most \(N_0 (10 a_k)\) of the possible coarse offsets when a margin of \(5 a_k\) is required. Thus one can first place all main patterns in such margins. At the fine lattice level the identical argument is a count of forbidden residues; scale divisibility gives the same strict inequalities.

Choose the exterior offset within one aligned cell of side \(a_l\). The second inequality in (5.13) lets us give every exterior point a margin of \(3 a_b\). This changes the main offset by at most \(a_l \leqslant a_k\). Allow also independent perturbations of the main points by at most \(a_k\) in every coordinate. Their original margin \(5 a_k\) then leaves a margin of \(3 a_k\). This is why the first placement uses \(5\), rather than \(3\), fine spans. The same estimate remains valid after rounding to the fine mesh: if two coordinates differ by at most \(Ka_N\) with \(K\) a nonnegative integer, their ceilings in units \(a_N\) differ by at most \(K\) lattice steps. Congruent unperturbed main patterns have identical coarse residues. Thus the number of forbidden patterns stays \(N_0\), even though every occurrence may be perturbed independently.

Apply (5.12) at that cutoff. To pass to continuum points, first take a nearby generic configuration where the prescribed point-limit theorem applies and where all the strict separation inequalities remain valid. At sufficiently large period there is no wrapping collision; at sufficiently fine mesh the rounded configuration has the required separations and margins. The centered bounds hold on the same mesh and period tails. Pass first to the mesh limit and then to the period limit. Finally approach the original configuration through generic configurations. Their density and smoothness of the limiting correlations give (5.12) there too. This argument uses independent perturbations of all slots, and so does not assume point convergence on a projection tie or on the lower-dimensional set of exactly congruent configurations.

5.3.3Paying for the main word before choosing its exterior

Let \(w \ne \varnothing\) lie in a time slab \((0, d)\) and have buffer \(r\), where \(0 < r \leqslant 1\): distinct points have sup distance at least \(r\), and every time coordinate lies in \([r, d - r]\). Choose \(m \geqslant F\) minimal with \(a_m < r / 32\), and put

\(\displaystyle s = a_m \lfloor d / a_m \rfloor .\)

Then \(s \leqslant d < s + a_m\). The word remains in the slab \((0, s)\) with buffer \(r / 2\), and \(s > 0\) because the nonempty word has both boundary margins. Crucially, \(s\) has been chosen before any exterior word.

For later use, if \(j \geqslant F\) is the first level with \(a_j < \varepsilon\), where \(0 < \varepsilon \leqslant 1\), then

\(\displaystyle t \varepsilon^2 \leqslant a_j < \varepsilon, \qquad t = \min (a_F, c_{\ast}) > 0.\) (5.14)

For \(j = F\) the lower bound follows from \(\varepsilon \leqslant 1\); otherwise \(a_{j - 1} \geqslant \varepsilon\) and (5.10) proves it. Set \(b_0 = t / 32^2\) and \(u = \min (b_0 / 21, 1)\). For \(n = |w| \geqslant 1\), choose \(k \geqslant F\) minimal with \(a_k < \delta = u (r / n)^2\). Then

\(\displaystyle 20 na_k < 20 n \delta = 20 ur^2 / n \leqslant 20 ur^2 < b_0 r^2 \leqslant a_m .\)

In particular \(k \geqslant m\). Applying (5.14) again gives

\(\displaystyle a_k \geqslant c (r / n)^4, \qquad c = tu^2 > 0.\) (5.15)

All main fields and their reflected versions can therefore be paid at this one level with bound \(A = Ja_k^{- P}\), and

\(\displaystyle A^n \leqslant C_0^n (n / r)^{4 Pn}, \qquad C_0 = Jc^{- P} > 0.\) (5.16)

To apply this to repeated insertion, write \(w^{\sharp} = \tau_s \theta w\) and \(I_w v = w \hspace{0.17em} \tau_s v\). The word \((I_{w^{\sharp}} I_w)^q v\) consists of \(2 q\) alternating copies of the main word followed by one translated exterior \(v\). Its reflection pairing contains \(4 qn\) main fields and \(2 |v|\) exterior fields. Every main field is congruent modulo \(a_m \mathbb{Z}^4\) to one of the \(2 n\) points of \(w\) and \(\theta w\), since \(s\) is a multiple of \(a_m\). Translation and reflection preserve separation. Consecutive slabs retain their buffer \(r / 2\), so the main points are mutually separated and separated from the exterior by at least \(r / 2 > a_m\). These statements also follow directly by induction on \(q\): attaching one reflected slab and one original slab preserves the boundary margins, adds \(2 n\) points, and translates the remaining word by \(2 s\). The final reflected pairing doubles the main count and creates exactly two exterior copies, independently of \(q\).

Fix now an arbitrary positive exterior word \(v\). Its finitely many points have some positive mutual and boundary buffer \(t_v\) and some finite grade and norm bounds. Choose \(l \geqslant k\) with \(a_l < t_v\), then \(b \geqslant l\) beyond its one-field threshold and so fine that \(2 |v| (6 a_b) < a_l\). Let \(X\) be its one-field bound at level \(b\). Neither \(l, b\) nor \(X\) depends on \(q\). The mixed estimate, with \(N_0 = 2 n\), yields

\(\displaystyle | [(I_{w^{\sharp}} I_w)^q v] |^2 \leqslant X^{2 |v|} A^{4 qn} \leqslant X^{2 |v|} (C_0^n (n / r)^{4 Pn})^{4 q} \qquad (q \geqslant 0) .\) (5.17)

For the empty exterior its cost is \(1\) and the same proof applies. The exterior constant may depend on \(v, w, r\); its independence of \(q\) is the essential conclusion.

5.3.4The actual bounded slab operators

The repeated-insertion argument of Lemma 5.7 applies to (5.17), with \(B_v = X^{2 |v|}\) and with the norm constant of that lemma equal to \(C_0^n (n / r)^{4 Pn}\). (That constant is called \(D\) there; it is unrelated to the grade bound \(D\) of this section.) It gives the operator at width \(s\) with norm at most \(C_0^n (n / r)^{4 Pn}\). To recover the original width \(d\), first apply the heat contraction \(E_{d - s}\) and then the width-\(s\) insertion. On a positive word this produces \(w \hspace{0.17em} \tau_s \tau_{d - s} v = w \hspace{0.17em} \tau_d v\), exactly the required insertion. The norm cannot increase. Thus there is a common family of slab operators satisfying

\(\displaystyle \|A_d (w)\| \leqslant C_0^{|w|} (|w| / r)^{K |w|}, \qquad K = 4 (D + 1),\) (5.18)

for every nonempty word with the stated grade, norm and buffer bounds. For the empty word take \(A_d (\varnothing) = E_d\) with norm at most \(1\); the right side is then interpreted as \(1\).

The choices used to find a bounded operator may depend on an affordable buffer or label bound for a given word. They nevertheless produce a single operator: two such choices agree on all planted positive words, whose linear span is dense, and are bounded. Hence they agree everywhere. The common operator inherits every bound obtained with any admissible \(D, R, r\). This proves the uniform all-copy estimate required by the phase construction, on the same hierarchy as the centered service.

5.4Regularity in operator norm

To pass from scalar correlation identities to the character construction, we need regularity in operator norm. Weak continuity of matrix elements alone would not provide it. The additional ingredient is a local uniform bound on the operators representing the differentiated insertions.

Lemma 5.11. (Operator remainder estimate) Let \(U\) be an open subset of a finite-dimensional real normed space \(E\), let \(H\) be a complex Hilbert space, and let \(\mathcal{D}\) be a dense linear subspace. Suppose \(A : U \to B (H)\) and \(B : U \to \mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{R}} (E, B (H))\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle D \langle u, A (\cdot) v \rangle (z) [h] = \langle u, B (z) [h] v \rangle \quad (u, v \in \mathcal{D}, z \in U, h \in E) .\)

If the segment from \(x\) to \(y\) lies in \(U\) and \(\|B (z) - B_0 \| \leqslant C\) along the segment, with \(C \geqslant 0\), then

\(\displaystyle \|A (y) - A (x) - B_0 [y - x]\| \leqslant C \|y - x\| .\) (5.19)

Consequently, local boundedness of \(B\) implies local Lipschitz continuity of \(A\); continuity of \(B\) implies \(DA = B\) in operator norm.

Proof. Fix \(u, v \in \mathcal{D}\). Along the segment, subtract the linear function \(z \mapsto \langle u, B_0 [z] v \rangle\). The derivative of the resulting complex-valued function has norm at most \(C \|u\| \|v\|\). The scalar mean-value inequality yields

\(\displaystyle | \langle u, (A (y) - A (x) - B_0 [y - x]) v \rangle | \leqslant C \|u\| \|v\| \|y - x\| .\)

This inequality also follows directly by composing with the segment and then with every real-linear functional of norm one on \(\mathbb{C}\), using the real mean-value theorem, and taking the supremum. Density extends the inequality to all \(u, v \in H\). For any bounded operator \(T\), the supremum of \(| \langle u, Tv \rangle |\) over unit vectors \(u, v\) is \(\|T\|\): Cauchy–Schwarz gives one inequality and, when \(Tv \ne 0\), the choice \(u = Tv / \|Tv\|\) gives the other. This proves (5.19).

On a sufficiently small ball where \(\|B\| \leqslant C\), use \(B_0 = 0\) to obtain the Lipschitz bound. If \(B\) is continuous at \(x\), then for any \(\epsilon > 0\) choose such a ball with \(\|B (z) - B (x)\| \leqslant \epsilon\). Taking \(B_0 = B (x)\) proves that the remainder is at most \(\epsilon \|y - x\|\), which is precisely Fréchet differentiability with derivative \(B (x)\).\(\Box\)

We apply the lemma to the slab operators of Subsection 5.2. For fixed width \(d > 0\), arity \(n\), and labels \(\boldsymbol{v}= (v_1, \ldots, v_n)\), write \(A_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x) = A_d ((x_1, v_1) \cdots (x_n, v_n))\) on

\(\displaystyle U_{d, n} = \{x : x_i \ne x_j (i \ne j), \quad 0 < (x_i)_0 < d\} .\)

This is open, since finitely many strict inequalities describe the time conditions and finitely many diagonals have been excluded.

The two quantitative and differential inputs are as follows. First, for each grade bound \(D\) and label norm bound \(R\), there are \(C > 0\) and \(K \in \mathbb{N}\) such that every slab word of length \(n\), with these label bounds, satisfies

\(\displaystyle \|A_d (w)\| \leqslant C^n (n / r)^{Kn}\) (5.20)

whenever \(0 < r \leqslant 1\), its points have mutual sup-norm distances at least \(r\), and each time coordinate lies in \([r, d - r]\). The constants are independent of \(n, d\) and the positions. For the empty word the right side is interpreted as one, in agreement with \(A_d (\varnothing) = E_d\). Second, for each raw label \(v\) of grade \(d'\) and each coordinate \(j\), choose a raw label \(\delta_j v\) of grade \(d' + 1\) representing its field derivative. Assume that the smooth scalar correlations obey the exact slot identity

\(\displaystyle \partial_{(x_i)_j} S_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x) = S_{\boldsymbol{v} [i \leftarrow \delta_j v_i]} (x)\) (5.21)

at distinct points, also with any finite list of exterior insertions. The choice of raw representative need not itself be linear. For a phase using the original field quotient, the identity includes the assertion that the chosen representatives have the indicated values in that phase.

Proposition 5.12. (Smooth slab operators) Under (5.20) and (5.21), \(A_{\boldsymbol{v}} : U_{d, n} \to B (\mathcal{H})\) is smooth in operator norm, and

\(\displaystyle \partial_{(x_i)_j} A_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x) = A_{\boldsymbol{v} [i \leftarrow \delta_j v_i]} (x) .\)

Proof. Fix a point \(x \in U_{d, n}\). Choose \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) below its finitely many positive time margins and pairwise distances. In a neighborhood of \(x\), the same margins and distances are at least \(r / 2\). For every fixed label tuple, (5.20) therefore gives a local operator bound. The same holds for every one-slot differentiated tuple, since there are finitely many labels and their grades and norms have finite maxima.

Define the real-linear derivative candidate

\(\displaystyle B_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x) [h] = \sum_{i = 1}^n \sum_{j = 0}^3 h_{i, j} A_{\boldsymbol{v} [i \leftarrow \delta_j v_i]} (x) .\)

The finite collection of local bounds gives a local bound on \(\|B_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x)\|\). For positive words \(p, q\),

\(\displaystyle \langle [p], A_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x) [q] \rangle = S ((\theta p) \hspace{0.17em} w (x, \boldsymbol{v}) \hspace{0.17em} \tau_d q) .\)

The exterior points lie respectively below zero and above \(d\), so all configurations remain distinct near \(x\). Differentiating by (5.21) gives exactly the matrix elements of \(B_{\boldsymbol{v}}\). Sesquilinearity extends this statement to finite word sums, a dense linear subspace. The remainder lemma now proves norm continuity of \(A_{\boldsymbol{v}}\), for every tuple.

In particular each differentiated tuple has norm-continuous operators. Thus the finite sum defining \(B_{\boldsymbol{v}}\) is norm-continuous. A second application of the remainder lemma proves \(DA_{\boldsymbol{v}} = B_{\boldsymbol{v}}\). Inductively, if all the tuple maps are \(C^k\), then each \(B_{\boldsymbol{v}}\) is \(C^k\) as a finite sum of fixed coordinate functionals times such maps. The derivative formula makes all tuple maps \(C^{k + 1}\). Starting from continuity proves smoothness of every finite order, with the stated coordinate derivatives.\(\Box\)

Corollary 5.13. (Norm separability of the compression algebra) If the scalar correlations are multilinear in the finite-dimensional raw label spaces, the hypotheses of Proposition 5.12 imply that \(\mathcal{A}\) is separable in operator norm.

Proof. Fix a finite basis in every raw label space. At fixed positions, the slab operator is multilinear in the labels: pairing the purported linear identity with any two word vectors reduces it to scalar multilinearity; density then proves equality of bounded operators. Every compressed word operator is therefore a finite linear combination of ones with basis labels.

Place a word in positive time and choose a positive rational width greater than all its time coordinates. Approximate its distinct positions by rational positions inside this slab. Operator-norm continuity from Proposition 5.12, followed by the bounded compression \(A \mapsto P_0 A|_{\mathcal{H}_0}\), proves norm convergence of the compressed operators. There are countably many rational widths, rational configurations, arities and finite basis-label tuples. Their compressed operators thus generate a norm-dense subalgebra. Star polynomials in this countable set with coefficients in \(\mathbb{Q}+ i\mathbb{Q}\) form a countable set dense in the whole norm-closed algebra: approximate the finitely many generators and coefficients in any given polynomial, and then use the definition of the closure. This is the required norm separability.\(\Box\)

The first analytic input, the estimate uniform in the length of the word, is stronger than the separated-correlation bound used to construct time translations. The second input is an exact differential identity. The argument above supplies operator smoothness and norm separability from these two inputs; it leaves their derivation from the lattice fields to the construction chapters.

5.5Characters and selection of a vacuum

The preceding section constructs a commutative algebra on the whole ground space. Its characters provide a useful way of separating the vacuum state into components. The argument below also explains why a one-dimensional ground space is first obtained almost everywhere, rather than assumed for the unselected table. After relativistic reconstruction, a uniform low-energy estimate extends vacuum uniqueness to every character.

Proposition 5.14. (The representing probability) Suppose the algebra \(\mathcal{A}\) of Proposition 5.10 is separable in operator norm. Its space \(\Xi\) of unital complex star characters, with the topology of pointwise convergence on \(\mathcal{A}\), is compact and metrizable. There is a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\Xi\), positive on every nonempty open set, such that

\(\displaystyle \int_{\Xi} \chi (A) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (\chi) = \langle \Omega, A \Omega \rangle \quad (A \in \mathcal{A}) .\) (5.22)

Proof. The algebra is a nonzero unital commutative \(C^{\ast}\)-algebra: it is a norm-closed star subalgebra of \(B (\mathcal{H}_0)\), and \(\| \Omega \| = 1\). The commutative Gelfand theorem identifies it isometrically, as a star algebra, with \(C (\Xi)\), by \(A \mapsto \hat{A}\), where \(\hat{A} (\chi) = \chi (A)\) [Wil24, Theorem 2.13]. Its character space is compact since the algebra is unital.

Choose a countable norm-dense sequence \((A_j)\) in \(\mathcal{A}\). The continuous map \(\chi \mapsto (\chi (A_j))_j\) into \(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}\) is injective: equality on the dense sequence implies equality everywhere by continuity of the characters. A continuous injection of a compact space into a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its image (images of closed sets are compact and hence closed). The countable product is metrizable, for example by \(\sum_j 2^{- j - 1} \min (1, |z_j - z'_j |)\), so \(\Xi\) is metrizable.

Transfer the functional \(A \mapsto \langle \Omega, A \Omega \rangle\) to \(C (\Xi)\). It is positive: a nonnegative continuous function is \(\bar{g} g\) for its continuous nonnegative square root \(g\), and its inverse Gelfand transform has the form \(B^{\ast} B\). The functional takes the value \(\|B \Omega \|^2 \geqslant 0\) there. The Riesz representation theorem supplies a positive Borel measure representing it [Saw, Chapter 6, Theorem 32]. Compactness gives \(C_c (\Xi) = C (\Xi)\), so that theorem applies directly. At the constant function one it gives \(\mu (\Xi) = \| \Omega \|^2 = 1\).

To prove full support, suppose a nonempty open set \(O\) has zero measure. Choose \(x \in O\) and \(r > 0\) with \(B (x, r) \subset O\). The function \(f (y) = \max (0, r - \text{dist} (x, y))\) is continuous, nonzero at \(x\), and zero outside \(O\). Let \(A\) be its inverse Gelfand transform. Then (5.22) applied to \(A^{\ast} A\) gives \(\|A \Omega \|^2 = \int |f|^2 \hspace{0.17em} d \mu = 0\). Separation of \(\Omega\) forces \(A = 0\), contradicting \(f (x) = r\). Thus every such open set has positive measure.\(\Box\)

For \(\chi \in \Xi\), define the whole-word values

\(\displaystyle S_{\chi} (w) = \chi (M (w)) \quad \text{for distinct-point words } w.\) (5.23)

It is essential to apply the character to the operator for the entire word. The compressed operator \(M (w_1 w_2)\) need not equal \(M (w_1) M (w_2)\) at finite separation. Equations (5.22) and (5.9) give the barycenter identity

\(\displaystyle S (w) = \int_{\Xi} S_{\chi} (w) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (\chi) .\) (5.24)

For a fixed word the integrand is continuous, and hence integrable.

Some properties of \(S_{\chi}\) already follow at this level. Normalization and translation invariance follow from \(M (\varnothing) = I\) and (5.9). It is also reflection positive. For positive words \(v_i\) and coefficients \(c_i\), the Gram identity in the preceding section gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i, j} \overline{c_i} c_j M ((\theta v_i) v_j) = \left( \sum_i c_i X (v_i) \right)^{\ast} \left( \sum_j c_j X (v_j) \right) \geqslant 0 \quad \text{in } B (\mathcal{H}_0) .\)

The operator on the left lies in \(\mathcal{A}\). Positivity is the same in a unital \(C^{\ast}\)-subalgebra as in the ambient operator algebra (spectral permanence and continuous functional calculus give its nonnegative square root in the subalgebra; see [Wil24, Theorems 2.17 and 2.19]). Characters take positive elements to nonnegative numbers. Applying \(\chi\) proves the desired Gram positivity. Regularity and the remaining covariance and field identities still need their own operator estimates; the barycenter formula alone does not establish them.

Lemma 5.15. (A common countable collection of words) Suppose the raw label spaces are finite-dimensional complex vector spaces indexed by the nonnegative integers. Suppose that each \(S_{\chi}\) is multilinear in its labels and continuous on the distinct-point configuration space, and has the Hilbert realization of Proposition 5.3. There is one countable collection of positive words whose vectors span a dense subspace in every \(\mathcal{H}_{\chi}\).

Proof. Choose a finite basis in every raw label space. Take all positive words with rational coordinates and with basis-vector labels, for every arity and every finite list of label grades; include the empty word. There are countably many choices. Every word is a finite linear combination of words with basis labels, by expanding each label in its basis. This identity holds for the Hilbert-space vectors: pair the difference with any word vector and use multilinearity of the correlation in the positive-word labels. All such inner products vanish, so density forces the difference to be zero. For a fixed basis-labelled positive word, approximate its positions by rational positions, remaining within the open set of positive distinct-point configurations. Its vectors converge in every \(\mathcal{H}_{\chi}\): the squared norm of their difference is the sum of two diagonal Gram values minus twice the real part of the cross Gram value, and continuity makes this tend to zero. The reflected and positive halves remain strictly separated throughout this limit. The full word span is dense by construction; thus the selected countable span is dense as well. The positions and bases were chosen without reference to \(\chi\), so the collection is common to every phase.\(\Box\)

Theorem 5.16. (A one-dimensional ground space in almost every phase) Assume every \(S_{\chi}\) has the Euclidean Hilbert-space dynamics of Theorem 5.5, with unit vacuum \(\Omega_{\chi}\). Assume there is a common countable collection of positive words whose vectors span densely in every phase Hilbert space. Then, for \(\mu\)-almost every \(\chi\),

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{H}_{\chi, 0} =\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\chi} .\)

In particular there exists a phase with this property.

Proof. Fix a positive word \(v\). For integers \(n \geqslant 0\), set

\(\displaystyle f_n (\chi) = \|E_{\chi, n} [v]_{\chi} \|^2 = S_{\chi} ((\theta \tau_n v) (\tau_n v)) .\)

Each is a continuous function of \(\chi\), by (5.23). Contractivity gives \(0 \leqslant f_n \leqslant f_0\); the latter is integrable on the compact probability space. The large-time projection theorem gives \(f_n (\chi) \to \|P_{\chi, 0} [v]_{\chi} \|^2\). Dominated convergence and the barycenter identity imply

\(\displaystyle \int_{\Xi} \|P_{\chi, 0} [v]_{\chi} \|^2 \hspace{0.17em} d \mu = \lim_{n \to \infty} \|E_n [v]\|^2 = \|P_0 [v]\|^2 .\) (5.25)

All limit functions here are measurable as pointwise limits of the continuous \(f_n\).

Apply (5.22) to \(M (v)^{\ast} M (v)\). Since characters preserve products and adjoints and \(M (v) \Omega = P_0 [v]\),

\(\displaystyle \int_{\Xi} |S_{\chi} (v) |^2 \hspace{0.17em} d \mu = \|P_0 [v]\|^2 .\)

Moreover \(\langle \Omega_{\chi}, P_{\chi, 0} [v]_{\chi} \rangle = S_{\chi} (v)\), because the projection fixes the vacuum. Orthogonal decomposition along the unit vector \(\Omega_{\chi}\) gives

\(\displaystyle \|P_{\chi, 0} [v]_{\chi} - S_{\chi} (v) \Omega_{\chi} \|^2 = \|P_{\chi, 0} [v]_{\chi} \|^2 - |S_{\chi} (v) |^2 .\)

The nonnegative right side is integrable and, by the two preceding identities, has integral zero. It is therefore zero almost everywhere.

Apply this conclusion to the common countable collection and intersect its full-measure sets. For every \(\chi\) in the intersection, the projection of its dense word span lies in \(\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\chi}\). This one-dimensional subspace is closed. Continuity of the projection therefore implies \(\text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} P_{\chi, 0} \subset \mathbb{C} \Omega_{\chi}\). The reverse inclusion holds because the vacuum is fixed. Finally a set of measure one in a probability space is nonempty.\(\Box\)

Remark 5.17. (Only the ground space is used in the selection) The character \(\chi_{\ast}\) is chosen from the full-measure set of Theorem 5.16. No condition on the short-distance behaviour of its correlations enters the choice. The trace asymptotic of Theorem 18.52 is proved afterwards at the selected character. The comparison bounds in Step 2 of Subsection 18.21 hold uniformly over all characters, and the identification in its Step 3 uses the null relations at the selected character. One could add a second condition to the selection: for every \(e \in \mathbb{R}\), the product \(r^8\) times the two-point function of the shifted trace \(\Theta + e\) tends to zero along a fixed subsequence of radii. This would change nothing. That two-point function equals \(T (r) + e^2\), because the one-point function of \(\Theta\) vanishes. The conclusion (19.32) gives \(r^8 T (r) \to 0\), so the condition holds at \(\chi_{\ast}\) for every \(e\) and along every sequence \(r \downarrow 0\).

This selection argument requires the phase Hilbert spaces and their contraction semigroups before it can be applied. It does not construct them from a probability decomposition alone. Retention of the raw-label null spaces, field derivatives, stress tensor and relativistic reconstruction remain additional obligations for the chosen phase.

5.5.1Vacuum uniqueness at every character

Almost-everywhere vacuum uniqueness does not by itself imply uniqueness at every character: a positive spectral value could approach zero at an exceptional character. The following quantitative argument excludes this possibility. It uses the relativistic reconstruction of each character without its vacuum-uniqueness clause. That construction uses the common phase caps and slab estimates of Subsection 5.6, the continuation and boundary arguments, and the word construction. Its forward spectrum follows from the first assertion of Theorem 6.33, which does not assume a unique vacuum. Thus the use of that assertion below is not circular.

For a character \(\chi\), write \(U_{\chi} (t, x)\) for the reconstructed translations, with \(t \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(x \in \mathbb{R}^3\), and let \(P_{\chi}\) be their momentum measure. Our convention is \(U_{\chi} (t, x) = \int e^{i (Et + p \cdot x)} \hspace{0.17em} dP_{\chi} (E, p)\). Write \(H_{\chi}\) for the nonnegative energy generator and \(Q_{\chi} = P_{\chi} (\{E = 0\})\). Its range is the translation-invariant space, since the forward cone intersects \(\{E = 0\}\) only at the origin. The vacuum belongs to this range.

A compactly smeared word sum is a finite linear combination \(A_{\chi}\) of products of fields with smooth compactly supported spacetime tests, acting on the common invariant domain. The labels, coefficients and tests are fixed independently of \(\chi\). Its formal adjoint \(A_{\chi}^{\ast}\) is obtained by reversing each word and conjugating labels, tests and coefficients. Both \(A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi}\) and \(A_{\chi}^{\ast} \Omega_{\chi}\) are well defined. All norms and scalar products below are vacuum word correlations, so their dependence on \(\chi\) is controlled by the common boundary construction.

Lemma 5.18. (A uniform low-energy estimate) For each compactly smeared word sum there is \(D_A < \infty\), independent of the character, such that its positive spectral measure

\(\displaystyle \mu_{\chi} (B) = \langle A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi}, P_{\chi} (B) A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} \rangle\)

satisfies

\(\displaystyle \mu_{\chi} \{0 < E \leqslant u\} \leqslant D_A u^2 \qquad (u > 0) .\) (5.26)

Consequently, for every \(s > 0\),

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \langle A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi}, (e^{- sH_{\chi}} - Q_{\chi}) A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} \rangle \leqslant 2 D_A s^{- 2} .\) (5.27)

Proof. Let \(\nu_{\chi}\) be the analogous spectral measure for \(A_{\chi}^{\ast} \Omega_{\chi}\). The boundary continuity in Proposition 19.3 makes both total masses continuous in \(\chi\). Compactness of the character space gives one \(M < \infty\) bounding their sum. Both measures are supported in the forward cone.

Choose \(T, S < \infty\) so that every test support lies in \(\{|t| \leqslant T, |x| \leqslant S\}\), and take \(R > 1 + 2 T + 2 S\). For \(|t| \leqslant 1\) and \(|x| > R\), every point in a test support translated by \((t, x)\) is spacelike separated from every point in any untranslated test support: the spatial distance exceeds \(|x| - 2 S > 1 + 2 T\), whereas the time difference is at most \(1 + 2 T\). Locality allows all factors in the translated word sum to commute past all factors of the untranslated adjoint word sum on the invariant domain. Vacuum translation invariance therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \delta_{\chi} (t, x) := \int e^{i (Et + p \cdot x)} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\chi} (E, p) - \int e^{- i (Et + p \cdot x)} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu_{\chi} (E, p) = 0.\)

Indeed these two terms are respectively \(\langle \Omega_{\chi}, A_{\chi}^{\ast} A_{\chi} (t, x) \Omega_{\chi} \rangle\) and \(\langle \Omega_{\chi}, A_{\chi} (t, x) A_{\chi}^{\ast} \Omega_{\chi} \rangle\). The same argument covers finite sums and the empty word.

Put

\(\displaystyle h (E) = \int_0^1 \sin (tE) \hspace{0.17em} dt = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} (1 - \cos E) / E, & E > 0,\\ 0, & E = 0, \end{array} \right. \qquad g_{\chi} (x) = \int_0^1 \frac{\delta_{\chi} (t, x) - \delta_{\chi} (- t, x)}{2 i} \hspace{0.17em} dt.\)

For \(E \geqslant 0\), \(0 \leqslant h (E) \leqslant 1\); for \(0 \leqslant E \leqslant 1\), \(\sin (tE) \geqslant 2 tE / \pi\) and hence \(h (E) \geqslant E / \pi\). Fubini is justified by the finite masses. It expresses \(g_{\chi}\) as the Fourier transform, with positive exponent, of the positive measure

\(\displaystyle \rho_{\chi} = (p)_{\ast} (h (E) \mu_{\chi}) + (- p)_{\ast} (h (E) \nu_{\chi}) \quad \text{on } \mathbb{R}^3 .\)

The plus sign in the second term is important: time reversal changes the sign of its sine as well as the sign preceding \(\nu_{\chi}\). We have \(|g_{\chi} (x) | \leqslant M\) and \(g_{\chi} (x) = 0\) for \(|x| > R\).

For \(0 < u \leqslant 1\), choose a smooth \(B_u : \mathbb{R}^3 \to [0, 1]\) equal to one on the ball of radius \(u\) and supported in the ball of radius \(2 u\). Define \(\hat{B}_u (x) = \int e^{- ip \cdot x} B_u (p) \hspace{0.17em} dp\). Then \(| \hat{B}_u (x) | \leqslant \text{vol} (B_{2 u})\). Fourier inversion and Fubini give

\(\displaystyle (2 \pi)^3 \int B_u \hspace{0.17em} d \rho_{\chi} = \int g_{\chi} (x) \hat{B}_u (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx \leqslant M \text{vol} (B_R) \text{vol} (B_2) u^3 .\)

For Fubini one may use the rapid decay of \(\hat{B}_u\) and the finite mass of \(\rho_{\chi}\). The left side is real and nonnegative; the inequality follows by taking the absolute value of the right side. On \(u / 2 < E \leqslant u\), cone support gives \(|p| \leqslant u\), so \(B_u (p) = 1\) and \(h (E) \geqslant u / (2 \pi)\). Positivity of both terms in \(\rho_{\chi}\) therefore yields

\(\displaystyle \mu_{\chi} \{u / 2 < E \leqslant u\} \leqslant C_A u^2, \qquad C_A = \frac{M \text{vol} (B_R) \text{vol} (B_2)}{(2 \pi)^2} .\)

The disjoint dyadic rings with upper endpoints \(2^{- j} u\) cover \(\{0 < E \leqslant u\}\). Summing their bounds gives at most \((4 / 3) C_A u^2 \leqslant 2 C_A u^2\). For \(u > 1\) use \(\mu_{\chi} (\mathbb{R}^4) \leqslant M \leqslant Mu^2\). Thus (5.26) holds with \(D_A = \max (M, 2 C_A)\).

Finally the spectral theorem identifies the middle expression of (5.27) with \(\int_{E > 0} e^{- sE} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\chi}\). Since \(e^{- sE} = \int_E^{\infty} se^{- st} \hspace{0.17em} dt\), Tonelli and (5.26) bound this by

\(\displaystyle s \int_0^{\infty} e^{- st} \mu_{\chi} \{0 < E \leqslant t\} \hspace{0.17em} dt \leqslant D_A s \int_0^{\infty} t^2 e^{- st} \hspace{0.17em} dt = 2 D_A s^{- 2} .\)

All constants depend on the fixed word sum, not on the character.\(\Box\)

Theorem 5.19. (The vacuum line in every reconstructed character) For every character \(\chi\), the translation-invariant vectors in its reconstructed Hilbert space are exactly \(\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\chi}\).

Proof. For almost every character, Theorem 5.16 and the heat-vector isometry imply this assertion by the last part of Theorem 6.33. Fix a compactly smeared word sum and \(s > 0\). The scalar function

\(\displaystyle \chi \longmapsto \langle A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi}, e^{- sH_{\chi}} A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} \rangle - | \langle \Omega_{\chi}, A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} \rangle |^2\)

is continuous by Proposition 19.3: the heat pairing is the same vacuum word distribution at a fixed nonnegative imaginary gap \(s\) between its two halves. Indeed the mixed Gram identity (6.17), integrated against the tests, gives this identity at strictly positive internal heights. Send those heights to zero using the independent-height vector limits of Subsection 6.7. The insertion of \(e^{- sH_{\chi}}\) is a bounded contraction and commutes with the translation intertwiner, so the vector pairings converge. The common weakly ordered boundary limit gives the stated fixed-gap distribution. On the full-measure set just obtained, \(Q_{\chi}\) is the vacuum projection, so (5.27) places this function in the closed interval \([0, 2 D_A s^{- 2}]\). The bound holds everywhere. Otherwise continuity would give a nonempty open set on which it fails, and full support of \(\mu\) would give this set positive measure, a contradiction.

Fix now any character and let \(s \to \infty\). Spectral dominated convergence gives \(e^{- sH_{\chi}} \to Q_{\chi}\) strongly. The preceding bound implies

\(\displaystyle \|Q_{\chi} A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} \|^2 = | \langle \Omega_{\chi}, A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} \rangle |^2 .\)

Since \(Q_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} = \Omega_{\chi}\), orthogonal decomposition along the unit vector \(\Omega_{\chi}\) shows

\(\displaystyle Q_{\chi} A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} = \langle \Omega_{\chi}, A_{\chi} \Omega_{\chi} \rangle \Omega_{\chi} .\)

Such compactly smeared word vectors span densely. To verify the compact support restriction, choose a smooth cutoff \(\zeta = 1\) near zero and replace each Schwartz test \(f\) by \(\zeta (\hspace{0.17em} \cdot / n) f\). These converge to \(f\) in every Schwartz seminorm, by Leibniz' rule and rapid decay on the escaping support of the cutoff derivatives. Joint continuity of word correlations implies convergence of the resulting word vectors: the squared norm difference is a finite sum of convergent Gram values. Cyclicity then gives density. Since \(Q_{\chi}\) is bounded and the vacuum line is closed, its range on the whole Hilbert space lies in that line. The reverse inclusion follows from vacuum invariance.\(\Box\)

5.6Regularity and bounded insertions in the component theories

Several hypotheses in the selection theorem can now be supplied from the unselected operator estimates. Assume in this section that (5.20) and (5.21) hold for the unselected theory, and that its correlations are multilinear and permutation symmetric. By Corollary 5.13, the representing probability of Proposition 5.14 is then available.

Proposition 5.20. (Smoothness and caps in every character) The whole-word functions \(S_{\chi}\) are multilinear in the raw labels, permutation symmetric, and smooth on distinct-point configurations. They obey the slot derivative identity (5.21). For every grade bound \(D\) and label norm bound \(R\), the constants \(C, K\) in (5.20) also give

\(\displaystyle |S_{\chi} (w) | \leqslant C^n (n / r)^{Kn}\) (5.28)

for all \(\chi\), all words with these label bounds, and \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) with pairwise sup-norm separations at least \(r\). For every fixed label tuple and derivative order \(q\), there are \(C_q \geqslant 0\) and \(A_q \in \mathbb{N}\), independent of \(\chi\) and the positions, such that

\(\displaystyle \|D^q S_{\chi, \boldsymbol{v}} (x)\| \leqslant C_q r^{- A_q}\)

on the same separated configurations.

Proof. Multilinearity of slab operators was proved in the norm-separability argument. Their permutation symmetry follows by the same dense matrix-element test, using scalar permutation symmetry. Compression and the linear character preserve both identities. For local smoothness, fix a distinct configuration, translate it into a slab, and keep the translation and width fixed in a small neighborhood. Proposition 5.12 gives smoothness of the operator-valued map and its slot derivative formula. Compression is bounded linear, and the character is bounded linear on \(\mathcal{A}\); their composition gives the same statements for \(S_{\chi}\). Equivalently one may extend the character to the ambient operator space by Hahn–Banach before composing; the extension agrees on every compressed word and its derivatives.

To obtain (5.28), translate a given word into a slab with one unit of padding at each time boundary. Translation leaves all pairwise separations unchanged, and the boundary padding is at least \(r\). Contractivity of characters and of orthogonal compression gives

\(\displaystyle |S_{\chi} (w) | = | \chi (M (w)) | \leqslant \|M (w)\| \leqslant \|A_d (w)\| \leqslant C^n (n / r)^{Kn} .\)

For the empty word use normalization. For nonzero arity, iterate the slot derivative formula \(q\) times. There are finitely many resulting label tuples, one for each sequence of \(q\) coordinate directions. Their grades and raw-label norms have finite maxima, so apply (5.28) with these maxima. Expand the real \(q\)-linear derivative in the standard coordinate basis of \(\mathbb{R}^{4 n}\). On unit arguments every coordinate coefficient has absolute value at most one, so its operator norm is bounded by the sum of the absolute values of its at most \((4 n)^q\) entries. This yields the claimed constants and the integer exponent \(A_q = Kn\) for the enlarged label bounds. At arity zero the function is constant one, with all positive-order derivatives zero.\(\Box\)

Lemma 5.21. (Original null relations hold in every character) Fix a grade \(d\). Let \(N_d\) be the space of raw labels \(v\) of grade \(d\) whose unselected separated correlations vanish whenever \(v\) is inserted, with arbitrary finitely many spectator labels of arbitrary grades. Define \(N_{\chi, d}\) by the same condition for \(S_{\chi}\). Then

\(\displaystyle N_d \subseteq N_{\chi, d} \qquad \text{for every } \chi \in \Xi .\)

Consequently every phase correlation factors, in each slot, through the unselected field quotient at that grade.

Proof. Let \(w\) be any distinct-point word containing a label \(v \in N_d\). Translate the whole word into a slab of width \(a > 0\), and denote its bounded insertion operator on the unselected Euclidean Hilbert space by \(A_a (w)\). For positive-time word vectors \([u]\) and \([z]\), the matrix-element identity expresses

\(\displaystyle \langle [u], A_a (w) [z] \rangle\)

as an unselected correlation with the translated word in the middle, a reflected copy of \(u\) below the slab and a shifted copy of \(z\) above it. All points are distinct: each exterior word is internally distinct, and the three groups have disjoint time ranges. This is an allowed all-spectator test of the label \(v\), so the matrix element is zero. By linearity it is zero on the spans of positive-time word vectors. Those spans are dense, and \(A_a (w)\) is bounded; hence all its matrix elements vanish and \(A_a (w) = 0\).

Its compression to the ground subspace is therefore zero. Placement independence identifies this compression with \(M (w)\), and evaluating any character gives \(S_{\chi} (w) = \chi (M (w)) = 0\). Permutation symmetry allows the null label in any slot. This proves the inclusion for every character, without discarding a set of measure zero. Finally, if two raw labels represent the same unselected class, their difference is in \(N_d\); multilinearity shows that replacing one by the other leaves every phase correlation unchanged.\(\Box\)

Corollary 5.22. (Phase Hilbert spaces and selection) Every character table has the Hilbert space and strongly continuous contraction semigroup of Subsection 5.1. The common countable family in Lemma 5.15 exists. Consequently Theorem 5.16 applies, giving a phase whose time-invariant subspace is exactly its vacuum line.

Proof. Normalization, translation invariance and time reflection positivity were proved after (5.23). The adjoint identity \(M (\theta w) = M (w)^{\ast}\) gives \(S_{\chi} (\theta w) = \overline{S_{\chi} (w)}\). For a word in arbitrary position, this operator identity follows by translating it into a slab, using its opposite word there and the slab adjoint identity, and then using placement independence of \(M\). Proposition 5.20 supplies permutation symmetry, continuity and the separated zeroth-order bound. Lemma 5.2 gives the boundedness under separating translations required in (5.2). All hypotheses of the Hilbert-space and contraction construction are therefore met for each \(S_{\chi}\). Multilinearity, finite-dimensional raw-label spaces and continuity give the common countable dense span by Lemma 5.15. The selection theorem now applies.\(\Box\)

Lemma 5.23. (Smeared reflection positivity) For any correlation table with continuous distinct-point correlations and point reflection positivity, the reflection form is nonnegative on smooth compactly supported positive-time configuration tests.

Proof. For one fixed label tuple, the map \(x \mapsto [w (x)]\) into the GNS space is norm-continuous on positive-time distinct-point configurations. Indeed its squared norm difference is a finite combination of continuous Gram entries, as in Lemma 5.15. On the compact support of a test \(\varphi\), it is bounded and its image is a separable compact set. Hence the compactly supported continuous vector function \(\varphi (x) [w (x)]\) is Bochner integrable. Denote its integral by \(\Psi_{\varphi}\). For two such tests, continuity of the inner product and Fubini give

\(\displaystyle \langle \Psi_{\varphi}, \Psi_{\psi} \rangle = \iint \overline{\varphi (x)} \psi (y) S ((\theta w (x)) w (y)) \hspace{0.17em} dx \hspace{0.17em} dy.\)

The scalar integrand is absolutely integrable: on the product of the two compact supports the two reflected halves have a positive time gap, each half has internally distinct points, and the continuous kernel is bounded. Different arities and labels cause no change to this argument; each pair has its own compact product support. The empty word uses the one-point zero-dimensional configuration space. A finite combination of tests has reflection form \(\| \sum_i c_i \Psi_{\varphi_i} \|^2 \geqslant 0\).\(\Box\)

Proposition 5.24. (Slab bounds survive character selection) Let \(A_{\chi, d} (w)\) denote insertion in the Hilbert space of a character table. Such an operator exists for every slab word, and

\(\displaystyle \|A_{\chi, d} (w)\| \leqslant \|A_d (w)\| .\)

In particular the uniform bound (5.20) holds in every phase with the same constants. Its slab operators are smooth in operator norm and obey the same slot derivative identities.

Proof. Let \(a = A_d (w)\) in the unselected Hilbert space and put \(c = \|a\|\). For a positive word \(v\), let \(v_n = (I_{w^{\sharp}} I_w)^n v\). The maps \(X (v) : \mathcal{H}_0 \to \mathcal{H}\) from Subsection 5.2 satisfy

\(\displaystyle X (I_w v) = aX (v), \qquad X (v_n) = (a^{\ast} a)^n X (v) .\)

The first equality is the slab concatenation law restricted to \(\mathcal{H}_0\); the second follows by induction and the adjoint law. Therefore \(\|X (v_n)\| \leqslant c^{2 n} \|X (v)\|\). For any \(D > c\), the character Gram identity gives

\(\displaystyle \|[v_n]_{\chi} \|^2 = \chi (M ((\theta v_n) v_n)) = \chi (X (v_n)^{\ast} X (v_n)) \leqslant \|X (v)\|^2 D^{4 n} .\)

Here \(X (v_n)^{\ast} X (v_n) \in \mathcal{A}\) by its equality with the compressed whole-word operator, and the character bound applies in that algebra. Lemma 5.7 produces a phase insertion operator with norm at most \(D\). These operators are all identical: their action on every positive word is prescribed, and the word span is dense. Taking the infimum over \(D > c\) proves the bound \(c\), also when \(c = 0\). The unselected uniform estimate therefore transfers. The character slot identities were proved in Proposition 5.20; applying Proposition 5.12 inside the phase Hilbert space proves its operator smoothness and derivative formulas.\(\Box\)

These results construct the time-axis Euclidean component theories from the unselected slab and differential estimates. Full orthogonal covariance of each character, and ordinary conjugation separately from reflected conjugation, still require the reflection-transport argument. Equality of null spaces requires the detector inputs in Subsection 5.8.

5.7Ordinary reflections and covariance of individual phases

Reflected conjugation is built into the Hilbert inner product. Ordinary reflection, which acts complex-linearly on labels, is a different operation. Proving that it fixes each compressed observable is the extra step needed to obtain orthogonal covariance in each character separately. We denote ordinary time reflection by \(\rho\); thus \(\theta\) also conjugates labels and reverses the word, whereas \(\rho\) does neither. Permutation symmetry makes the reversal immaterial to \(M\).

We use the following relation between the two label operations, which is how the reflected conjugate is defined. Write \(v \mapsto \bar{v}\) for the conjugation of raw labels and \(R_{\rho}\) for the complex-linear label action of the time reflection, one of the signed coordinate transformations. Then the reflected conjugate of \(v\) is

\(\displaystyle Jv = R_{\rho} \bar{v}, \qquad \text{and} \qquad R_{\rho} \bar{v} = \overline{R_{\rho} v} .\)

Here the second identity is the case \(s = \rho\) of the commutation

\(\displaystyle R_s \bar{v} = \overline{R_s v} \qquad \text{for every signed coordinate transformation } s,\)

of conjugation with the signed coordinate actions on raw labels. Two facts used below follow from the definition of \(J\) alone: if \(\bar{v} = v\) then \(Jv = R_{\rho} v\); and, for every word, \(\theta w\) is the reversal of \(\rho \bar{w}\), where \(\bar{w}\) conjugates each label, because \(\theta\) reflects, reverses and applies \(J\) letter by letter. The commutation is not needed for these two facts. It is what makes the two possible orders of the operations agree, \(R_{\rho} \bar{v} = \overline{R_{\rho} v}\), and it gives \(J^2 v = R_{\rho} \overline{R_{\rho} \bar{v}} = R_{\rho} R_{\rho} v = v\), hence \(\theta^2 = 1\), as assumed in Subsection 5.1.

Lemma 5.25. (Positivity from bounded real approximations) Suppose real numbers \(b_t (i, j)\), for integers \(i < j\), satisfy \(b_t (i, j) \to a\) as \(t \to \infty\), with the same limit \(a\) for every pair. Suppose there are \(\epsilon_k \geqslant 0\) tending to zero and finite \(R_k \geqslant 0\) with the following property. For every \(k\) and finite \(m\), for all sufficiently large \(t\) there exist simultaneous real random variables \(X_1, \ldots, X_m\), possibly on cutoff-dependent probability spaces, such that eventually in the ordered cutoff limit

\(\displaystyle |X_i | \leqslant R_k, \qquad \mathbb{E} (X_i X_j) \leqslant b_{\text{cut}, t} (i, j) + \epsilon_k \quad (i < j),\)

where \(b_{\text{cut}, t} (i, j) \to b_t (i, j)\) in that cutoff limit. Then \(a \geqslant 0\).

Proof. The variables are bounded and measurable, so their products are integrable. Nonnegativity of a square gives

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \mathbb{E} \left( \sum_{i = 1}^m X_i \right)^2 \leqslant mR_k^2 + 2 \sum_{i < j} (b_{\text{cut}, t} (i, j) + \epsilon_k) .\)

For fixed \(k, m, t\), all eventual conditions can be imposed together: only finitely many variables and pairs occur. Remove the cutoff in the specified order, then send \(t \to \infty\), obtaining \(0 \leqslant mR_k^2 + m (m - 1) (a + \epsilon_k)\). For \(m \geqslant 2\), divide by \(m (m - 1)\) and send \(m \to \infty\). This yields \(a + \epsilon_k \geqslant 0\). Now send \(k \to \infty\). The order of these limits matters: \(R_k\) may diverge as \(k\) grows.\(\Box\)

The lattice construction applies the lemma to a real-labelled word \(w\) at an internally generic grid configuration, minus its ordinary reflection. Translate several copies along one vector with strictly positive coordinates, spacing the \(i\)-th copy by \(it\) times that vector. For fixed distinct copies and large \(t\), their combined configurations are generic in every coordinate. The covariance to be approximated is the four-term expression

\(\displaystyle S (w_i w_j) - S (w_i (\rho w)_j) - S ((\rho w)_i w_j) + S ((\rho w)_i (\rho w)_j),\)

or its real part. By Lemma 5.9, its large-separation limit is

\(\displaystyle a = \text{Re} \langle \Omega, (M (w) - M (\rho w))^2 \Omega \rangle .\) (5.29)

In the lemma, \(b_t (i, j)\) is this four-term expression for the copies \(i < j\) at spacing \(t\), and \(b_{\text{cut}, t} (i, j)\) is the same four-term expression for the lattice correlations at finite cutoff. Two inputs are required here, and they are distinct. The first is the existence of the bounded real variables in Lemma 5.25, with errors independent of the number and offsets of copies. The second is the convergence \(b_{\text{cut}, t} (i, j) \to b_t (i, j)\) in the ordered cutoff limit. The second is not supplied by the approximations; it follows from the ordered point limits of Proposition 4.19 for the same constructed lattice family, extraction order and hierarchy table. In this application the construction and hierarchy hypotheses hold, each token of the chosen real basis is fixed by conjugation, and the original grid configuration is internally generic, as required in the proposition below. Signed reflection preserves that genericity. For each fixed pair \(i < j\), the positive-coordinate displacement makes each of the four combined configurations generic for all sufficiently large \(t\). Fix such a \(t\) and apply the point limits to the four correlations, taking the mesh limit at each selected period before the period limit. The resulting convergence is for the real four-term covariance. The separate bounded approximation input provides the simultaneous real approximants and their uniform error bounds. Wilson positivity then supplies the nonnegative square.

Proposition 5.26. (Ordinary reflection fixes compressed words) Assume the bounded real approximation property just described holds on a dense set of internally generic grid configurations for each tuple of real basis labels, and that at those configurations the four-term cutoff covariances converge in the ordered cutoff limit as just described. Assume the norm-regularity hypotheses of Subsection 5.4 and that each raw label space has a basis fixed by conjugation. Then \(M (\rho w) = M (w)\) for every distinct-point word.

Proof. First take a real basis-labelled word at an admitted configuration. Its labels are fixed by conjugation, so \(\theta w\) is the reversal of \(\rho w\) by the relation \(Jv = R_{\rho} \bar{v}\) above. Permutation symmetry and the adjoint identity therefore give \(M (\rho w) = M (\theta w) = M (w)^{\ast}\). Thus \(D = M (w) - M (\rho w)\) satisfies \(D^{\ast} = - D\). The previous lemma and (5.29) imply

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \text{Re} \langle \Omega, D^2 \Omega \rangle = - \|D \Omega \|^2 .\)

Hence \(D \Omega = 0\), and separation of \(\Omega\) for \(\mathcal{A}\) gives \(D = 0\). Norm continuity of compressed words and their reflected words extends the identity from the dense admitted configurations to all distinct configurations for these basis labels. Both \(M\) and ordinary reflection are complex-multilinear in the labels. Expanding an arbitrary tuple in the real basis therefore extends the equality to every tuple of complex labels.\(\Box\)

We make explicit one other algebraic point used below. Define a null raw label to be one whose insertion gives zero correlation with every finite list of exterior insertions at distinct points. A slab operator containing a null label is zero: pair it with two arbitrary word vectors, use the null-label property on the resulting whole correlation, and use density. Consequently \(M (w)\) depends only on the quotient classes of its labels. Assume that the orthogonal action on those classes is an actual group action and agrees, for signed coordinate transformations, with the raw signed-label action after projection. These are the precise compatibilities used when composing rotations below.

Proposition 5.27. (Orthogonal covariance in each character) Under these compatibilities and Proposition 5.26, \(M (Kw) = M (w)\) for every \(K \in O (4)\) and distinct-point word \(w\). Thus \(S_{\chi} (Kw) = S_{\chi} (w)\) for every character.

Proof. Write \(F (p, w) = \langle \Omega, M (p) M (w) \Omega \rangle\). We first show simultaneous invariance

\(\displaystyle F (Kp, Kw) = F (p, w) \qquad (K \in O (4)) .\) (5.30)

The cluster argument in Proposition 5.10 already proves this whenever there exists a vector \(b\) with \(b_0 > 0\) and \((Kb)_0 > 0\). Put \(a = (Ke_0)_0\). If \(a > - 1\), choose \(b = e_0 + K^{- 1} e_0\); orthogonality gives \(b_0 = (Kb)_0 = 1 + a > 0\). If \(a = - 1\), then \((\rho Ke_0)_0 = 1\), so the argument applies to \(\rho K\). Individual invariance under \(\rho\) removes the extra reflection from each factor and again gives (5.30).

For any \(J \in O (4)\), rotate a pair backwards by \(J^{- 1}\), apply ordinary reflection to its second factor alone, and rotate forwards by \(J\). Equation (5.30) gives

\(\displaystyle F (p, w) = F (J^{- 1} p, J^{- 1} w) = F (J^{- 1} p, \rho J^{- 1} w) = F (p, J \rho J^{- 1} w) .\)

There is no ambiguity from raw representatives, by quotient independence. Pair values separate compressed operators: if \(F (p, w) = F (p, z)\) for every \(p\), replace \(p\) by \(\theta v\) to obtain

\(\displaystyle \langle M (v) \Omega, (M (w) - M (z)) \Omega \rangle = 0.\)

The linear span of \(M (v) \Omega\) is dense in \(\mathcal{H}_0\), so \((M (w) - M (z)) \Omega = 0\); separation gives \(M (w) = M (z)\). Thus \(M\) is individually invariant under every conjugate \(J \rho J^{- 1}\).

These conjugates are all hyperplane reflections: map \(e_0\) to any chosen unit normal by an orthogonal map. For completeness, hyperplane reflections generate \(O (d)\). Given an orthogonal map \(K\), if \(Ke_1 \ne e_1\), reflection in the hyperplane normal to \(Ke_1 - e_1\) sends \(Ke_1\) to \(e_1\); this follows immediately from the reflection formula and \(|Ke_1 | = |e_1 | = 1\). Composing with this reflection gives a map fixing \(e_1\). It preserves \(e_1^{\perp}\), and induction on dimension writes its restriction as a product of reflections there, extended to fix \(e_1\). If it already fixes \(e_1\), start with that restriction. Dimension zero is trivial. Invariance under products follows from the group action on quotient labels. This proves individual invariance under every orthogonal map, and evaluation by a character gives the asserted phase covariance.\(\Box\)

Ordinary conjugation now follows as well. Since \(\theta w\) is a permutation of \(\rho \bar{w}\), by the same relation,

\(\displaystyle S_{\chi} (\bar{w}) = S_{\chi} (\rho \bar{w}) = S_{\chi} (\theta w) = \overline{S_{\chi} (w)} .\)

Signed coordinate covariance follows from the compatibility of the quotient action with the raw signed action. Transporting time-axis reflection positivity by signed permutations gives reflection positivity about every coordinate axis: choose a signed permutation taking that axis to the time axis, rotate each positive word and its labels, and use covariance and the conjugation compatibility in the Gram matrix. Lemma 5.23 then supplies the corresponding smeared positivity. The bounded real approximation on arbitrarily fine generic grids is constructed in Subsection 13.11 and applied in Subsection 16.9; its uniformity over finitely many replicated copies is indispensable.

5.8Keeping the same field space in each phase

A field label may disappear when one passes to a component of a mixture. For the selected phase to represent the intended fields, one must rule out additional null labels. The following argument explains the role of the finite detectors and the uniform moment estimates used for this purpose.

Lemma 5.28. (Moment bounds at every character) Let \(A \in \mathcal{A}\), let \(t \geqslant 0\), and suppose

\(\displaystyle \langle \Omega, (A^{\ast} A)^n \Omega \rangle \leqslant t^{2 n} \qquad (n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots) .\)

Then \(| \chi (A) | \leqslant t\) for every \(\chi \in \Xi\).

Proof. The representing measure gives \(\int | \chi (A) |^{2 n} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu \leqslant t^{2 n}\). If \(| \chi_0 (A) | > t\), choose \(p\) strictly between these numbers. The nonempty open set \(O = \{\chi : | \chi (A) | > p\}\) has positive measure \(c\) by full support. Hence \(cp^{2 n} \leqslant \int | \chi (A) |^{2 n} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu \leqslant t^{2 n}\). Since \(p > 0\), division gives \(c \leqslant (t / p)^{2 n} \to 0\), a contradiction. This also covers \(t = 0\), when the right side already vanishes for positive \(n\).\(\Box\)

Here is the elementary two-point calculation to which these moment estimates are applied. At a fixed scale \(k\), let \(a, b\) denote two insertions at distinct points and let \(j_a, j_b, \kappa \in \mathbb{C}\) be the prescribed comparison one-point values and connected two-point value. Form the elements

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} D_a & = M (a) - j_a I, \qquad D_b = M (b) - j_b I,\\ D_{ab} & = M (ab) - j_a M (b) - j_b M (a) + (j_a j_b - \kappa) I. \end{array}\)

Assume the preceding lemma's moment bounds hold for each of these three elements with the same number \(\epsilon_k \geqslant 0\). It follows for every character that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} |S_{\chi} (a) - j_a | & \leqslant \epsilon_k, \hspace{3cm} \text{(5.31)} \\ |S_{\chi} (ab) - (\kappa + j_a j_b) | & \leqslant \epsilon_k (1 + |j_a | + |j_b |) . \hspace{3cm} \text{(5.32)} \end{array}\)

Indeed, evaluating \(D_{ab}\) and rearranging gives

\(\displaystyle S_{\chi} (ab) - (\kappa + j_a j_b) = \chi (D_{ab}) + j_a (S_{\chi} (b) - j_b) + j_b (S_{\chi} (a) - j_a),\)

so the triangle inequality proves (5.32). If \(\epsilon_k \to 0\) and the prescribed one-point values converge, these errors tend to zero in every phase, with a bound independent of the character. This conclusion requires moments of the full corrected operators \(D_{ab}\), not only pointwise estimates for the unselected two-point correlations.

Proposition 5.29. (Finite detectors prevent an additional null space) Let \(E\) be a complex vector space of raw labels, and let \(N \subset N_{\chi}\) be subspaces. Write \(\pi : E \to E / N = V\) and \(\pi_{\chi} : E \to E / N_{\chi} = V_{\chi}\). Let \(W\) be a finite-dimensional complex normed space. Suppose there are linear maps \(J_k : W \to E\), linear tests \(\ell_{k, i} : V_{\chi} \to \mathbb{C}\), \(1 \leqslant i \leqslant r\), and an injective linear map \(F : W \to \mathbb{C}^r\) such that:

  1. \(\pi J_k\) is surjective for all sufficiently large \(k\);

  2. for every vector of a fixed basis of \(W\),

    \(\displaystyle F_k w := (\ell_{k, i} (\pi_{\chi} J_k w))_{i = 1}^r \longrightarrow Fw.\)

Then \(N_{\chi} = N\).

Proof. Convergence on a finite basis implies convergence in operator norm. To see this, let \(e_1, \ldots, e_s\) be that basis with continuous coordinate functionals \(e_j^{\ast}\). Then

\(\displaystyle \|(F_k - F) w\| \leqslant \sum_{j = 1}^s |e_j^{\ast} (w) | \hspace{0.17em} \|(F_k - F) e_j \| \leqslant \|w\| \sum_{j = 1}^s \|e_j^{\ast} \| \hspace{0.17em} \|(F_k - F) e_j \| .\)

The finite sum tends to zero. If \(W \ne \{0\}\), compactness of its unit sphere and injectivity of \(F\) give \(c = \min_{\|w\| = 1} \|Fw\| > 0\). Choose \(k\) late enough that \(\|F_k - F\| < c / 2\) and \(\pi J_k\) is onto. Then \(\|F_k w\| \geqslant (c / 2) \|w\|\), so \(F_k\) is injective. If \(W = \{0\}\), surjectivity already gives \(V = 0\), and the result is immediate.

Now take \(e \in N_{\chi}\). By surjectivity choose \(w \in W\) with \(\pi J_k w = \pi e\). Thus \(J_k w - e \in N \subset N_{\chi}\), and since \(e \in N_{\chi}\), also \(J_k w \in N_{\chi}\). All tests vanish on \(\pi_{\chi} J_k w = 0\); hence \(F_k w = 0\). Injectivity forces \(w = 0\), so \(\pi e = \pi J_k 0 = 0\), or \(e \in N\). This proves \(N_{\chi} \subset N\); the reverse inclusion was assumed.\(\Box\)

In the application at label degree \(d\), \(W\) is the comparison field space, \(J_k\) consists of its corrected labels at the chosen scales, and the tests \(\ell_{k, i}\) are one- or two-point correlations whose exterior labels are the corrected labels at comparison index \(k\); they depend on \(k\), as the proposition allows. The test points are \(x_{k, i} = \sigma_k y_{k, i}\), where \(\sigma_k \to 0\) is the comparison scale and the rescaled configurations \(y_{k, i}\) converge to configurations \(y_i\) of distinct points; the physical configurations \(x_{k, i}\) do not converge to a configuration of distinct points. Estimates (5.31)–(5.32) compare the phase correlations at \(x_{k, i}\) with the free values at \(y_{k, i}\), with errors \(\epsilon_k \to 0\) uniform in the character. Continuity of the free comparison table at the distinct configurations \(y_i\) then gives convergence of \(F_k\) to \(F\) on a basis, where \(F\) is given by the free tests at \(y_i\). The comparison limit \(k \to \infty\) is taken after the mesh and period limits that define the table. The comparison tests separate \(W\), so their joint map \(F\) is injective. The first hypothesis of the proposition is the eventual spanning statement for the corrected labels in the unselected quotient. The same-label construction of Subsection 16.8, followed by the exact-kernel argument in Subsection 16.12, supplies this spanning statement. Subsection 16.9 supplies the corrected-operator moment bounds on those trials, and Theorem 16.6 supplies the separating free tests. These applications retain the same corrected lifts and all separated correlations when passing to the smooth table. Lemma 5.21 supplies \(N \subseteq N_{\chi}\). The proposition then proves equality in every grade for every character. Thus the finite-dimensional argument uses the actual comparison and moment constructions; finite dimensionality alone does not supply them.

5.8.1Field inclusions from simultaneous tested representatives

In this subsection we reuse notation: \(A_d\) denotes the raw space, \(E_d\) its quotient, \(E\) their union, \(J_d\) a response map, and \(W\) a target space; these differ from the letters in Proposition 5.29.

Equality of the null spaces at each grade does not construct a map between those grades. We now give that construction from a separate realization input, on one fixed table. No change of phase or of the extraction defining the table is made in this argument.

Let \(A_d\) be a finite-dimensional complex raw label space for each \(d \in \mathbb{N}\). A raw label includes its grade. Fix a table \(S\) whose correlations

\(\displaystyle S (x ; (d_1, a_1), \ldots, (d_m, a_m)), \qquad x \in \text{Conf}_m (\mathbb{R}^4), \quad a_j \in A_{d_j},\)

are complex multilinear in the labels, smooth in \(x\), and symmetric under simultaneous permutation of labels and positions. Here \(\text{Conf}_m (\mathbb{R}^4)\) is the open set of pairwise distinct positions. Spectator tuples may have arbitrary finite length and arbitrary grades; no upper grade or arity bound is imposed on them.

An exterior \(\xi = (x ; \boldsymbol{b})\) contains a tuple \(\boldsymbol{b}\) of \(n\) raw spectators and a configuration of \(n + 1\) distinct positions, \(x \in \text{Conf}_{n + 1} (\mathbb{R}^4)\). Allow \(n = 0\). Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be the set of all such exteriors and define the linear response map

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{R}_d : A_d \longrightarrow \mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{X}}, \qquad (\mathcal{R}_d a) (x ; \boldsymbol{b}) = S (x ; (d, a), \boldsymbol{b}) .\)

Set

\(\displaystyle N_d = \ker \mathcal{R}_d, \qquad E_d = A_d / N_d, \qquad \pi_d : A_d \longrightarrow E_d .\)

Thus nullity means vanishing against every exterior, not just a finite detector family. The spaces \(E_d\) are finite-dimensional. The response map descends to an injective linear map \(J_d : E_d \to \mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{X}}\): if \(J_d \pi_d a = 0\), then \(a \in N_d\), so \(\pi_d a = 0\).

Choose a basis \((a_{d, j})_{j \in B_d}\) of each \(A_d\). The incoming simultaneous tested realization is the following statement. For every \(d \leqslant e\) and every \(j \in B_d\), there is one \(b_{de, j} \in A_e\) such that, for every spectator tuple \(\boldsymbol{b}\) of length \(n\) and every \(\varphi \in C_c^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_{n + 1} (\mathbb{R}^4))\),

\(\displaystyle \int \varphi (x) S (x ; (e, b_{de, j}), \boldsymbol{b}) \hspace{0.17em} dx = \int \varphi (x) S (x ; (d, a_{d, j}), \boldsymbol{b}) \hspace{0.17em} dx.\) (5.33)

The order of these quantifiers matters: \(b_{de, j}\) is chosen before the spectators and the test. A representative depending on either would not supply a linear map of label spaces. This input asks only for raw representatives of insertion functionals; it assumes neither quotient inclusions nor their injectivity or coherence.

Proposition 5.30. (Conditional construction of the field inclusions) For the fixed smooth symmetric table above, the simultaneous tested realization supplies unique linear maps \(\iota_{de} : E_d \to E_e\) for \(d \leqslant e\) preserving every exterior response. They are injective and satisfy

\(\displaystyle \iota_{dd} = 1, \qquad \iota_{ef} \iota_{de} = \iota_{df} \quad (d \leqslant e \leqslant f) .\)

Correlations descend to the quotient labels in every slot. For any fixed configuration of distinct points, any slot, and arbitrary quotient spectators, replacing a label \(v \in E_d\) in that slot by \(\iota_{de} v \in E_e\) leaves the correlation unchanged.

Proof. First define a raw linear map by basis expansion:

\(\displaystyle K_{de} \left( \sum_{j \in B_d} c_j a_{d, j} \right) = \sum_{j \in B_d} c_j b_{de, j} .\)

For each fixed spectator tuple and test, multilinearity and the finite sum in this definition extend (5.33) to every \(a \in A_d\), with \(b_{de, j}\) replaced by \(K_{de} a\). The same map works for all tests and all spectators because its basis images were fixed first.

We spell out why this tested equality is pointwise. For a fixed \(a\) and spectator tuple, let

\(\displaystyle h (x) = S (x ; (e, K_{de} a), \boldsymbol{b}) - S (x ; (d, a), \boldsymbol{b}) .\)

It is continuous on the open configuration space and has zero integral against every smooth compactly supported test there. If \(h (x_0) \ne 0\), choose \(c \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(|c| = 1\) and \(ch (x_0) = |h (x_0) |\). There is a ball about \(x_0\), with closure in the configuration space, on which \(\text{Re} (ch) > |h (x_0) | / 2\). Choose a nonnegative smooth function \(\psi\), supported in that ball, with positive integral. Such a function is obtained by translating and rescaling \(\exp (- 1 / (1 - |x|^2))\) on \(|x| < 1\), extended by zero outside. Then

\(\displaystyle 0 = \text{Re} \left( c \int \psi h \right) > \frac{|h (x_0) |}{2} \int \psi > 0,\)

a contradiction. Thus \(h = 0\) on the configuration space. In particular,

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{R}_e K_{de} =\mathcal{R}_d .\) (5.34)

No equality at coincident points is used or asserted.

If \(a \in N_d\), this identity gives \(K_{de} a \in N_e\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle \iota_{de} (\pi_d a) = \pi_e K_{de} a\)

is well defined and linear. If different simultaneous representatives, or a different basis, give a raw map \(K'_{de}\) with the same tested property, then (5.34) holds for both maps. Their difference takes values in \(N_e\), so they induce the same quotient map. For every \(v \in E_d\) we have

\(\displaystyle J_e \iota_{de} v = J_d v.\) (5.35)

Since \(J_d\) is injective, this also proves injectivity of \(\iota_{de}\). Since \(J_e\) is injective, it proves uniqueness of a response-preserving map \(E_d \to E_e\).

For completeness, null labels vanish in every slot. Permute the chosen slot and its position to the first position; symmetry expresses the correlation as a head insertion against another raw exterior. If the chosen label belongs to its \(N_d\), that head correlation is zero by definition. Thus changing any raw representative by a null label changes no correlation. Changing the finitely many representatives one at a time proves well-defined multilinear correlations on quotient tuples. Smoothness and permutation symmetry pass to these correlations by taking fixed raw representatives. The pointwise identity already proved at the head, followed by the same permutation, gives explicitly

\(\displaystyle S (x ; v_1, \ldots, \iota_{de} v_i, \ldots, v_m) = S (x ; v_1, \ldots, v_i, \ldots, v_m), \qquad v_i \in E_d,\) (5.36)

where the other labels have any fixed grades and the changed slot is assigned grade \(e\) on the left. Repeated application gives the identity when any finite collection of slots is raised, even to different grades. Integration against a compactly supported test preserves the identity.

Finally \(J_d \iota_{dd} = J_d\) implies \(\iota_{dd} = 1\). If \(d \leqslant e \leqslant f\), then

\(\displaystyle J_f \iota_{ef} \iota_{de} = J_e \iota_{de} = J_d = J_f \iota_{df} .\)

Injectivity of \(J_f\) proves composition. These identities are conclusions on the quotients; the chosen raw maps need not compose or equal the raw identity when \(d = e\).\(\Box\)

Proposition 5.31. (The common union and its universal property) Under the hypotheses of Proposition 5.30, the spaces \(E_d\) have an algebraic direct limit \(E\), with injective maps \(j_d : E_d \to E\) and \(j_e \iota_{de} = j_d\). Every element of \(E\) comes from one grade, and every finite collection comes from one common grade. The table correlations are well defined on finite tuples from \(E\). Fix any unital ring endomorphism \(\sigma : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}\); surjectivity is not assumed. If \(W\) is a complex vector space and additive maps \(F_d : E_d \to W\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle F_d (cv) = \sigma (c) F_d (v), \qquad F_e \iota_{de} = F_d \quad (d \leqslant e),\)

there is a unique additive \(\sigma\)-semilinear map \(F : E \to W\) with \(Fj_d = F_d\) for every \(d\). The complex-linear universal property is the case \(\sigma = \text{id}\).

Proof. Realize the union in the single response space:

\(\displaystyle E = \bigcup_{d \geqslant 0} J_d (E_d) \subseteq \mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{X}} .\)

Equation (5.35) makes these ranges increasing. If \(u = J_d v\) and \(w = J_e z\), put \(D = \max (d, e)\). For any scalars \(a, b \in \mathbb{C}\),

\(\displaystyle au + bw = J_D (a \iota_{dD} v + b \iota_{eD} z) \in E.\)

The zero vector belongs to every range, so \(E\) is a vector subspace. Take \(j_d\) to be \(J_d\) with codomain restricted to \(E\). These maps are injective and satisfy the stated compatibility. If \(u_1, \ldots, u_m \in E\), choose \(u_i = j_{d_i} v_i\) and let \(D = \max_i d_i\). Then \(u_i = j_D \iota_{d_i D} v_i\) for every \(i\). For the empty collection choose \(D = 0\). Surjectivity of \(\pi_D\) also gives simultaneous raw representatives in \(A_D\) for any such finite collection. No bound uniform over all elements of \(E\) is implied.

To evaluate a correlation of \(u_1, \ldots, u_m\), choose these representatives at a common grade and use the quotient correlation constructed above. Suppose a second choice uses grade \(D'\). At \(L = \max (D, D')\), the two representatives of each \(u_i\) have the same image under the injective map \(j_L\) and hence are equal in \(E_L\). Raising all slots to \(L\) leaves the correlation unchanged by (5.36). Thus the value is independent of both the common grade and all representatives. The same argument treats initially different grades slot by slot. Multilinearity, symmetry and smoothness follow by evaluating each finite tuple at one fixed common grade; the zero-point correlation is the original vacuum value and requires no label representative.

Define \(F (j_d v) = F_d v\). If \(j_d v = j_e z\), then at \(D = \max (d, e)\),

\(\displaystyle j_D \iota_{dD} v = j_D \iota_{eD} z, \qquad \text{hence} \qquad \iota_{dD} v = \iota_{eD} z.\)

Compatibility of the \(F_d\) proves \(F_d v = F_e z\), so \(F\) is well defined. For \(u = j_d v\) and \(w = j_e z\), put \(D = \max (d, e)\) as before. Then

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} F (u + w) & = F_D (\iota_{dD} v + \iota_{eD} z)\\ & = F_D \iota_{dD} v + F_D \iota_{eD} z = F (u) + F (w) . \end{aligned}\)

Also \(F (0) = F_d (0) = 0\), and for each \(c \in \mathbb{C}\),

\(\displaystyle F (cu) = F (j_d (cv)) = F_d (cv) = \sigma (c) F_d (v) = \sigma (c) F (u) .\)

Only the image \(\sigma (c)\) is used; no preimage under \(\sigma\) is chosen. Its restriction to each grade is \(F_d\). Any other such map agrees on every \(j_d (E_d)\), whose union is \(E\), and therefore equals \(F\). This is the algebraic direct-limit property of complex vector spaces; no topology or multiplication on the union is being asserted.\(\Box\)

These conclusions apply to a selected phase only with the realization receipt for that same selected table. More generally, suppose a second smooth symmetric table \(T\) on the same raw spaces has \(N_d^S \subseteq N_d^T\) at every grade, and the same raw maps \(K_{de}\) satisfy (5.33) for both tables. The natural surjections \(q_d : E_d^S \to E_d^T\), \(q_d \pi_d^S a = \pi_d^T a\), then obey

\(\displaystyle q_e \iota_{de}^S \pi_d^S a = \pi_e^T K_{de} a = \iota_{de}^T q_d \pi_d^S a.\)

Surjectivity of \(\pi_d^S\) proves compatibility on all quotient labels. The universal property gives the induced map between the two unions. Correlations of \(T\) on the \(S\)-quotients are well defined by null-space containment, and their inclusion compatibility follows from the same pointwise tested identity for \(T\). If the null spaces are equal, each \(q_d\) is an isomorphism, and the induced union map is an isomorphism: its inverse is obtained from the compatible inverses \(q_d^{- 1}\). The numerical correlations of the two tables need not be equal. Without the tested realization for \(T\) on these same raw maps, grade-wise null-space equality alone supplies none of these cross-grade correlation identities. Derivative maps and their slot identities, and compatibility with calibrated actions, require their own receipts. The reflected involution is constructed below only under its explicit additional raw symmetry and inclusion-compatibility hypotheses.

5.8.2Attaching one table and passing its inclusions to every character

The same-table realization input.
There is a conditional supplier for (5.33) in the preceding constructions. Fix one finite-volume family, its raw reference spaces \(A_d\), its terminal calibrations, and one retained extraction. Let \(S\) be the smooth symmetric table representing the ordered smeared limits of that same family. All the hypotheses of the insertion comparison in Subsection 16.11, the projection and all-test kernel construction in Subsection 16.12, and the normalized test argument in Subsection 7.4 are retained on these data. In particular, this retains their local service and equation inputs, comparison and rank purchases, inverse-calibration bounds, and convergence of the smeared correlations to this \(S\). The ordered limit permits the mesh threshold to depend on the selected period; no mesh threshold uniform in the period or diagonal limit is substituted.

Here is the precise application. Fix \(d \leqslant e\) and one basis label \(a_{d, j}\). The insertion comparison gives one row \(u_c \in A_e\), chosen before any spectator tuple or compact separated test \(\theta\), whose finite-cutoff tested values tend to the tested values \(M_{\theta}\) of \(a_{d, j}\) on \(S\). Write \(P_{e, c}\) and \(P_e\) for the finite and limiting projections in the kernel construction, and \(f_{\theta, c}, f_{\theta}\) for the corresponding tested linear functionals on \(A_e\). Their convergence and exact kernel identity are the hypotheses of (7.31). The subpower local equation input applies also to

\(\displaystyle \frac{u_c - P_{e, c} u_c}{1 + \|P_{e, c} u_c \|} \in \ker P_{e, c} .\)

Indeed this is exactly the normalized residual treated following (7.32): its first term has subpower bare norm by the relay bound, and its second is bounded in reference coordinates, hence has subpower bare norm by (7.30). Here the bare or inverse-calibrated norm is the norm in the actual inverse terminal coordinates, \(|X_{e, c}^{- 1} v|_1\), with \(X_{e, c}\) the same terminal calibration used by the relay. Thus both limits in (7.32) hold, on the same filter and with the same row \(u_c\). The compactness argument there supplies one \(b_{de, j} \in \text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} P_e\) with \(f_{\theta} (b_{de, j}) = M_{\theta}\) for every \(\theta\). This is (5.33). Its one compact refinement is made for this row, not separately for each test. The functionals \(f_{\theta}\) and values \(M_{\theta}\) are fixed limits along the original ordered filter. Each resulting representative therefore satisfies an identity between refinement-independent quantities. Different basis labels may use different compact refinements; no joint extraction of those refinements is needed to assemble their representatives. Applying it to the basis labels supplies the raw maps \(K_{de}\) above. This attachment uses only the inclusion comparison; it asserts neither a derivative identity nor the production of the retained analytic hypotheses.

Proposition 5.32. (The same inclusions in every whole-word character) Let \(S\), \(A_d\) and the raw maps \(K_{de}\) satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition 5.30. Assume in addition the unselected Hilbert space, bounded slab insertions, translation and permutation identities, and placement-independent compressed operators \(M (w)\) of Subsection 5.2 for this same \(S\). Let \(S_{\chi} (w) = \chi (M (w))\), \(\chi \in \Xi\), be its whole-word character tables as in (5.23). Assume these tables are continuous on each distinct-point configuration space, as supplied, with its own hypotheses, by Proposition 5.20. Then, for every character, every \(d \leqslant e\), every \(a \in A_d\), every finite raw spectator tuple \(\boldsymbol{b}\) of arbitrary grades, and every \(x \in \text{Conf}_{n + 1} (\mathbb{R}^4)\),

\(\displaystyle S_{\chi} (x ; (e, K_{de} a), \boldsymbol{b}) = S_{\chi} (x ; (d, a), \boldsymbol{b}) .\) (5.37)

The identity holds in any slot and after integration against every smooth compactly supported test in configuration space. The maps \(K_{de}\) are those fixed for \(S\), independent of the character and of the exterior. They induce coherent injective inclusions between the phase field quotients and a common algebraic union in each phase. The natural maps from the original quotients and union to these phase quotients and union commute with all inclusions. If, for a given character, \(N_d = N_{\chi, d}\) for every \(d\), these comparison maps are isomorphisms.

Proof. Proposition 5.30 has already converted the tested input on \(S\) to the pointwise all-exterior identity (5.34). Fix \(d, e, a, x, \boldsymbol{b}\), and let \(w\) and \(w'\) be the words at these same positions, with distinguished labels \((d, a)\) and \((e, K_{de} a)\) respectively. Translate both into the same open slab of width \(h > 0\). This is possible because their common finite set of times is bounded: choose the translation so that its smallest time is positive and then choose \(h\) larger than its largest time. Denote the two bounded insertion operators by \(B\) and \(B'\).

Use the matrix-element identity from the proof of Lemma 5.21. For any positive-time words \(u, z\), the two quantities

\(\displaystyle \langle [u], B [z] \rangle, \qquad \langle [u], B' [z] \rangle\)

are unselected correlations obtained by placing a reflected copy of \(u\) below the slab and a shifted copy of \(z\) above it. The exterior words and all their labels are the same in both expressions. Their points are internally distinct, and the three time ranges are disjoint; hence these are legitimate separated exteriors. Translation and permutation invariance of \(S\) put the distinguished label first and undo the common translation. Equation (5.34), with the original spectators and these additional exterior labels, makes the two matrix elements equal. This step uses all finite spectators, not merely a finite family of two-point tests.

Sesquilinearity gives equality for finite linear combinations of positive-time word vectors on both sides. Those vectors span a dense subspace of the unselected Hilbert space. Since \(B - B'\) is bounded, continuity gives \(\langle v, (B - B') z \rangle = 0\) for all Hilbert vectors \(v, z\). Taking \(v = (B - B') z\) proves \((B - B') z = 0\) for every \(z\), so \(B = B'\). Compression to the ground subspace preserves this equality. Placement independence identifies the compressed operators with \(M (w)\) and \(M (w')\). Applying any character proves (5.37). There is no exceptional set of characters. Equivalently, one may apply a continuous linear extension of the character after compression; only its values on these whole-word operators are used. No multiplicativity for separated subwords, positivity of such an extension, or differentiability of a slab insertion is needed for this step.

Multilinearity and permutation symmetry of the slab operators, obtained by the same dense matrix-element test from those properties of \(S\), pass to every \(S_{\chi}\). It moves the distinguished slot to any prescribed slot. Continuity on configuration space makes each side integrable against a compactly supported smooth test there; integrating the pointwise identity proves the tested identity. Empty spectator tuples are included in this argument; the comparison then concerns one insertion. The empty word has no slot to raise and keeps its original normalization.

For clarity, this also gives the identity formulated using the original quotient labels, without imposing linearity on a choice of raw representatives. If \(v = \pi_d a\) and \(r_d (v)\) is any representative of \(v\), then \(r_d (v) - a \in N_d\). Similarly,

\(\displaystyle r_e (\iota_{de} v) - K_{de} a \in N_e, \qquad \iota_{de} v = \pi_e K_{de} a.\)

Here we use only the proof of Lemma 5.21: bounded slab insertion, its matrix-element identity, density and placement independence imply original-null containment. All these hypotheses are already assumed here; the stronger derivative and all-copy bounds standing in its section are not used in that proof. It follows that both differences vanish in every character correlation against arbitrary spectators. The same is true for any changes of spectator representatives. Thus the identity on the fixed original quotient plan and the raw identity (5.37) agree. The linear map is induced by \(K_{de}\) on the quotient, not by the representative section \(r_d\).

Finally apply the construction of Proposition 5.30 to the phase response maps: (5.37) gives \(\mathcal{R}_{\chi, e} K_{de} =\mathcal{R}_{\chi, d}\) directly. The descent, injectivity, uniqueness and composition proof there uses this identity, linearity and symmetry only. Its earlier smoothness assumption was used to obtain the pointwise identity from the tested one; that step is already proved here. Proposition 5.31 therefore constructs the phase union with continuous correlations; its smoothness clauses are replaced by continuity in this application. Smoothness follows only when the additional hypotheses of Proposition 5.20 are supplied. By Lemma 5.21, \(N_d \subseteq N_{\chi, d}\), so the two-table comparison preceding this subsection applies to these same raw maps. Explicitly, with \(q_{\chi, d} \pi_d a = \pi_{\chi, d} a\),

\(\displaystyle q_{\chi, e} \iota_{de} \pi_d a = \pi_{\chi, e} K_{de} a = \iota^{\chi}_{de} q_{\chi, d} \pi_d a.\)

Surjectivity of \(\pi_d\) gives the commuting identity on \(E_d\) and hence on its algebraic union. If the null spaces are equal in every grade, the \(q_{\chi, d}\) and their compatible union map are isomorphisms as proved there. This last conclusion compares field spaces and their inclusions; it does not assert equality of numerical correlations of \(S\) and \(S_{\chi}\).\(\Box\)

This supplies the same-map selected-table attachment conditional on the stated unselected realization and whole-word construction. It does not supply the local service, calibrated comparison, rank or ordered-limit inputs in the same-table realization paragraph. Equality of the null spaces, when needed for isomorphisms, retains the separate hypotheses of the earlier phase-null argument. Derivative and Ward identities remain separate statements. The next subsection treats only the reflected conjugation, under its stated additional symmetry and orientation inputs.

5.8.3A compatible reflected adjoint on the common union

We retain the raw spaces, null quotients and inclusions already constructed. The following are additional inputs, not consequences of equality of null spaces. On each \(A_d\) assume a conjugate-linear involution \(a \mapsto \bar{a}\) and a complex-linear action \(R_{d, s}\) of the signed coordinate transformations, commuting with conjugation. Assume that the same table \(S\) obeys, at every distinct-point configuration,

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} S (x ; \bar{\boldsymbol{a}}) & = \overline{S (x ; \boldsymbol{a})},\\ S (sx ; R_s \boldsymbol{a}) & = S (x ; \boldsymbol{a}) . \end{aligned}\) (5.38)

Here each operation acts at the grade of its label, and \(sx\) transforms all positions. These are the reality and signed covariance receipts for this table. Fix a coordinate reflection \(\rho\), so \(\rho^2 = 1\). The raw reflected conjugation is \(a^{\dagger_{\rho}} = R_{d, \rho} \bar{a}\). This is the fixed-grade operation of Subsection 5.7, not ordinary reflection alone. Its conjugate linearity and square-one identity follow from the commuting real structure and signed action, as proved there. We now check descent and compatibility with this particular field union.

First these operations preserve each null space. If \(a \in N_d\), then for arbitrary raw spectators \(\boldsymbol{b}\) and separated \(x\),

\(\displaystyle S (x ; \bar{a}, \boldsymbol{b}) = \overline{S (x ; a, \bar{\boldsymbol{b}})} = 0.\)

For a signed transformation \(s\), covariance likewise gives

\(\displaystyle S (x ; R_{d, s} a, \boldsymbol{b}) = S (s^{- 1} x ; a, R_{s^{- 1}} \boldsymbol{b}) = 0.\)

The transformed positions remain distinct, and the transformed spectators are admissible raw labels of the same grades. Thus conjugation and \(R_{d, s}\) preserve \(N_d\). Applying the inverse operations shows that they carry it onto itself. Consequently the quotient maps

\(\displaystyle \textsf{c}_d (\pi_d a) = \pi_d \bar{a}, \qquad \textsf{m}_{d, s} (\pi_d a) = \pi_d R_{d, s} a\)

are well defined: replacing \(a\) by \(a + n\) with \(n \in N_d\) changes either image by a null class. Additivity and conjugate homogeneity of \(\textsf{c}_d\), and complex linearity of \(\textsf{m}_{d, s}\), follow by applying these formulas to sums and scalar multiples of raw representatives. Surjectivity of \(\pi_d\) passes the raw group law, commutation with conjugation and square-one identities to the quotient. In particular

\(\displaystyle \delta_{d, \rho} = \textsf{m}_{d, \rho} \textsf{c}_d : E_d \longrightarrow E_d\)

is conjugate-linear and satisfies \(\delta_{d, \rho}^2 = 1\).

To pass to the union retain the inclusion-only orientation identities

\(\displaystyle \textsf{c}_e \iota_{de} = \iota_{de} \textsf{c}_d, \qquad \textsf{m}_{e, \rho} \iota_{de} = \iota_{de} \textsf{m}_{d, \rho} \quad (d \leqslant e) .\) (5.39)

It suffices to supply these for the chosen reflection; the stronger signed orientation receipt supplies the second identity for every signed \(s\). For the raw maps used above, these assumptions have the precise equivalent form

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} \overline{K_{de} a} - K_{de} \bar{a} & \in N_e,\\ R_{e, \rho} K_{de} a - K_{de} R_{d, \rho} a & \in N_e \qquad (a \in A_d) . \end{aligned}\) (5.40)

Indeed, substitute \(\iota_{de} \pi_d a = \pi_e K_{de} a\) in each side of (5.39). Their difference is the projection of the corresponding expression in (5.40); its projection vanishes exactly when it lies in \(N_e\). Every quotient label is a projection, so this proves both directions. Exact commutation of arbitrarily chosen raw representatives is not required. These orientation receipts are retained explicitly here; neither grade-wise null equality nor the choice of basis images for \(K_{de}\) is being used as their proof.

Proposition 5.33. (Reflected adjoint of the field union) Under the hypotheses above, the same algebraic union \(E\) has a unique conjugate-linear map \(\mathcal{J}_{\rho} : E \to E\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{J}_{\rho} j_d \pi_d a = j_d \pi_d a^{\dagger_{\rho}} \qquad (d \geqslant 0, a \in A_d) .\) (5.41)

It is an involution and hence a bijection. In particular it preserves every level subspace \(j_d (E_d)\), with restriction equal to the already specified reflected adjoint of that grade. No topology on \(E\) is required.

Proof. The two identities in (5.39) imply

\(\displaystyle \delta_{e, \rho} \iota_{de} = \textsf{m}_{e, \rho} \textsf{c}_e \iota_{de} = \textsf{m}_{e, \rho} \iota_{de} \textsf{c}_d = \iota_{de} \delta_{d, \rho} .\)

Thus \(F_d = j_d \delta_{d, \rho} : E_d \to E\) is a compatible family:

\(\displaystyle F_e \iota_{de} = j_e \delta_{e, \rho} \iota_{de} = j_e \iota_{de} \delta_{d, \rho} = F_d .\)

Each \(F_d\) is additive and semilinear for \(\sigma (c) = \bar{c}\). Proposition 5.31 supplies the unique such map \(\mathcal{J}_{\rho}\), and its restriction formula gives (5.41). More concretely, if the same element is represented as \(j_d v = j_e z\), then at \(D = \max (d, e)\) its representatives are equal. Applying \(\delta_{D, \rho}\) and the compatibility just proved gives \(j_d \delta_{d, \rho} v = j_e \delta_{e, \rho} z\), which is the representative independence needed for this formula. For any \(u \in E\) choose \(u = j_d v\). Then

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{J}_{\rho}^2 u =\mathcal{J}_{\rho} j_d \delta_{d, \rho} v = j_d \delta_{d, \rho}^2 v = j_d v = u.\)

Hence \(\mathcal{J}_{\rho}\) is its own inverse. The same computation within a fixed grade shows that its image of \(j_d (E_d)\) is all of that level subspace. Any competing conjugate-linear map with the raw restriction formula agrees on every \(j_d (E_d)\), since \(\pi_d\) is onto, and therefore agrees on their union. This proves the stated uniqueness.\(\Box\)

For a second table \(T\), comparison of adjoints requires the same raw conjugation and signed actions, its own reality and covariance receipts (5.38), and compatible inclusion maps for both tables as in (5.39). Assume also \(N_d^S \subseteq N_d^T\) and the same-\(K_{de}\) correlation identities required by the earlier two-table comparison. The resulting maps \(q_d : E_d^S \to E_d^T\) then satisfy, on every raw class,

\(\displaystyle q_d \delta^S_{d, \rho} \pi_d^S a = \pi_d^T a^{\dagger_{\rho}} = \delta^T_{d, \rho} q_d \pi_d^S a.\)

They therefore intertwine the grade adjoints. If \(q : E^S \to E^T\) is the induced union map, the defining identities for \(q\) and \(\mathcal{J}_{\rho}\) give \(q\mathcal{J}^S_{\rho} j_d^S =\mathcal{J}^T_{\rho} qj_d^S\) at every grade, hence \(q\mathcal{J}^S_{\rho} =\mathcal{J}^T_{\rho} q\) on the union. If the null spaces are equal, \(q\) is the earlier union isomorphism and this identity also intertwines its inverse. This applies to a character table only when the stated common raw actions and its covariance and orientation receipts are supplied. Original-null containment alone does not assert them. Nor does this algebraic construction prove compatibility with derivatives, a calibrated reference identification, continuous rotation or Lorentz actions, or any Ward identity.

5.8.4Constructing the field derivatives from finite differences

Proposition 5.34. (Derivative representatives on the same table) Retain the finite-volume family, calibrations, ordered extraction and smooth mixed table of paragraph 5.8.2, with the insertion and difference comparisons of Subsection 16.11. There are linear maps

\(\displaystyle \partial_{\alpha} : E_d \longrightarrow E_{d + 1}, \qquad \alpha = 1, \ldots, 4,\)

compatible with the grade inclusions, such that insertion of \(\partial_{\alpha} A\) in any slot differentiates the correlation in that slot's \(\alpha\) coordinate. They commute with one another, kill the unit, and commute with conjugation. Under a signed orthogonal coordinate symmetry \(g\) they obey \(\rho (g) \partial_{\alpha} = \sum_{\beta} g_{\beta \alpha} \partial_{\beta} \rho (g)\). These identities hold in the common field union and in every whole-word character correlation. In particular they supply (5.21) with any chosen raw representatives.

Proof. Fix a raw label \(v\) at grade \(d\) and a coordinate \(\alpha\). Apply the finite-difference comparison to

\(\displaystyle Z_c (o) = a_N^{- 1} \{O_c (v ; o + e_{\alpha}) - O_c (v ; o)\}, \qquad c = (N, p) .\)

It supplies one comparison label \(u_c \in A_{d + 1}\), independent of spectators and tests, with subpower inverse terminal coordinates and vanishing tested discrepancy. We identify its tested limit explicitly. For a smooth compactly supported separated joint test \(f\), the lattice sum for \(Z_c\) can be reindexed in its distinguished slot. On its compact support, the mesh is eventually smaller than the separation margin; at every suitable fixed period there is also a positive margin to the boundary of the chosen coordinate box. The reindexing is therefore legitimate (or equivalently is reindexing of the periodic sum), and gives

\(\displaystyle a_N^{4 n} \sum_{\boldsymbol{x}} f (\boldsymbol{x}) \mathbb{E} [Z_c (x_i) \prod_{j \ne i} O_c (v_j ; x_j)] = a_N^{4 n} \sum_{\boldsymbol{x}} \frac{f (\boldsymbol{x}- a_N e_{i, \alpha}) - f (\boldsymbol{x})}{a_N} \mathbb{E} [\prod_j O_c (v_j ; x_j)],\)

where \(v_i = v\). The test on the right converges in every smooth norm, on one fixed compact separated set, to \(- \partial_{i, \alpha} f\). The zeroth-order compact-uniform bounds of Theorem 4.28 bound the absolute lattice pairing on that set by a constant times the supremum norm of its test: the mesh weight times the number of sites there is bounded, and the correlations are uniformly bounded there on the relevant late tail. Consequently replacing that varying test by its limit changes the pairing by a quantity tending to zero. First take the mesh limit at each suitable period and then the retained volume limit. The resulting functional is

\(\displaystyle M_{\theta} = - \int (\partial_{i, \alpha} f) (x) S_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx = \int f (x) \partial_{i, \alpha} S_{\boldsymbol{v}} (x) \hspace{0.17em} dx.\)

The last equality is ordinary integration by parts on the open separated configuration domain; the support is compactly contained in it. This argument does not interchange mesh and volume limits or require a mesh threshold uniform in the period.

Use the same finite and limiting grade-\((d + 1)\) projectors as in paragraph 5.8.2. Their exact all-test kernel identity and the local equation estimate apply to \((u_c - P_c u_c) / (1 +\|P_c u_c \|)\): the first summand has subpower inverse terminal norm by the difference relay, and the second has bounded reference norm and hence subpower inverse terminal norm. Thus the two limits in (7.32) hold with the \(M_{\theta}\) just computed. That compactness argument supplies a single \(w \in \text{ran} \hspace{0.17em} P_{d + 1}\) with \(f_{\theta} (w) = M_{\theta}\) for all spectator tuples and separated tests. It does not select a different label for each test. Smoothness of both correlation kernels turns this distributional identity into their pointwise equality. Define \(\partial_{\alpha} [v]_d = [w]_{d + 1}\).

Any two such representatives have zero correlations against all spectators, and hence differ by the defining null space. In particular, any derivative plan on this same table satisfying the all-spectator slot identity agrees with these maps on the quotient; previously retained derivative fields are thereby identified, not independently reselected. If \(v\) is null, all its correlations vanish identically, so their derivatives vanish and \(w\) is null. This proves well-definedness on the quotient. Linearity follows by differentiating the linear correlation identities and invoking the same null-space criterion. For two representatives of the same field at different grades, the inclusion slot identity (5.36), differentiated in that slot, shows that the resulting derivatives agree after inclusion. Thus the maps define operators on the common union.

Equality of mixed partial derivatives of the smooth table, followed again by the all-spectator null criterion, gives \(\partial_{\alpha} \partial_{\beta} = \partial_{\beta} \partial_{\alpha}\) in \(E_{d + 2}\).
