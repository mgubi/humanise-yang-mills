\section{Regularity and bounded insertions in the component theories}
\label{ex-phase-regularity}

Several hypotheses in the selection theorem can now be supplied from the
unselected operator estimates. Assume in this section that
\eqref{ex-all-copy-bound} and \eqref{ex-raw-slot-identity} hold for the
unselected theory, and that its correlations are multilinear and
permutation symmetric. By Corollary~\ref{ex-norm-separable}, the
representing probability of Proposition~\ref{ex-character-measure}
is then available.

\begin{proposition}[Smoothness and caps in every character]
\label{ex-phase-caps}
The whole-word functions \(S_\chi\) are multilinear in the raw labels,
permutation symmetric, and smooth on distinct-point configurations.
They obey the slot derivative identity \eqref{ex-raw-slot-identity}.
For every grade bound \(D\) and label norm bound \(R\), the constants
\(C,K\) in \eqref{ex-all-copy-bound} also give
\begin{equation}\label{ex-phase-zeroth-cap}
 |S_\chi(w)|\le C^n(n/r)^{Kn}
\end{equation}
for all \(\chi\), all words with these label bounds, and
\(0<r\le1\) with pairwise sup-norm separations at least \(r\).
For every fixed label tuple and derivative order \(q\), there are
\(C_q\ge0\) and \(A_q\in\mathbb N\), independent of \(\chi\)
and the positions, such that
\[
 \|D^qS_{\chi,\boldsymbol v}(x)\|\le C_qr^{-A_q}
\]
on the same separated configurations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multilinearity of slab operators was proved in the norm-separability
argument. Their permutation symmetry follows by the same dense
matrix-element test, using scalar permutation symmetry. Compression and
the linear character preserve both identities. For local smoothness,
fix a distinct configuration, translate it into a slab, and keep the
translation and width fixed in a small neighborhood. Proposition
\ref{ex-smooth-slabs} gives smoothness of the operator-valued map and
its slot derivative formula. Compression is bounded linear, and the
character is bounded linear on \(\mathcal A\); their composition gives
the same statements for \(S_\chi\). Equivalently one may extend the
character to the ambient operator space by Hahn--Banach before composing;
the extension agrees on every compressed word and its derivatives.

To obtain \eqref{ex-phase-zeroth-cap}, translate a given word into a
slab with one unit of padding at each time boundary. Translation leaves
all pairwise separations unchanged, and the boundary padding is at least
\(r\). Contractivity of characters and of orthogonal compression gives
\[
 |S_\chi(w)|=|\chi(M(w))|\le\|M(w)\|\le\|A_d(w)\|
 \le C^n(n/r)^{Kn}.
\]
For the empty word use normalization. For nonzero arity, iterate the slot
derivative formula \(q\) times. There are finitely many resulting label
tuples, one for each sequence of \(q\) coordinate directions. Their
grades and raw-label norms have finite maxima, so apply
\eqref{ex-phase-zeroth-cap} with these maxima. Expand the real
\(q\)-linear derivative in the standard coordinate basis of
\(\mathbb R^{4n}\). On unit arguments every coordinate coefficient
has absolute value at most one, so its operator norm is bounded by the
sum of the absolute values of its at most \((4n)^q\) entries.
This yields the claimed constants and the integer exponent \(A_q=Kn\)
for the enlarged label bounds. At arity zero the function is constant
one, with all positive-order derivatives zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Original null relations hold in every character]
\label{ex-phase-original-null}
Fix a grade $d$. Let $N_d$ be the space of raw labels $v$ of grade $d$
whose unselected separated correlations vanish whenever $v$ is inserted,
with arbitrary finitely many spectator labels of arbitrary grades.
Define $N_{\chi,d}$ by the same condition for $S_\chi$. Then
\[
                         N_d\subseteq N_{\chi,d}
                         \qquad\hbox{for every }\chi\in\Xi.
\]
Consequently every phase correlation factors, in each slot, through the
unselected field quotient at that grade.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $w$ be any distinct-point word containing a label $v\in N_d$.
Translate the whole word into a slab of width $a>0$, and denote its
bounded insertion operator on the unselected Euclidean Hilbert space
by $A_a(w)$. For positive-time word vectors $[u]$ and $[z]$, the
matrix-element identity expresses
\[
                  \langle[u],A_a(w)[z]\rangle
\]
as an unselected correlation with the translated word in the middle,
a reflected copy of $u$ below the slab and a shifted copy of $z$
above it. All points are distinct: each exterior word is internally
distinct, and the three groups have disjoint time ranges. This is an
allowed all-spectator test of the label $v$, so the matrix element is
zero. By linearity it is zero on the spans of positive-time word
vectors. Those spans are dense, and $A_a(w)$ is bounded; hence all
its matrix elements vanish and $A_a(w)=0$.

Its compression to the ground subspace is therefore zero. Placement
independence identifies this compression with $M(w)$, and evaluating
any character gives $S_\chi(w)=\chi(M(w))=0$. Permutation symmetry
allows the null label in any slot. This proves the inclusion for every
character, without discarding a set of measure zero. Finally, if two
raw labels represent the same unselected class, their difference is
in $N_d$; multilinearity shows that replacing one by the other leaves
every phase correlation unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Phase Hilbert spaces and selection]\label{ex-phase-dynamics}
Every character table has the Hilbert space and strongly continuous
contraction semigroup of Section~\ref{ex-reflection}. The common
countable family in Lemma~\ref{ex-phase-horizon} exists. Consequently
Theorem~\ref{ex-pure-ground} applies, giving a phase whose time-invariant
subspace is exactly its vacuum line.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Normalization, translation invariance and time reflection positivity
were proved after \eqref{ex-phase-word}. The adjoint identity
\(M(\theta w)=M(w)^*\) gives
\(S_\chi(\theta w)=\overline{S_\chi(w)}\). For a word in arbitrary
position, this operator identity follows by translating it into a slab,
using its opposite word there and the slab adjoint identity, and then
using placement independence of \(M\). Proposition~\ref{ex-phase-caps}
supplies permutation symmetry, continuity and the separated zeroth-order
bound. Lemma~\ref{ex-cap-proof} gives the boundedness under separating
translations required in \eqref{ex-cap}. All hypotheses of the
Hilbert-space and contraction construction are therefore met for each
\(S_\chi\). Multilinearity, finite-dimensional raw-label spaces and
continuity give the common countable dense span by
Lemma~\ref{ex-phase-horizon}. The selection theorem now applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Smeared reflection positivity]\label{ex-phase-smeared-rp}
For any correlation table with continuous distinct-point correlations
and point reflection positivity, the reflection form is nonnegative
on smooth compactly supported positive-time configuration tests.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one fixed label tuple, the map \(x\mapsto[w(x)]\) into the GNS
space is norm-continuous on positive-time distinct-point configurations.
Indeed its squared norm difference is a finite combination of continuous
Gram entries, as in Lemma~\ref{ex-phase-horizon}. On the compact support
of a test \(\varphi\), it is bounded and its image is a separable
compact set. Hence the compactly supported continuous vector function
\(\varphi(x)[w(x)]\) is Bochner integrable. Denote its integral by
\(\Psi_\varphi\). For two such tests, continuity of the inner product
and Fubini give
\[
 \langle\Psi_\varphi,\Psi_\psi\rangle
 =\iint\overline{\varphi(x)}\psi(y)
      S((\theta w(x))w(y))\,dx\,dy.
\]
The scalar integrand is absolutely integrable: on the product of the
two compact supports the two reflected halves have a positive time gap,
each half has internally distinct points, and the continuous kernel is
bounded. Different arities and labels cause no change to this argument;
each pair has its own compact product support. The empty word uses the
one-point zero-dimensional configuration space. A finite combination of
tests has reflection form \(\|\sum_i c_i\Psi_{\varphi_i}\|^2\ge0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Slab bounds survive character selection]
\label{ex-phase-slab-bounds}
Let \(A_{\chi,d}(w)\) denote insertion in the Hilbert space of a
character table. Such an operator exists for every slab word, and
\[
 \|A_{\chi,d}(w)\|\le\|A_d(w)\|.
\]
In particular the uniform bound \eqref{ex-all-copy-bound} holds in every
phase with the same constants. Its slab operators are smooth in operator
norm and obey the same slot derivative identities.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(a=A_d(w)\) in the unselected Hilbert space and put
\(c=\|a\|\). For a positive word \(v\), let
\(v_n=(I_{w^\sharp}I_w)^nv\). The maps
\(X(v):\mathcal H_0\to\mathcal H\) from Section~\ref{ex-phases}
satisfy
\[
 X(I_wv)=aX(v),\qquad X(v_n)=(a^*a)^nX(v).
\]
The first equality is the slab concatenation law restricted to
\(\mathcal H_0\); the second follows by induction and the adjoint
law. Therefore \(\|X(v_n)\|\le c^{2n}\|X(v)\|\).
For any \(D>c\), the character Gram identity gives
\[
 \|[v_n]_\chi\|^2
 =\chi\bigl(M((\theta v_n)v_n)\bigr)
 =\chi\bigl(X(v_n)^*X(v_n)\bigr)
 \le\|X(v)\|^2D^{4n}.
\]
Here \(X(v_n)^*X(v_n)\in\mathcal A\) by its equality with the
compressed whole-word operator, and the character bound applies in
that algebra. Lemma~\ref{ex-train-bound} produces a phase insertion
operator with norm at most \(D\). These operators are all identical:
their action on every positive word is prescribed, and the word span is
dense. Taking the infimum over \(D>c\) proves the bound \(c\), also
when \(c=0\). The unselected uniform estimate therefore transfers.
The character slot identities were proved in Proposition~\ref{ex-phase-caps};
applying Proposition~\ref{ex-smooth-slabs} inside the phase Hilbert space
proves its operator smoothness and derivative formulas.
\end{proof}

These results construct the time-axis Euclidean component theories from
the unselected slab and differential estimates. Full orthogonal covariance
of each character, and ordinary conjugation separately from reflected
conjugation, still require the reflection-transport argument. Equality of
null spaces requires the detector inputs in Section~\ref{ex-phase-fields}.
