1The construction problem and the order of its limits

The aim is to construct a relativistic field theory from finite-dimensional Wilson integrals. There are two limiting operations. First the lattice spacing tends to zero while the physical period stays fixed. Then the period tends to infinity. The observables and bare action coefficients must be chosen coherently before either operation: changing the family in response to a requested sequence of periods would give a weaker statement.

A caution about the target of this section.
The public target formulated after Subsection 1.1 is one statement that the source verifies by machine. Read on its own, it is weaker than a reader might expect from the words “existence of four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory”. It can be satisfied by a generalized free scalar field, that is, a quantum field whose vacuum correlations are determined by its two-point function through sums over pairings, here with a continuous distribution of masses; and the lattice observables that the target asks for can then be chosen independently of the gauge configuration. Subsection 1.3 states this precisely and explains which features of the target allow it. The target alone therefore does not express the specifically Yang–Mills content of the construction. That content lies in how the later sections reach the target: they work with specific local gauge-invariant functions of the Wilson links, and every limiting object is obtained from the Wilson measure. The observation does not affect that construction. It does mean that a main theorem intended to express this content should also record the chosen lattice observables and their connections to the limiting theory. The source has such a detailed statement, and the group reviewing the source bases its assessment of these connections on a stronger target of this kind (Subsection 1.3). Subsection 1.1 proposes a stronger headline retaining these connections, with references to the detailed construction and an explicit account of the remaining assembly.

We assume familiarity with finite-dimensional Lie groups, probability Haar measure on compact groups, Lebesgue integration and dominated convergence, and elementary Hilbert-space theory. The specific compactness, quotient and operator arguments used below are supplied where they enter the construction.

Fix a closed subgroup \(G \subset U (r_G)\), with its specified faithful matrix representation. Its real Lie algebra is

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{g}= \{X \in M_{r_G} (\mathbb{C}) : \exp (tX) \in G \text{ for every } t \in \mathbb{R}\} .\)

We assume that \(\mathfrak{g}\) is nonabelian and simple: its only real subspaces invariant under every \(\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} X\), \(X \in \mathfrak{g}\), are \(0\) and \(\mathfrak{g}\). The group \(G\) is compact, being closed in \(U (r_G)\). It is not assumed to be connected, and the given representation is not assumed to be irreducible. The Wilson action of Subsection 3.1 is normalized by the dimension \(r_G\) of this representation, so the same abstract group in two representations gives two instances of the problem. Coordinates on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) are written \(x = (\boldsymbol{x}, t)\), with time last. The Minkowski form is \(t^2 - |\boldsymbol{x}|^2\), and the closed forward cone is \(\bar{V}_+ = \{(\boldsymbol{p}, E) : |\boldsymbol{p}| \leqslant E\}\).

1.1A construction-bound main theorem

The following theorem retains the connection between classical observables, lattice representatives and quantum fields. Its normalizations and local statements are specified below. Theorem 19.7 assembles their proofs on one retained Wilson family and one limiting hierarchy.

Let \(V_d\) be the finite-dimensional space of gauge-invariant polynomials in radial curvature jets of weighted degree at most \(d\), a curvature derivative of order \(j\) having weight \(j + 2\); see Sections 7.9 and 7.10. Fix a real polynomial basis, homogeneous in the number of curvature factors, and use its complexification as coordinates on \(V_d\), with the constant polynomial as the unit coordinate. A local section \(L_d\) assigns to \(v \in V_d\) a polynomial in the entries of finitely many links and their conjugates, linearly in \(v\), invariant under lattice gauge transformations. Its stencil is independent of spacing and period. It preserves the unit, conjugation and signed coordinate symmetries. For every radial jet \(J\) it satisfies the normalization

\(\displaystyle \left. \frac{d^k}{dh^k} L_d (v ; H (J, h)) \right|_{h = 0} = \left. \frac{d^k}{dh^k} v (h \cdot J) \right|_{h = 0}, \qquad 0 \leqslant k \leqslant d.\) (1.1)

Here \(H (J, h)\) is parallel transport along lattice links for the polynomial radial connection at scale \(h\), and \(h \cdot J\) multiplies the weight-\(j + 2\) component by \(h^{j + 2}\). Thus small-field testing recovers the prescribed classical polynomial.

Write \(a_N = D_N^{- 1}\) for the lattice spacing, \(P_p\) for the physical period, and \(U\) for a link configuration on the corresponding torus. For each \(d\) choose a reference level \(r_d\). At cutoff \((N, p)\), \(N \geqslant r_d\), the renormalized insertion has the form

\(\displaystyle I^{(d)}_{N, p} (v ; x ; U) = a_N^{- d} L_d (D_{\theta_N^{- 1}} R_{d, N, p}^{- 1} v ; x ; U) .\) (1.2)

Here \(\theta_N > 0\) is the small-field threshold and \(D_t\) multiplies a coordinate with \(s\) curvature factors by \(t^s\). The invertible linear map \(R_{d, N, p}\) is fixed by transporting the inserted Wilson density from level \(N\) to \(r_d\) and reading its polynomial coefficient there. This is exact transport of the unnormalized inserted density by the same block integrations as the uninserted density, with the scale factors and remainder bounds of Sections 7.9, 13.7 and 13.8. In particular, transporting \(R_{d, N, p}^{- 1} v\) gives coefficient \(v\) at the reference level. The maps in (1.2) must have this transport and normalization; freely chosen renormalization matrices are not an alternative definition. The same maps are used at every translated anchor. Choices at the finitely many levels \(N < r_d\) do not affect the limits.

Remark 1.1. (The local normalization has content) If \(L_d (v)\) is constant, with value \(c\), then (1.1) says that the polynomial \(h \mapsto v (h \cdot J)\) has constant term \(c\) and all other coefficients zero: its degree is at most \(d\). Taking \(h = 1\) gives \(v (J) = c\) for every \(J\). In particular \(L_d\) is injective, and a nonconstant curvature polynomial cannot have a constant local representative. Since both \(D_t\) for \(t > 0\) and \(R_{d, N, p}\) are invertible, the insertion space in (1.2) contains nonconstant functions whenever \(V_d\) contains nonconstant polynomials. On sufficiently large tori the stencil does not wrap, so its link assignments extend to torus configurations and this remains true there. This rules out the specific replacement of all lattice insertions by constants. It does not by itself prove that the continuum field space contains a field independent of the unit, or that its correlations are non-Gaussian.

Theorem 1.2. (The Wilson construction) For every compact matrix group \(G\) with nonabelian simple Lie algebra as specified above, and every positive blocking penalty \(b\), one can choose one sequence of lattice spacings \(a_N \downarrow 0\), one increasing sequence of physical periods \(P_p \uparrow \infty\), bare Wilson coefficients \(\beta_{N, p}\), and local observables (1.2) satisfying (1.1), all on a single Wilson construction. The coefficients satisfy (1.4). These choices precede any choice of a subsequence of periods.

For every pair of strictly increasing index maps \((u, p)\) there is a refinement \((u \circ v, p \circ q)\) and one common system of limiting fields, correlations and phases with the following properties.

  1. Wilson limits and field labels. The physical fields of degree at most \(d\) are quotients \(\mathcal{F}_d = V_d /\mathcal{N}_d\), where \(\mathcal{N}_d\) consists of labels whose insertion vanishes in all separated mixed correlations with arbitrary spectators. They have compatible injective grade inclusions, a unit, conjugation, derivatives and spacetime symmetry actions. Write \([v]_d\) for the field represented by \(v \in V_d\). At grade \(d\) the unit is represented by \(a_{r_d}^d \boldsymbol{1}\), not in general by the constant polynomial \(\boldsymbol{1}\). The grade inclusions are the compatible maps constructed in Subsection 5.8; they need not send the class of a polynomial to the class of the same polynomial at a larger grade. For every finite list of graded labels \(v_i \in V_{d_i}\), \(1 \leqslant i \leqslant n\), and every smooth compactly supported test \(f\) away from the coincidence diagonals,

    \(\displaystyle \ \begin{array}{l} \lim_{k \to \infty} \lim_{N \to \infty} a_N^{4 n} \hspace{-0.17em} \sum_{\boldsymbol{x}} f (\boldsymbol{x}) \mathbb{E}_{\mu_{N, p (q (k))}} \hspace{-0.17em} \left[ \prod_{i = 1}^n I^{(d_i)}_{N, p (q (k))} (v_i ; x_i ; U) \right]\\ = \int f (\boldsymbol{x}) S^{\text{mix}}_{[v_1]_{d_1}, \ldots, [v_n]_{d_n}} (\boldsymbol{x}) \hspace{0.17em} d\boldsymbol{x}. \end{array}\) (1.3)

    Here \(\mu_{N, p}\) is the Wilson probability measure of Subsection 3.1, and the sum uses the physical lattice points in its fundamental box, with the convention of (1.5). The inner limit exists along the full mesh sequence at every suitable fixed period. One refinement works for all labels, arities and tests. Auxiliary field comparisons may use the refined mesh \(u \circ v\); the Wilson limit displayed here does not.

  2. Phases of that same limit. There is a compact metrizable space \(X\) with a full-support probability measure \(\nu\) and smooth separated correlations \(S^{\chi}\), multilinear in the fields, such that

    \(\displaystyle S^{\text{mix}}_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{x}) = \int_X S^{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{x}) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\chi) .\)

    These are the characters of the commutative algebra obtained by compressing the mixed theory's slab observables to its zero-energy space; \(\nu\) represents its vacuum state. Thus the phase space and component correlations come from the mixed Wilson limit, as in Sections 5.2 and 5.5. Every component has reflection positivity and the same field null relations as the mixed hierarchy; see Sections 5.6 and 5.8.

  3. Relativistic reconstruction. For every \(\chi \in X\), these correlations reconstruct a nontrivial local relativistic field theory on a Hilbert space with a common dense invariant domain, cyclic vacuum, adjoints, tempered fields, strongly continuous Poincaré symmetry and translation spectrum in the closed forward cone. The translation-invariant vectors form precisely the vacuum line, and nonzero field labels act nontrivially. Its vacuum distributions are the analytic boundary values of \(S^{\chi}\), in the sense of Definition 1.3 with \(\chi\) in place of \(\chi_{\ast}\). The boundary distributions also obey the phase-mixture identity on joint Schwartz tests. The analytic and field constructions are described in Theorem 6.5 and Subsection 6.8. There is a character \(\chi_{\ast} \in X\), with one-dimensional ground space as in Theorem 5.16, for which all the conclusions below hold; they use these same data.

  4. Local products and the physical stress tensor. There is one operator-product expansion for the mixed state and every component, with the coefficient and remainder bounds (19.30)–(19.31). The physical field system contains a symmetric conserved stress tensor \(T_{\mu \nu}\) whose translation and rotation charges implement the field derivatives and rotation action. Its normalization is that of Subsection 17.3; its separated Ward identities are those of Subsection 17.9. In the selected theory set

    \(\displaystyle \Theta = \sum_{\mu = 1}^4 T_{\mu \mu} - \left\langle \sum_{\mu = 1}^4 T_{\mu \mu} \right\rangle_{\chi_{\ast}} \boldsymbol{1}.\)

    This very field, rather than a separately chosen grade-four field, satisfies

    \(\displaystyle \lim_{r \downarrow 0} r^8 \log^2 (r^{- 1}) S^{\chi_{\ast}}_{\Theta, \Theta} (- re_t / 2, re_t / 2) = \frac{3 \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}}{\pi^4}, \qquad e_t = (0, 0, 0, 1) .\)

    Its vacuum one-point functional vanishes and its two-point function has the finite mass-shell-profile exclusion of Subsection 18.19. The comparison and limit use this same normalized stress tensor; see Lemma 18.41 and Theorem 18.52.

The quotients, phases and reconstructed theories may depend on the requested order and refinement. The lattice family does not.

Scope.
This formulation selects conclusions of the construction; it does not restate every source result. Contact extensions, nested operator products, effective couplings and Maxwell scaling comparisons require separate precise statements and are not asserted here. The running slope in (1.4) is \(11 C_{\mathfrak{g}} / (24 \pi^2)\), with the Casimir for the normalized trace inner product. Proposition 12.4 and (19.29) identify this value with the bare coefficients of this very Wilson family.

No uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit or independence of the spacing sequence, local sections or blocking scheme is claimed. The result constructs correlations and fields, not a probability measure on continuum connections, Wilson-loop observables, particle states or a mass gap. The operator-product bounds do not assert agreement with a perturbative series, and the trace test does not exclude every generalized free-field description. These distinctions matter in comparing the result with the Yang–Mills existence and mass-gap problem; see [Yan26, Sections 1–2, 7–9]. In that comparison, the axiomatic field theory and its curvature-polynomial representatives address the first two requirements. The stress tensor, running law, operator-product bounds and trace asymptotic give specific short-distance conclusions, but not the full agreement with perturbative renormalization theory requested in the third requirement. The fourth requirement, a positive spectral gap above the vacuum, remains outside the result. The inverse reference maps are constructed with quantitative bounds; explicit formulas for all their matrix entries are not part of the assertion.

Forgetting the normalization, the character-algebra construction, the reconstructions away from \(\chi_{\ast}\) and the stress-tensor identification gives, together with the unit, covering and token compatibility specified below and proved in the field construction, the weaker public target. Its insufficiency is discussed in Subsection 1.3. Theorem 19.7 proves the stronger same-data assertion: normalized insertions, field quotients, phase algebra, every-character reconstruction and physical stress all use the same extraction. Proposition 19.6 records the logical projection to the weaker target. In particular the every-character assertion in item 3 is supplied by its own vacuum argument, whereas the trace asymptotic in item 4 concerns the distinguished selected character.

1.2The weaker public target

The lattice family consists of strictly increasing positive integer sequences \(P_p\) and \(D_N\), a spacing \(a_N = D_N^{- 1}\), real coefficients \(\beta_{N, p}\), and local gauge-invariant insertion functions \(O_{N, p, v} (x ; U)\) indexed by tokens \(v\). The periodic lattice has \(P_p D_N\) sites along each axis and physical period \(P_p\). Subsection 3.1 describes its Wilson probability measure and moments. We call the family regular when its insertions are continuous in \(U\), invariant under every lattice gauge transformation, and uniformly local in the following precise sense. For each token \(v\) there is a finite stencil \(\Sigma_v \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\), independent of \(N, p, x\), such that two configurations agreeing on all links with both endpoints in \(x + \Sigma_v\), reduced modulo \(P_p D_N\), give the same insertion value. The fourth regularity condition is

\(\displaystyle \exists \sigma > 0 \quad \forall \varepsilon > 0 \quad \exists N_0 \quad \forall N \geqslant N_0 \quad \forall p : \qquad \left| \frac{\beta_{N, p}}{\log (a_N^{- 1})} - \sigma \right| < \varepsilon .\) (1.4)

In particular, the choice of \(N_0\) is uniform in the period index.

Let \(\text{Conf}_n (\mathbb{R}^4)\) be the set of ordered \(n\)-tuples of pairwise distinct points. A separated test is a smooth complex function with compact support in this set, extended by zero outside it. For insertion tokens \(\boldsymbol{v}= (v_1, \ldots, v_n)\), write \(M_{N, p} (\boldsymbol{v}; \boldsymbol{x})\) for their Wilson moment and put

\(\displaystyle W_{N, p} (\boldsymbol{v}; f) = a_N^{4 n} \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \Lambda_{N, p}^n} f (a_N \boldsymbol{x}) M_{N, p} (\boldsymbol{v}; \boldsymbol{x}) .\) (1.5)

Here the coordinates of \(\boldsymbol{x}\) are chosen in the centered fundamental cell. A period is suitable for \(f\) if its support lies strictly inside that physical cell in every coordinate.

The limiting data have several related components. For every integer grade \(d \geqslant 0\) there is a finite-dimensional complex space \(\mathcal{F}_d\). It has a complex-linear injection into a common field-label space; every label is represented at some grade. Tokens map onto the disjoint union \(\coprod_d \mathcal{F}_d\). A compact metric space \(X\), a probability measure \(\nu\) of full support, and a selected character \(\chi_{\ast} \in X\) describe the phases. For graded fields \(\boldsymbol{A}\), let \(S^{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{x})\) and \(S^{\text{mix}}_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{x})\) denote the phase and mixed separated correlations. They are smooth on configuration space, permutation symmetric, and continuous in \(\chi\), with

\(\displaystyle S^{\text{mix}}_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{x}) = \int_X S^{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{x}) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\chi) .\) (1.6)

The mixed correlations are the Wilson limits. The selected correlations are the Euclidean correlations to be continued into the reconstructed theory. These two roles should be kept distinct even when the phase space has only one point. At the level of this target, only the selected point is required to carry a reconstructed relativistic theory. The other points are required to satisfy the displayed correlation properties and the common operator-product estimates. Multilinearity of all these correlations in the graded fields is not an additional condition of the target.

Definition 1.3. (Reconstructed selected theory) The selected theory consists of a complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\), a normalized vacuum \(\Omega\), a common dense invariant domain \(\mathcal{D}\) containing \(\Omega\), and complex-linear fields \(\Phi (A, f) : \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{D}\), linear separately in the label and test, where \(f \in \mathcal{S} (\mathbb{R}^4)\). For fixed \(A, \psi\), the map \(f \mapsto \Phi (A, f) \psi\) is continuous in the Schwartz topology. The domain is the span of field words applied to the vacuum. There is a conjugate-linear label adjoint \(A \mapsto A^{\ast}\) with

\(\displaystyle \langle \Phi (A, f) \psi, \eta \rangle = \langle \psi, \Phi (A^{\ast}, \bar{f}) \eta \rangle .\)

A strongly continuous unitary representation of the proper orthochronous Poincaré group preserves the domain and the vacuum, and transforms fields with the specified action on labels and the ordinary affine pullback of tests. Its translation spectral measure is supported in \(\bar{V}_+\), and every translation-invariant vector is a scalar multiple of \(\Omega\). Fields commute on \(\mathcal{D}\) when their test supports are spacelike separated. Every nonzero label acts nontrivially for some test, and at least one smeared field is nonzero.

For fixed spatial positions the selected correlations have a holomorphic continuation \(F_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{z}; \boldsymbol{x})\) on \(\text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} z_1 < \cdots < \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} z_n\). At strictly ordered Euclidean times its value at \(z_i = it_i\) is \(S^{\chi_{\ast}}_{\boldsymbol{A}} ((\boldsymbol{x}_i, t_i)_i)\). For every strictly increasing real height vector \(\boldsymbol{h}\) and all \(f_1, \ldots, f_n \in \mathcal{S} (\mathbb{R}^4)\),

\(\displaystyle \ \begin{aligned} \lim_{\epsilon \downarrow 0} \int_{(\mathbb{R}^4)^n} & F_{\boldsymbol{A}} ((t_j + i \epsilon h_j)_j ; (\boldsymbol{x}_j)_j) \prod_{j = 1}^n f_j (\boldsymbol{x}_j, t_j) \hspace{0.17em} d\boldsymbol{x} \hspace{0.17em} d\boldsymbol{t}\\ & = \langle \Omega, \Phi (A_1, f_1) \cdots \Phi (A_n, f_n) \Omega \rangle . \end{aligned}\) (1.7)

In this target clause the integral has value zero when its integrand is not Lebesgue integrable. Thus the clause prescribes a boundary limit without separately requiring absolute integrability at positive height. When ordinary integrals are used in the construction, their integrability must be supplied by the analytic estimates, as in Lemma 6.4 and Theorem 6.5.

The existence target is one regular Wilson family such that, for every strictly increasing period-index sequence \(p (k)\), a strictly increasing \(q (k)\) and one hierarchy with the preceding properties can be found. For each separated test there are row limits \(w_p (\boldsymbol{v}; f)\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} \lim_{N \to \infty} W_{N, p} (\boldsymbol{v}; f) & = w_p (\boldsymbol{v}; f) & & \text{at each suitable fixed } p, \hspace{3cm} \text{(1.8)} \\ \lim_{k \to \infty} w_{p (q (k))} (\boldsymbol{v}; f) & = \int f (\boldsymbol{x}) S^{\text{mix}}_{\boldsymbol{A}} (\boldsymbol{x}) \hspace{0.17em} d\boldsymbol{x}, & & \hspace{3cm} \text{(1.9)} \end{array}\)

where \(A_i\) is the class of \(v_i\). Suitability holds eventually along the selected period sequence. The same \(q\) and hierarchy work for all arities, tokens and tests. The hierarchy may depend on the requested sequence; the Wilson family may not.

The full endpoint also specifies a common operator-product expansion and a short-distance test on one centered field of grade four, described in Subsection 19.6. These are additional obligations on the same hierarchy. The scope of the target should be read literally. The spectral conclusions are that the energy-momentum spectrum lies in the closed forward cone and that the translation-invariant vectors form the line \(\mathbb{C} \Omega\); no gap above the vacuum in the spectrum is asserted. The short-distance test prescribes the leading behaviour \(r^{- 8} \log^{- 2} (r^{- 1})\) of one two-point function on the time axis and, as a consequence, excludes its coincidence near \(r = 0\) with any finite combination of the mass-shell profiles of Subsection 18.19; it does not exclude infinite or continuous superpositions of masses and makes no assertion about truncated correlations of higher order. The target requires a positive limit of \(\beta_{N, p} / \log (a_N^{- 1})\) but does not prescribe its value. The grade-four field is existentially chosen, subject to the stated test (vanishing vacuum one-point functional, the exact asymptotic coefficient, and the profile exclusion); the target contains no stress-tensor or Ward-identity normalization identifying it physically, and that identification belongs to the detailed construction. More generally the target concerns a theory of the constructed gauge-invariant observables: the insertions \(O_{N, p, v}\) are part of what is constructed, no lattice formula is prescribed for them beyond regularity and the joint convergence requirements, and the lattice measure enters the target only through the mixed correlations. Identifying a particular label with a separately prescribed physical observable requires its own construction argument. The detailed construction is parameterized by a positive blocking penalty; the target is obtained from the value \(1\) in the conditional assembly of Proposition 19.6. No uniqueness assertion is made.

Remark 1.4. (What the target alone ties to the Wilson measure) The Wilson measure enters the target only through (1.8)–(1.9), that is, through the mixed correlations. These are tied to the selected correlations only by the mixture identity (1.6) and by the common operator-product estimates. The target does not require the correlations to be multilinear in the graded fields, nor the correlations at a non-selected phase to be reflection positive. Consequently the public clauses alone do not force the selected theory to arise from the Wilson measure: they admit Wilson families whose insertions do not depend on the gauge configuration.

To see this, suppose hierarchy data are given that satisfy every requirement of the target except the two convergence statements, with selected correlations \(S = S^{\chi_{\ast}}\), and suppose that some \(u \in \mathcal{F}_0\), \(u \ne 0\), enters \(S\) homogeneously: replacing the tag \((0, u)\) by \((0, 2 u)\) in any one slot doubles \(S\). Put \(m (A) = 1\) if \(A = (0, u)\) and \(m (A) = 0\) for every other tag, and \(M_{\boldsymbol{A}} = \prod_i m (A_i)\), with \(M_{\emptyset} = 1\). Let the tokens be the tags themselves, with the identity token map; take \(P_p = p + 1\), \(D_N = N + 2\), \(\beta_{N, p} = \log (N + 2)\) and the insertions \(O_{N, p, A} (x ; U) = m (A)\), which are constant, hence regular with empty stencils, and satisfy (1.4) with \(\sigma = 1\). Since the Wilson measure is a probability measure, the moments are \(M_{\boldsymbol{A}}\), and Riemann sums give \(W_{N, p} (\boldsymbol{A}; f) \to M_{\boldsymbol{A}} \int f\) at every suitable \(p\). Replace the phase data by the two-point space \(\{+, -\}\) with the uniform measure and selected point \(+\), and set

\(\displaystyle S^+ = S, \qquad S^- = 2 M - S, \qquad S^{\text{mix}} = M.\)

The mixture identity holds, the selected theory is unchanged, and the volume limit is \(\int fM\) along every period sequence. In the operator-product data add two grade-zero channels with actors \(u\) and \(2 u\) and coefficients \(h_a\) and \(- \tfrac{1}{2} h_a\), where \(h_a = \prod_k m (a_k) - \sum_i c^{0, i}_a \hspace{0.17em} m ((0, E_{0, i}))\) is the defect of the old grade-zero channels \((E_{0, i}, c^{0, i}_a)\) on \(M\). The coefficient bounds for the new channels follow from those of the old ones, because \(|m| \leqslant 1\). At the selected point the two new terms cancel by homogeneity; in the mixed state the expansion reproduces \(M\) exactly, because \(M\) vanishes on every actor of positive grade; and the remainder at \(-\) is minus that at \(+\). Every requirement other than the convergence statements mentions only the selected point, the common operator-product data or the listed properties of the phase correlations, and these have just been checked. The lattice moment of every field of positive grade, including the grade-four field of the trace test, is zero in this example.

The detailed construction does not use any of these freedoms: its correlations are multilinear in the fields, each of its phases carries its own positive reconstruction, and its insertions are fixed local functions of the links tied to the Wilson measure by the construction. The remark therefore concerns only what the displayed target certifies; it says nothing against the construction of the later sections.

1.3The target is satisfied by a generalized free field

Remark 1.4 supplies the lattice clauses of the target, with insertions that do not depend on the gauge configuration, once hierarchy data are given that satisfy the remaining requirements and the grade-zero homogeneity hypothesis of the remark. Such data need not come from a gauge theory.

Proposition 1.5. (A generalized free-field solution of the target) For every group \(G\) as in this section, the existence target, including the operator-product requirements (19.30)–(19.31) and the trace requirement (19.32) with its profile exclusion, is satisfied by a regular lattice family whose insertions do not depend on \(U\) and by a hierarchy whose selected theory is the field system of Wick polynomials and derivatives of one generalized free scalar field. The continuum data depend on \(G\) only through \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}\) in the normalization of the grade-four field of the trace requirement.

The proposition has been verified by machine against the formal statement of the source, using that statement and none of the source's construction. The formal development and its review are documented in the accompanying blueprint. We give the construction here.

The field.
Let \(\mu\) be the measure on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) with density
\(\displaystyle w (p) = \frac{(p^2)^2}{\log^2 (e + p^2)}, \qquad p^2 = E^2 - |\boldsymbol{p}|^2,\)

on the closed forward cone \(\bar{V}_+\) and zero outside it. It is invariant under the proper orthochronous Lorentz group and polynomially bounded. Let \(\phi\) be the generalized free scalar field with two-point distribution \(\int e^{i \langle y - x, p \rangle} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (p)\), where \(\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle\) is the Euclidean pairing of \(\mathbb{R}^4\) and no normalizing factor is inserted. Relative to the invariant measure \(d^3 \boldsymbol{p}/ (2 \sqrt{s + |\boldsymbol{p}|^2})\) on the mass shell of mass squared \(s\), its Källén–Lehmann measure is \(s^2 \log^{- 2} (e + s) \hspace{0.17em} ds\) on \(s \geqslant 0\): the mass spectrum is continuous. Write \(\xi_{\alpha} = i^{\alpha_t} \partial^{\alpha} \phi\) for a multi-index \(\alpha \in \mathbb{N}_0^4\) with time component \(\alpha_t\); the factor \(i\) for each time derivative matches the Euclidean and relativistic conventions. The labels are the finite complex linear combinations of Wick monomials \(: \hspace{-0.17em} \xi_{\alpha_1} \cdots \xi_{\alpha_k} \hspace{-0.17em}\):. A monomial has grade \(\sum_j (4 + | \alpha_j |)\), the empty monomial is the unit, and \(\mathcal{F}_d\) is the span of the monomials of grade at most \(d\), which is finite-dimensional. The density \(w\) is strictly positive in the interior of the forward cone. Together with the linear independence of the polynomials in the momenta that distinct Wick monomials produce, this shows that every nonzero label acts nontrivially.

The relativistic theory.
The Hilbert space is the closed span of the vectors obtained by applying field words to the vacuum; these vectors are written down explicitly as families of square-integrable functions of \(k\) momenta, \(k \geqslant 0\). The properties of Definition 1.3 are verified directly: linearity, the adjoint, continuity in the test, Poincaré covariance, the support of the spectral measure in \(\bar{V}_+\), uniqueness of the vacuum (in every sector with \(k \geqslant 1\) the total energy is positive almost everywhere; no mass gap is needed), and locality. Let
\(\displaystyle S (\xi) = \int e^{- | \xi | E} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (p), \qquad \xi \in \mathbb{R}^4 \setminus \{0\},\)

which for \(\xi = (\boldsymbol{u}, \tau)\) with \(\tau > 0\) equals \(\int e^{- \tau E + i\boldsymbol{u} \cdot \boldsymbol{p}} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (p)\). At pairwise distinct insertion points, the selected correlations are sums over complete pairings of the individual factors \(\xi_{\alpha}\), with no pair joining two factors of the same Wick monomial; each pair contributes the corresponding derivative of \(S\), and the correlation vanishes if there is no such pairing. Their continuation to complex times and the boundary values (1.7) are obtained from the same momentum integrals.

Operator products.
The expansion of a cluster of Wick monomials is given by Wick's theorem followed by Taylor expansion, about the base point, of the factors that are not contracted within the cluster. Each coefficient is a finite sum, over partial contractions and Taylor multi-indices, of products of derivatives of \(S\) at differences of cluster points with monomials in the displacements and inverse factorials. One family of coefficients serves every truncation degree. The Wick decomposition and the Taylor remainder, together with \(| \partial^{\gamma} S (\xi) | \leqslant C_{\gamma} | \xi |^{- 8 - | \gamma |}\), give the powers of \(r\) required in (19.30) and (19.31), at first with constants depending on the fields, the truncation degree and the geometry parameters. The separation of the spectators makes these estimates uniform in the allowed positions, and for fixed parameters the factor \(r^{- \epsilon}\) absorbs the constants.
The trace requirement.
On the time axis, with \(e_t = (\boldsymbol{0}, 1)\),
\(\displaystyle \lim_{r \downarrow 0} r^8 \log^2 (r^{- 1}) \hspace{0.17em} S (re_t) = 384 \pi .\)

Take \(\Theta = \lambda \phi\) with \(\lambda > 0\) and \(\lambda^2 = \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}/ (128 \pi^5)\). Then \(\Theta\) has grade four, its vacuum one-point functional vanishes, and (19.32) holds with the required constant. The profile exclusion follows from this asymptotic alone, by Theorem 18.40.

The lattice clauses.
The correlations just described are multilinear in the fields, so the unit \(u \in \mathcal{F}_0\) enters them homogeneously, and Remark 1.4 applies: the tokens are the tags, the insertions are the constants \(m (A)\), the phase space has two points, and two grade-zero channels are added to the operator-product data. This completes the data. The formal proof uses a variant of the remark in which \(m\) is the indicator of the zero vector of \(\mathcal{F}_0\) and one channel with zero field is added in each grade; it relies on the vanishing of the correlations when a field is zero.
Why the target admits this.
Three features of the target, each already noted above, combine.

  1. The Wilson measure enters only through the mixed correlations, and these are tied to the selected theory only by the mixture identity and the common operator-product estimates. The target imposes no multilinearity on the phase and mixed correlation functions and no positivity or reconstruction at a non-selected phase.

  2. No particular nonconstant gauge-invariant observable is prescribed for a field of the hierarchy. Beyond the lattice conditions, the group and its representation enter the requirements only through \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}\) in (19.32).

  3. The profile exclusion constrains one two-point function. It excludes the specified finite sums of products of mass-shell kernels, while the continuous spectral weight chosen above gives exactly the required short-distance behaviour. The operator-product requirements are upper bounds, and they hold for this hierarchy of Wick polynomials. No clause imposes a condition on truncated correlations of higher order that would exclude a generalized free generating field.

Relation to the review of the source.
The weakness of the target is not a new observation in its general form. The assessment of the source by its review group [Yan26, Sections 7.3 and 9] records that the authors of the source informed the group that the original public target “omitted some of the connections to the lattice construction established by its proof”, and it bases its assessment of these connections on a stronger combined target that retains the lattice observables, limits, phases and reconstructed fields of the construction itself. The same assessment notes that the trace requirement excludes the specified finite free-field descriptions “but does not rule out every description in terms of composites of Gaussian fields”. Proposition 1.5 and Remark 1.4 make both remarks precise for the original target: the omitted connections can be dispensed with altogether, and the hierarchy of Wick polynomials and derivatives of one generalized free scalar field supplies the remaining continuum requirements. They give no information about the stronger combined target, which refers to the lattice family of the construction.
What this does and does not say.
The proposition concerns the target as a specification. It shows that the target alone does not express the specifically Yang–Mills content of the construction: a proof of it need not involve Yang–Mills theory. It does not show any defect in the construction. The detailed theorem of the source concerns mixed correlations obtained as limits of Wilson expectations of a fixed family of local gauge-invariant insertions, a phase decomposition with a positive reconstruction at every phase, and a grade-four field identified with the centered trace of a stress tensor that satisfies translation and rotation Ward identities; none of this is touched by the proposition. Nor does the proposition bear on the question whether the theory so constructed could itself be a generalized free field. That question is not settled by the target, since the target is satisfied by one, and it is not addressed in this article.
