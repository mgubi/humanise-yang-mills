\section{Bounded local approximations on the original phase grid}
\label{sec:actual-phase-grid-approximants}

The comparison argument requires bounded real observables which approximate
whole replicated fields under the original finite-volume law. A bound on a
single expectation would not suffice. We construct these observables using
one insertion at the terminal scale and a second insertion at an intermediate
scale. Their difference is controlled by the marked estimate, before taking
any limit or choosing a phase.

Fix the selected family $C$ and the extraction order already used for its
centered estimates. Write $a_i$ for the physical mesh, $\sigma_i$ for the
running scale, $c_i$ for the column count, and $\ell_i$ for the accumulated
column logarithm. Thus $a_i\to0$ whereas $\sigma_i\to+\infty$.
At grade $d$ the finite coefficient space has coordinate norm $\|\cdot\|_1$,
coordinate basis $(e_s)$, and reference depth $r_d$. The finite-volume
observable associated with $L$ is denoted by $O_{N,p}(L)$, with its base
point at the origin. All expectations below use the original Wilson
probability measure $\mu_{N,p}$.

We use the paid selected-history data of
Sections~\ref{sec:actual-marked-portfolio-calibration}
and~\ref{sec:selected-centered-service}: the coefficient transport on this
history, its adjacent density and forced-profile identities, and its height,
transition, occupancy and support estimates. In particular the positive
heights $\zeta_{d,i}$ and weights $\phi_i$ satisfy
\[
 0<\phi_i\leq1,\qquad
 \phi_i L_i^{d+1}\zeta_{d,i}\leq\zeta_{d,i+1},\qquad
 L_i=a_i/a_{i+1}.
\]
These data are used with the same local sections and transition kernels at
every occurrence below. The availability of this entire paid history is an
input here; it is not inferred merely from the existence of an unmarked
Wilson measure.

\subsection{Transporting a bounded local observable}

For $k\leq N$, let $\mathsf B_{k,N}$ be the iterated averaging operator
which carries a scale-$k$ observable to a function of the scale-$N$
configuration. At the step from $i$ to $i+1$ it uses the transition kernel
of the selected history, with that history's coupling at $i+1$ and its
fixed terminal indices $(N,p)$. Its adjoint $\mathsf P_{k,N}$ pushes
densities from $N$ to $k$, relative to product Haar measure. Normalization
of each transition kernel, followed by induction, gives
\begin{align*}
 \|\mathsf B_{k,N}F\|_\infty&\leq\|F\|_\infty,\\
 \mathsf B_{k,N}\overline F&=\overline{\mathsf B_{k,N}F},\\
 \int (\mathsf P_{k,N}g)F\,d\nu_k
   &=\int g\,\mathsf B_{k,N}F\,d\nu_N.
\end{align*}
Here $\nu_i$ is product Haar measure at scale $i$, $F$ is bounded
measurable, and $g$ is integrable. The last identity follows first at one
step by integration against the kernel, and then by iteration. The same
argument with $F=1$ proves preservation of the density integral.

The local geometry supplies a useful stronger identity. Fix a coarse depth
$J\leq k$ and a window radius $R$. Choose $k$ large enough that the
propagated radius-$R$ windows, based at distinct $J$-cell centers, remain
inside their respective chambers at every intervening depth. This is the
room condition. To verify it, let $h_i$ be the calendar index at depth
$i$, so $h_{i+1}\leq h_i+1$, and recall $L_i\geq3$. Starting with
$R_k=R$, the kernel read set has propagated radius
\[
 R_{i+1}=L_iR_i+10(h_{i+1}+3)L_i.
\]
Put $T_i=30a_i(h_i+5)$. Since $a_{i+1}=a_i/L_i$,
$T_{i+1}\leq(2/3)T_i$ and
$a_{i+1}R_{i+1}-a_iR_i\leq T_i/3$. Therefore
\[
 a_iR_i+T_i\leq a_kR+T_k\qquad(i\geq k).
\]
Both $a_kR$ and $T_k$ tend to zero. Choose $k$ so that
$2(a_kR+T_k)<a_J$. Then $2a_iR_i<a_J$ at every later depth,
which places the window inside the centered $J$-chamber, using the
odd-subdivision chamber convention. This proves the required room
condition uniformly in terminal depth, period and number of cells. If $F_i$ depends only on the window at cell $i$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-grid-factor}
 \mathsf B_{k,N}\left(\prod_{i\in A}F_i\right)
       =\prod_{i\in A}\mathsf B_{k,N}F_i
\end{equation}
for every finite set $A$ of distinct cells. Indeed, at one transition the
kernel variables read by different chambers are disjoint. Integrating
their product therefore factors. The propagated-window condition permits
this argument at every subsequent transition. Induction over the depth
proves the formula. Covariance of the kernels under signed lattice motions,
and their compatibility with conjugation, also show that transport commutes
with the centered copy maps. These are identities of the kernels; no
factorization of the Wilson probability measure is asserted.

At scale $k$, define $F_{k,L}$ to be the physically rescaled exponential
entry of the primary coefficient $L$. More explicitly it is
\[
 F_{k,L}(U)=a_k^{-d}L_0+
       \mathbf1_{\mathrm{healthy}(k)}(U)\,\mathcal Q_{k,L}(U),
\]
where $\mathcal Q_{k,L}$ is the continuous gauge-invariant candidate of the
local section including its physical factor $a_k^{-d}$, and the indicator
tests the section's footprint. The
candidate estimate gives a bound on $|\mathcal Q_{k,L}|$ independent of
$N\geq k$ and $p$. The indicator has values zero and one; thus $F_{k,L}$
is bounded and measurable uniformly in these indices. Its complete read
set, including the mask's halo, lies in the window of radius $R_d+4$,
where $R_d$ is the stencil radius. Consequently there is $A_k\geq0$ such
that
\[
 \sup|F_{k,e_s}|\leq A_k\qquad\hbox{for every basis index }s,
\]
uniformly in $N,p$. For example one may sum the finitely many individual
bounds. Continuity of the masked field is not needed.

\subsection{The marked discrepancy estimate}

Fix an opened pair of depths $J\leq m$, with $m\geq r_d$, and choose
$k\geq m$ satisfying the room condition above. Put
\[
 E_k=4(1+c_k),\quad
 G(k,i)=\exp\bigl((\sigma_k-\sigma_i)/8\bigr),\quad
 S_{J,m}=\exp\left((a_J/a_m)^4\mathcal T_m+5\right),
\]
where $\mathcal T_m$ is the vacuum treasury. Choose $w>0$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-grid-price}
 w a_m^{-d}\frac{E_k}{G(k,m)}\leq\frac{\zeta_{d,m}}2.
\end{equation}
The opening supplies migrating centers for the full $J$-cell census,
separated at every depth between $m$ and $N$. Their occupancy is one and
therefore fits the nondecreasing paid occupancy schedule.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:actual-grid-basis}
For every basis vector $e_s$ and every $N>k$, there is a coefficient $b_s$
in the same history's coefficient transport such that
\[
 \|e_s-I_kb_s\|_1\leq\tfrac14,
 \qquad
 \rho_{J;N,p}\bigl(\mathsf B_{k,N}F_{k,e_s}
                    -O_{N,p}(T_Nb_s)\bigr)\leq S_{J,m}/w.
\]
Here $I_k$ is the coefficient identification at $k$, $T_N$ is the terminal
identification, and $\rho_{J;N,p}$ is the centered full-census root test
for $\mu_{N,p}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the backward coefficient construction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:actual-marked-antilles} to the primary $e_s$ at depth $k$.
Its initial remainder and forced profile vanish, its format is the primary
format, and its coefficient norm is at most one. Recenter the resulting
backward coefficients by the negative of their bottom primary $b_s$.
Writing the resulting voice as $Z_i$ and its remainder price as $g_i$,
the construction gives, for $m\leq i\leq k$,
\[
 g_i+\|Z_i.x\|_1\leq E_k/G(k,i),\qquad
 Z_k.x=e_s-I_kb_s.
\]
The terminal cone estimate is
$32(1+c_k)^2\|Z_k.x\|_1\leq2(1+c_k)$.
Since $c_k\geq1$, it implies the stated quarter bound.

Let $\tau_i=w\prod_{j=m}^{i-1}\phi_j$. The physical rescaling changes
$a_i^{-d}$ by $L_i^d$ at the next step, and $G(k,i)^{-1}$ changes by at
most $L_i$. Thus the height recurrence and
\eqref{eq:actual-grid-price} imply
\[
 \tau_i a_i^{-d}E_k/G(k,i)\leq\zeta_{d,i}/2
       \qquad(m\leq i\leq k).
\]
Moreover $\|I_kb_s\|_1\leq1+\|Z_k.x\|_1\leq2E_k$.
It follows that the negative played coefficient at $k$ costs at most
$\zeta_{d,k}$ with weight $\tau_k$. This is the input needed for the
selected insertion service above $k$.

The matching at $k$ is exact, not just a norm comparison. Linearity of
the played exponential entry and of its forced-profile map gives
\[
 Z_k^{\mathrm{exp}}=\mathrm{primary}(e_s)^{\mathrm{exp}}
                       +\mathrm{played}(-b_s)^{\mathrm{exp}},\qquad
 Z_k^{\mathrm{forced}}=\mathrm{played}(-b_s)^{\mathrm{forced}}.
\]
The second identity uses the zero forced profile of the primary. Hence we
can first insert the negative terminal observable
$-O_{N,p}(T_Nb_s)$, transport its marked density down to $k$, and then
multiply by the positive local factors $F_{k,e_s}$. The selected service
of Section~\ref{sec:selected-centered-service} applies above $k$ with
the just verified played-coefficient price. Adding the local factors
changes its exponential entries to those of $Z_k$ and leaves its forced
profiles unchanged.

Below $k$ the backward coefficients supply the adjacent density identities
and finite-support forced-profile comparisons. We verify both transition
prices. Put $D_i=\operatorname{orbitCost}_d(\psi_i+2c_{i+1})$, where
$\psi_i$ is the adjacent comparison price. The exact comparison bounds
its next-level weighted remainder by
$D_i(g_{i+1}+\|Z_{i+1}.x\|_1)$; its lower-level remainder is $g_i$.
The paid room and coin inequalities are
$2D_i\zeta_{d,i+1}\leq1$ and $c_i\zeta_{d,i}\leq1$.
The marched height estimate, with the harmless factor $1/2$ discarded,
therefore gives
\[
 2\tau_{i+1}a_{i+1}^{-d}
     D_i(g_{i+1}+\|Z_{i+1}.x\|_1)
       \leq2D_i\zeta_{d,i+1}\leq1,
\]
and, since $\tau_{i+1}\leq\tau_i$ and $c_i\geq1$,
\[
 \tau_{i+1}a_i^{-d}g_i
       \leq\tau_i a_i^{-d}g_i\leq\zeta_{d,i}\leq1.
\]
The finite-support comparison is transported through the actual sound
gauge door and physically rescaled by the exact adjacent scale identity.
Its profiles consequently have precisely these two prices. The selected
transition estimate now descends by induction from $k$ to $m$.
At each step the centers migrate along the
same embeddings, the occupancy and separation estimates persist, and the
pushed singleton coefficients equal the prescribed local densities.
Thus the marked estimate of Section~\ref{ex-centered-sail} applies at
$m$. Its local entry price is at most $2$, its forced price at most $1$,
and its action allowance is $1$, giving total price $2+1+2=5$ per cell.
These are the same prices derived in the finite-census argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:actual-marked-figment}; the new feature here is the
intermediate positive insertion and the exact matching just proved.

For clarity we identify the coefficient being bounded. Let $\mathcal I$
be the full set of $J$-cells and $M=|\mathcal I|>0$. Put
$U=O_{N,p}(T_Nb_s)$ and $F=F_{k,e_s}$. For a subset
$A\subseteq\mathcal I$, the coefficient after the insertion at $k$ is
\[
 \sum_{B\subseteq A}
 \mathsf P_{k,N}\left(e^{-S_N}
                \prod_{i\in A\setminus B}(-\mathcal C_i^NU)\right)
                 \prod_{i\in B}\mathcal C_i^kF .
\]
Here $e^{-S_N}$ denotes the unnormalized Wilson density and
$\mathcal C_i^j$ the centered copy at depth $j$, including its conjugation
when prescribed by the tiling. Duality, \eqref{eq:actual-grid-factor},
and commutation with copies show that its integral is
\[
 Z_{N,p}\,\mathbb E_{N,p}
       \prod_{i\in A}\mathcal C_i^N(\mathsf B_{k,N}F-U).
\]
This follows by expanding the product over all subsets $B$; the negative
old factors are retained together inside their pushed density. All
integrals exist because the terminal fields are continuous on compact
configuration spaces and $F$ is bounded measurable. Further density
transport to $m$ preserves this integral, coefficient by coefficient.

For $A=\mathcal I$, denote the last expectation by $\Omega$. Applying
the marked estimate and dividing by the positive original partition
function $Z_{N,p}$ gives
\[
 |\Omega|w^M\leq
     \exp\bigl(|\mathrm{Site}(m,p)|\mathcal T_m+5M\bigr).
\]
The aligned four-dimensional count is
$|\mathrm{Site}(m,p)|=M(a_J/a_m)^4$. Taking the $M$th root proves
$|\Omega|^{1/M}\leq S_{J,m}/w$, which is precisely the asserted root
test. No change of law or extraction order has entered the calculation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Arbitrary real labels and a common coarse depth}

For a label $L$ of grade $d$, put
$K_k(L)=2e^{\ell_k}\|L\|_1$.
The vectors $I_kb_s$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:actual-grid-basis} form a basis by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ex-public-frame-recovery}. If $t=T_N^{-1}L$, solve
$\sum_s v_sI_kb_s=I_kt$. Then
\[
 L=\sum_s v_sT_Nb_s,\qquad
 \sum_s|v_s|\leq2\|I_kt\|_1\leq K_k(L).
\]
The last inequality is the backward coefficient budget of the same
selected history. Define
$B^{\mathbb C}_{N,p}=\sum_s v_s\mathsf B_{k,N}F_{k,e_s}$.
Its supremum norm is at most $K_k(L)A_k$. The room condition puts every
summand in the origin chamber of the $J$-tiling; eventually the physical
observables of this fixed grade also lie there. The rooted triangle
inequality on chamber-interior fields therefore gives
\[
 \rho_{J;N,p}(B^{\mathbb C}_{N,p}-O_{N,p}(L))
                  \leq K_k(L)S_{J,m}/w.
\]
The coefficients $v_s$ may depend on $N,p$; their common bound makes the
supremum estimate uniform in these indices.

Choose
\[
 w_k=\frac{\zeta_{d,m}G(k,m)}{2a_m^{-d}E_k}.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:actual-grid-price} is an equality. The column estimates
$\ell_k=o(\sigma_k)$ and $\log(1+c_k)=o(\sigma_k)$ imply
$K_k(L)S_{J,m}/w_k\to0$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ex-public-radial-choice}. In this limit $J,m,d,L$ are fixed.
Thus we can choose $k$ to meet both the room condition and any prescribed
error $\varepsilon>0$, and subsequently take $N>k$ on the original late
set.

If $L$ is star-real, the exact reality identity makes $O_{N,p}(L)$ real.
Take $B_{N,p}=\operatorname{Re}B^{\mathbb C}_{N,p}$. It has the same
supremum bound and chamber support. Since the root test is invariant under
conjugation and subadditive on these fields,
\[
 \rho_{J;N,p}(B_{N,p}-O_{N,p}(L))
 \leq\tfrac12\rho_{J;N,p}(B^{\mathbb C}_{N,p}-O_{N,p}(L))
     +\tfrac12\rho_{J;N,p}
          (\overline{B^{\mathbb C}_{N,p}-O_{N,p}(L)})
 \leq\varepsilon.
\]
Here real fields are identified with their complex-valued inclusions.

For a finite grade ceiling $D$ and a requested coarse depth $J_0$, choose
\[
 J=\max\{J_0,r_0,\ldots,r_D\}.
\]
Use the opening schedule once to select $m\geq J$ with the required
opening. The preceding construction then applies to every star-real label
of grade at most $D$ and each accuracy $\varepsilon_q=(q+1)^{-1}$.
Its approximation depth and supremum bound may depend on the label and
$q$, but its coarse depth $J$ and extraction order do not. This is the
coherent family of origin approximations needed for the comparison grid.

\subsection{From origin approximations to whole grid words}

The same origin approximations must be used for all translated and
reflected copies. For a real label $v$ and a copy of spin frame $s$, first
apply the inverse spin frame to $v$, then copy its origin approximation.
The exact covariance identity turns the corresponding copied physical
field into the field labelled by $v$ at the desired site. A conjugated
copy of a real approximant has the same value as its ordinary real
pullback. All transformed labels remain real. Since the spin-frame set
is finite, summing their supremum bounds gives a bound $M_{v,q}$ valid
for every cell at accuracy $q$.

There is also an accuracy-independent bound $A_v$ on the root tests of
the physical origin fields in these finitely many frames. To obtain it,
choose a finer depth $j\geq J$ with $9a_j\leq a_J$ and use the centered
one-field estimate at that depth. The origin is in the margin of width
$3a_j$ of its $J$-chamber. The centered comparison estimate embeds the
fine test into this chamber and bounds its root test there. Sum these
bounds over the finite spin frames. For each fixed $q$, intersect their
finitely many late sets with those for the origin approximations. We
obtain, for every cell, chamber-interior fields $X_v,Y_v$ satisfying
\[
 \rho(X_v)\leq A_v,\qquad
 \rho(Y_v-X_v)\leq\varepsilon_q,
 \qquad |\mathcal C_iY_v|\leq M_{v,q}.
\]
The copies of $X_v$ are exactly the physical fields. The prescription for
$Y_v$ depends only on the label, accuracy and cell frame, and thus works
simultaneously for arbitrary finite layouts.

For a word $v_1,\ldots,v_n$, set $M=1+\sum_i A_{v_i}$. Since
$\varepsilon_q\leq1$, both $\rho(X_{v_i})$ and $\rho(Y_{v_i})$ are at
most $M$. For any injective layout in the actual finite torus,
Lemma~\ref{lem:ex-public-replace-word} gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_{N,p}\prod_i\mathcal C_{x_i}Y_{v_i}
        -\mathbb E_{N,p}\prod_i\mathcal C_{x_i}X_{v_i}\right|
                 \leq n\varepsilon_q M^{n-1}.
\]
The new word is real, measurable and bounded by $\prod_iM_{v_i,q}$.
This bound holds even for colliding layouts; only the error estimate needs
injectivity. An empty word has error zero.

Finally consider two fixed injective integer-grid layouts. Translate their
copies numbered $i<j$ by $ti(1,1,1,1)$ and $tj(1,1,1,1)$. For all
sufficiently large $t$, their first coordinates separate, so their union
is injective. For each such fixed $t$, eventual growth of the period
makes the union injective on the torus as well. Apply the preceding word
estimate to the concatenated labels. Taking real parts gives the required
comparison with the real part of the \emph{whole} complex product, since
$|\operatorname{Re}z|\leq|z|$ and integration commutes with real parts.
The order of choices is accuracy, pair of copy numbers, sufficiently large
separation, then the original late cutoff/period limit.

Apply this construction to each of the four pairs of plain and reflected
words. The difference between their two bounded real approximants has
supremum bound equal to the sum of their individual bounds. Expanding the
product of two such differences gives four terms, with total error at
most $\varepsilon_q$ times the sum of the four accuracy-independent
word constants. This tends to zero. Theorem~\ref{thm:ex-public-grill}
therefore applies on the same extraction order and the same coarse grid.
This supplies its local approximation and separated-pair inputs from the
paid selected history, rather than assuming those approximations as an
additional phase hypothesis.
