12.1Uniqueness of the limiting background paths

The path coefficients require their own convergence argument. Uniform bounds give convergent subsequences, but do not identify their limits. The identification uses the order of the Taylor coefficients in the stationary action: first the cubic term, then the quartic term, and finally the quadratic loop correction. We explain both the finite-step comparison and the passage from subsequences to the whole sequence.

At each scale \(m\) write a path as its prescribed one-form row and three coefficient arrays

\(\displaystyle (a_m, b_m, c_m) .\)

Here \(a_m\) represents the quadratic loop correction, \(b_m\) the cubic term, and \(c_m\) the quartic term. Each array belongs to the selected closed subspace of its real coefficient Hilbert space, which is defined as follows. For \(q = 2, 3, 4\) let \(H_{m, q}\) be the real Hilbert space of square-summable coefficient arrays of degree \(q\), and let \(A_{m, q} \subset H_{m, q}\) be its closed subspace of admissible arrays: those whose atoms satisfy the linear invariance conditions of the path bounds, together with the linear loop normalization conditions when \(q = 2\). Let \(n_m (p)\), \(p \geqslant 0\), be the period sequence prescribed at scale \(m\) by the schedule. For each \(p\) let \(T_{m, q, p}\) be the continuous linear map which sends an array to the degree-\(q\) Taylor coefficient at zero of its path sum on the torus of side \(n_m (p)\). Put

\(\displaystyle K_{m, q} = A_{m, q} \cap \bigcap_{p \geqslant 0} \ker T_{m, q, p}, \qquad A^{\text{sel}}_{m, q} = A_{m, q} \cap K_{m, q}^{\perp} .\)

This is the canonical choice of Subsubsection 11.21.3, with the admissible subspace and with the readouts on the prescribed periods only. Two selected arrays with the same readouts on every prescribed period are equal: their difference lies in \(K_{m, q}\) and in \(K_{m, q}^{\perp}\). This statement concerns the arrays themselves, not just their evaluation on one torus. The orthogonal complement of the common kernel in the whole space \(H_{m, q}\) would not do: its elements need not be admissible.

For \(q = 2, 3, 4\) and the fixed encoding width \(w_{m, q}\), use the norm

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{n}_{m, q} (C) = \sum_{a, \nu} e^{- (w_{m, q} - v_m) \ell (a)} |C (a, \nu) |, \qquad w_{m, q} > v_m .\)

Here \(v_m\) is the paid path width, and the canonical lengths and finite color index \(\nu\) are as in Subsection 10.13. All weights are strictly positive. Thus this expression is a norm on the coefficient space on which it is finite; homogeneity and the triangle inequality follow term by term. Let \(s_{m, q} > 0\) be its paid allowance. The ordinary outputs satisfy \(\mathfrak{n}_{m, q} \leqslant s_{m, q}\). The extended input domain permits \(64 s_{m + 1, q}\), and keeps the other two arrays at their ordinary allowances. The same finite-step construction is available for every input in this domain. We use this actual extended domain below; existence for one selected input alone would not suffice.

12.1.1The data and the statement

The convergence proof is an argument about given finite-step data. It does not construct them. We list what is used; every item refers to one scale schedule, one horizon of beds and towers, and one choice of chart, clipping data and post-cost constants.

  1. Finite steps on the extended domain. Let \(m < N\), and let \(R\) be a path whose one-form row is the root row of the bed at \((N, m + 1)\), which is attached to that bed, and which satisfies the path bounds of scale \(m + 1\) with the row, cubic and quartic allowances and the loop allowance each multiplied by \(64\). Then there are an output path \(T\), a scalar \(\alpha\) and stationary maps \(\rho^{p, F}\), one for each period index \(p\) and allowed forest \(F\), with the following properties. The path \(T\) has the root row of the bed at \((N, m)\), is attached to it and satisfies the ordinary path bounds of scale \(m\). On every prescribed period and forest the bound gap, stationarity and classical matching relations between \(R\), \(T\) and \(\rho^{p, F}\) hold, and so does the one-loop comparison used below, with error at most \(\Gamma e^{- cn_m (p)}\), with \(\Gamma, c > 0\) fixed at the stage. The scalar satisfies \(| \alpha + \lambda_G \log L_m | + 1 \leqslant D_{m + 1}\), where \(\lambda_G\) is the slope constant of the group and \(D_{m + 1}\) the drift allowance. The three nonlinear arrays of \(T\) are encoded by selected arrays \(a \in A^{\text{sel}}_{m, 2}\), \(b \in A^{\text{sel}}_{m, 3}\), \(c \in A^{\text{sel}}_{m, 4}\) whose Hilbert norms are at most the loop allowance, for \(a\), and the path allowance, for \(b\) and \(c\).

  2. The actual history. For every \(N\) there are paths \(R_{N, m}\), \(m \leqslant N\), scalars \(\alpha_{N, m}\) and stationary maps \(\rho^{p, F}_{N, m}\), \(m < N\), forming the finite history of Subsection 11.21 with all its finite-cutoff path conditions. In particular each \(R_{N, m}\) has the ordinary bounds and the root row of its bed, and for every \(m < N\) the data \((R_{N, m + 1}, R_{N, m}, \alpha_{N, m}, \rho_{N, m})\) form a step with all the properties in the first item.

  3. Widths. The three encoding widths at each scale are strictly larger than the paid path width, the loop allowance is at least one, and the root rows of the beds at each fixed scale have, for all large \(N\), a strip bound at one width strictly larger than the paid path width.

  4. The horizon. The beds and towers have their stagewise strip bounds, all-grade floors on every torus and transition identities, the sworn conditions hold on every prescribed period, and each of the ten retained bed and tower rows converges coefficientwise as \(N \to \infty\) at fixed scale. Every blocking step satisfies the size conditions of the schedule relating its patch to its fine and coarse periods. The chart is the local-ratio chart of Subsection 8.18: the exponential map is injective on a ball of positive radius, and on it the normalized Haar measure has a positive real-analytic density, equal to one at the origin, times a positive scale. The color index set is nonempty.

The first two items and the widths are outputs of the one-step analytic construction and of the finite backward assembly in Subsection 11.21; the factor \(64\) is the input allowance of Subsubsection 11.21.6. The row convergence is Subsection 11.20. None of these is proved in the present section, and the application to the selected history, with the verification that the four items refer to the same objects, is made in Subsection 14.21.

Under these hypotheses we prove the following. At every fixed scale \(m\) there are a path \(R_m\) with the ordinary path bounds, a scalar \(\alpha_m\) and, for every period index and forest, a stationary germ \(\rho^{p, F}_m\), such that as \(N \to \infty\) along the whole sequence of cutoffs: every coefficient of the selected arrays of \(R_{N, m}\) converges to the corresponding coefficient of the selected arrays of \(R_m\), with the Hilbert bounds of the first item retained; \(\alpha_{N, m} \to \alpha_m\); and every derivative at zero of \(\rho^{p, F}_{N, m}\) converges to that of \(\rho^{p, F}_m\). The Hilbert bounds of the first item and its bound on \(\alpha\) are what make the compactness step below possible. The weighted norms \(\mathfrak{n}_{m, q}\) alone would not: their weights decay exponentially in the length.

12.1.2The triangular affine identities

Fix the fine and coarse one-form rows. Consider three admissible finite-step inputs and outputs, indexed by \(0, 1, t\), with the same rows and the same forest, coordinates and normalization conventions. The following identities hold whenever the indicated interpolation is admissible:

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} b^{\text{in}}_t = b^{\text{in}}_0 + t (b^{\text{in}}_1 - b^{\text{in}}_0) & \Longrightarrow b^{\text{out}}_t = b^{\text{out}}_0 + t (b^{\text{out}}_1 - b^{\text{out}}_0), \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.1)} \\ c^{\text{in}}_t = c^{\text{in}}_0 + t (c^{\text{in}}_1 - c^{\text{in}}_0) & \Longrightarrow c^{\text{out}}_t = c^{\text{out}}_0 + t (c^{\text{out}}_1 - c^{\text{out}}_0), \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.2)} \\ a^{\text{in}}_t = a^{\text{in}}_0 + t (a^{\text{in}}_1 - a^{\text{in}}_0) & \Longrightarrow \left\{ \begin{array}{l} a^{\text{out}}_t = a^{\text{out}}_0 + t (a^{\text{out}}_1 - a^{\text{out}}_0),\\ \alpha_t = \alpha_0 + t (\alpha_1 - \alpha_0) . \end{array} \right. \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.3)} \end{array}\)

In (12.2) the three input cubic arrays must agree. In (12.3) both their cubic and quartic arrays must agree. No equality of those arrays is needed in (12.1).

Here is the stationary-action argument for the first two identities. In finite-dimensional chart coordinates let \(S (x, y)\) be the analytic action, \(y = \psi (x)\) its stationary graph, and \(S_{yy} (0, 0) = H\) its invertible Hessian in the eliminated coordinates. All these stationary graphs have \(\psi (0) = 0\). Write \(z (x) = (x, \psi (x))\). Differentiating \(S_y (x, \psi (x)) = 0\) once determines \(D \psi (0)\) from the common quadratic jet of \(S\). Repeated differentiation determines each next derivative by an equation

\(\displaystyle HD^r \psi (0) = - R_r,\)

where \(R_r\) is a finite sum of contractions of derivatives of \(S\) of order at most \(r + 1\) and derivatives of \(\psi\) of order less than \(r\). This follows directly by the multivariable chain rule: the only term containing \(D^r \psi\) is obtained by differentiating the \(y\) argument of \(S_y\) once and the graph \(r\) times.

Suppose three actions have the same jet through order \(r\), with \(r = 2\) or \(3\), and their order-\((r + 1)\) jets have the displayed affine relation. Their graph jets through order \(r - 1\) agree. In \(D^{r + 1} (S \circ z) (0)\), terms containing a single highest graph derivative cancel by stationarity. Terms containing \(D^r z\) pair with \(D^2 S\) and \(Dz\) and vanish by the derivative of stationarity, since \(D^r z\) is vertical. All remaining terms involve the common lower jets, except the single term

\(\displaystyle D^{r + 1} S (0) (Dz (0), \ldots, Dz (0)) .\)

It follows that the reduced order-\((r + 1)\) jet has the same affine relation. Equivalently, one may subtract the three chain-rule expansions: every common lower-jet term cancels, and the displayed term is the only possible difference.

Cubic path terms vanish through order two, and quartic terms through order three. The quadratic action depends only on the common row. Thus the preceding calculation with \(r = 2\) proves the affine cubic identity for the reduced actions. The finite-step classical matching equates these jets with the coarse path jets. Subtracting their common coarse-row contribution and using the injectivity of the selected cubic readout gives (12.1). If the input cubic arrays agree, this identity first makes the output cubic arrays agree. The calculation with \(r = 3\) then applies; subtracting the common coarse-row and cubic contributions gives (12.2) by the quartic readout.

For the loop identity the classical action, including its cubic and quartic terms, is the same for all three inputs. The implicit function theorem and the invertibility of \(H\) give the same stationary germ. Its Hessian determinant and the coordinate Jacobian contribution are therefore the same. The remaining one-loop expression is the loop correction evaluated on that germ; it is linear in \(a^{\text{in}}\). Its quadratic jet is affine. On the torus of side \(n = n_m (p)\), with site set \(\Lambda_n\), the coarse one-loop decomposition of the quadratic jet has the form

\(\displaystyle \alpha Q_{0, p} + Q_{a, p} +\mathcal{E}_p,\)

where \(Q_{0, p}\) is the quadratic form of the common normalized row, \(Q_{a, p} = T_{m, 2, p} a^{\text{out}}\) is the loop readout, and \(\mathcal{E}_p\) is the finite-period error. The error has the exponential bound of the finite step: on every pair of real plane waves whose amplitudes have entries of modulus at most one, its modulus is at most \(| \Lambda_n | \Gamma e^{- cn}\). Form the combination, with coefficients \(1, - (1 - t), - t\), of the three decompositions indexed by \(t, 0, 1\). The left sides cancel, because the quadratic jet of the one-loop expression is affine. With

\(\displaystyle \delta \alpha = \alpha_t - \alpha_0 - t (\alpha_1 - \alpha_0), \qquad \delta a = a^{\text{out}}_t - a^{\text{out}}_0 - t (a^{\text{out}}_1 - a^{\text{out}}_0),\)

the forms \(G_p = \delta \alpha \hspace{0.17em} Q_{0, p} + Q_{\delta a, p}\) therefore satisfy the same plane-wave bound with \(\Gamma\) replaced by \((2 + 2| t|) \Gamma\).

Both families \(Q_{0, p}\) and \(Q_{a, p}\) are recorded by a symbol. By this we mean that there is a matrix-valued function of the momentum, holomorphic and bounded on a strip of positive width about the real momenta and independent of \(p\), such that on every prescribed period the coefficient of the form on two complex plane waves is zero unless their momenta are opposite, and is then \(| \Lambda_n |\) times the entry of the symbol at that momentum. For \(Q_{0, p}\) the symbol is built from the common bed metric and its strip bound; for \(Q_{a, p}\) it is the loop symbol of the path, linear in \(a\), with the path width. Hence \(G_p\) is recorded by \(\hat{G} = \delta \alpha \hspace{0.17em} \hat{Q}_0 + \hat{Q}_{\delta a}\).

The all-period detection statement is the following. Let forms \(G_p\) on the prescribed periods be recorded by one symbol \(\hat{G}\) as above, and suppose their values on pairs of real plane waves with amplitude entries of modulus at most one are bounded by \(| \Lambda_n | b_p\) with \(b_p \to 0\). Then \(\hat{G}\) vanishes at every real momentum, and \(G_p = 0\) for every \(p\). For the proof, a complex plane wave is a combination of two real ones with coefficients \(1\) and \(i\), so the recorded coefficients are at most \(4 | \Lambda_n | b_p\) and each symbol entry is at most \(4 b_p\) at every momentum of the torus of side \(n_m (p)\). Fix a real momentum \(k\). Since \(n_m (p) \to \infty\), there are momenta \(k_p\) of these tori converging to \(k\) modulo the reciprocal lattice. Continuity of \(\hat{G}\) gives \(\hat{G} (k) = \lim_p \hat{G} (k_p) = 0\). The recorded coefficients of each \(G_p\) are values of \(\hat{G}\) at real momenta, so they all vanish; plane waves span the complexified field space, whence \(G_p = 0\).

Apply this to the forms \(G_p\) above, with \(b_p = (2 + 2| t|) \Gamma e^{- cn_m (p)}\). The normalization functional \(\mathcal{N}\) is defined from the symbol alone. Fix a color index \(i\), and let \(c_i (\hat{Q})\) be the second-order Taylor coefficient at zero momentum, along the coordinate direction \(0\), of the diagonal entry of the symbol with link direction \(1\) and colors \(i, i\); put \(\mathcal{N} (\hat{Q}) = 2 \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} c_i (\hat{Q})\). It is real linear. The normalized row has \(c_i (\hat{Q}_0) = 1 / 2\), so \(\mathcal{N} (\hat{Q}_0) = 1\), and \(\mathcal{N}\) vanishes on the loop symbol of every array satisfying the loop normalization conditions. Applying it to \(\hat{G} = 0\) gives \(\delta \alpha = 0\), which is the identity for \(\alpha\). The residual loop readouts \(Q_{\delta a, p} = T_{m, 2, p} \delta a\) then vanish on every prescribed period, and \(\delta a \in A^{\text{sel}}_{m, 2}\); injectivity on the selected loop subspace gives \(\delta a = 0\), the identity for \(a^{\text{out}}\). This identifies exactly where quadratic normalization and all-period detection enter the proof.

12.1.3An enlarged input ball gives contraction

Choose one of the three arrays, subject to equality of its preceding arrays in the order \(b, c, a\). Write \(s' = s_{m + 1, q}\) and \(s = s_{m, q}\). Let \(x, y\) be two ordinary inputs and put \(z =\mathfrak{n}_{m + 1, q} (y - x)\). If \(z = 0\), then \(x = y\), and the affine identity at parameter zero gives equal selected outputs. If \(z > 0\), use the real parameter

\(\displaystyle t = 32 s' / z.\)

The replacement \(x + t (y - x)\) has norm at most

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{n}_{m + 1, q} (x) + tz \leqslant 33 s' \leqslant 64 s' .\)

Keeping the other two arrays fixed preserves their invariant subspaces and bounds. In the loop case it also preserves the linear loop normalization constraints. Thus this is an allowed extended input. Its output and that of \(x\) both have norm at most \(s\). By the affine identity, their difference is \(t\) times the difference of the original two outputs. Hence

\(\displaystyle t\mathfrak{n}_{m, q} (y^{\text{out}} - x^{\text{out}}) \leqslant 2 s, \qquad \frac{\mathfrak{n}_{m, q} (y^{\text{out}} - x^{\text{out}})}{s} \leqslant \frac{1}{16} \frac{z}{s'} \leqslant \frac{1}{4} \frac{z}{s'} . \) (12.4)

We retain the weaker factor \(1 / 4\) used in the history argument. The extrapolated parameter need not lie in \([0, 1]\); the extended input domain is essential for this proof.

12.1.4Passing finite histories to cluster histories

Fix any sequence of terminal cutoffs tending to infinity. At each fixed scale \(m\), the three selected coefficient arrays of the step outputs have Hilbert norms at most the loop allowance and the path allowance, and \(| \alpha_{N, m} | \leqslant D_{m + 1} + | \lambda_G \log L_m |\); both are part of the first hypothesis. These bounds do not depend on \(N\). Enumerate the countably many scales and coefficient coordinates. Successive extraction in bounded real intervals, followed by the diagonal subsequence, gives convergence of all those coordinates and all scalars at once. Finite sums of coordinate squares show that each limiting array has the same Hilbert bound. A closed linear constraint is retained: for a test vector in its orthogonal complement, approximate by a finite-coordinate vector; the uniform Hilbert bound controls the omitted inner product. Thus the limits remain in the selected subspaces.

The one-form rows have their common limit from the retained kernel horizon. The width argument in Subsection 10.13 gives convergence of the path sums at every smaller width. It also gives convergence of every finite chart derivative. Indeed, on a sufficiently small complex chart ball each length-\(\ell\) path and each fixed derivative costs a polynomial in \(\ell\) times \(e^{\epsilon \ell}\). Choose \(\epsilon\) smaller than the available width difference; the remaining exponential factor sums the polynomial. Termwise differentiation is then justified by uniform absolute convergence.

The limiting eliminated Hessian obeys the same positive lower bound, by passage to the limit in its quadratic form. The analytic implicit function theorem supplies a stationary germ for the limiting action. The derivative recursion above proves convergence of every derivative of the finite stationary germs: inversion of the finite Hessian is continuous on the matrices with this common lower bound, and each \(R_r\) is a finite expression in previously convergent derivatives. The limits also have the same classical matching through degree four. For the one-loop expression, the derivatives of \(\log \det H\) are finite sums of products of \(H^{- 1}\) and derivatives of \(H\). The positive lower bound and the same recursion therefore give convergence of its quadratic jet, including the fixed coordinate Jacobian. All finite-period error inequalities pass to the limit. The row normalization does too. Thus the resulting cluster history satisfies the same local relations and paid output bounds as the finite histories.

The input at scale \(m\) equals the output at scale \(m + 1\) in these cluster histories. Indeed the selected arrays of each path \(R_{N, m + 1}\) are chosen once and used in both of its occurrences, as the output of the step at \(m + 1\) and as the input of the step at \(m\). The equality of input and output arrays is thus an exact finite-history identity for every \(N > m + 2\), and coordinatewise passage to the limit preserves it. All periods and forests needed here have already been fixed by the schedule. The derivative recursion defines their limiting stationary germs from the same limiting path; no independent subsequence is chosen for each germ.

12.1.5Comparison of two cluster histories

Let \(F\) and \(H\) be two cluster histories with the same retained row horizon. A finite-step comparison cannot simply be applied to them as if they came from the same finite cutoff. Instead fix a scale and an array type, with its preceding input arrays equal. Take finite steps tending to \(F\), and replace just this array in each fine input by the corresponding array of \(H\). The replacement is an ordinary admissible input: the bounds and linear invariance constraints hold separately for its three arrays. Construct its output at that very same cutoff. Extract a further subsequence for these modified outputs and their scalar corrections.

The preceding limit argument gives a valid cluster step. Its chosen input array is that of \(H\), and its preceding arrays are also those of \(H\). The affine identity at parameter zero shows that its chosen output array equals that of \(H\). Apply (12.4) before taking the subsequence limit. The weighted norms of the differences converge by the strict-width dominated convergence argument. We obtain

\(\displaystyle g_m \leqslant \tfrac{1}{4} g_{m + 1}, \qquad g_m := \frac{\mathfrak{n}_{m, q} (H_m - F_m)}{s_{m, q}}, \qquad 0 \leqslant g_m \leqslant 2.\)

Iteration gives \(g_m \leqslant 2 \hspace{0.17em} 4^{- h}\) for every integer \(h \geqslant 0\); hence \(g_m = 0\). Apply this first to the cubic arrays at every scale, which require no preceding equality. Then apply it to the quartic arrays, using the cubic equality just obtained. Finally apply it to the loop arrays, using both equalities. All three arrays in the two cluster histories agree.

Their rows already agree, so their full input and output paths agree. The loop identity at parameter zero identifies their scalar corrections. Uniqueness of the stationary germ identifies all its derivatives for every fixed forest and period. We have therefore proved uniqueness of the entire cluster history, including the scalar and stationary data needed in the next scale comparison.

If the original sequence failed to converge to this history in any one coordinate, scalar, or fixed chart derivative, a subsequence would stay outside some neighborhood of its asserted limit. The compactness construction applies to that subsequence and gives a further subsequence with the unique cluster limit, a contradiction. Thus the entire cutoff sequence converges. The strict-width argument upgrades its coefficientwise convergence to the weighted path convergence used for fixed-scale normalized moments. This proves the statement of the second subsection. It is conditional on the four hypotheses listed there: the finite-step construction on the extended domain, the actual history, the widths and the retained row horizon must be supplied on the selected history.

12.2Analytic bounds from real coupling estimates

The parameter estimates used for the background history have a useful feature: they are obtained from real configurations. The local weights contain exponentials whose exponents need not be bounded, and bounding their absolute values before using the real estimates would lose the required smallness. We prove the interpolation argument that avoids this loss. The real local estimates to which it is applied retain their full derivative and support weights.

12.2.1An algebra of parameter series

For a real power series \(f (t) = \sum_{r \geqslant 0} f_r t^r\) and \(R \geqslant 1\), set

\(\displaystyle |f|_R = \sum_{r \geqslant 0} |f_r | R^r .\)

Only series with finite displayed norm will be used. Their products are given by the absolutely convergent Cauchy product, and \(|fg|_R \leqslant |f|_R |g|_R\). Consequently \(| \exp f|_R \leqslant \exp (|f|_R)\). If the constant coefficient \(f_0\) is real, it is better to keep its sign:

\(\displaystyle | \exp f|_R \leqslant e^{f_0} \exp (|f - f_0 |_R) .\) (12.5)

These assertions follow by summing the nonnegative absolute coefficient bounds for \(\sum_{m \geqslant 0} f^m / m!\); absolute convergence also identifies that series with the exponential on \([- R, R]\). For a finite real test \(\lambda = \sum_h a_h \delta_{t_h}\), \(|t_h | \leqslant 1\), put

\(\displaystyle M_R (\lambda) = \sup_{r \geqslant 0} \left| \sum_h a_h (t_h / R)^r \right| .\)

Termwise summation then gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_h a_h f (t_h) \right| \leqslant |f|_R M_R (\lambda) .\) (12.6)

Thus an absolute coefficient estimate supplies every finite-test estimate simultaneously.

Fix a real normed space \(E\), a point \(x \in E\), and an integer \(j \geqslant 0\). For a \(C^j\) function \(V\) near \(x\), write \(D_u^i V (x) = \left. \frac{d^i}{ds^i} V (x + su) \right|_{s = 0}\). A diagonal jet bound with amplitude \(C\) and derivative scale \(d\) means \(|D_u^i V (x) | \leqslant Cd^i\) for \(0 \leqslant i \leqslant j\) and \(\|u\| \leqslant 1\). When \(i = 0\) we use \(d^0 = 1\), including when \(d = 0\). The parameter estimates below hold for each such derivative, with one common amplitude. Conversion to full multilinear derivative norms uses the fixed finite-order polarization constants already included in the local derivative bounds.

12.2.2The larger real nodes

Let \(\ell (t) = a + bt\), and suppose

\(\displaystyle R \geqslant 1, \qquad 1 \leqslant a - 6 R |b|, \qquad a + 6 R |b| \leqslant 1 + \delta, \qquad \delta \leqslant 1.\) (12.7)

In particular \(0 \leqslant \delta \leqslant 1\), and \(1 \leqslant \ell (t) \leqslant 1 + \delta\) whenever \(|t| \leqslant 6 R\). Set \(p (t) = 1 - \ell (t)^2\). For every real \(q\) define

\(\displaystyle A (q) = (1 - a^2) q + 2 a |bq| R + b^2 |q| R^2 .\)

By (12.5),

\(\displaystyle | \ell |_R \leqslant 1 + \delta, \qquad | \exp (pq) |_R \leqslant e^{A (q)} .\) (12.8)

The first inequality uses \(a \geqslant 1\) and \(a + R |b| \leqslant a + 6 R |b|\). No estimate on \(|q|\) is required.

Choose \(\sigma = - 1\) if \(qb \geqslant 0\), and \(\sigma = 1\) otherwise. For \(r = 0, \ldots, 2 j\), put

\(\displaystyle u_r = 3 + \frac{r}{2 j + 1}, \qquad \xi_r = \sigma u_r, \qquad t_r = R \xi_r .\)

These are distinct real points in \([- 6 R, 6 R]\). At every one of them,

\(\displaystyle A (q) \leqslant p (t_r) q.\) (12.9)

Here is the sign-sensitive estimate. Put \(d_{\ast} = R |b|\); then \(a \geqslant 1 + 6 d_{\ast}\) and \(qbR \sigma = - |q| d_{\ast}\). For any \(3 \leqslant u \leqslant 6\), we have

\(\displaystyle 2 ad_{\ast} + d_{\ast}^2 \leqslant 3 ad_{\ast} \leqslant ad_{\ast} u \leqslant 2 ad_{\ast} u - d_{\ast}^2 u^2 .\)

The last inequality is \(d_{\ast} u \leqslant a\), multiplied by \(d_{\ast} u\); the other two follow from \(d_{\ast} \leqslant a\) and \(u \geqslant 3\). Expanding \(p (R \sigma u) q\), and using \(q \leqslant |q|\), now gives

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} p (R \sigma u) q & = (1 - a^2) q + 2 a |q| d_{\ast} u - qd_{\ast}^2 u^2\\ & \geqslant (1 - a^2) q + |q| (2 ad_{\ast} u - d_{\ast}^2 u^2) \geqslant A (q) . \end{aligned}\)

This proves (12.9) for either sign of \(q\).

12.2.3Interpolating the local weight with its derivatives

Let \(L, \Gamma : E \to \mathbb{R}\) be \(C^j\) near \(x\), and let \(n\) be a nonnegative integer. Consider

\(\displaystyle J (t, y) = L (y) \ell (t)^n e^{p (t) \Gamma (y)} .\) (12.10)

Choose the preceding nodes with \(q = \Gamma (x)\). Suppose that at those \(2 j + 1\) nodes the real local estimates give

\(\displaystyle |D_u^i J (t_r, x) | \leqslant Cd^i \quad (0 \leqslant i \leqslant j, \|u\| \leqslant 1), \qquad C, d \geqslant 0.\) (12.11)

There is a constant \(K_j\), depending only on \(j\), such that each function \(t \mapsto D_u^i J (t, x)\) has an absolutely convergent series with

\(\displaystyle |D_u^i J (\cdot, x) |_R \leqslant K_j (1 + \delta)^n Cd^i .\) (12.12)

In particular this controls all finite tests by (12.6). The constant is independent of \(a, b, R, n, C, d\) and of the value of \(\Gamma (x)\).

We prove the assertion, including the reason that only finitely many real nodes suffice. Differentiating along \(x + su\) and factoring out the exponential gives

\(\displaystyle D_u^i J (t, x) = P_{i, u} (t) \ell (t)^n e^{p (t) \Gamma (x)}, \qquad \deg P_{i, u} \leqslant 2 i.\) (12.13)

Indeed, for the exponential alone its \(i\)th derivative is the exponential times a sum, over partitions of \(\{1, \ldots, i\}\), of products \(\prod_B p (t) D_u^{|B|} \Gamma (x)\). Each product has at most \(i\) factors \(p (t)\), each of degree two. Leibniz's rule with \(L\) gives (12.13), also for \(i = 0\). No division by \(L\) or by a derivative of \(\Gamma\) occurs.

Let \(\mathcal{L}_r (z)\) be the Lagrange basis polynomials for the nodes \(\xi_0, \ldots, \xi_{2 j}\). Polynomial interpolation gives

\(\displaystyle P_{i, u} (t) = \sum_{r = 0}^{2 j} P_{i, u} (t_r) \mathcal{L}_r (t / R) .\)

Define \(K_j\) as the larger, for the two possible signs \(\sigma\), of \(\sum_r |\mathcal{L}_r |_1\). This is finite, since all the nodes are distinct. Also \(|\mathcal{L}_r (\cdot / R) |_R = |\mathcal{L}_r |_1\). By (12.11), (12.13), and \(\ell (t_r)^n \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle |P_{i, u} (t_r) | e^{p (t_r) q} \leqslant Cd^i .\)

Multiplying each interpolation term by \(\ell (t)^n e^{p (t) q}\) and using (12.8) and (12.9), its coefficient norm is at most

\(\displaystyle |P_{i, u} (t_r) | (1 + \delta)^n e^{A (q)} |\mathcal{L}_r |_1 \leqslant (1 + \delta)^n Cd^i |\mathcal{L}_r |_1 .\)

Summing proves (12.12). Notice that the node sign may depend on \(\Gamma (x)\). Both sets of nodes belong to the same allowed real interval, and \(K_j\) controls either choice; there is no need to differentiate the choice of sign.

12.2.4The Gaussian density and the local support count

For a fixed Gaussian coordinate \(z\), let \(Q (z) \geqslant 0\) be its quadratic energy in the reference density. Under the rescaling \(z \mapsto \ell (t) z\) in \(D\) real dimensions, the density ratio, including the Jacobian, is

\(\displaystyle g (t, z) = \ell (t)^D e^{(1 - \ell (t)^2) Q (z)} .\)

The same argument, now with \(q = Q (z) \geqslant 0\), yields

\(\displaystyle |g (\cdot, z) |_R \leqslant (1 + \delta)^D .\) (12.14)

Indeed (12.9) gives \(A (q) \leqslant p (t_r) q \leqslant 0\), since \(\ell (t_r) \geqslant 1\). The bound is uniform in \(z\). Thus it can be multiplied into a local coefficient majorant before integration against the fixed reference Gaussian measure.

In the local application, the exponent \(n\) in (12.10) counts the seed coordinates actually present in the local weight, each with its color dimension. The Gaussian dimension \(D\) is likewise that of the relevant marginal. These counts are determined by the local support; replacing them by the dimension of the entire torus would destroy a bound uniform in volume. The exact local change-of-density identity is used before applying (12.14). The remaining support-dependent powers \((1 + \delta)^{n + D}\) are included in the cover-bound reserve.

12.2.5Keeping the small amplitude of ordinary activities

There is a second, complementary estimate. Let \(f : \mathbb{R} \times E \to \mathbb{R}\) be real-valued, and suppose that \(t \mapsto f (t, y)\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(k\) on \([- 1, 1]\) for every \(y\), where \(k\) is fixed. Choose distinct fixed nodes \(s_0, \ldots, s_k \in [- 1, 1]\). Suppose that each \(f (s_r, \cdot)\) is \(C^j\) near \(x\) and that

\(\displaystyle |D_u^i (e^{f (s_r, \cdot)} - 1) (x) | \leqslant vd^i, \qquad 0 \leqslant v \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2} .\) (12.15)

There are constants \(A, B \geqslant 1\), depending only on \(j, k\) and the nodes, such that, whenever \(BR^k v \leqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle |D_u^i (e^{f (\cdot, \cdot)} - 1) (x) |_R \leqslant AR^k vd^i .\) (12.16)

In this formula the derivative is in the second variable and the series norm in the first. The series norm refers to the polynomial extension: for each \(y\) let \(\hat{f} (\cdot, y)\) be the polynomial of degree at most \(k\) which agrees with \(f (\cdot, y)\) on \([- 1, 1]\), and read (12.16) for \(e^{\hat{f}} - 1\). Its series represents \(e^f - 1\) at every \(t \in [- 1, 1]\), which is where the finite tests are taken. Nothing is asserted about the values of \(f\) at parameters outside \([- 1, 1]\).

To check the estimate, write \(h = e^{f (s_r, \cdot)} - 1\) at a fixed node. At \(x\) we have \(|h (x) | \leqslant v \leqslant 1 / 2\) and \(f (s_r, \cdot) = \log (1 + h)\) near \(x\). The zeroth derivative is bounded by \(2 v\), by integrating \(1 / (1 + s)\) between \(0\) and \(h (x)\). For a positive-order derivative, the chain rule is a finite sum over partitions, with one derivative of \(\log (1 + s)\) and products of derivatives of \(h\). The former is bounded on \([- 1 / 2, 1 / 2]\); a term with \(r \geqslant 1\) factors of \(h\) is bounded by a constant times \(v^r d^i \leqslant vd^i\). Hence \(|D_u^i f (s_r, \cdot) (x) | \leqslant C_j vd^i\) at every node.

Polynomial interpolation in \(t\) at the nodes \(s_r\) gives \(\hat{f} (t, y) = \sum_r f (s_r, y) \mathcal{L}_r (t)\) for every \(y\) near \(x\), with the Lagrange polynomials \(\mathcal{L}_r\) of these nodes, and so also after the indicated derivatives. In the rest of the proof \(f\) stands for \(\hat{f}\). The coefficient norm of any fixed degree-\(k\) Lagrange basis polynomial at radius \(R \geqslant 1\) is bounded by \(R^k\) times its norm at radius one. It follows that

\(\displaystyle |D_u^i f (\cdot, x) |_R \leqslant C_{\ast} R^k vd^i .\)

Choose \(B \geqslant C_{\ast}\). Put \(\varepsilon = C_{\ast} R^k v \leqslant 1\). In the Banach algebra of parameter series, the zeroth-order estimate \(|e^f - 1|_R \leqslant e^{\varepsilon} - 1 \leqslant e \varepsilon\) follows from the exponential series. For \(i > 0\), the same partition formula for derivatives of \(e^f\) gives a factor \(|e^f |_R \leqslant e\) and products bounded by \(\varepsilon^r d^i \leqslant \varepsilon d^i\). The finite sum gives (12.16), with \(A\) large enough for all \(i \leqslant j\). This proof also covers \(d = 0\): all positive derivatives then vanish.

The incoming ordinary logarithmic occurrence in the coupling family has degree at most two in this real parameter; the function to which (12.16) is applied is its exponential minus one. Thus the radius cost in this last argument is \(R^2\), with constants fixed by the derivative order. The local stock weight uses the preceding real-node argument instead, because its exponent need not be small. Together with the local real jet estimates, the fixed Gaussian change of density, and the summable support bounds, these two arguments provide the parameter estimates for the finite forest integrals. Summation over connected supports and the subsequent local projections still require their stated quantitative bounds; the interpolation proved here does not replace those bounds.

12.3Summable parameter estimates for the sector expansion

We now combine the real-parameter interpolation with the local Gaussian and connected-support estimates. The important point is that one uses the same supports, labels and protected fine reads throughout the real parameter family. A separate estimate at each parameter, with an unrelated choice of labels, would not bound a finite linear combination of the resulting activities.

Fix the group, the block side \(L\), a derivative order \(j\), a nonnegative integer \(m\) measuring the spatial weight in the derivative budget, and all the positive safety and support reserves. Write \(y = \beta_f (x)\) for the reference fine coupling, which tends to infinity with the scale parameter \(x\). A nonnegative function has upper exponent \(a\) below if, for each \(a' > a\), it is eventually at most \(y^{a'}\). Finite sums, products and fixed powers have the corresponding upper-exponent bounds. Constants, local cover counts, and all the fixed-order derivative factors of upper exponent zero may therefore be absorbed using a strict exponent reserve.

The incoming sector is assumed to satisfy the local hypotheses of Sections 8.28 and 8.26, uniformly for all real points \(|t| \leqslant 6 R\) in the coupling band. Uniformity is meant in the following sense. The quantitative local data of those sections (the local jets of the four species, the safety inequality, the compensated chart and Jacobian bounds with their derivative scales, and the smoothness of the local Gaussian density) are given with one set of constants for the whole family, and not by a separate choice at each \(t\). The order of choices is: first the lower bound on the block side; then the block side, the stage orders, the positive reserves and the other fixed data listed above; then one threshold in \(x\), valid for all allowed periods, sectors and real parameter points. The path, clipping rule, ordinary and forced support dictionaries, and held fine configurations are common. The coupling is \(\beta (t) = \beta_0 \ell (t)^2\), where (12.7) holds. The incoming ordinary activity values and forced activity values on each fixed support are polynomials of degree at most two on \([- 1, 1]\). We take

\(\displaystyle R = y^{1 / 256}, \qquad \delta = y^{- 1 / 128} .\) (12.17)

The geometric radius \(B (x)\) and its ball count \(b_B = (2 B + 1)^4\) have upper exponent zero. Fix the incoming support exponent \(E\) and the desired outgoing exponent \(e\) with \(E > e \geqslant 0\). All estimates below are eventually uniform in the allowed periods, sectors and configurations. The threshold is chosen after the fixed data just listed. Construction of those incoming sectors and of their uniform safety estimates is still required; the purpose of this section is to prove the analytic output estimates from them.

12.3.1Common bounds before integration

The original optional-activity rooted allowance has upper exponent \(a \leqslant - 7 / 16\). To obtain one bound valid along the parameter family, sum the nonnegative bounds at the three nodes \(- 1, 0, 1\) for each ordinary and forced label. The sum over all such labels through a root increases by at most a factor three, and each individual bound is at most three times the old rooted allowance. This last assertion uses nonempty supports and nonnegative support weights.

The complete local logarithmic factor is still polynomial of degree at most two: the old vacuum and action terms either acquire the factor \(\ell (t)^2\) or remain constant, the incoming regular occurrence is quadratic by hypothesis, and the enforcement factor uses held fine reads and is parameter-independent. The forced local factor likewise is its incoming quadratic value multiplied by a fixed enforcement factor. Apply the small-activity estimate of Subsection 12.2 to the ordinary exponential-minus-one factors. Its smallness conditions are satisfied eventually: if \(g (x)\) is the incoming allowance, then \(3 g (x) \to 0\) and \(R^2 g (x)\) has negative upper exponent. For a forced factor use ordinary degree-two interpolation at the same three nodes; there is no logarithm to take. The resulting common bounds cost at most a fixed constant times \(1 + R^2\). Their upper exponent is at most

\(\displaystyle a + \frac{2}{256} \leqslant - \frac{55}{128} < - \frac{2}{5} .\)

Thus both are paid by an enlarged target with upper exponent \(- 2 / 5\), after increasing the scale threshold. This explains the specific reserve in (12.17).

For seed packets, use the larger-real-node estimate with the full stock weight. If a packet contains \(s \geqslant 1\) seeds, its real bound has the form

\(\displaystyle \exp \{(C\mathfrak{d}^5 - \beta \mu \tau^2) s\},\)

where \(\mathfrak{d} \geqslant 1\) is the local geometric control function. Its stock-coordinate count satisfies \(n \leqslant C_1 s\mathfrak{d}^5\). The interpolation factor is \(K_j (1 + \delta)^n\). Since \(0 \leqslant \delta \leqslant 1\), \((1 + \delta)^n \leqslant e^n\), and \(K_j \leqslant e^{K_j}\) for \(K_j \geqslant 0\). Hence increasing \(C\) to \(C + C_1 + K_j\) absorbs the entire factor, because \(s\mathfrak{d}^5 \geqslant 1\). The negative packet energy is unchanged. The absorption and root-count argument of Subsection 8.25 consequently still applies.

Warnings are constant in this parameter when evaluated with the specified held fine reads and rescaled fluctuation coordinate. Their coarse-field jets retain the common reduced-margin excursion indicator of Subsection 8.28. No derivative of that indicator is taken. Products of the four species thus have one common nonnegative coefficient majorant, with the same excursion event and the same support set as before interpolation.

There is one further factor from the local Gaussian ratio and the local stock coordinate count. Let \(I (S)\) be the set of fine bonds read by the local term with coarse support \(S\), and let \(d_G\) be the real color dimension. Its combined power is bounded by \(2 |I (S) | d_G\). If \(c_B (S)\) is the coarse cover number, then

\(\displaystyle |I (S) | \leqslant |S| \hspace{0.17em} \text{dens} (L) \leqslant c_B (S) b_B \text{dens} (L), \qquad b_B = (2 B + 1)^4 .\)

For any fixed \(\alpha > 0\), (12.17) gives, eventually, \(2 \delta b_B \text{dens} (L) d_G \leqslant \alpha\). Using \(1 + \delta \leqslant e^{\delta}\) proves

\(\displaystyle (1 + \delta)^{2 |I (S) | d_G} \leqslant e^{\alpha c_B (S)} .\) (12.18)

Only the dimension of the local read set has been charged. The factor is paid by a support-exponent reserve of size \(\alpha\).

12.3.2The moment estimate after a finite parameter test

Let \(A\) be a connected finite set of primitive labels. Denote its assigned union support by \(S_A\) and its warning set by \(W_A\). Its shape charge is the total cost of Subsection 8.2,

\(\displaystyle s_A = \sum_{l \in A} (c_B (S_l) + d_l),\)

where \(S_l\) is the support of the label \(l\) and \(d_l \geqslant 0\) its extra cost: the purchased fixed multiple of its number of seeds for a packet, and the purchased optional cost for an optional label. Let \(a_l \geqslant 0\) be the common primitive bound for label \(l\), with its warning gain included only after Gaussian integration. Put \(P_A = 1 + s_A \geqslant 1\). The estimates just proved, the compensated Jacobian estimates, and the product rule give a coefficient bound for every derivative through order \(j\) of the local integrand. After a finite test \(\lambda\), its norm is bounded by

\(\displaystyle M_R (\lambda) \hspace{0.17em} C_j e^{\alpha c_B (S_A)} \left( \prod_{l \in A} a_l^{\circ} \right) P_A^j \hspace{0.17em} \boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{E}_A} (z) \hspace{0.17em} F_{I (A)} (z)^{Kj} .\) (12.19)

Here \(a_l^{\circ}\) is the bound before inserting the warning probability gain, \(\mathcal{E}_A\) is the joint reduced-margin excursion event, \(F_{I (A)} \geqslant 1\) is the Gaussian coordinate control function on the local read set, and \(K\) is the fixed Jacobian polynomial order. The constant \(C_j\) in this display may be replaced by a nonnegative function of upper exponent zero; this includes the compensated derivative scales. More explicitly, if those scales are \(r\) and \(L' F_{I (A)}^K\), their product-rule cost is bounded by

\(\displaystyle \{1 + (2^K L' + 2 r)\}^j P_A^j F_{I (A)}^{Kj} .\)

Indeed \(F_{I (A)}, P_A \geqslant 1\) and \(s_A \leqslant P_A\), so \(1 + 2^K L' F_{I (A)}^K + 2 rs_A\) is at most \(\{1 + (2^K L' + 2 r)\} P_A F_{I (A)}^K\). Fixed polarization constants are included in \(C_j\).

For clarity, the probabilistic step in this estimate is as follows. The local Gaussian moment calculation in Subsection 8.27 supplies an integer \(k\) and a function \(V (x)\) of upper exponent zero such that

\(\displaystyle \left( \int F_{I (A)}^{2 Kj} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu \right)^{1 / 2} \leqslant (1 + |V (x) |) P_A^k .\)

The common warning estimate is \(\nu (\mathcal{E}_A) \leqslant \rho^{2 |W_A |}\). Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \int \boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{E}_A} F_{I (A)}^{Kj} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu \leqslant (1 + |V (x) |) P_A^k \rho^{|W_A |} .\)

This is the source of the warning bound in \(a_l\); no independence of the warning events is assumed. Combining with (12.19), and then averaging the finitely many forest and tagging choices, gives the derivative bound

\(\displaystyle p_A = \pi (x) e^{\alpha c_B (S_A)} \left( \prod_{l \in A} a_l \right) P_A^{k + j},\) (12.20)

where \(\pi\) has upper exponent zero and is independent of \(\lambda\). The bound on the tested derivative is \(M_R (\lambda) p_A\).

Passing derivatives through the integral is justified by the nearby integrable bounds in Subsection 8.26. There are only finitely many parameter points in a test, so finite summation commutes with derivatives and integrals. A finite average over forests preserves the same bound. On healthy supports the protected gauge average equals the unaveraged local expression as a germ: the safety plateau is open and the gauge change leaves the protected expression invariant. Thus the estimate is for the actual coarse activity, including its derivatives. An inactive support gives the zero germ. All of these comparisons use the common support dictionary, not a parameter-dependent relabeling.

The mixed connected-set estimate (8.10) of Subsection 8.2, applied to the enlarged primitive bounds, gives

\(\displaystyle \sup_z \sum_{A : z \in S_A} p_A e^{(E - \alpha) c_B (S_A)} \leqslant \pi (x) u_{k + j} (x) .\) (12.21)

Here \(E\) is the incoming support exponent. For an integer \(n \geqslant 0\), \(u_n (x)\) denotes the connected allowance of the enlarged target with polynomial order \(n\): the right side of (8.11) for that target, with no restriction on the label count, the total cost or the number of seeds. The vertex condition of that estimate is the purchase of the enlarged target made in the first subsection; the factor \(P_A^{k + j} = (1 + s_A)^{k + j}\) in \(p_A\) is its polynomial in the total cost. The allowance \(u_{k + j}\) has upper exponent \(- 2 / 5\). To see the support bookkeeping, the left side contains \(e^{\alpha c_B (S_A)} e^{(E - \alpha) c_B (S_A)} = e^{Ec_B (S_A)}\). Thus the mixed connected-set estimate is applied with its original support weight, and its shape moment is exactly \(k + j\). This invokes the same connected-set and moment bound used for the non-parameter sector, with the enlarged primitive bounds just constructed.

12.3.3The second connected expansion

Recover an absolute parameter series for each derivative from the finite-test estimate with radius \(R\). Subsection 12.7 gives radius \(R / 2\) and bound \(2 p_A\), once \(R \geqslant 2\). The argument can first be applied to each scalar derivative; the fixed-order polarization bound controls the full derivative. The derivatives of the outgoing budget are iterated group increments in directions of norm at most the threshold \(\theta\), whereas the local estimates are stated in the compensated charts. The passage between the two is part of the uniform local hypotheses: appending one such increment increases the controlled chart scale by at most a fixed multiple \(c_v\) of the threshold scale, and the chart scale \(v\) is chosen with \(jc_v \leqslant v\), as in Subsection 9.10. Thus the at most \(j\) increments of a derivative of the budget stay inside the chart estimates used above. A logarithmic cluster with \(q\) occurrences is a product of these primitive integrated activities times its Ursell coefficient and \(1 / q\)!. Derivatives through order \(j\) cost at most \(q^j\). Since \(q \leqslant 2^q\) for \(q \geqslant 1\), this is paid by multiplying each vertex bound by \(2^j\). Consequently the absolute cluster bound is

\(\displaystyle w_{\Gamma} = \frac{|u (\Gamma) |}{q!} \prod_{r = 1}^q (2^{j + 1} p_{A_r}) .\)

This includes repeated labels and their tuple factorial exactly as in Subsection 8.1.

Fix the desired outgoing exponent \(e\) with \(0 \leqslant e < E\), and set \(\alpha = (E - e) / 4 > 0\). Put

\(\displaystyle Y (x) = (1 + 2^{j + 1} \pi (x)) u_{k + j} (x) .\)

Equation (12.21) pays the enlarged vertex bounds at exponent \(e + 2 \alpha\), because \(e + 2 \alpha \leqslant E - \alpha\). The connected-tree degree criterion is

\(\displaystyle \frac{4 b_B Y (x)}{\alpha} \leqslant \frac{1}{2} .\)

It holds eventually: \(b_B\) has upper exponent zero and \(Y\) has upper exponent \(- 2 / 5\). The cluster estimate then gives

\(\displaystyle \sup_z \sum_{\Gamma : z \in S_{\Gamma}} w_{\Gamma} e^{(e + \alpha) c_B (S_{\Gamma})} \leqslant 2 Y (x) .\)

The spatially weighted derivative budget at a root costs at most \(\{s_B (1 + c_B (S_{\Gamma}))\}^{mj}\), where \(s_B = 4 (2 B + 1)\). To verify the geometry, connect the balls of a minimal connected cover. A path through at most \(c_B (S_{\Gamma})\) balls bounds the distance between two sites of \(S_{\Gamma}\) by \(c_B (S_{\Gamma}) (4 B + 2) + 2 B\), which is at most \(s_B (1 + c_B (S_{\Gamma}))\). Locality allows the field variation to be restricted to this support. The weighted direction norm on that support is therefore increased by the stated distance factor, and taking at most \(j\) derivatives gives the power \(mj\). For every fixed integer \(d \geqslant 0\),

\(\displaystyle P_{\alpha, d} := \sup_{r \geqslant 0} (1 + r)^d e^{- \alpha r} < \infty .\)

For example, expand \((1 + r)^d\) and bound each \(r^l\) by \(l! \alpha^{- l} e^{\alpha r}\), using the exponential series. The extra spatial factor is thus paid by the remaining \(\alpha\) reserve. Including the fixed polarization factor \(C_j'\) we obtain the final rooted derivative bound

\(\displaystyle 2 C_j' s_B^{mj} P_{\alpha, mj} Y (x) .\) (12.22)

It has upper exponent \(- 2 / 5\). Since \(- 2 / 5 < - 3 / 8\), it is eventually at most \(y^{- 3 / 8}\). This is the ordinary parameter-test allowance at radius \(R / 2\), on the actual connected-cluster dictionary.

12.3.4The forced common majorant

For forced terms no coarse derivatives are required. The same finite-test and radius-halving argument gives ordinary constituent series bounds with rooted allowance \(2 u_0 (x)\) and forced constituent bounds with rooted allowance \(2 u_0 (x) e^{- M (x)}\). The nonnegative mark \(M (x)\) is the seed mark of the enlarged target: the mark per seed which the mixed estimate (8.11) extracts once for every seed of a component, and which is paid from the seed expense of the packet bounds. It is available here because every forced constituent has a nonempty flag inventory, each flag supplies a seed in one of its packets, and hence its components contain at least one seed. It is distinct from the warning probability gain \(\rho^{|W_A |}\), which has already been included in the primitive bounds \(a_l\) and is present in ordinary and forced constituents alike. Form the exclusion-correction activities exactly as in Subsection 8.31. Each is a finite product of forced factors and powers of regular cluster factors, with the same multiplicity factorials. Absolute coefficient norms multiply, so (8.234) is also a majorant for the whole parameter series at radius \(R / 2\).

The remaining eligibility and flag indicators depend on the fixed coarse configuration and common supports, and are independent of the parameter. Multiplication by such an indicator only decreases the coefficient bound. This yields one majorant per final support, valid for all coarse configurations, rather than a separate majorant selected after evaluating a configuration.

\(\displaystyle \sup_z \sum_{D : z \in S_D} k_D e^{ec_B (S_D)} \leqslant e^{- M (x)} \frac{Z (x)}{1 - 1 / 2} .\) (12.23)

This keeps the compulsory exponential seed gain through arbitrarily many regular exclusion corrections. In particular, the parameter extension has not consumed that gain.

12.4Recovering a local density from five auxiliary periods

The local projection in the nonlinear step uses five small periodic boxes. Their side lengths differ by two. A fourth finite difference cancels all the contributions from folded boundary faces and retains the interior contribution. We give the construction, since both the exact identity and its derivative cost matter for the parameter estimates.

12.4.1Folded fields and boundary types

For an integer \(w \geqslant 1\), fold the cyclic set \(\mathbb{Z}/ (2 w) \mathbb{Z}\) onto \(\{0, \ldots, w\}\) by

\(\displaystyle \pi_w (q) = \min (\bar{q}, 2 w - \bar{q}), \qquad 0 \leqslant \bar{q} < 2 w.\)

Its two endpoints have one preimage each and its other points have two. Apply this map in each of the four coordinates. Adjacent sites map to adjacent sites, with direction either preserved or reversed. For a gauge field \(U\) on a larger torus and an anchor \(o\), the folded field \(\Phi_{w, o} U\) on the side-\(2 w\) torus reads the link of \(U\) between \(o + \pi_w (q)\) and \(o + \pi_w (q + e_{\mu})\); a reversed link is read as its inverse. Here and below a lattice point is reduced modulo the large period when used to index \(U\).

Fix \(r \geqslant 1\) and \(w \geqslant 2 r\). In one coordinate, give a folded point \(p \in \{0, \ldots, w\}\) its boundary type:

\(\displaystyle \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} (\text{left}, p), & p < r,\\ (\text{right}, w - p), & w - p < r,\\ \text{interior}, & r \leqslant p \leqslant w - r. \end{array} \right.\)

The cases are disjoint. For an offset \(s\) with \(|s| \leqslant r\), the folded displacement from \(p\) is respectively

\(\displaystyle |p + s| - p, \qquad d - |d - s| (d = w - p), \qquad s.\) (12.24)

These identities follow directly from the two affine branches of \(\pi_w\). They also describe links in a local box when both endpoints are in the box. Thus, after moving the anchor to cancel the folded base point, the local field near a site depends only on its four boundary types. The displacement formulas do not depend on \(w\). An all-interior type gives the original, unfolded field near the chosen anchor.

For a left or right type of depth \(d \in \{0, \ldots, r - 1\}\), the number of cyclic points of that type is \(1\) if \(d = 0\) and \(2\) otherwise. The interior count is

\(\displaystyle 2 (w - 2 r + 1) .\)

Indeed its folded coordinates are \(r, \ldots, w - r\), all strictly between the two endpoints, and each has two preimages. In four dimensions the number of sites of a prescribed type vector is the product of its four one-dimensional counts.

Let \(\mathcal{T}_r\) be the finite set of four-coordinate type vectors, and let \(\tau_{\ast}\) be the all-interior type. For any function \(H : \mathcal{T}_r \to \mathbb{R}\), the preceding count proves

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i = 0}^4 c_i \sum_{q \in (\mathbb{Z}/ 2 (w + i)\mathbb{Z})^4} H (\text{type}_{w + i, r} (q)) = H (\tau_{\ast}), \qquad c_i = \frac{(- 1)^i \binom{4}{i}}{2^4 \cdot 4!} .\) (12.25)

To verify this exactly, fix a type vector with \(d\) interior coordinates. Its multiplicity at side \(2 (w + i)\) is a polynomial in \(i\) of degree \(d\). The fourth difference annihilates that polynomial when \(d < 4\). For the all-interior type the polynomial is \(2^4 (w + i - 2 r + 1)^4\), whose fourth difference is \(2^4 \cdot 4\)!. Division by that factor gives one. Summing over the finitely many type vectors proves (12.25). In particular, no boundary term has been discarded before cancellation.

12.4.2Compatible local densities

Let \(\Lambda\) be the site set of the large torus. Write \(f_{\Lambda} (o, U)\) for a density on the large torus and \(f_i (q, V)\), \(0 \leqslant i \leqslant 4\), for densities on the five auxiliary tori of sides \(2 (w + i)\). The compatibility conditions needed here are explicit: the auxiliary densities transform covariantly under signed lattice symmetries, the values of any two of them on globally healthy fields agree whenever their centered radius-\(r\) link patterns agree, and the same agreement holds between each of them and \(f_{\Lambda}\) on globally healthy fields. Finally \(f_{\Lambda} (o, \cdot)\) is local on the radius-\(r\) neighbourhood of \(o\). The large period is at least \(4 r + 16\). These are compatibility of local functions at fixed coupling and fixed path; no equality of partition functions on different periods is required.

Set \(\tilde{f}_i (q, V) = f_i (q, V) - f_i (q, \boldsymbol{1})\) and define \(\tilde{f}_{\Lambda}\) in the same way. The compatibility properties persist after this centering: the identity fields are healthy and have identical local link patterns. Define

\(\displaystyle g (o, U) = \sum_{i = 0}^4 c_i \sum_{q \in \Lambda_i} \tilde{f}_i (q, \Phi_{w + i, o} U), \qquad \Lambda_i = (\mathbb{Z}/ 2 (w + i)\mathbb{Z})^4 .\)

Choose \(z \geqslant w + 4\). The function \(g (o, \cdot)\) reads only the radius-\(z\) neighbourhood of \(o\). For each auxiliary site \(q\) set \(o_{i, q} = o - \pi_{w + i} (q)\), coordinatewise, and put

\(\displaystyle h (o, U) = \sum_{i = 0}^4 c_i \sum_{q \in \Lambda_i} \boldsymbol{1}_{\{U \text{ healthy on } N_z (o_{i, q})\}} \tilde{f}_i (q, \Phi_{w + i, o_{i, q}} U) .\) (12.26)

The shift ensures that the physical point corresponding to \(q\) is \(o\). Here \(N_z (a)\) denotes the radius-\(z\) lattice neighbourhood of \(a\).

If \(U\) is healthy on \(N_{2 z} (o)\), every indicator in (12.26) equals one: \(\text{dist} (o, o_{i, q}) \leqslant w + i \leqslant z\) and \(N_z (o_{i, q}) \subseteq N_{2 z} (o)\). The folded auxiliary fields are then globally healthy. A folded plaquette is either an original plaquette with an orientation change, a conjugate of such a plaquette, or a degenerate loop with identity holonomy. The plaquette chord is invariant under conjugation and inversion; thus its bound follows from the healthy large-field bound on the read set.

Reflection covariance first moves \(q\) to its canonical folded representative. The displacement formulas (12.24), followed by compatibility, show that the summand depends only on its type vector. For the all-interior vector it equals \(\tilde{f}_{\Lambda} (o, U)\). One can make the last comparison using a globally healthy extension on the original large torus. In each cyclic coordinate of length \(c \geqslant 4 r\), take the track that increases from \(0\) to \(2 r\) in \(2 r\) steps, decreases back to \(0\) in the next \(2 r\) steps, and remains at \(0\) for the remaining \(c - 4 r\) steps. Place its image at \(o - r\) and center its argument at the midpoint of the increasing segment. The resulting field agrees with \(U\) on the radius-\(r\) box about \(o\) and reads only that box everywhere. Each plaquette is again an original plaquette with orientation changed or an identity loop, so the extension is globally healthy. Compatibility applies to this field on the original period; locality then identifies its \(f_{\Lambda}\) value with \(f_{\Lambda} (o, U)\). Applying (12.25) gives

\(\displaystyle h (o, U) = \tilde{f}_{\Lambda} (o, U) \quad \text{if $U$ is healthy on } N_{2 z} (o) .\) (12.27)

The compatibility in the selected construction is established by passing two given periods to their product period. Replicate each field to that period; the short local density is preserved by replication. Translate both base points to zero. Their local patterns still agree there, so locality of the common-period density gives equality. The selected auxiliary representations use the same path, coupling, profile and finite cutoff. It is these equalities that permit the replication comparison. The required replication and local-generation identities are part of the short-density construction; this argument does not assert compatibility for independently chosen histories. For the selected densities they are proved in Subsection 12.9, where the five auxiliary densities are formed with the physical path, incoming budget, coupling, scalar correction and finite label cutoff. When the present section is applied, \(f_{\Lambda}\) and \(f_0, \ldots, f_4\) are those densities; the four compatibility conditions above are then conclusions of that section and not additional assumptions.

12.4.3The exact additive decomposition

Let \(F_a (U)\) be the ordinary logarithmic entries retained in a finite short-support panel. Their allocation coefficients \(\omega_{oa}\) are nonnegative, sum to one in \(o\) for each \(a\), and satisfy \(S_a \subseteq N_r (o)\) whenever \(\omega_{oa} \ne 0\). Put

\(\displaystyle E (o, U) = \sum_a \omega_{oa} \boldsymbol{1}_{\{U \text{ healthy on } S_a \}} F_a (U) .\)

Its sum over \(o\) is exactly the masked contribution of the panel. The unmasked short density is \(f_{\Lambda} (o, U) = \sum_a \omega_{oa} F_a (U)\). Translation covariance makes \(f_{\Lambda} (o, \boldsymbol{1})\) independent of \(o\). Define the discrepancy

\(\displaystyle d (o, U) = E (o, U) - f_{\Lambda} (o, \boldsymbol{1}) - h (o, U) .\)

This definition, followed by the change of variable \(o \mapsto o - \pi_{w + i} (q)\) in each finite site sum, proves

\(\displaystyle \sum_a \boldsymbol{1}_{\{U \text{ healthy on } S_a \}} F_a (U) = | \Lambda | f_{\Lambda} (0, \boldsymbol{1}) + \sum_o \boldsymbol{1}_{\{U \text{ healthy on } N_z (o)\}} g (o, U) + \sum_o d (o, U) .\) (12.28)

Indeed this change of variable identifies \(\sum_o h (o, U)\) with the middle sum. It does not require that \(U\) itself be healthy. If \(U\) is healthy on \(N_{2 z} (o)\), the allocation supports near \(o\) are healthy, so \(E (o, U) = f_{\Lambda} (o, U)\). Equation (12.27) then gives \(d (o, U) = 0\). Thus the discrepancy is supported on an unhealthy enlarged neighbourhood. The regular output retains \(g\), the constant contribution is extracted, and the forced correction uses \(e^{- d} - 1\). The negative sign follows by solving (12.28) for the new logarithmic sum in the density convention of the nonlinear step.

12.4.4The derivative cost of folding

Let the derivative order be \(j\), the spatial weight power be \(m\), and the small-direction threshold be \(\theta > 0\). Suppose a function \(F\) on an auxiliary torus is \(C^j\) in local exponential charts at every globally healthy field, and has there all iterated left derivatives through order \(j\) bounded by \(q\) when each direction has norm at most \(\theta\). On a healthy radius-\(z\) neighbourhood, the corresponding bound for \(F \circ \Phi_{w + i, o}\), with directions allowed to grow as \(\theta (1 + \text{dist} (o, x))^m\), is

\(\displaystyle 2^j (1 + z)^{mj} q.\) (12.29)

The factor two per derivative is needed because a reversed link transforms a left variation into a right variation. We justify that point rather than identifying the two derivatives.

On a forward auxiliary link the induced variation is a left variation by the corresponding original direction; on a reversed link it is a right variation by the negative original direction. The two types have disjoint link coordinates, fixed by the folding map. Consequently left and right variations on different types commute. For a list of \(k\) derivatives, expand each variation into its left and right part. There are at most \(2^k\) terms. In each term, right derivatives can be moved past all left derivatives, and at the fixed field converted to left derivatives by adjoint conjugation. Their norms are unchanged because the group metric is adjoint invariant. Explicitly, start with the empty list. For a leading mixed direction \((v_L, v_R)\) and each converted list \(s\) for the remaining directions, the two new lists are \(v_L : : s\) and \(s + \hspace{-0.17em} + [\text{Ad}_V \hspace{0.17em} v_R]\), with the adjoint chosen to turn the right action at the fixed field \(V\) into a left action. Thus the right directions occur in reverse order. The disjoint link supports ensure that this recursion introduces no derivatives of a varying conjugating field. Each resulting direction has norm at most \(X \theta\), where \(X = (1 + z)^m\). Multilinearity and rescaling by \(X^{- 1}\) bound the term by \(X^k q\). Summation and \(k \leqslant j\) prove (12.29). The smooth chart property under composition follows since a link read, its inverse and a constant identity link are smooth maps.

It follows that the ordinary localized density has derivative bound

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{O}_z (q_0, \ldots, q_4) = \sum_{i = 0}^4 |c_i | \hspace{0.17em} 2^j (1 + z)^{mj} q_i,\) (12.30)

when \(q_i\) bounds the centered total auxiliary density \(\sum_q \tilde{f}_i (q, \cdot)\). The support is \(N_z (o)\). For the ordinary derivative budget, each entry has an assigned anchor; its budget at a root \(o\) sums only entries assigned to \(o\), weighted by \(e^{ec_B (S)}\). Thus if \(c_B (N_z (o)) \leqslant C_z\), the entry \(g (o)\) costs \(\mathcal{O}_z (q) e^{eC_z}\) in that anchored budget. A sum over every support containing a fixed site would instead cost the additional factor \((2 z + 1)^4\). A fixed signed-orientation average and its local allocation multiply this bound by the already purchased orientation cost. All these factors depend only on the fixed local geometry, and have upper exponent zero in the scale parameter.

12.5Parameter estimates after the local projection

We transfer the estimates of Subsection 12.3 to the actual ordinary and forced arrays after localization. All finite tests below use the common parameter family, and every local cutoff is applied to its original fixed support. We retain the compatibility and local-generation identities specified in Subsection 12.4.

The ordinary budget below is anchored: at site \(o\) it sums the nonnegative derivative bounds of entries whose assigned anchor is \(o\), with their exponential support weights. The forced budget sums all supports containing the site. The sector bounds imply the anchored ordinary bounds by restricting their support sums to the assigned anchors.

Let \(P\) be the outgoing derivative budget, with derivative order \(j\), spatial weight power \(m\), radius \(B\), threshold \(\theta\) and support exponent \(b\). Put \(b_{\ast} = b + 3\). Let \(y = \beta_f (x)\) and \(R = y^{1 / 256}\), as before. Assume the path rows have their purchased strip and coefficient bounds, their regular local readings are smooth on healthy supports, and the support reserve fits inside half the path strip. Their ordinary rooted allowance, before the finite orientation average, is at most \(y^{- 7 / 16}\). These are the path inputs to the present argument. There are four types of path rows. The cubic and quartic path-atom rows carry the coupling as a factor, and are therefore multiplied by \(\ell (t)^2\) along the parameter line. The low-path rows, whose coefficient is the path correction fixed along the line, and the quadratic loop-atom rows are unchanged. Since \(| \ell^2 |_R \leqslant (1 + \delta)^2 \leqslant 4\) and \(|1|_R = 1\), the finite-test allowance of all four types at radius \(R / 2\) is at most four times the original allowance.

Let \(T_P > 0\) be the fixed orientation and derivative allocation cost. Combining the path terms and the sector logarithmic terms, and applying that allocation, gives the bound

\(\displaystyle W (x) = T_P \{y^{- 3 / 8} + 4 y^{- 7 / 16} \} .\) (12.31)

For every finite test \(\lambda\), the ordinary family has rooted derivative bound \(M_{R / 2} (\lambda) W (x)\) at exponent \(b_{\ast}\). A fixed finite short-support panel may be removed from this family with the same bound: keep the original nonnegative bound on the retained addresses and put zero on the deleted ones. Neither the parameter dictionary nor its support sets change. This observation includes every finite truncation used in the short-density construction, with no dependence of \(W\) on the truncation order.

12.5.1Allocating and testing the auxiliary densities

For the five auxiliary periods \(\Lambda_i\) of Subsection 12.4, form the same finite-panel local densities \(f_i (q, U)\). The nonnegative allocation coefficients are bounded by one, sum to one over \(q\), and only assign an entry to anchors in its prescribed neighbourhood. Write \(s\) for the radius of that neighbourhood and \(b_s = (2 s + 1)^4\).

For a tested density at a fixed site \(q\), take absolute values in its allocation formula. Each nonzero entry is assigned to an anchor within \(N_s (q)\). Summing first over those anchors and then using the rooted bound gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_h a_h f_i (t_h, q, U) \right| \leqslant M_{R / 2} (\lambda) b_s W (x)\) (12.32)

on globally healthy \(U\). The same estimate holds for the masked panel on an arbitrary field: outside its healthy support an entry is zero. Here the mask is independent of the parameter, so testing commutes with masking.

The total auxiliary density is exactly the sum of the panel entries, since the allocations sum to one. Summing the derivative bounds over anchors therefore bounds its derivatives by

\(\displaystyle | \Lambda_i | W (x) M_{R / 2} (\lambda) .\)

Subtracting its value at the identity field costs at most twice that amount, uniformly through derivative order \(j\); positive derivatives of the subtracted constant vanish. Thus the two probe bounds needed below are

\(\displaystyle p_i = 2 | \Lambda_i | W (x), \qquad q_i = b_s W (x) .\) (12.33)

The first bounds derivatives of the centered total density; the second bounds each uncentered local density on healthy fields. The identity field is healthy because its plaquette holonomies are all the identity and \(\theta > 0\).

12.5.2The ordinary output bound

Let \(g (t, o, U)\) be the five-period local density and \(d (t, o, U)\) its discrepancy. Put

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{O}_z (p) = \sum_{i = 0}^4 |c_i | \hspace{0.17em} 2^j (1 + z)^{mj} p_i, \qquad C_z \geqslant \sup_o c_B (N_z (o)) .\)

All operations in the formula for \(g\) are finite linear operations on the densities. Hence a finite test commutes with that formula. Apply (12.29) to the tested auxiliary total densities; they are finite sums of the regular local readings, hence have the smoothness on healthy fields which that estimate requires. The result is the regular support bound \(M_{R / 2} (\lambda) \mathcal{O}_z (p)\) at each anchor. Multiplying by the support weight gives \(M_{R / 2} (\lambda) \mathcal{O}_z (p) e^{b_{\ast} C_z}\). The existing complementary panel has bound \(M_{R / 2} (\lambda) W (x)\). After the orientation allocation, the total ordinary output bound is therefore

\(\displaystyle A (x) = W (x) + T_P \mathcal{O}_z (p) e^{b_{\ast} C_z} .\) (12.34)

This is a bound on the full derivative budget on each healthy support, not just on the sum of the array values.

In the selected geometry, \(s, z\) and all five auxiliary volumes have upper exponent zero, and the cover bound \(C_z\) is a constant. Explicitly, with \(r\) the locality radius of the short density, the auxiliary sides are \(2 (w + i)\) with \(w = 4 r + 100 (1 + B)\), and \(z = w + 5\); here \(r\) is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(B\). Hence there is \(C_b\), depending only on the fixed halo parameter of the target, on which \(r\) depends, with \(2 z \leqslant C_b B\) for \(B \geqslant 1\), and every neighbourhood \(N_u (o)\) with \(u \leqslant 2 z\) is covered by at most \((2 C_b + 1)^4\) balls of radius \(B\). Thus \(C_z\) and the constant \(C_{2 z}\) used below may be taken equal to \((2 C_b + 1)^4\), independently of the scale. This is needed: a cover bound of upper exponent zero would not make \(e^{b_{\ast} C_z}\) harmless. The derivative order and spatial weight power are fixed before the scale is increased. Consequently \(A\) has upper exponent \(- 3 / 8\), and

\(\displaystyle A (x) \leqslant y^{- 1 / 4}\) (12.35)

eventually. The strict exponent gap absorbs every fixed orientation constant and every factor of upper exponent zero. This is the broad ordinary bound used in the finite-test sharpening of Subsection 12.6; its radius is \(R / 2 = y^{1 / 256} / 2\).

12.5.3A common series majorant for the discrepancy

A discrepancy value is the difference between the allocated masked panel, its identity-field constant, and the shifted masked auxiliary sum. Applying (12.32) to the first two terms gives \(2 b_s W (x) M_{R / 2} (\lambda)\). For the last term, each centered auxiliary local value is bounded by \(2 q_i M_{R / 2} (\lambda)\) when its mask is one, since the folded field is then globally healthy. It is zero when its mask is zero. Hence, for every field \(U\) and anchor \(o\),

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_h a_h d (t_h, o, U) \right| \leqslant M_{R / 2} (\lambda) \left\{ 2 b_s W (x) + \sum_{i = 0}^4 |c_i || \Lambda_i | \hspace{0.17em} 2 q_i \right\} .\)

Write \(D\) for the expression in braces. The finite-test functional argument gives coefficients \(d_n (o, U)\) satisfying \(|d_n (o, U) | \leqslant D (2 / R)^n\) for every \(U\), with the same \(D\). Therefore \(D (2 / R)^n\) is a common coefficientwise envelope; its weighted sum at radius \(R / 4\) is \(\sum_n D 2^{- n} = 2 D\). Thus

\(\displaystyle |d (\cdot, o, U) |_{R / 4} \leqslant a (x), \qquad a (x) = 2 \left\{ 2 b_s W (x) + \sum_{i = 0}^4 |c_i || \Lambda_i | \hspace{0.17em} 2 q_i \right\} .\) (12.36)

This bound is uniform in \(U\), and \(R \geqslant 4\) eventually. In particular there is one coefficient majorant for the discrepancy at each fixed support. The sum over orientation copies uses the normalized finite orientation average; for values it has norm one. Assigning each copy its actual averaging weight and counting the anchors whose radius-\(2 z\) support contains a root gives the rooted bound

\(\displaystyle H (x) = (4 z + 1)^4 a (x) e^{(b + 2) C_{2 z}}, \qquad C_{2 z} \geqslant \sup_o c_B (N_{2 z} (o)) .\) (12.37)

This has upper exponent \(- 3 / 8\). The signs of the discrepancy are retained in the functions; they disappear only in this nonnegative bound.

12.5.4Fusing the discrepancy with the forced array

Let \(\gamma (x)\) be the forced series allowance supplied by (12.23), and let \(\phi (x)\) be the prescribed outgoing forced allowance. Use the sector allowance \(\gamma\) at support exponent \(b + 2\), weakening its available \(b + 3\) bound. The discrepancy allowance \(H\) is also at exponent \(b + 2\). The final forced bound will have exponent \(b + 1\), since the connected-set estimate consumes the reserve of one. Both allowances are evaluated at the same scale and the same reference band. The numerical payment chooses \(\kappa \geqslant 0\) so that, with

\(\displaystyle I = e^{\kappa} \gamma + 2 H, \qquad b_B = (2 B + 1)^4,\)
\(\displaystyle H \leqslant 1, \qquad b_B I \leqslant 1, \qquad e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant \phi .\) (12.38)

These have the form of the three inequalities in (19.1), with the output bound replaced by \(\phi\); but the payment is made relative to \(\phi\), as follows. Here \(\phi = \phi (y, \beta)\) is the forced allowance of the chosen forecast at the coupling \(\beta\) of the band. Two numerical inputs are used, for all sufficiently large \(x\) and uniformly over the band:

\(\displaystyle \gamma \leqslant \tfrac{1}{8} \phi, \qquad \phi \hspace{0.17em} y^{1 / 16} \leqslant 1.\) (12.39)

The first compares the compulsory exponentially small mark retained in \(\gamma\) with the forecast; the second says that the forecast is itself small. Neither is proved in this section. Both belong to the purchase of the forecast, whose hard rate is fixed below a fraction of the coefficient of the mark before the delays are chosen.

Given (12.39) and \(\phi > 0\), put \(p = y^{1 / 16}\) and \(\kappa = - \log (\phi p) \geqslant 0\). Then \(e^{\kappa} \gamma \leqslant (8 p)^{- 1}\), so \(I \leqslant (8 p)^{- 1} + 2 H\). Since \(H\) has upper exponent \(- 3 / 8\) and \(b_B\) has upper exponent zero, eventually \(H \leqslant y^{- 1 / 8} \leqslant 1\) and \(b_B I \leqslant 1\); in particular \(I \leqslant 1\) and \(e^I - 1 \leqslant 2 I\). Finally

\(\displaystyle e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant 2 \phi pI \leqslant \phi \left( \tfrac{1}{4} + 4 pH \right) \leqslant \phi,\)

because \(pH \leqslant y^{- 1 / 16} \leqslant 3 / 16\) eventually. The threshold does not depend on the coupling in the band, while \(\kappa\) may.

Here is why those inequalities pay the analytic coefficients, not only the real values. Give an old forced entry with series majorant \(k_l\) the inflated bound \(e^{\kappa} k_l\), and give a discrepancy entry with series bound \(h_l\) the bound \(2 h_l\). A nonempty support and the rooted bound imply \(h_l \leqslant H \leqslant 1\). The coefficient algebra therefore gives

\(\displaystyle |e^{- d_l} - 1|_{R / 4} \leqslant e^{h_l} - 1 \leqslant 2 h_l .\)

The last inequality holds on \([0, 1]\), for example by convexity of the exponential between its endpoint values and \(e - 1 < 2\). Every final forced component contains at least one old forced entry. Its exact product of the old forced factors and the exponential-minus-one discrepancy factors consequently has series norm at most

\(\displaystyle e^{- \kappa} \prod_{l \in D} F_l, \qquad F_l = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} e^{\kappa} k_l, & l \text{ old forced},\\ 2 h_l, & l \text{ discrepancy} . \end{array} \right.\)

Indeed each old forced factor contributes \(e^{- \kappa}\); retaining just one of them only increases the bound since \(\kappa \geqslant 0\). The eligibility predicate for a component is independent of \(t\), so it preserves the same majorant. The distinct connected-set estimate (7.35) with reserve one, used as in (8.242), applies to the constituent bounds \(F_l\): their rooted sum at exponent \(b + 2\) is at most \(I\), and \(b_B I \leqslant 1\). It bounds the rooted sum of \(\prod_{l \in D} F_l\) at exponent \(b + 1\) by \(e^I - 1\). The final rooted allowance is therefore \(e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant \phi\). This proves a common coefficient majorant at radius \(R / 4\) for all configurations and all entries of the actual final forced array.

The exact additive decomposition of Subsection 12.4 identifies these ordinary and forced outputs with the selected one-step source. Their dictionary is fixed by the common path and incoming supports. The auxiliary test representations may be used outside \([- 1, 1]\) to provide the real-node bounds, but on \([- 1, 1]\) they agree with the given parameter family. Thus all the finite tests and series recoveries above concern that same family. The ordinary bound (12.35) and the forced common majorant are precisely the two broad analytic inputs retained in Subsection 12.6. Construction of the funded path and its real-sector hypotheses remains separate.

12.6Sharpening the finite-test bounds

The real parameter tests of Subsection 12.7 need small bounds on one fixed parameter interval. The local expansion first provides a bound on a growing interval and a better pointwise bound. A finite polynomial interpolation combines these two estimates. We give the interpolation and its numerical purchase, keeping the underlying wide-interval analytic estimate visible.

12.6.1A finite interpolation estimate

Let \(\mathcal{E}\) be a vector space of families on a fixed support dictionary, with a nonnegative budget \(\mathcal{N}\) satisfying the triangle inequality and absolute homogeneity. A budget defined as an infimum over admissible summable majorants has these properties: add or scale the majorants and then take their infima. The argument below equally applies by retaining the majorants themselves. A finite test is a finitely supported real signed measure on \([- 1, 1]\),

\(\displaystyle \lambda = \sum_{h = 1}^{h_0} a_h \delta_{t_h}, \qquad a_h \in \mathbb{R}, \quad t_h \in [- 1, 1],\)

and for a function \(G\) on \([- 1, 1]\) with values in a real vector space we write \(\int G \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda = \sum_h a_h G (t_h)\). Set

\(\displaystyle M_r (\lambda) = \sup_{n \geqslant 0} \left| \int (t / r)^n \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda (t) \right|, \qquad r \geqslant 1.\)

Let \(F : [- 1, 1] \to \mathcal{E}\). Suppose

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{N} \left( \int F \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda \right) \leqslant BM_v (\lambda), \qquad \mathcal{N} (F (t)) \leqslant b \quad (- 1 \leqslant t \leqslant 1),\)

where \(1 \leqslant q \leqslant v\) and \(b, B \geqslant 0\), the first bound being assumed for every finite test \(\lambda\). The integrals here are finite linear combinations; no measurability of \(F\) is required. For each integer \(k \geqslant 0\), a constant \(U = U (k, q) \geqslant 0\), independent of the family, its dictionary and its volume, gives

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{N} \left( \int F \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda \right) \leqslant M_q (\lambda) \{ Ub + (1 + U) (q / v)^{k + 1} B \} .\) (12.40)

Choose the \(k + 1\) distinct points \(t_i = i / (k + 1)\), \(0 \leqslant i \leqslant k\), and their Lagrange polynomials \(\ell_i\) of degree at most \(k\). Write \(\ell_i (t) = \sum_{j = 0}^k c_{ij} t^j\) and put

\(\displaystyle U_i = \sum_{j = 0}^k |c_{ij} | q^j, \qquad U = \sum_{i = 0}^k U_i .\)

For \(\eta = M_q (\lambda)\), we have \(| \int \ell_i \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda | \leqslant \eta U_i\). Define

\(\displaystyle \Pi \lambda = \sum_{i = 0}^k \left( \int \ell_i \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda \right) \delta_{t_i} .\)

Lagrange interpolation of each polynomial of degree at most \(k\) gives \(\int t^j \hspace{0.17em} d (\lambda - \Pi \lambda) = 0\) for \(j \leqslant k\). For \(j > k\), use \(|t_i / q| \leqslant 1\) to get

\(\displaystyle \left| \int (t / q)^j \hspace{0.17em} d (\lambda - \Pi \lambda) \right| \leqslant \eta + \sum_i \left| \int \ell_i \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda \right| \leqslant \eta (1 + U) .\)

Since \(q / v \leqslant 1\), multiplication by \((q / v)^j\) proves

\(\displaystyle M_v (\lambda - \Pi \lambda) \leqslant \eta (1 + U) (q / v)^{k + 1} .\)

The pointwise bound and the triangle inequality give \(\mathcal{N} (\int F \hspace{0.17em} d \Pi \lambda) \leqslant \eta Ub\). Adding this to the wide-interval bound on \(\lambda - \Pi \lambda\) proves (12.40). Thus the same constant works for the full regular derivative budget, including all its site sums; no count of dictionary entries enters the estimate.

12.6.2The fixed-radius bound at large coupling

Fix the stage and block length, and put \(y = x + a\), where \(a > 0\) is the fixed reference coupling increment. The broad analytic estimate to be sharpened has radius and bound

\(\displaystyle v (x) = \tfrac{1}{2} y^{1 / 256}, \qquad B (x) = y^{- 1 / 4} .\)

The local pointwise bound has the form

\(\displaystyle b (x) = 3 tC_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10} + R (x),\)

where \(t > 0\) is a small response parameter and \(C_{\text{st}} > 0\) is the fixed stage conversion constant. The remainder \(R\) has upper exponent at most \(- 23 / 20\): concretely, for every \(\epsilon > 0\), \(|R (x) | \leqslant C_{\epsilon} y^{- 23 / 20 + \epsilon}\) eventually. This estimate comprises the local comparison and truncation errors; it is separate from the broad analytic bound. For the selected one-step source it is the regular budget proved in Subsection 12.9. There the principal and complementary regular bounds are purchased as \(E / 16\) and \(2 E / 16\), with \(E = C_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10}\); here they are purchased with the fraction \(t \leqslant 1 / 16\) in place of \(1 / 16\); the smaller regular reserve is the one permitted in Subsection 13.1 and is chosen before the entry delays. The offering section states its budget only for the fraction \(1 / 16\). Its proof uses the principal and complementary bounds only through their sum in the truncation bound (12.60), so with the hypotheses \(tE\) and \(2 tE\) the same proof gives the leading term \(3 tC_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10}\) in place of \(3 E / 16\). The other terms of that budget are unchanged: in the notation of that section,

\(\displaystyle R = C_{\text{turn}} g + g + C_{\text{turn}} c' e^{A_c M}, \qquad g = y^{- 23 / 20},\)

where \(c'\) is the bound of the folded counterterms. Both \(g\) and \(c'\) have upper exponent \(- 23 / 20\), and \(C_{\text{turn}}\), \(A_c\), \(M\) are fixed at the stage. The bound holds at every point of the parameter line, for the raw family in its one common dictionary.

Fix \(q \geqslant 2\) and \(s > 0\), take \(k = 300\) in (12.40), and choose \(t\) small enough that \(6 Ut \leqslant s\). The leading pointwise contribution is at most \((s / 2) C_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10}\). The other part of the bound is

\(\displaystyle UR (x) + (1 + U) (2 q)^{301} y^{- 301 / 256 - 1 / 4} .\)

Its upper exponent is at most \(- 23 / 20\), because \(301 / 256 + 1 / 4 = 365 / 256 > 23 / 20\). Since \(23 / 20 > 11 / 10\) and \(y / x \to 1\), that expression is eventually bounded above by \((s / 2) C_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10}\). Hence the fixed-radius test bound is

\(\displaystyle sC_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10} .\) (12.41)

There is no requirement that \(U\) be small. It depends only on \(300\) and \(q\); the response parameter is chosen after it, and the coupling threshold after all those choices. For example \(t \leqslant s / [6 (1 + U)]\) always suffices. Since \(v (x) \to \infty\), the condition \(q \leqslant v (x)\) also holds beyond that threshold.

12.6.3The reset and the final regular bound

The reset and local Taylor subtraction are linear in the raw logarithmic family and in its extracted coupling coefficient. On a fixed stage, the regular output bound is at most

\(\displaystyle C_0 H + C_1 | \alpha | \theta (x)^3,\) (12.42)

where \(C_0, C_1 \geqslant 0\) are independent of the coupling, period and input family, \(H\) is the raw test bound, and \(\theta (x) = x^{- 1 / 2} \exp ((\log \log x)^2)\) is the fluctuation threshold. The two constants collect respectively the reset, low-path and high-path derivative bounds, and the high-path remainder. This is the same local subtraction estimate used in the nonlinear transition, applied to a finite linear combination before taking its majorant. It is the regular cost \(E_{\text{new}}\) of Subsection 12.10, with the threshold of that section equal to \(\theta (x)\): in the notation of (12.75),

\(\displaystyle C_0 = B^{\text{reset}} = C_{\text{reset}} + C_{\text{turn}} (L_0 + L_1), \qquad C_1 = v^{\text{reset}} = C_{\text{turn}} L_1 .\)

The estimate is used under the hypotheses of that section, for the path, clipping data and outgoing budget of the transition under consideration. The path satisfies its paid bounds at the path width \(\sigma\). The relaxation radius \(r_c\) of the clipping data and the support exponent \(b\) and radius \(B\) of the budget satisfy \(12 \leqslant r_c \sigma\) and \((b + 1) / B \leqslant \sigma / 4\). The direct clipping coefficient is at most one, \(B \geqslant 2\), and \(2 \leqslant \theta (x) e^{(B - 2) / r_c}\), as in (12.64). The group is simple. The path correction \(\alpha\) is one fixed number along the whole parameter line, with \(| \alpha | \leqslant A\). With these data the constants \(C_0, C_1\) depend only on the stage.

The stage conversion constant is selected as

\(\displaystyle C_{\text{st}} = \frac{1}{64 (1 + C_0)} .\)

This is the allocation \(c^{\text{reset}}\) of (12.75). It is positive and satisfies both \(C_{\text{st}} \leqslant 1 / 64\) and \(C_0 C_{\text{st}} \leqslant 1 / 64\). Fix \(d > 0\), take \(0 < s \leqslant d\) in (12.41), and retain the fixed bound \(| \alpha | \leqslant A\). The first term of (12.42) is at most \((d / 64) x^{- 11 / 10}\). The second is \(o (x^{- 11 / 10})\), since

\(\displaystyle \frac{\theta (x)^3}{x^{- 11 / 10}} = x^{- 2 / 5} \exp (3 (\log \log x)^2) \longrightarrow 0.\)

Increasing the coupling threshold therefore gives regular output bound at most \(dx^{- 11 / 10}\).

This remains a finite-test statement. If the moment bound of the finite test is \(\eta\), the raw bound is \(\eta H\), the constant path correction becomes the same finite combination of \(\alpha\), and its modulus is at most \(\eta A\) by the zeroth moment test. The linear reset and the triangle inequality thus multiply (12.42) by \(\eta\). Dividing by the positive regular normalization gives the regular part of (12.49). The scalar coupling coefficient is a bounded linear projection of the raw family, with bound \(H / \theta (x)^2\). Consequently its finite-test bound is at most the scalar allowance \(f_m = sC_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10} / \theta (x)^2\) used there.

12.6.4The forced bound and the remaining analytic input

A finite-test bound alone may choose its majorant after the test, so recovery of a power series for each value separately does not yet produce one common support majorant. We supply that step. Let \(S_i\) be the countable list of nonempty supports and let \(e \geqslant 0\) be the cover exponent. Suppose that for every finite test \(\lambda\) there are numbers \(k_{\lambda} (i) \geqslant 0\) such that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{c} \left| \int F (t, i, U) \hspace{0.17em} d \lambda (t) \right| \leqslant k_{\lambda} (i) \quad \text{for every configuration } U,\\ \sum_{i : z \in S_i} k_{\lambda} (i) e^{eC (S_i)} \leqslant BM_q (\lambda) \quad \text{at every root } z. \end{array}\)

Here \(C (S_i)\) is the cover number. Nonemptiness and \(e \geqslant 0\) imply \(k_{\lambda} (i) \leqslant BM_q (\lambda)\) for each \(i\). For each entry and configuration, the closed-span argument of Subsection 12.7 therefore extends finite-test evaluation to a bounded functional \(T_{i, U}\) on the closed span of the moment vectors \(v (t) = ((t / q)^n)_n\).

Fix \(n\). Choose finite tests \(\lambda_j\) whose moment vectors \(v (\lambda_j)\) converge in sup norm to \(e_n\); these approximants exist by the coordinate-vector construction above. In particular \(M_q (\lambda_j) \to 1\). Choose their majorants \(k_j\) as in the hypothesis. Their coordinates lie in one common bounded interval. A diagonal subsequence on the countable indices \(i\) gives \(k_j (i) \to b_{n, i} \geqslant 0\). For each finite set of indices and root \(z\), pass to the limit in its weighted sum. Taking the supremum over finite sets yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i : z \in S_i} b_{n, i} e^{eC (S_i)} \leqslant B.\)

Also \(|T_{i, U} e_n | \leqslant b_{n, i}\), by continuity of \(T_{i, U}\). This last conclusion holds for every \(U\) on the same subsequence: the chosen majorants bounded all configurations from the start, and convergence of the moment vectors is independent of \(U\). No subsequence indexed by configurations is selected.

For \(1 \leqslant r < q\), put

\(\displaystyle K_i = \sum_{n \geqslant 0} (r / q)^n b_{n, i} .\)

The bounds \(b_{n, i} \leqslant B\) make each series finite. Tonelli's theorem for the nonnegative weighted series gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i : z \in S_i} K_i e^{eC (S_i)} \leqslant \frac{B}{1 - r / q} .\)

The coefficients \((r / q)^n T_{i, U} e_n\) have total absolute sum at most \(K_i\) and reconstruct \(F (t, i, U)\) as a series in \(t / r\). Thus one common support majorant works for every configuration. Applied to the soft correction from the linear reset with \(r = q / 2\), this proves the required factor-two bound and radius loss.

The compatible finite-subset products preserve absolute coefficient summability: their coefficients obey convolution, and the sum of coefficient norms of a product is bounded by the product of the corresponding sums. The proof is Tonelli's theorem for the nonnegative coefficient majorants. Thus the forced absorption argument of Subsection 8.32 applies also to these parameter coefficient bounds, with the same enlarged support reserve and the same scalar parameter \(\kappa\). Its inputs are the following. The hard bound \(a_{\text{h}}\) is the common coefficient majorant of the raw forced family at radius \(q / 2\) and support exponent \(b + 1\), where \(b\) is the outgoing support exponent. The soft bound is any \(c_{\text{s}} \geqslant 2 S\), where \(S\) is the soft cost \(S_{\text{new}}\) of Subsection 12.10 at the raw bound \(H\) and the fixed correction \(\alpha\); the factor two is the radius loss just proved. If \(K_m > 0\) denotes the prescribed forced normalization and \(\kappa \geqslant 0\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle c_{\text{s}} \leqslant 1, \qquad (2 B + 1)^4 I \leqslant 1, \qquad e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant dK_m, \qquad I = e^{\kappa} a_{\text{h}} + 2 c_{\text{s}},\) (12.43)

then the final forced array has one common coefficient majorant at radius \(q / 2\) and exponent \(b\) with rooted bound at most \(dK_m\). These inequalities are paid on the selected schedule as follows. Write \(y = x + c\) for the fixed stage increment and \(P (x) = \exp ((\log \log x)^2)\). Subsection 13.1 gives \(K_m = e^{- P (x)^{1 / 5}}\) and a hard forecast bound \(a_{\text{h}} \leqslant Ce^{- gP (y)^2}\), with fixed \(C < \infty\) and \(g > 0\). This also includes the fixed radius and support reserves. For any fixed \(d > 0\), put \(\phi = dK_m\) and \(p = y^{1 / 16}\). Since \(P (y) / P (x) \to 1\) and \(P (x) \to \infty\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{a_{\text{h}}}{\phi} \leqslant \frac{C}{d} \exp \{- gP (y)^2 + P (x)^{1 / 5} \} \longrightarrow 0.\)

The soft reset cost is a stage constant times a polynomial in \(\log x\) times \(x^{- 1 / 2} P (x)\), plus terms with smaller powers of \(x\). The factor-two radius loss above is fixed. Thus eventually \(c_{\text{s}} \leqslant y^{- 1 / 8}\). Also \(\phi p \leqslant 1\) eventually, because \(P (x)^{1 / 5} / \log x \to \infty\). Choose the mesh-independent stage threshold so that all these bounds hold and \(a_{\text{h}} / \phi \leqslant 1 / 8\). Define

\(\displaystyle \kappa = - \log (\phi p) \geqslant 0.\)

Then \(I \leqslant 1 / (8 p) + 2 c_{\text{s}} \to 0\). The block radius \(B\) is polynomial in \(\log x\), so \((2 B + 1)^4 I \leqslant 1\) eventually. For \(0 \leqslant I \leqslant 1\), \(e^I - 1 \leqslant 2 I\), whence

\(\displaystyle e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant \phi p \{1 / (4 p) + 4 c_{\text{s}} \} = \phi (1 / 4 + 4 pc_{\text{s}}) \leqslant \phi = dK_m\)

after one further increase of the threshold. This proves (12.43). The constants and bounds are uniform throughout the same incoming coupling band; only its entry delay has been increased, not its history or parameter line. The test inequality follows by applying each finite moment test to the absolutely summable coefficient series. This supplies the forced clause of (12.49) whenever the broad ordinary and forced analytic estimates have been supplied.

The broad estimates used in this section are substantive: the ordinary raw logarithmic family must have radius \(y^{1 / 256} / 2\) and bound \(y^{- 1 / 4}\) in the full derivative budget, and the raw forced family must have its common coefficient majorant at the paid broad radius. The pointwise remainder \(R\) must have the stated upper exponent. The interpolation and reset estimates above do not themselves prove those statements. They explain exactly how those local analytic estimates, once obtained for the same transition and parameter line, give the small bounds needed by the coupled history argument.

12.7Real parameter tests and the background contraction

The small Lipschitz constant used in the background history does not follow from a small bound on the values alone. It follows from the paid bounds on finite linear combinations along each real parameter line. We prove the conversion explicitly. Only real configurations are used in these tests.

12.7.1Recovering a convergent series from finite tests

Let \(E\) be a real Banach space, \(p > 1\), \(C \geqslant 0\), and \(f : [- 1, 1] \to E\). Suppose that, for every finite list \(t_1, \ldots, t_k \in [- 1, 1]\) and real weights \(a_1, \ldots, a_k\),

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_{j = 1}^k a_j f (t_j) \right| \leqslant C \sup_{n \geqslant 0} \left| \sum_{j = 1}^k a_j (t_j / p)^n \right| .\) (12.44)

No continuity of \(f\) is assumed here. There are vectors \(b_n \in E\) with \(\|b_n \| \leqslant C\) such that

\(\displaystyle f (t) = \sum_{n = 0}^{\infty} (t / p)^n b_n \qquad (- 1 \leqslant t \leqslant 1) .\) (12.45)

To prove this, use the sup norm on sequences and let \(v (t) = ((t / p)^n)_{n \geqslant 0}\). Its coordinates tend to zero. Define, on the span of these sequences,

\(\displaystyle T \left( \sum_j a_j v (t_j) \right) = \sum_j a_j f (t_j) .\)

If the sequence on the left is zero, (12.44) makes the proposed value zero. Thus \(T\) is well defined and linear, and its norm is at most \(C\). It extends uniquely to the closed span, because \(E\) is complete.

Every coordinate vector \(e_n\) lies in that closed span. For \(n = 0\), use \(v (0) = e_0\). Inductively assume this for \(e_0, \ldots, e_{n - 1}\). For \(0 < t \leqslant 1\), put \(r = t / p\). The vector

\(\displaystyle r^{- n} \left( v (t) - \sum_{j < n} r^j e_j \right)\)

has its first \(n\) coordinates zero, its \(n\)th coordinate one, and subsequent coordinates \(r, r^2, \ldots\). Its sup distance from \(e_n\) is \(r \to 0\) as \(t \downarrow 0\). This proves the claim. Set \(b_n = Te_n\), so \(\|b_n \| \leqslant C\). The geometric series \(v (t) = \sum_n (t / p)^n e_n\) converges in the sup norm. Apply \(T\) to it to obtain (12.45).

For \(1 \leqslant a < p\), the same identity can be written \(f (t) = \sum_n (t / a)^n c_n\) with \(c_n = (a / p)^n b_n\) and

\(\displaystyle \sum_n \|c_n \| \leqslant \frac{C}{1 - a / p} .\) (12.46)

This is the radius loss used when passing from finite tests to absolutely summable analytic coefficients. In particular, taking \(a = p / 2\) costs at most a factor two when \(p \geqslant 2\). The argument also applies to maps into the completion of a seminormed space; no completeness of its original representatives is needed.

Two consequences will be used. First, a test with one point and weight one gives \(\|f (t)\| \leqslant C\). Second, subtracting the series at zero gives

\(\displaystyle \|f (t) - f (0)\| \leqslant C \frac{|t|}{p - |t|} .\) (12.47)

Both conclusions hold even if the initial pointwise construction of \(f\) involved arbitrary choices: the finite tests themselves force the displayed series and hence continuity on \([- 1, 1]\).

12.7.2Applying the tests to the actual activity coordinates

At one adjacent pair of scales, retain the actual common dictionary of regular and forced arrays. Its norm is the maximum of the normalized regular and forced budgets, with all the fixed symmetry and support constraints retained. Finite linear combinations remain in this vector space. Fix the transition from the fine scale \(m + 1\) to the coarse scale \(m\) at one terminal index, the reference coupling \(\hat{\beta} = \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}\) of the fine scale, and \(w = \hat{\beta}^{- 1 / 32}\). A line of inputs has normalized coupling \(\lambda + t \mu\) and activity array \(x + ty\), \(|t| \leqslant 1\). We call it admissible if

\(\displaystyle \frac{33}{40} \leqslant w \lambda \leqslant \frac{47}{40}, \qquad | \mu | \leqslant 1, \qquad \|x + ty\| \leqslant 1 \quad (- 1 \leqslant t \leqslant 1) .\) (12.48)

The condition on the direction \(\mu\) is separate from the first one: for \(w\) below the purchased threshold, the two together keep the coupling of every point of the line inside the paid band of the fine scale, where the transition is defined. For an admissible line let \(F (t)\) be its selected output array in the completion and \(Q (t)\) its scalar coupling correction.

The exact finite-test receipts used here are the following. They are required for every admissible line, with constants \(d \geqslant 0\) and \(f_m \geqslant 0\) fixed before the line: \(d\) is the normalized activity allowance and \(f_m\) the scalar allowance of the step from \(m + 1\) to \(m\). For any finite weights \(a_j\) and points \(t_j \in [- 1, 1]\), write

\(\displaystyle \eta_r = \sup_{n \geqslant 0} \left| \sum_j a_j (t_j / r)^n \right| .\)

The combined regular output array has normalized budget at most \(d \eta_{q / 2}\), as does the combined forced output array. The scalar correction satisfies

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_j a_j Q (t_j) \right| \leqslant f_m \eta_q .\)

The forced statement follows from its single common summable coefficient majorant; the regular statement is a bound on the actual mixed arrays, with their derivative bounds, not just on values of their gas. The definition of the array norm and the isometric embedding into its completion consequently give

\(\displaystyle \left| \sum_j a_j F (t_j) \right| \leqslant d \eta_{q / 2}, \qquad \left| \sum_j a_j Q (t_j) \right| \leqslant f_m \eta_q .\) (12.49)

These are precisely the two instances of (12.44) needed below. Padding the dictionaries with zero entries, or using another representative of the same array, preserves them.

We spell out why the admissible lines are the ones used in the finite-test construction. Their activity values are affine in \(t\) on each fixed support. On a healthy configuration the regular mask is one and the value is the corresponding affine combination; on an unhealthy configuration the common mask is zero and the same equality holds. Forced values are affine without a derivative condition. The inverse coupling along the line is \(\hat{\beta} \{w (\lambda + t \mu)\}^2\), with the same reference \(\hat{\beta}\) and \(w = \hat{\beta}^{- 1 / 32}\). Thus it is exactly the prescribed quadratic coupling line, not an independent choice of complexified gauge fields. The protected supports, normalization, path data and transition are the same for all \(t\). The analytic estimates that pay (12.49) are retained as quantitative inputs here; the deduction of the contraction from those estimates is complete below.

12.7.3The full-band Lipschitz estimates

Take \(q \geqslant 4\), \(d, f_m \geqslant 0\), \(2 d \leqslant \gamma = 1 / 128\), and \(s_m = 2 f_m\). Assume \(w \leqslant 1 / 128\), as purchased by the coupling threshold. Consider two inputs \(z = (\lambda, x)\) and \(z' = (\lambda', x')\) with \(\|x\|, \|x' \| \leqslant 1 / 8\) and \(17 / 20 \leqslant w \lambda, w \lambda' \leqslant 23 / 20\). Use the product norm

\(\displaystyle h = \|z' - z\| = \max \{| \lambda' - \lambda |, \|x' - x\|\} .\)

The constant-line tests give \(\|F (z)\|, \|F (z')\| \leqslant d\) and \(|Q (z) |, |Q (z') | \leqslant f_m\). If \(h > 1 / 4\), the triangle inequality therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \|F (z') - F (z)\| \leqslant 2 d \leqslant 8 \gamma h, \qquad |Q (z') - Q (z) | \leqslant 2 f_m \leqslant 8 f_m h = 4 s_m h.\)

For \(h = 0\), no equality of raw representatives is needed. For any \(0 < \varepsilon \leqslant 1\), take the line \(z + t (z' - z) / \varepsilon\), \(|t| \leqslant 1\). Its coupling direction is zero and its activity direction has seminorm zero. Every point therefore has the same activity norm as \(z\), at most \(1 / 8\), so the line satisfies (12.48). Its value at \(t = \varepsilon\) is \(z'\). Equation (12.47) bounds the output differences by \(d \varepsilon / (q / 2 - \varepsilon)\) and \(f_m \varepsilon / (q - \varepsilon)\), respectively. Letting \(\varepsilon \downarrow 0\) proves equality of the output classes and of the scalar correction. This also proves that the map respects the seminorm kernel; it does not identify regular representatives on unhealthy configurations.

For \(0 < h \leqslant 1 / 4\), set \(v = (z' - z) / (4 h)\). Its norm is \(1 / 4\), and the full line \(z + tv\), \(|t| \leqslant 1\), is admissible in the sense of (12.48): its base point lies in the narrower coupling interval, its coupling direction satisfies \(|v_1 | \leqslant 1 / 4\), and its activity norm is at most \(1 / 8 + 1 / 4 < 1\). In fact its normalized coupling lies in the broader interval

\(\displaystyle 33 / 40 \leqslant w (\lambda + tv_1) \leqslant 47 / 40,\)

since \(w |tv_1 | \leqslant 1 / 512 < 1 / 40\), the margin between the narrower and broader intervals. The prescribed coupling-line construction therefore supplies (12.49) on this line. Apply (12.47) at \(t = 4 h \leqslant 1\) to get

\(\displaystyle \|F (z') - F (z)\| \leqslant \frac{4 d}{q / 2 - 1} h \leqslant 4 dh \leqslant 8 \gamma h, \qquad |Q (z') - Q (z) | \leqslant \frac{4 f_m}{q - 1} h \leqslant 8 f_m h.\)

This proves exactly (12.52) and (12.53). The output bounds also follow from the constant-line tests, since \(d \leqslant 1 / 256 < 1 / 8\) and \(f_m = s_m / 2\).

The use of one-dimensional series here is sufficient for these Lipschitz estimates. The same real finite-test method also produces multilinear Taylor coefficients by polarization when a full analytic parameterization is required. That additional conclusion is not needed in the convergence argument of Subsection 12.8.

12.8Convergence of the coupling and activity histories

We next prove convergence of the background on which the marked comparison depends. The coupling and the activities must be treated together. The activity recursion contracts towards coarser scales, whereas the coupling is fixed at the bottom scale. A finite telescoping identity joins these two directions. In particular, the proof does not assume that the activity space has compact bounded balls.

12.8.1The normalized variables and their paid estimates

Fix a period. Let \(\hat{\beta}_m > 0\) be the reference coupling at scale \(m\), and set

\(\displaystyle w_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 32}, \qquad B_m = \hat{\beta}_m w_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{31 / 32}, \qquad b_m (\lambda) = \hat{\beta}_m (w_m \lambda)^2 .\)

A coupling \(\beta\) is represented by \(\lambda = w_m^{- 1} \sqrt{\beta / \hat{\beta}_m}\). On the paid band its normalized variable satisfies

\(\displaystyle 17 / 20 \leqslant w_m \lambda \leqslant 23 / 20.\)

Thus, for two variables in that band,

\(\displaystyle B_m | \lambda - \lambda' | \leqslant |b_m (\lambda) - b_m (\lambda') | .\) (12.50)

Indeed the difference on the right is \(B_m | \lambda - \lambda' | \hspace{0.17em} |w_m \lambda + w_m \lambda' |\), whose last factor is at least \(17 / 10 > 1\).

Let \(\mathcal{H}_m\) be the completion of the seminormed space of actual regular and forced activity arrays with the prescribed weighted derivative and value bounds. The embedding preserves distances. In particular, equality of evaluated gases alone is not the equivalence relation used here. Let \(x_m\) denote the embedded array of the finite history with terminal index \(N\), for \(m \leqslant N\); it also depends on \(N\), and we write \(x_{m, N}\) when this matters. We use \(\|x_m \| \leqslant 1 / 8\) for every \(m \leqslant N\). At the terminal scale \(m = N\) this is a hypothesis: there the array is that of the Wilson startup representation at the terminal coupling. The startup family of Proposition 12.6 has regular and forced costs at most the allowances \(\delta_E\) and \(\delta_K\), which in the normalized norm used here gives only \(\|x_N \| \leqslant 1\). The hypothesis is the strengthened purchase in which these two costs are at most \(4 a \delta_E\) and \(a \delta_K\) for some \(a > 0\) with \(8 a \leqslant 1 / 128\); then \(\|x_N \| \leqslant 4 a < 1 / 8\). This strengthened startup purchase follows from the stronger reserve at the end of Subsection 12.15. Indeed, with its notation,

\(\displaystyle \frac{E_{\text{start}}}{\delta_E} \leqslant \exp \{C - 2 s / 5 + 5 (\log s)^2 \} \longrightarrow 0, \qquad \frac{K_{\text{start}}}{\delta_K} \leqslant \exp \{- s_{\ast} M^2 / 128 + M^{1 / 5} \} \longrightarrow 0.\)

For any fixed \(a > 0\) with \(8 a \leqslant 1 / 128\), increase the same stage entry threshold until these ratios are at most \(4 a\) and \(a\). The startup estimates hold throughout the coupling band and at every period, so this is one threshold on the same family. For \(m < N\) it then follows by descending induction from the first bound in (12.51) below, whose input condition \(\|x_{m + 1} \| \leqslant 1 / 8\) is the previous case. For a terminal index \(N\), write

\(\displaystyle x_m = F_{m, N} (\lambda_{m + 1}, x_{m + 1}), \qquad q_m = Q_{m, N} (\lambda_{m + 1}, x_{m + 1}) \quad (m < N)\)

for its activity transformation and the scalar correction to the coupling. These maps are initially defined on the actual allowed arrays; the notation does not assume an extension to every element of the completion. Their output and comparison estimates are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} \|F_{m, N} (\lambda, x)\| & \leqslant 1 / 8, & |Q_{m, N} (\lambda, x) | & \leqslant s_m / 2, \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.51)} \\ \|F_{m, N} (\lambda, x) - F_{m, N} (\lambda', x')\| & \leqslant c \max \{| \lambda - \lambda' |, \|x - x' \|\}, & & \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.52)} \\ |Q_{m, N} (\lambda, x) - Q_{m, N} (\lambda', x') | & \leqslant 4 s_m \max \{| \lambda - \lambda' |, \|x - x' \|\}, & & \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.53)} \end{array}\)

where \(c = 8 / 128 = 1 / 16\) and \(s_m \geqslant 0\). The numerical schedule also pays

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j < m} s_j \leqslant \frac{1}{128} B_m .\) (12.54)

The coupling recursion is

\(\displaystyle b_m (\lambda_m) - b_{m + 1} (\lambda_{m + 1}) - \alpha_{m, N} = q_m,\) (12.55)

where \(\alpha_{m, N}\) is the path correction, already convergent at each fixed \(m\) by Subsection 12.1.

We use the following precise continuity statement for the local transformation at a fixed scale \(m\) and period. Take two sequences of terminal indices tending to infinity and, for each index, an allowed input of the transition from \(m + 1\) to \(m\) at that terminal index. If the input normalized couplings of the two sequences have a common limit, and their actual input arrays are Cauchy with difference tending to zero in \(\mathcal{H}_{m + 1}\), then the differences of their output arrays, in \(\mathcal{H}_m\), and of their scalar corrections tend to zero. This statement concerns arbitrary allowed inputs, including the truncated histories constructed below. The output arrays are those of the actual transition outputs, in the fixed dictionary of scale \(m\).

This continuity statement is an input of the present section, not a consequence of it. Its premises on the background are the path limits of Subsection 12.1, the free-horizon limits of Subsection 11.20 and the fixed dictionaries, all for the same history as the maps \(F_{m, N}\) and \(Q_{m, N}\). Its analytic content is the common-variable comparison of Subsection 10.12 without a mark, together with the weighted head-and-tail argument of Subsection 10.11; the compensated estimates which that comparison needs on the actual inputs are those of Sections 9.8, 9.9 and 9.10. The application to the selected history is made in Subsection 14.21. Two points of that application are specific to the present setting and are explained now. Here is the precise tail implication. For a regular entry put \(b_f (i) = \sup_U |f_i^{\text{reg}} (U) |\), with its actual mask, and for a forced entry put \(b_f (i) = \sup_U |f_i^{\text{forced}} (U) |\). Summing the site's paid budgets gives, separately for these two families,

\(\displaystyle \sum_i b_f (i) \leqslant C_m \|f\|, \qquad b_f (i) \leqslant b_{f - g} (i) + b_g (i) .\)

The constants are the finite number of sites times the corresponding positive regular or forced normalization. Fix one sufficiently late array \(f_{N_0}\) and choose a finite set outside which its \(b\)-sum is small. For every later array, its tail sum is at most that fixed tail plus \(C_m \|f_N - f_{N_0} \|\). Enlarge the finite set for the finitely many earlier arrays. This gives the uniform value-bound tails needed by the compensated comparison. It does not assert that arbitrary activity arrays can be truncated in their full derivative norm.

For the scalar output, use convergence of the finite projected coupling coefficient itself: its projection is bounded by the local derivative bound, whose convergence is supplied by the compensated comparison. This is in addition to convergence of the output activity array. Subtracting the convergent incoming coupling and path correction gives continuity of \(Q\) by (12.55). The bounds (12.51)–(12.53) are the quantitative one-step hypotheses used in this section; their purchase is kept separate from the convergence proof.

12.8.2First fix the normalized coupling history

Suppose two sequences of allowed normalized coupling histories \(\lambda_{m, N}\) and \(\lambda'_{m, N'}\) have the same limit for every fixed \(m\). For an integer \(h \geqslant m\), construct a truncated activity history by putting \(x_h^{[h]} = 0\) and applying the same finite-history maps backwards down to \(m\). This construction is allowed for every prescribed coupling history in the band: (12.51) keeps all its arrays in the ball of radius \(1 / 8\). It does not impose the coupling recursion on this auxiliary history.

Compare it with the original activity history for the same terminal index and the same prescribed couplings. At scale \(h\) the difference is at most \(1 / 4 \leqslant 1\). By (12.52), backwards induction gives

\(\displaystyle \|x_m - x_m^{[h]} \| \leqslant c^{h - m} .\) (12.56)

For fixed \(h\), the two truncated histories have common limits at every scale at most \(h\). At \(h\) they are both zero. If this is known at \(i + 1\), their arrays are Cauchy and their difference tends to zero there; the local continuity statement and the common normalized coupling limit give output differences tending to zero at \(i\). To get convergence, apply this comparison to arbitrary pairs of subsequences of either truncated history. If that history were not Cauchy, one could select indices \(u_n, v_n \geqslant n\) whose distances stay above some \(\varepsilon > 0\), contradicting the comparison. Completeness of \(\mathcal{H}_i\) then gives a limit, common to the two histories. This completes the finite backwards induction. It also proves common limits of their scalar corrections at scales below \(h\).

Now choose \(h - m\) so large that \(2 c^{h - m} < \varepsilon / 2\). Equation (12.56) and the convergence of the two truncated histories show that the original activity difference is eventually below \(\varepsilon\). Apply the same arbitrary-subsequence argument once more. The original activity histories converge in \(\mathcal{H}_m\) to the same limit. Their scalar corrections then have common limits by local continuity at the next scale. Thus convergence of all the normalized couplings implies convergence of all the activities and costs.

12.8.3Identifying the possible coupling limits

At each fixed scale the band for \(\lambda_{m, N}\) is a compact real interval. Successive subsequence selection at scales \(0, 1, \ldots\) followed by the diagonal subsequence gives a subsequence on which every normalized coupling converges. The preceding argument attaches activity and scalar-correction limits to it. Denote one such limiting history by \((\lambda_m, x_m, q_m)\) and another by \((\lambda'_m, x'_m, q'_m)\). Suppose the bottom couplings of the two sequences have a common limit. Passing to the limit in (12.55) and subtracting gives the exact finite identity

\(\displaystyle b_m (\lambda_m) - b_m (\lambda'_m) = - \sum_{j < m} (q_j - q'_j) .\) (12.57)

The common limits of \(\alpha_{j, N}\) cancel. There is no infinite sum and no terminal boundary term in this identity.

The one-step inequalities also pass to these limits, despite being initially defined only for actual input arrays. To see this without postulating an extension of \(F_{m, N}\), construct at each terminal index a new backwards activity history with the fixed normalized coupling script \((\lambda'_i)_i\) and zero terminal array. Its couplings are allowed, because the band is closed. By the previous subsection, its activities and costs have precisely the limits \(x'_m, q'_m\). Compare it to the first history at the same terminal index using (12.52) and (12.53), then pass to the limit. Writing

\(\displaystyle d_m = \max \{| \lambda_m - \lambda'_m |, \|x_m - x'_m \|\},\)

we obtain

\(\displaystyle \|x_m - x'_m \| \leqslant cd_{m + 1}, \qquad |q_m - q'_m | \leqslant 4 s_m d_{m + 1} .\) (12.58)

First observe that \(d_m \leqslant 1 / 4\) for all \(m\). The activity bound follows from \(\|x_m \|, \|x'_m \| \leqslant 1 / 8\). For the normalized coupling, (12.50), (12.57), the bound \(|q_j |, |q'_j | \leqslant s_j / 2\) and (12.54) give

\(\displaystyle B_m | \lambda_m - \lambda'_m | \leqslant \sum_{j < m} s_j \leqslant B_m / 128.\)

Thus \(D = \sup_m d_m\) exists and lies in \([0, 1 / 4]\). Using the sharper cost comparison now gives

\(\displaystyle B_m | \lambda_m - \lambda'_m | \leqslant 4 \sum_{j < m} s_j d_{j + 1} \leqslant \frac{4 D}{128} B_m .\)

Together with (12.58) this yields \(d_m \leqslant (8 / 128) D\) for every \(m\). Taking the supremum, \(0 \leqslant D \leqslant D / 16\), hence \(D = 0\). Both limiting normalized coupling and activity histories are identical. The costs are identical too, by their one-step comparison.

Every subsequence has a further subsequence with all normalized couplings convergent, and all such limits have just been identified. If the original normalized coupling at some scale failed to converge to this value, a subsequence would remain outside one fixed neighborhood, contradicting its convergent further subsequence. Thus the entire normalized coupling sequence converges at every scale. The first part of the proof then gives convergence of the entire activity and correction sequences. In particular this applies when the bottom coupling is fixed exactly, as in the selected public construction.

12.8.4Why the cumulative payment can be imposed

The numerical inequality (12.54) is a finite cumulative bound, not summability of a fixed sequence of costs over all scales. We record its purchase to distinguish the two assertions. On a stage with positive reference increment \(a\), put \(B (x) = x^{31 / 32}\). For \(x \geqslant a > 0\), the mean value theorem and monotonicity of \(x^{- 1 / 32}\) give

\(\displaystyle B (x + a) - B (x) \geqslant a \frac{31}{32} 2^{- 1 / 32} x^{- 1 / 32} .\)

The corresponding cost allowance is twice the activity allowance divided by the squared fluctuation threshold. Its paid form is

\(\displaystyle s (x) = \frac{2 vC_{\text{st}} x^{- 11 / 10}}{t (x)^2}, \qquad t (x) = x^{- 1 / 2} \exp ((\log \log x)^2),\)

where \(v, C_{\text{st}} > 0\) are fixed at this stage. For sufficiently large \(x\), \(t (x) \geqslant x^{- 1 / 2}\), so \(s (x) \leqslant 2 vC_{\text{st}} x^{- 1 / 10}\). Since \(- 1 / 10 < - 1 / 32\), increasing the entry threshold gives

\(\displaystyle s (x) \leqslant \frac{1}{128} \{B (x + a) - B (x)\} .\)

There are only finitely many permitted next stages at each stage, so their thresholds can be increased together after their block lengths, positive increments and constants have been fixed. The reference recursion is \(\hat{\beta}_{m + 1} = \hat{\beta}_m + a_m\). Summing the preceding inequalities over \(j < m\) telescopes and gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j < m} s_j \leqslant \frac{1}{128} (B_m - B_0) \leqslant \frac{1}{128} B_m .\)

This proves the cumulative estimate required in the uniqueness argument. It preserves the chosen maps and block lengths, just as the other entry-threshold increases do.

12.9One nonlinear return on a whole parameter interval

The local estimates must supply a return which varies continuously with its input. Selecting a new support dictionary independently at every parameter would not suffice. We explain how to obtain the exact integral identity, both activity bounds and continuity in one dictionary on an arbitrary compact parameter interval. The scale purchases used below are fixed before that interval and the finite volume are chosen.

Fix a finite cutoff \(N\), adjacent levels \(m + 1, m\) with \(m < N\), and a period. Write \(p_f, p_c\) for the two activity budgets, \(x_0\) for the reference coarse coupling and \(L\) for the block side. The fine reference coupling is \(\beta_F (x_0) = x_0 + s_G \log L\). Fix the already selected incoming and outgoing paths, their block bridge and the scalar shift \(\gamma = \alpha_{N, m}\). Let \(I = [a, b]\) with \(a \leqslant b\), and let \(Q_x\), \(x \in I\), be a continuous family of fine representations in the permitted fine band. Continuity here includes the regular and forced coefficient arrays in their fixed labelled norms. Each \(Q_x\) has the selected fine path and satisfies the same history constraints.

We use the following purchased estimates on these data. The regular principal bound is at most \(E / 16\), the complementary regular bound at most \(2 E / 16\), and the preliminary forced bound at most \(K (x) / 8\), where

\(\displaystyle E = c_{\text{st}} \delta_E (x_0), \qquad K (x) = K_{\text{forecast}} (Q_x . \beta), \qquad \delta_E (x_0) = x_0^{- 11 / 10}, \quad c_{\text{st}} > 0.\)

These are the principal and complementary bounds of the preceding local expansion, in the actual coarse budget. In the application \(c_{\text{st}}\) is the stage constant \(c^{\text{reset}}\) of (12.75), fixed before the block length, and \(K_{\text{forecast}}\) is the forecast forced bound fixed in Subsection 13.1; the simultaneous purchase of the three displayed bounds at these values is made there. The selected path has its full quadratic, cubic, quartic and loop bounds, with width \(\sigma_c > 0\); its loop allowance is the selected allowance at level \(m\). The initial action is admissible in this same budget. If \(A_c\) denotes the support exponent used before the finite symmetry average, the scale purchases also give

\(\displaystyle A_c = b_c + 3 \leqslant a_{\text{earn}}, \qquad A_c / B_c \leqslant \sigma_c / 4.\) (12.59)

Here \(b_c\) is the coarse support exponent, \(B_c\) its radius and \(a_{\text{earn}}\) the earned exponent of the local expansion. In particular, these width and exponent conditions concern the same output path as the bounds, rather than a less expensive substitute.

12.9.1Keeping the physical and auxiliary data fixed

At each \(x \in I\), apply the local expansion to \(Q_x\) on the physical torus. Its hypotheses are supplied as follows. The block equation and both adjacent kernel bounds come from the selected horizon. Its all-period positive lower bounds apply to the two selected kernel levels; the bridge intertwining identities and the periodized rows are those of that same horizon. The input representation supplies the small-field and forced budgets and the fine band condition. The expansion is applied with the selected derivative orders, spatial weights, earned exponent, and its two error reserves. Its differentiated remainder bounds are retained with its output, as well as the undifferentiated integral identity. They cannot be recovered from that identity alone.

Use the expansion at \(x = a\) as the reference dictionary. For every other \(x\), identify elementary labels by the identity: the forests, geometric supports, sector labels and component types are unchanged. The path identity and the fixed regular and forced input dictionaries make this an admissible identification. Thus all output arrays can be read in the reference dictionary. This is precisely the comparison used in Subsection 9.9; no claim that an arbitrary choice of coordinates depends continuously on \(x\) is needed. That section has the pointwise compensated comparison estimate (9.63) as a hypothesis; equality of labels does not supply it. For the present output it is the collection of local estimates provided in Subsection 9.10, applied with the same paths, clips, forests and tags at both parameters. The two conclusions used later are the following: at every \(x \in I\) the output arrays at nearby parameters converge entry by entry to those at \(x\), and every sequence in \(I\) converging to \(x\) has a subsequence on which the regular and forced entries have common summable majorants.

The auxiliary tori used for local comparisons carry the same block bridge and the same two paths. On such a torus the reference fine representation is the bare path action with the zero activity array, all of whose regular and forced coefficients vanish. Its forced gas is the indicator that no fine site is flagged, so its density is

\(\displaystyle \boldsymbol{1}_{\{ \text{all fine sites healthy} \}} \exp \{- \beta \mathcal{K}_f -\mathcal{L}_f \} .\)

Here \(\mathcal{K}_f\) and \(\mathcal{L}_f\) are the fine kinetic and loop terms. The zero array has zero regular cost and zero forced cost. The fine path bounds and the clock inequality for the loop allowance therefore give the required incoming budgets. The fine threshold, radius and band are exactly those at \(\beta_F (x_0)\). The all-period kernel bounds apply on every such torus, so the local expansion and its differentiated remainder bounds are available there too. The auxiliary expansions provide local comparisons; the density reconstruction below is performed on the physical torus.

12.9.2The finite short density and its complement

The regular dictionary contains two kinds of entries: connected interaction clusters and path atoms. Divide them into a retained infinite part and an exhaustible short part. The selected interaction clusters have at most two polymer vertices, each formed from at most two elementary path letters of word length at most \(B_c\). Their connected coefficient is nonzero and each polymer is admissible. These letters have empty input roster: they contain neither incoming regular insertions nor incoming forced slots. Every cluster outside this particular class is retained in the complementary array. Its regular bound is at most \(3 E / 16\) by the principal and complementary estimates. The retained path part consists of atoms of word length greater than \(B_c\). Let \(\mathcal{P}_d\) contain the selected interaction clusters and the short path atoms whose elementary indices lie below the finite cutoff \(d\). The panels are increasing and exhaust every nonretained entry. Leave the forced array unchanged at this intermediate operation, and set the regular entries of \(\mathcal{P}_d\) to zero. Call the resulting array \(T_{x, d}\).

This complement is the sum of the retained infinite part and the unselected short tail, entry by entry. For a selected short entry both sides vanish; for a retained entry only the retained summand is present; for every other entry only the tail is present. Thus the triangle inequality adds their regular budgets without changing any support. The path tail requires a separate estimate. If its original width is \(\sigma_c\) and its word length exceeds \(B_c\), exponential summability and \(A_c / B_c \leqslant \sigma_c / 4\) give a tail bound

\(\displaystyle e^{- \sigma_c B_c / 4} \mathcal{C}_{\text{path}} \leqslant g, \qquad g = \beta_F (x_0)^{- 23 / 20} .\)

Here \(\mathcal{C}_{\text{path}}\) is the finite path-jet bound at the chosen stage. The first inequality follows by splitting the exponential row weight into the part paying the support weight and the part paying the tail; the remaining positive weight is discarded. The second is one of the scale purchases, imposed after the stage is fixed. The finite symmetry average costs \(C_{\text{turn}}\), so the retained total has budget \(3 E / 16 + C_{\text{turn}} g\).

The unselected short tail has budget at most \(g\) for all sufficiently large \(d\), uniformly in \(x \in I\). Here is the uniformity argument in the actual jet norms. On the fixed torus changing an anchor costs at most \(((1 + D)^p)^j\), where \(D\) is its diameter and \(p, j\) are the spatial power and derivative order. The sum of the rooted regular majorants is finite, since there are finitely many anchors. Thus at any fixed \(x\) there is a summable majorant in the reference anchors, and exhaustion of the short labels makes its unselected sum tend to zero. Norm continuity gives a neighborhood of \(x\) where the difference array has arbitrarily small total bound, so the same truncation works there. A finite subcover of \(I\) gives one truncation for the whole interval. Consequently

\(\displaystyle \|T_{x, d}^{\text{reg}} \|_{p_c} \leqslant h_0 := \frac{3}{16} E + C_{\text{turn}} g + g.\) (12.60)

The dependence of the tail index on the fixed torus and interval is allowed; its bound \(h_0\) uses the purchased, volume-independent budget.

Allocate each entry \(F_a\) of the finite panel among its allowed anchors with nonnegative weights \(\omega_{oa}\) summing to one. 12.10.1The scalar coefficient extracted from the local Hessians

We first record how the Hessian of a regular entry acts on affine fields. Let \(f\) be a regular entry on a support \(X\) which is nonwrapping at its anchor \(o\), and let \(D^2 f\) denote the second derivative at zero of \(A \mapsto f (\exp A)\), where \(A\) runs over Lie-algebra valued link fields. An affine field anchored at \(o\) is a link field

\(\displaystyle A_j (x) = a_j + \sum_k (x - o)_k \hspace{0.17em} b_{jk},\)

with Lie-algebra coefficients \(a_j, b_{jk}\); the difference \(x - o\) is read in \(\mathbb{Z}^4\), which is unambiguous on a nonwrapping support. Its curl is the constant two-form \(w_{jk} = b_{kj} - b_{jk}\), \(j < k\). We claim that \(D^2 f (A, A')\), for two affine fields, depends only on their curls. There are four steps.

First, \(Df = 0\) at the identity. A constant gauge transformation acts on link directions by the adjoint action on every link. Invariance of \(f\) makes the restriction of \(Df\) to one link an invariant linear functional on the Lie algebra. Such a functional vanishes on commutators, and a simple Lie algebra is spanned by its commutators.

Second, \(D^2 f (\nabla \phi, u) = 0\) for every Lie-algebra valued site function \(\phi\) and every direction \(u\), where \((\nabla \phi)_j (x) = \phi (x + e_j) - \phi (x)\). Indeed, let \(g_s\) be the gauge transformation \(x \mapsto \exp (- s \phi (x))\). Near zero the field \(g_s \cdot \exp A\) has a smooth logarithm \(c (A, s)\) with \(c (0, 0) = 0\) and derivative \((A, s) \mapsto A + s \nabla \phi\) there. Gauge invariance says \(f (\exp c (A, s)) = f (\exp A)\). Differentiate this identity twice at zero. Since \(Df = 0\) at the identity, the second derivative of the chart \(c\) does not enter, and

\(\displaystyle D^2 f (A + s \nabla \phi, A' + s' \nabla \phi) = D^2 f (A, A')\)

for all \((A, s)\) and \((A', s')\). Take \((A, s) = (0, 1)\) and \((A', s') = (u, 0)\).

Third, \(D^2 f\) is symmetric, and \(D^2 f (A, A')\) is unchanged when \(A\) or \(A'\) is modified on links not contained in \(X\), since \(f\) reads only the links of \(X\).

Fourth, on a nonwrapping support a curl-free affine field is a lattice gradient. If \(b_{jk} = b_{kj}\), put

\(\displaystyle \phi (x) = \sum_j (x - o)_j \left( a_j - \tfrac{1}{2} b_{jj} \right) + \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{j, k} (x - o)_j (x - o)_k b_{jk} .\)

Then \(\phi (x + e_d) - \phi (x) = a_d - \tfrac{1}{2} b_{dd} + \tfrac{1}{2} \left( 2 \sum_k (x - o)_k b_{dk} + b_{dd} \right) = A_d (x)\) on every link of the support.

Now let \(A, \tilde{A}\) be affine fields with the same curl. Their difference is a curl-free affine field, hence agrees on the links of \(X\) with a gradient. The second and third steps give \(D^2 f (A, u) = D^2 f (\tilde{A}, u)\) for every \(u\), and symmetry gives the same statement in the second argument. This proves the claim.

For a plaquette pair \(a = (i, j)\), \(i < j\), and a Lie-algebra element \(v\), let \(W_a (v)\) be the affine field whose links in direction \(j\) have the value \((x - o)_i v\) and whose other links vanish. Its curl is \(v\) in the plane \(a\) and zero in the other planes. Every affine field with curl \(w\) therefore has the same curl as \(\sum_a W_a (w_a)\). The bilinear form \((v, v') \mapsto D^2 f (W_a (v), W_d (v'))\) is invariant under the adjoint action, by constant gauge invariance. Simplicity of the Lie algebra identifies every invariant bilinear form with a scalar multiple of the invariant inner product. Hence there are real numbers \(Q_f (a, d)\) with

\(\displaystyle D^2 f (A, A') = \sum_{a, d} Q_f (a, d) \langle w_a, w'_d \rangle\) (12.65)

for all affine fields \(A, A'\) anchored at \(o\), with curls \(w, w'\). Symmetry of the Hessian gives \(Q_f (a, d) = Q_f (d, a)\).

For each \(i\) put \(Z_i = X_i \cup \text{halo}_2 \{o_i \}\). On a nonwrapping \(Z_i\), let \(Q_i (a, d) = Q_{f_i} (a, d)\) be the coefficient in (12.65) for the entry \(f_i\) and its anchor \(o_i\). Set \(Q_i = 0\) when \(Z_i\) wraps. This convention is preserved by the label symmetries. The Hessian estimates give

\(\displaystyle t^2 |Q_i (a, a) | \leqslant u_i, \qquad t^2 |Q_i (a, d) | \leqslant 3 u_i .\) (12.66)

Indeed, test a unit colour vector on one affine curvature direction, scaled by \(t\), for the first inequality. Its link size is bounded by \(t \text{dist} (e_-, o)\), hence by \(t (1 + \text{dist} (e_-, o))^p\), so it is an admissible direction. For the second use the direction \(t (A_a + A_d) / 2\). Expanding its symmetric Hessian gives four times this diagonal test minus the two single-direction tests, divided by two. The derivative bounds give \((4 + 1 + 1) u_i / 2 = 3 u_i\). For wrapping supports the inequalities hold because \(Q_i = 0\).

The series \(Q_o (a, d) = \sum_{i : o_i = o} Q_i (a, d)\) converges absolutely by (12.63). Reindexing the label series by a translation shows that \(Q_o\) is independent of \(o\). Reindexing by a reflection of an axis lying in exactly one of two distinct plaquette pairs changes the sign of the corresponding off-diagonal coefficient, so that coefficient is zero. An unsigned permutation carries any plaquette pair to any other, so all diagonal coefficients agree. Consequently there is one real number \(\Delta\) with

\(\displaystyle Q_o (a, d) = \Delta \hspace{0.17em} \delta_{ad}, \qquad t^2 | \Delta | \leqslant H.\) (12.67)

The last bound uses the diagonal estimate in (12.66) at a single anchor; no volume factor occurs.

The curvature read in the counterterm is the signed clover. For an axis pair \(a = (i, j)\), put

\(\displaystyle \phi_a (o, U) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{\epsilon, \eta \in \{\pm 1\}} \epsilon \eta \hspace{0.17em} \text{curv}_G (U (o ; \epsilon e_i, \eta e_j, - \epsilon e_i, - \eta e_j)),\)

where the argument is the holonomy of the indicated four-step loop based at \(o\), and \(\text{curv}_G\) is the fixed normalized Lie-algebra projection. Each loop lies in the radius-two halo. A gauge transformation acts by the adjoint at \(o\); a signed lattice symmetry permutes these four loops and their signs. This gives exactly the signed two-form transformation law at the common anchor. On the exponential line of an affine link field of constant curvature \(w\), the derivative of each oriented loop's curvature at zero is \(\epsilon \eta w_a\). The average therefore has derivative \(w_a\), with no extra normalization factor. Define

\(\displaystyle P_i (U) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{a, d} Q_i (a, d) \langle \phi_a (o_i, U), \phi_d (o_i, U) \rangle, \qquad r_i = f_i - f_i (1) - P_i .\)

The affine Hessian of \(r_i\) vanishes when \(Z_i\) is nonwrapping, and \(r_i (1) = 0\). To see the first assertion, let \(A, A'\) be affine fields anchored at \(o_i\), with curls \(w, w'\). Each \(\phi_a (o_i, \cdot)\) vanishes at the identity and has derivative \(w_a\) along \(A\), as just shown. The product rule and \(Q_i (a, d) = Q_i (d, a)\) give

\(\displaystyle D^2 P_i (A, A') = \sum_{a, d} Q_i (a, d) \langle w_a, w'_d \rangle .\)

By (12.65) this equals \(D^2 f_i (A, A')\), and the constant \(f_i (1)\) has zero Hessian. Hence \(D^2 r_i (A, A') = 0\). No assertion is made for wrapping \(Z_i\), where \(Q_i = 0\).

Write \(\chi_X\) for the healthy-set indicator on \(X\). A site is unflagged precisely when every plaquette having it as a corner is healthy. In particular \(\chi_{\{o_i \}} = 1\) makes the four clover plaquettes at \(o_i\) healthy. Define the supported discrepancy

\(\displaystyle d_i = f_i (1) + \chi_{\{o_i \}} P_i - \chi_{X_i} f_i + \chi_{Z_i} r_i .\)

It vanishes when \(Z_i\) is healthy: all three indicators then equal one, and the four terms cancel by the definition of \(r_i\). When \(Z_i\) is not healthy the last term is absent. In both cases

\(\displaystyle \chi_{X_i} f_i = f_i (1) + \chi_{\{o_i \}} P_i - d_i + \chi_{Z_i} r_i .\)

This has the form of the masked splitting of Subsection 7.9, but it is not a special case of that identity. There the extracted section is masked by the health of the stencil on which it is supported. Here the quadratic counterterm is masked by the health of the single anchor \(o_i\), which is what allows the counterterms of all entries with a common anchor to be summed below. The displayed identity is therefore verified directly, as was just done. Put

\(\displaystyle c_0 = \sum_i f_i (1), \quad D = \sum_i d_i, \quad R_0 = \sum_i \chi_{Z_i} r_i, \quad h (U) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_o \chi_{\{o\}} (U) \sum_a | \phi_a (o, U) |^2 .\)

All sums converge absolutely. For example each summand is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(u_i\), and summing (12.63) over the finite set of anchors bounds \(\sum_i u_i\) by \(VH\). The isotropic coefficient identity now gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_i \chi_{X_i} f_i = c_0 + \Delta h - D + R_0 .\) (12.68)

The common singleton indicator at each anchor is essential to summing the quadratic counterterms before using isotropy.

12.10.2Replacing the quadratic counterterm by the selected path

Write \(\mathcal{K}\) for the actual clipped kinetic action and \(\mathcal{L}\) for its loop part. The early return is

\(\displaystyle e^{V \tau - \beta \mathcal{K} (U) -\mathcal{L} (U) - \sum_i \chi_{X_i} (U) f_i (U) - \alpha \mathcal{Q} (U)} \mathcal{G} (U) .\) (12.69)

Here \(\mathcal{G}\) is the early forced gas and \(\mathcal{Q}\) is the masked low-channel presentation of the quadratic action. It includes the kept-row halo in each support. Thus it is specified before the reset; it is not the full kinetic action on arbitrary fields.

We describe the needed identity channel by channel. A low channel is either a plaquette term or an ordered pair of entries in one row of the selected path. Denote its coefficient by \(a_d\), its unmasked quadratic field by \(P_d\), its local constant-curvature substitute by \(Q_d\), and its actual clipped or kept field by \(L_d\). For a row pair,

\(\displaystyle a_d = c_i c_j, \qquad P_d = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle \phi_i, \phi_j \rangle, \qquad Q_d = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle \phi_{a_i}, \phi_{a_j} \rangle .\)

The latter curvature reads are at the common anchor. For a plaquette, \(a_d\) is the direct coefficient, \(P_d\) is its plaquette action and \(Q_d\) is half the squared local curvature. Let \(Y_d\) be the union of the radius-\(B\) anchor halo with the two walks when present. On its healthy set, \(L_d = a_d P_d\). Define

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} F_d & = \Delta a_d (Q_d - P_d),\\ J_d & = (\alpha + \Delta) L_d - \Delta a_d \chi_{\{o\}} Q_d \quad \text{on the complement of the healthy set of } Y_d, \end{array}\)

and set \(J_d = 0\) on that healthy set. Directly in these two cases,

\(\displaystyle (\alpha + \Delta) L_d + \chi_{Y_d} F_d = \alpha \chi_{Y_d} a_d P_d + \Delta a_d \chi_{\{o\}} Q_d + J_d .\) (12.70)

This also proves that the discrepancy is supported on an unhealthy \(Y_d\). No derivative of its indicator is needed.

Average these identities over signed symmetries and sum over translations, with normalization \(1 / |\mathcal{S}|\) for the signed group \(\mathcal{S}\). This normalization is applied to every channel, including its discrepancy. To identify the quadratic substitute, let \(S (0)\) be the actual zero-momentum row matrix. Absolute row summability permits the row-pair sum to be regrouped as

\(\displaystyle \sum_d a_d Q_d (w) = \tfrac{1}{2} \{c_{\text{direct}} \|w\|^2 +\|S (0) w\|^2 \} = \tfrac{1}{2} \|w\|^2 .\)

The second equality is the selected metric normalization \(c_{\text{direct}} I + S (0)^T S (0) = I\). Multiplying by the singleton healthy indicator and summing anchors gives \(h\); the signed average preserves the squared norm of the clover. Thus the averaged sum of \(a_d \chi_{\{o\}} Q_d\) is exactly \(h\). The averaged sum of \(\chi_{Y_d} a_d P_d\) is \(\mathcal{Q}\). For every cubic or quartic path atom \(A_d\), add the regular correction \(- (\alpha + \Delta) A_d\) on its original mask. These atoms have zero affine Hessian because their curvature factors vanish at the identity and their degrees are at least three. Denote the complete averaged regular correction by \(F\), and the averaged sum of \(J_d\) by \(J\). Since the low and high channels sum to \(\mathcal{K}\), equation (12.70) becomes

\(\displaystyle (\alpha + \Delta) \mathcal{K}+ F = \alpha \mathcal{Q}+ \Delta h + J.\) (12.71)

The low correction also has zero affine Hessian: on an affine field at the identity, transport along each path has the same first curvature derivative as its constant-curvature substitute. Thus \(Q_d - P_d\) has zero second derivative there. Polarization gives the mixed assertion. This argument is only used on nonwrapping supports, where the affine field and its walks have their specified lifts.

Combining (12.68) and (12.71) in (12.69) gives

\(\displaystyle e^{V (\tau - c_0 / V) - (\beta + \alpha + \Delta) \mathcal{K}-\mathcal{L}- (R_0 + F)} \mathcal{G} \hspace{0.17em} e^{D + J} .\) (12.72)

Every sign follows by substituting the two displayed identities. This equality holds even where \(\mathcal{G}\) vanishes or changes sign. Absorb \(D + J\) into that gas using the supported exponential construction of Subsection 8.32. Its hypotheses hold because each discrepancy vanishes on its healthy support. This constructs the actual output at coupling \(\beta + \alpha + \Delta\) and vacuum parameter \(\tau - c_0 / V\).

12.10.3The regular and forced costs of this reorganization

We give the numerical bounds so that this identity is accompanied by estimates for its actual arrays. Let \(C_{\text{pair}}\) be the fixed ordered-derivative bound for a pair of local curvatures, and put

\(\displaystyle C_{\text{sub}} = 2 + \tfrac{3}{2} |\mathcal{A}|^2 C_{\text{pair}}, \qquad C_{\text{reset}} = e^{81 b} C_{\text{sub}},\)

where \(\mathcal{A}\) is the six-element set of plaquette axes. Equation (12.66) and the product rule bound both the regular norm of \(r_i\) and the value of \(d_i\) by \(C_{\text{sub}} u_i\). The added radius-two halo increases the cover number by at most \(81\), whence the regular cost is at most \(C_{\text{reset}} H\). Its hit cost, denoted \(S (H)\), is the footprint payment with population radius \(4 B + 2\), support increment \(81\), input exponent \(b + 3\) and output exponent \(b + 1\). In the notation of the explicit footprint bound \(\mathcal{P}\) of Subsubsection 3.13.2,

\(\displaystyle S (H) =\mathcal{P} (1, 4 B + 2, 0, 1, C_{\text{sub}}, H, b + 1, 81) .\)

This is the ordinary application, at exponent \(b + 1\) and width reserve one; it is not the raised application displayed in that section. It is linear in \(H\) and has only polynomial dependence on \(B\) at fixed stage.

Let \(C_{\text{turn}}\) be the ordered-derivative cost of a signed pullback. For the low and high channels let \(L_0, L_1\) be their rooted regular sums after support weighting but before this pullback cost. Let \(L_h\) be the low-channel hit sum, with the relaxation factor \(e^{\ell_d / r_c}\) included. The channel estimates give

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \|F_{\text{low}} \| & \leqslant C_{\text{turn}} | \Delta | t^2 L_0,\\ \|F_{\text{high}} \| & \leqslant C_{\text{turn}} | \alpha + \Delta | t^3 L_1, \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.73)} \\ \|J\|_{\text{hit}} & \leqslant \left( 2 t| \alpha + \Delta | + \tfrac{1}{2} C_{\text{pair}} t^2 | \Delta | \right) L_h . \end{array}\)

Here is the summability behind these costs. A low support satisfies \(Bc_B (Y_d) \leqslant 85 B + \ell_d\) and a high support satisfies \(Bc_B (Y_d) \leqslant 9 B + \ell_d\). The weighted row-pair sum is the product of the two weighted row sums, hence is bounded by \(C^2\); the direct plaquette contribution adds its fixed coefficient. Derivatives of fixed order multiply these charges by a fixed polynomial in \(1 + \ell_d\). The inequality \((1 + \ell)^k \leqslant C_{k, \epsilon} e^{\epsilon \ell}\), obtained by maximizing \((1 + x)^k e^{- \epsilon x}\) on \(x \geqslant 0\), pays this polynomial from half the width. The remaining half pays the support weight. For the hit sum, \(r_c^{- 1} \leqslant \sigma / 12\) and \((b + 1) / B \leqslant \sigma / 4\) leave positive width for the rooted footprint sum. These statements hold term by term with absolute coefficients, so also for the countable channel sums.

The low regular estimate in (12.73) follows from the two curvature factors in \(P_d, Q_d\). For its discrepancy, the kept-row bound is \(|L_d | \leqslant 2 te^{\ell_d / r_c} |a_d |\), and the singleton-masked substitute has size at most \(C_{\text{pair}} t^2 / 2\). The triangle inequality gives the third estimate. The high estimate uses \(t^4 \leqslant t^3\) and the cubic and quartic path bounds. Finally (12.67) and \(t \leqslant 1\) imply

\(\displaystyle | \alpha + \Delta | t^3 \leqslant | \alpha | t^3 + H, \qquad 2 t| \alpha + \Delta | + \tfrac{1}{2} C_{\text{pair}} t^2 | \Delta | \leqslant 2 t (| \alpha | + H / t^2) + \tfrac{1}{2} C_{\text{pair}} H.\)

Thus the full regular and soft costs are bounded respectively by

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} E_{\text{new}} & = C_{\text{reset}} H + C_{\text{turn}} \{HL_0 + (| \alpha |t^3 + H) L_1 \},\\ S_{\text{new}} & = S (H) + \{2 t (| \alpha | + H / t^2) + \tfrac{1}{2} C_{\text{pair}} H\} L_h . \end{array}\)

The reorganization requires two numerical purchases for its output allowances \(\delta\) and \(\gamma\). The regular one is \(E_{\text{new}} \leqslant \delta\). The forced one asks for numbers \(\mu, \kappa \geqslant 0\) with

\(\displaystyle S_{\text{new}} \leqslant \mu \leqslant 1, \qquad (2 B + 1)^4 I \leqslant 1, \qquad e^{- \kappa} (e^I - 1) \leqslant \gamma, \qquad I = e^{\kappa} w + 2 \mu .\) (12.74)

These are the hard/soft absorption inequalities (19.1) for the pair \((w, \mu)\) and target \(\gamma\). Under them the output has regular cost \(\delta\) and forced cost \(\gamma\), with the original locality, symmetry and affine-Hessian conditions. These numerical purchases must be imposed on this same output budget.

12.10.4The stage constants and the early allocation

The regular purchase is obtained by fixing in advance how small the early bound \(H\) must be. The coefficients \(C_{\text{reset}}, C_{\text{turn}}, L_0, L_1\) depend only on the derivative order, spatial power, support exponent, clip, width and path bound of the output stage. They do not depend on the radius \(B\) or the threshold \(t\): the cover-number increments \(81\), \(85\), \(9\) and the polynomial derivative factors above were bounded without reference to either. Put

\(\displaystyle B^{\text{reset}} = C_{\text{reset}} + C_{\text{turn}} (L_0 + L_1), \qquad v^{\text{reset}} = C_{\text{turn}} L_1, \qquad c^{\text{reset}} = \frac{1}{64 (1 + B^{\text{reset}})} .\) (12.75)

Collecting the coefficients of \(H\) and of \(| \alpha | t^3\) in the display for \(E_{\text{new}}\) gives the identity

\(\displaystyle E_{\text{new}} = B^{\text{reset}} H + v^{\text{reset}} | \alpha | t^3 .\)

Since \(B^{\text{reset}} \geqslant 0\),

\(\displaystyle 0 < c^{\text{reset}} \leqslant \frac{1}{64}, \qquad B^{\text{reset}} c^{\text{reset}} \leqslant \frac{1}{64} .\) (12.76)

Now let \(\delta_E > 0\) be the regular allowance of the output level, and let \(A \geqslant 0\). Suppose

\(\displaystyle H = c^{\text{reset}} \delta_E, \qquad | \alpha | \leqslant A, \qquad 64 \hspace{0.17em} v^{\text{reset}} A \hspace{0.17em} t^3 \leqslant \delta_E .\) (12.77)

Then (12.76) gives

\(\displaystyle E_{\text{new}} \leqslant \frac{\delta_E}{64} + \frac{\delta_E}{64} = \frac{\delta_E}{32} .\)

Thus the regular purchase holds with \(\delta = \delta_E / 32\). The first condition in (12.77) is an allocation: it is the fraction of the output allowance which the local return of Subsection 12.9 is asked to meet, and is the constant called \(c_{\text{st}}\) there. It is a choice made before the block length, not a consequence of the reorganization. The third condition is a stage-entry purchase. With the uniform bound \(A = A_j\) of the next subsection and \(t = x^{- 1 / 2} e^{(\log \log x)^2}\), \(\delta_E = x^{- 11 / 10}\), the ratio \(t^3 / \delta_E = x^{- 2 / 5} e^{3 (\log \log x)^2}\) tends to zero, so the condition holds beyond an entry threshold depending on the stage and on the block length through \(A_j\).

Subsection 19.1 proves the soft-bound estimate (19.2), which bounds \(S_{\text{new}}\) by a stage constant times \((1 + B)^4 t\) when \(| \alpha | \leqslant A\) and \(H \leqslant t^2\), and the elementary payment (19.1). It does not contain the regular inequality \(E_{\text{new}} \leqslant \delta\); that is the allocation just described. The comparison of \(H, \delta, \gamma\) with the bounds of the selected recipe is proved in Subsection 13.1. The instantiation of (12.74) at the particular forecast pair \((w, \gamma)\) of the selected history remains an input here: it requires witnesses \(\mu, \kappa\) satisfying all its inequalities for that pair. Subsection 13.1 describes this purchase in words but does not exhibit the witnesses. The present section is conditional on (12.77) and (12.74).

12.10.5Continuity in the parameter

Let the parameter \(y\) range over the compact interval \(T\) fixed at the start, and write \(f_i (y), k_i (y), \Delta (y)\) for the early arrays and the extracted coefficient. The output arrays must be continuous in \(y\) in the labelled regular and forced norms. This does not follow from continuity of finitely many operations, because the forced absorption involves infinitely many labels. We use the two comparison properties of the early arrays supplied by Subsection 12.9, both in the one dictionary fixed there:

  • for every \(y \in T\) and every label, the regular entry \(f_i (y')\) converges to \(f_i (y)\) in its regular norm, and the forced entry \(k_i (y')\) converges to \(k_i (y)\) uniformly in the field, as \(y' \to y\) in \(T\);

  • every sequence in \(T\) converging to \(y\) has a subsequence along which the regular norms of the \(f_i\) and the suprema of the \(k_i\) have summable majorants \(e_i, h_i\) common to the subsequence and its limit.

On the fixed finite torus every support has cover number at most the number of sites, so the support weights are bounded and plain summability of majorants is all that is needed below. The first step is the absorption itself.

Lemma 12.1. (Continuity of the forced absorption at a fixed period) Fix a finite torus, a radius \(B\), a support exponent \(b\), and one dictionary of hard labels \(i\) and logarithmic labels \(j\) as in Subsection 8.32, with supports, inventories and eligibility tests independent of a parameter \(y \in T \subseteq \mathbb{R}\). Let \(K_i (y, \cdot)\) and \(\ell_j (y, \cdot)\) be the hard factors and logarithms. Assume:

  1. for every label and every \(y \in T\), \(\sup_U |K_i (y', U) - K_i (y, U) | \to 0\) and \(\sup_U | \ell_j (y', U) - \ell_j (y, U) | \to 0\) as \(y' \to y\) in \(T\);

  2. there are numbers \(p_i, v_j \geqslant 0\), independent of \(y\), with \(|K_i (y, U) | \leqslant p_i\) and \(| \ell_j (y, U) | \leqslant v_j\) for all \(y \in T\) and all fields \(U\), and with \(\sum_i p_i + \sum_j v_j < \infty\).

Then the absorbed activities \(\hat{K}_C (y, \cdot)\) of (8.238) satisfy

\(\displaystyle \sum_C e^{b \hspace{0.17em} c_B (Z_C)} \sup_U | \hat{K}_C (y', U) - \hat{K}_C (y, U) | \longrightarrow 0 \qquad (y' \to y \text{in } T) .\)

Proof. At every field, \(\hat{K}_C (y, U)\) is the product of two indicators that do not depend on \(y\) and of the finitely many factors \(r_v (y, U)\), \(v \in C\), where \(r_i = K_i\) and \(r_j = e^{\ell_j} - 1\). Put \(a_i = p_i\) and \(a_j = e^{v_j} - 1\). Each factor is bounded by \(a_v\) uniformly in \(y\) and \(U\), because \(|e^x - 1| \leqslant e^{|x|} - 1\), and by the first hypothesis each factor converges uniformly in \(U\). Writing the difference of two finite products as a telescoping sum shows that each term of the series tends to zero. Each term is at most \(2 e^{b | \Lambda |} \prod_{v \in C} a_v\), since a cover number is at most the number \(| \Lambda |\) of sites. The numbers \(a_v\) are summable: \(a_j \leqslant v_j e^{v_j}\) and the \(v_j\) are bounded. The labels \(C\) are finite sets of constituent labels, so

\(\displaystyle \sum_C \prod_{v \in C} a_v \leqslant \prod_v (1 + a_v) - 1 \leqslant \exp \left( \sum_v a_v \right) - 1 < \infty .\)

Dominated convergence for series gives the claim.\(\Box\)

The bound obtained in this proof depends on the number of sites. It is used only for continuity at a fixed period, never as a volume-independent bound. The volume-independent forced cost of the output is the separate estimate (8.242), paid by (12.74).

We apply the lemma as follows. First, \(\Delta\) is continuous on \(T\). The difference of two early arrays in the same dictionary is again a family of regular entries, and the projection is linear. Applying (12.67) to the difference gives \(t^2 | \Delta (y') - \Delta (y) | \leqslant d\) whenever the regular norms of the differences \(f_i (y') - f_i (y)\) have majorants whose support-weighted sum over all labels is at most \(d\). Such a \(d\) can be taken arbitrarily small for \(y'\) near \(y\): otherwise there would be a sequence \(y_n \to y\) along which this fails, and on its dominated subsequence the entrywise convergence and the common summable majorant \(2 e_i\) would give a contradiction by dominated convergence.

Second, every output entry depends continuously on \(y\). The entries \(r_i\) and \(d_i\) are linear in \(f_i\), with norm at most \(C_{\text{sub}}\) times that of \(f_i\), so they inherit the entrywise convergence. The path corrections \(F_d\) and \(J_d\) are fixed functions multiplied by \(\Delta\) or by \(\alpha + \Delta\), so they are continuous because \(\Delta\) is.

Third, let \(y_n \to y\) and pass to the subsequence with common early majorants \(e_i, h_i\). Along it \(| \Delta (y_n) | \leqslant | \Delta (y) | + 1\) eventually. The output regular and logarithmic entries then have the common summable majorants \(C_{\text{sub}} e_i\) for the subtracted entries and the channel bounds of (12.73) with \(| \Delta |\) replaced by \(| \Delta (y) | + 1\) for the path entries; the hard entries keep the majorants \(h_i\). Summability of the channel bounds is the summability established after (12.73), which did not use the size of \(\Delta\).

Fourth, on this subsequence together with its limit point the hypotheses of Lemma 12.1 hold: the first by the second step, the second by the third step. The regular output entries are not changed by the absorption, and their weighted difference sums tend to zero by the same dominated convergence. Hence the full output arrays converge along the subsequence. Since every sequence converging to \(y\) has such a subsequence, the output arrays are continuous at \(y\): a sequence violating continuity would have a dominated subsequence still violating it.

12.10.6The two healthy tests and the actual reset

Suppose the early cost obeys \(H \leqslant F / 4\), where \(F\) is the scalar fund used in Subsection 12.11. On a globally healthy field all masks are one and the early gas is one. The early density is consequently

\(\displaystyle H_x (U) = \exp \{V \tau - \beta \mathcal{K}(U) - \alpha \mathcal{K}_2 (U) -\mathcal{L}(U) - E (U)\}, \qquad |E (U) | \leqslant VH.\)

Here \(\mathcal{K}_2\) is the quadratic part and \(E\) the early regular sum. At \(U = 1\), the three path terms vanish, so positivity is explicit and

\(\displaystyle | V^{- 1} \log H_x (1) - \tau | \leqslant H, \qquad |E (U) - E (1) | \leqslant 2 VH \leqslant VF / 2.\)

The difference of the full kinetic action and its quadratic part has size at most \(2 VCt^3\): each cubic and quartic atom is bounded by its row charge times \(t^3\) or \(t^4\), respectively, and \(t^4 \leqslant t^3\). The selected path construction bounds \(| \alpha | \leqslant A_j\), uniformly in cutoff and period, where \(A_j\) is its drift bound plus \(|s_G \log L_j |\) for the fixed stage. Purchase \(2 A_j Ct^3 \leqslant F / 2\). This is possible at each fixed stage since \(t = x^{- 1 / 2} e^{(\log \log x)^2}\), while \(F\) is a positive stage constant times \(x^{- 11 / 10}\); their ratio tends to zero. Adding the two errors proves the precise surplus bound \(VF\) at the nontrivial healthy test. The vacuum error is bounded by \(F / 4\).

The output arrays in (12.72) depend on the computed \(\Delta\), and are fixed while the trial dial varies. Their dial-motion cost is therefore zero. Their continuity in the original parameter was proved in the preceding subsection. The scalar selection therefore applies to these arrays. Its residual is \((d - \Delta) \mathcal{K} (U_{\ast})\), so the selected dial is exactly \(\Delta\). Its vacuum value is exactly \(\tau - c_0 / V\) by (12.72) at the identity.

Finally retain this constructed family as the witness for the history step. The recipe conditions refer to its actual supports, normalized density identity, regular and forced bounds, and bound \(t^2 | \Delta | \leqslant H\). They are supplied by the construction above and the separately purchased recipe permissions. Equality of densities by itself would not supply those permissions. Once the common funding provides them, this is the continuous one-step map used in the surviving-interval induction.

12.11Selecting the coupling and the vacuum constant

An exact local return contains a small correction to the kinetic coefficient. We must show that this correction lies in the purchased interval and depends continuously on the incoming representation. Two healthy configurations suffice to select the two scalar parameters. The exact density identity at other configurations comes from the local return, and must be retained separately.

12.11.1A separating configuration in every volume

Assume the gauge Lie algebra is nonzero, as it is for our simple gauge group. Let \(V_{\Lambda} = | \Lambda |\) be the number of sites in an even-sided torus. Let \(a_0 > 0\) be the direct plaquette coefficient of the selected clipped kinetic action \(\mathcal{K}\), and let \(C \geqslant 0\) bound its cubic and quartic arrays. Fix a healthy-field threshold \(0 < t \leqslant 1\). There is a number \(\eta > 0\), depending on the stage bounds but not the torus or \(t\), and an all-healthy configuration \(U_{\ast}\) such that

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{K} (U_{\ast}) \geqslant V_{\Lambda} \eta t^2 .\) (12.78)

Here and below the trivial configuration is denoted by \(1\).

To construct it, first choose \(\epsilon > 0\) such that every chord value in \([0, \epsilon]\) occurs in \(G\). Indeed, take a nonzero Lie-algebra vector \(c\). The derivative at zero of the curvature of \(\exp (sc)\) is \(c\), so its chord cannot vanish identically. Continuity and the intermediate value theorem along that path give the assertion. Set

\(\displaystyle r = \min \{\epsilon, 1 / 4, a_0 / [512 (1 + C)]\} > 0, \qquad h = rt,\)

and choose \(u \in G\) of chord \(h\). Set the links in direction \(0\) equal to \(u\) on even coordinate-\(1\) slices and to \(1\) on odd slices; set all other links equal to \(1\). Even side length makes this periodic. Every \((0, 1)\) plaquette has holonomy \(u\) or \(u^{- 1}\) and hence chord \(h\). Every plaquette has chord at most \(4 h \leqslant t\), by the four-link triangle inequality. Thus the configuration is healthy.

For each signed lattice symmetry the sum of the direct plaquette terms is at least \(V_{\Lambda} a_0 h^2 / 2\): the symmetry permutes plaquettes, and the indicated plane already contributes that amount. The other quadratic terms are nonnegative on this healthy configuration. The absolute cubic-plus-quartic contribution per site is at most

\(\displaystyle C (4 h)^3 + C (4 h)^4 \leqslant 128 Ch^3 \leqslant a_0 h^2 / 4.\)

The first inequality uses \(4 h \leqslant 1\), and the second uses \(512 Ch \leqslant a_0\). Averaging the signed actions preserves the lower bound \(V_{\Lambda} a_0 h^2 / 4\). This proves (12.78) with \(\eta = a_0 r^2 / 4\).

12.11.2The scalar equation on a purchased interval

Fix the coarse path, clip and budget. Let \(x\) range in an interval \(I\), and let \(H_x (U)\) be the actual unnormalized transferred density. Assume \(H_x (1), H_x (U_{\ast}) > 0\). Write

\(\displaystyle c (x) = \log H_x (1) - \log H_x (U_{\ast}), \qquad b (x) = \beta_f (x) + \alpha_{N, m} .\)

For a trial correction \(d \in [- w, w]\), \(0 < w \leqslant 1\), write its density as

\(\displaystyle e^{V_{\Lambda} v} e^{- (b (x) + d) \mathcal{K} (U) -\mathcal{L} (U) - A_{x, d} (U)} \mathcal{G}_{x, d} (U) .\) (12.79)

Here \(\mathcal{L}\) is the fixed loop action, \(A_{x, d}\) the regular activity sum, and \(\mathcal{G}_{x, d}\) the forced gas. The regular entries vanish at \(1\), and \(\mathcal{K} (1) =\mathcal{L} (1) = 0\) because each path atom vanishes at the trivial configuration. The forced gas equals \(1\) on healthy configurations. In particular the vacuum parameter is forced to be

\(\displaystyle v (x) = V_{\Lambda}^{- 1} \log H_x (1) .\) (12.80)

Put \(k =\mathcal{K} (U_{\ast}) > 0\) and define the residual

\(\displaystyle f (x, d) = (b (x) + d) k +\mathcal{L} (U_{\ast}) + A_{x, d} (U_{\ast}) - c (x) .\)

The trial matches \(H_x\) at \(1\) and \(U_{\ast}\) exactly when \(v\) has value (12.80) and \(f (x, d) = 0\).

Here are the estimates required for this selection. Let \(\delta \geqslant 0\) be the regular allowance per site and \(F \geqslant 0\) the reset allowance per site. Assume

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} |b (x) k +\mathcal{L}(U_{\ast}) - c (x) | & \leqslant V_{\Lambda} F, \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.81)} \\ |A_{x, 0} (U_{\ast}) | & \leqslant V_{\Lambda} \delta,\\ |A_{x, d'} (U_{\ast}) - A_{x, d} (U_{\ast}) | & \leqslant V_{\Lambda} F |d' - d|,\\ 2 (F + \delta) & \leqslant \eta t^2 w. \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.82)} \end{array}\)

The arrays have fixed labels, are continuous in their regular and forced norms along parameter curves, and have locally summable comparison majorants. These conditions imply continuity of their evaluated sums along the parameter and dial: truncate a summable majorant, use continuity for the finite part, and bound the two tails by that majorant. The same argument works if domination is supplied on a further subsequence of every convergent sequence. From (12.78) and (12.82),

\(\displaystyle V_{\Lambda} (F + \delta) \leqslant kw / 2, \qquad V_{\Lambda} F \leqslant k / 2.\)

\(\displaystyle f (x, d') - f (x, d) \geqslant \tfrac{1}{2} k (d' - d), \qquad |f (x, 0) | \leqslant kw / 2.\)

Thus \(f (x, - w) \leqslant 0 \leqslant f (x, w)\). Continuity in \(d\) gives a zero \(d (x) \in [- w, w]\), and the strict slope bound makes it unique in that interval. It also gives the useful inverse estimate

\(\displaystyle |d (x) - d (y) | \leqslant \frac{2}{k} |f (x, d (y)) - f (y, d (y)) | .\) (12.83)

For fixed \(y\), the right side tends to zero as \(x \to y\); this proves continuity of \(d\) including at endpoints. 12.12Logarithmic asymptotics of the running inverse coefficient

Let \(G \subset U (r_G)\) be a closed matrix subgroup, in the indicated unitary representation, with nonabelian simple real Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}\), where \(r_G \geqslant 1\). Explicitly,

\(\displaystyle \mathfrak{g}= \{X \in M_{r_G} (\mathbb{C}) : \exp (tX) \in G \text{ for every } t \in \mathbb{R}\} .\)

Since \(U (r_G)\) is closed in \(\text{GL} (r_G, \mathbb{C})\), so is \(G\). Thus Hall's matrix Lie group results apply: this set is a finite-dimensional real vector space closed under \([X, Y] = XY - YX\) [Hal00, Definition 3.13 and Theorem 3.16]. Since \(\exp (tX)\) is unitary for \(X \in \mathfrak{g}\) and real \(t\), differentiating \(\exp (tX)^{\ast} \exp (tX) = I\) at \(t = 0\) gives \(X^{\ast} = - X\). Thus the symmetric real bilinear form \(\langle X, Y \rangle = - r_G^{- 1} \text{Retr} (XY)\) is positive definite: for \(X \ne 0\),

\(\displaystyle \langle X, X \rangle = r_G^{- 1} \text{tr} (X^{\ast} X) = r_G^{- 1} \sum_{a, b} |X_{ab} |^2 > 0.\)

Nonabelianness implies \(\mathfrak{g} \ne \{0\}\), so \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g} \geqslant 1\). Write

\(\displaystyle b_0 (G) = \frac{11 \hspace{0.17em} C_{\mathfrak{g}}}{48 \pi^2}, \qquad C_{\mathfrak{g}} = - \frac{1}{\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}} \sum_j \text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}} \left( (\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} e_j)^2 \right),\)

where \((e_j)\) is an orthonormal basis for this inner product and \(\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} X (Y) = [X, Y] = XY - YX\). Here \(\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\) is the real trace of an endomorphism of \(\mathfrak{g}\). This constant is independent of the orthonormal basis: if \(f_i = \sum_j q_{ij} e_j\) with \(q\) orthogonal, then

\(\displaystyle \sum_i (\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} f_i)^2 = \sum_{j, k} \left( \sum_i q_{ij} q_{ik} \right) \text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} e_j \text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} e_k = \sum_j (\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} e_j)^2 .\)

Taking traces proves the assertion. Invariance of the matrix trace gives \(\langle [X, Y], Z \rangle = - \langle Y, [X, Z] \rangle\), so each \(\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} e_j\) is skew-adjoint. Consequently \(- \text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}} ((\text{ad} \hspace{0.17em} e_j)^2) = \sum_{\ell} \|[e_j, e_{\ell}]\|^2\). If every basis commutator vanished, bilinearity would make all commutators vanish. Noncommutativity therefore makes at least one term in the resulting double sum positive. In particular, \(b_0 (G) > 0\).

Input from the lattice construction.
Fix a construction family \(\mathcal{C}\) satisfying the construction conditions. For \(N, p, m \in \mathbb{N}\) with \(m \leqslant N\), let \(\beta_{N, p, m}\) be its action coefficient at stage \(m\) of the trajectory started at lattice level \(N\) and period index \(p\). The spacing at stage \(m\) is \(a_m = (\prod_{j < m} B_j)^{- 1}\), where each block factor \(B_j\) is an integer at least \(3\). Thus \(s_m = \log (a_m^{- 1}) = \sum_{j < m} \log B_j \geqslant m \log 3\), so \(s_m \to + \infty\). The running estimate for the family provides a fixed reference intercept \(\bar{\beta}\) and, for every \(\eta > 0\), a threshold \(m_0 (\eta)\) such that
\(\displaystyle | \beta_{N, p, m} - (\bar{\beta} + 2 b_0 (G) s_m) | \leqslant \eta s_m \qquad (m \geqslant m_0 (\eta), N \geqslant m, p \in \mathbb{N}) . \) (12.85)

The threshold is uniform over the starting level and period index. We use the running estimate as an input to the comparison below.

Proposition 12.2. (Uniform extraction) Let \(s_m \to + \infty\), and let \(A, c \in \mathbb{R}\) be fixed. Suppose the real coefficients \(\beta_{N, p, m}\) satisfy, for every \(\eta > 0\), an estimate \(| \beta_{N, p, m} - (A + cs_m) | \leqslant \eta s_m\) for all sufficiently large \(m\), uniformly over \(N \geqslant m\) and \(p \in \mathbb{N}\). Then for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), for all sufficiently large \(m\) one has \(s_m > 0\) and

\(\displaystyle \left| \frac{\beta_{N, p, m}}{s_m} - c \right| < \varepsilon \qquad (N \geqslant m, p \in \mathbb{N}) .\)

Proof. Fix \(\varepsilon > 0\). Use the assumed estimate with \(\eta = \varepsilon / 2\), and enlarge its threshold so that \(s_m > 2 (|A| + 1) / \varepsilon > 0\). Then, for every \(N \geqslant m\) and \(p\),

\(\displaystyle \left| \frac{\beta_{N, p, m}}{s_m} - c \right| \leqslant \frac{| \beta_{N, p, m} - (A + cs_m) |}{s_m} + \frac{|A|}{s_m} \leqslant \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{|A|}{s_m} < \varepsilon .\)

Both thresholds are independent of \(N, p\). No positivity assumption on \(c\) is used in this proposition.\(\Box\)

Proposition 12.3. (Logarithmic asymptotic) Let \(N, k : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) be arbitrary sequences with \(i \leqslant N (i)\) for all sufficiently large \(i\). For \(\beta_{N, p, m} > 0\) set \(g_{N, p, m} = (\sqrt{\beta_{N, p, m}})^{- 1}\). Then \(\beta_{N (i), k (i), i} > 0\) for all sufficiently large \(i\), and

\(\displaystyle \lim_{i \to \infty} g_{N (i), k (i), i} \sqrt{\log (a_i^{- 1})} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 b_0 (G)}} .\)

Proof. Apply Proposition 12.2 to (12.85), with \(A = \bar{\beta}\) and \(c = 2 b_0 (G) > 0\). For each \(\varepsilon > 0\), enlarge the resulting threshold until \(i \leqslant N (i)\) holds. Substituting \(m = i\), \(N = N (i)\) and \(p = k (i)\) gives

\(\displaystyle \left| \frac{\beta_{N (i), k (i), i}}{s_i} - c \right| < \varepsilon\)

for every subsequent \(i\). Thus \(x_i = \beta_{N (i), k (i), i} / s_i \to c\) on the tail where \(s_i > 0\). Taking \(\varepsilon = c / 2\) shows \(x_i > c / 2 > 0\) eventually; hence \(\beta_{N (i), k (i), i} = s_i x_i > 0\) there. No condition on the sequence \(k\) was needed, because the original threshold was uniform in \(p\).

On this positive tail the square-root limit rule [Leb, Proposition 2.2.6] gives \(\sqrt{x_i} \to \sqrt{c}\). All denominator terms and their limit are nonzero, so the quotient rule [Leb, Proposition 2.2.5(iv)], with constant numerator \(1\), gives \(1 / \sqrt{x_i} \to 1 / \sqrt{c}\). Finally,

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{\sqrt{x_i}} = \frac{\sqrt{s_i}}{\sqrt{\beta_{N (i), k (i), i}}} = g_{N (i), k (i), i} \sqrt{\log (a_i^{- 1})} .\)

The first identity follows by squaring the positive quantities \(\sqrt{x_i}\) and \(\sqrt{\beta_{N (i), k (i), i}} / \sqrt{s_i}\) and using uniqueness of the nonnegative square root. All expressions are interpreted on the positive tail and may be assigned arbitrary values at earlier indices: changing finitely many terms preserves convergence by enlarging each tolerance's threshold beyond those indices. This proves the stated limit.\(\Box\)

At \(N (i) = i\) the coefficient is the bare lattice action coefficient. For a general admissible \(N (i)\), the comparison concerns the coefficient read from the trajectory at spacing \(a_i\). Relating this inverse-square-root proxy to a coupling defined through a physical observable would require a separate matching theorem.

12.13.1Restricting a curve without crossing a barrier

We first record the elementary interval argument. Let \(f\) be continuous on \([x, y]\), and suppose \(f (x) \leqslant d \leqslant u \leqslant f (y)\). There are \(z \leqslant w\) in \([x, y]\) such that

\(\displaystyle f (z) = d, \quad f (w) = u, \quad f ([z, w]) \subset [d, u] .\)

The case \(d = u\) follows by taking one point of the nonempty level set. Otherwise let \(w\) be the first point of \([x, y]\) at which \(f = u\). The level set is nonempty by the intermediate value theorem, and compactness makes its minimum belong to it. Let \(z\) be the last point of \([x, w]\) at which \(f = d\); this level set is likewise nonempty and compact. If \(f (c) < d\) at some \(c \in [z, w]\), the intermediate value theorem between \(c\) and \(w\) gives a later point with value \(d\), contrary to the choice of \(z\). If \(f (c) > u\), the same theorem between \(x\) and \(c\) gives an earlier point with value \(u\), contrary to the choice of \(w\). This proves the claim without any monotonicity assumption on \(f\).

Define inner barriers

\(\displaystyle \ell_i = \bar{\beta} + \tfrac{s}{2} S_i, \qquad u_i = \bar{\beta} + 2 sS_i .\)

Both lie in the permitted band, since \(S_i \geqslant 0\). If an input at level \(i + 1\) has coupling \(\ell_{i + 1}\), its returned coarse coupling satisfies, by (12.87),

\(\displaystyle \beta_i \leqslant \ell_{i + 1} - s \log L_i + \varepsilon_{i + 1} \leqslant \ell_i - \tfrac{s}{4} \log L_i < \ell_i .\)

If the input coupling is \(u_{i + 1}\), the corresponding lower bound is

\(\displaystyle \beta_i \geqslant u_{i + 1} - s \log L_i - \varepsilon_{i + 1} \geqslant u_i + \tfrac{3 s}{4} \log L_i > u_i .\)

Thus the two endpoints cross the next two barriers in the required order, even though the intermediate output curve may oscillate.

12.14.1Transporting the terminal quadratic columns

Write the old terminal full column, at any period, as

\(\displaystyle A = \left( \begin{array}{c} \sqrt{c_0} I\\ R \end{array} \right), \qquad E = \left( \begin{array}{c} I\\ 0 \end{array} \right), \qquad Z = A + E.\)

The terminal metric identity says \(A^{\ast} A \hspace{0.17em} d = d\), where \(d\) is the lattice exterior derivative on one-forms. Since \(E^{\ast} A = A^{\ast} E = \sqrt{c_0} I\), it implies

\(\displaystyle Z^{\ast} Ad = Z^{\ast} Ed.\)

Moreover

\(\displaystyle Z^{\ast} Z = (1 + \sqrt{c_0})^2 I + R^{\ast} R\)

is positive definite. Put

\(\displaystyle P = Z (Z^{\ast} Z)^{- 1} Z^{\ast}, \qquad U = 2 P - I.\)

Direct multiplication gives \(P^{\ast} = P\), \(P^2 = P\) and \(U^{\ast} U = I\). Also \(P (Ad + Ed) = Ad + Ed\) and \(PAd = PEd\), whence

\(\displaystyle UAd = Ed.\)

Construct the analogous reflection \(U_0\) for the desired terminal column \(A_0 = (\sqrt{c_0} I, \sqrt{1 - c_0} I)^{\textsf{T}}\). Then \(X = U_0^{\ast} U\) is an isometry and \(XAd = A_0 d\).

These are periodizations of common exponentially decaying kernels. To check the uniform estimate, suppose the old row kernel has an exponentially weighted absolute coefficient bound at width \(\eta > 0\). Its Fourier symbol and reflected transpose are holomorphic on a smaller strip. On the real torus the Gram matrix is bounded below by \((1 + \sqrt{c_0})^2 I\). The coefficient bound controls uniformly the change of its symbol under a small imaginary displacement. Choose a smaller positive width so that this change has operator norm at most half that lower bound. The Neumann series then constructs a holomorphic inverse on the whole smaller strip, bounded by \(2 / (1 + \sqrt{c_0})^2\). Fourier coefficient estimates on a further smaller strip give an exponentially weighted absolute sum: shift the contour in each coordinate in the sign of the Fourier index and sum the resulting geometric series. Matrix dimension is fixed by the row types, so these constants are independent of period and block length. Multiplication and transposition give the same kind of bound for \(U, U_0, X\). This is the strip inverse argument used for the free kernels.

Modify only the finest transfer column \(m_f\), replacing it by \(Xm_f\). Since \(X^{\ast} X = I\), its Gram matrix is unchanged. Its mixed identity with the terminal curvature column is unchanged after replacing that column by \(A_0\), because \(XAd = A_0 d\). The other transfer columns, including the mean column and the transport matrix, are unchanged. Thus all quadratic transport and orthogonality identities remain valid. The exponential bounds for the modified column follow by convolution of its old bound with the bound for \(X\). There is only this one modification at the terminal step, so its cost does not accumulate along the history.

At each stage choose the new strip width below both the original width and the width just obtained; choose the new upper bound above one, the old bound and this convolution bound; and choose the new lower bound below one and the old lower bound. These choices depend only on the stage profiles and old kernel bounds. They precede the block lengths, delays and realization. The resulting horizon agrees with the old one below its terminal level, its towers agree when \(m + 1 < N\), and their mean and transport columns agree also at \(m + 1 = N\). Its terminal direct metric is the identity. This proves the revised-horizon assertions used in Subsection 13.1.

12.14.2Finite jets of stationary values

We record the analytic calculation controlling the change of path. Let \(F, G : X \times Y \to \mathbb{R}\) be analytic germs at zero on finite-dimensional real spaces. Suppose their derivatives through order \(k + 1\) agree. Let \(u, v : X \to Y\) be analytic germs with \(u (0) = v (0) = 0\) and

\(\displaystyle D_Y F (x, u (x)) = 0, \qquad D_Y G (x, v (x)) = 0.\)

Assume the vertical Hessian of \(F\) at zero is invertible. Then the graphs \((x, u (x))\) and \((x, v (x))\) agree through order \(k\), and the stationary values \(F (x, u (x))\), \(G (x, v (x))\) agree through order \(k + 1\).

To prove the graph assertion, use the analytic local diffeomorphism

\(\displaystyle \Psi_F (x, y) = (x, D_Y F (x, y)) .\)

Its derivative is block triangular with invertible diagonal blocks \(I\) and \(D_Y^2 F (0)\). The corresponding map \(\Psi_G\) has the same jets through order \(k\). On the two stationary graphs these maps both equal \((x, 0)\). Composing their jet identities with the local inverse of \(\Psi_F\) proves equality of graph jets through order \(k\). On a stationary graph the chain rule reduces to

\(\displaystyle D [F (x, u (x))] = D_X F (x, u (x)),\)

because the vertical derivative vanishes. The right sides for \(F, u\) and \(G, v\) agree through order \(k\), using the graph identity and the derivative jets of \(F, G\). Their values at zero also agree. This proves equality of stationary-value jets through order \(k + 1\).

In the application \(k = 3\). The old terminal action and the new startup action both match the Wilson action through order four. For the old one this is item (2) of Definition 11.36, a property of the old plan which is additional to its quadratic metric identity; for the new one it is proved in Subsection 12.15. This matching holds after substitution of the real fine-field chart on each forest slice. Indeed that chart is analytic and sends zero to the identity field; compose the matched real jets with its local logarithm. The noise term is identical in the two tested actions, so adding it preserves the matching.

The vertical Hessians at zero are equal, hence the old positive gap is preserved exactly. The analytic implicit function theorem constructs the new critical graph. Near zero the tested-action germs are independent of the branch tag under the chosen kernel's branch hypothesis; thus this one graph solves every tested critical equation. Its first derivative agrees with the old graph, so it has the same linear mean. The preceding calculation also preserves the classical stationary-value jets through order four. This is the precise classical matching required by the outgoing coarse path, which is left unchanged.

12.14.3The one-loop coefficient and retained limits

The vertical Hessian matrices of the two tested actions have matching jets through order two, since their actions match through order four. The determinant is a finite polynomial in matrix entries, so the determinants have matching two-jets. Composing with the stationary graphs preserves this matching: the graphs agree through order three, more than is needed here. Their positive determinant at zero allows the real logarithm.

The Haar correction is a finite product of the same local chart Jacobian \(J\) along the vertical graph coordinates. Since \(J\) is analytic near zero and \(J (0) = 1\), these products and their logarithms also have matching two-jets. Both terminal loop potentials are zero: the old terminal path has vanishing loop coefficients by item (2) of Definition 11.36, and the startup path has no loop term. It follows that the two thermal functions

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{2} \log \det D_Y^2 F (x, u (x)) - \log \hspace{-0.17em} \prod_b J (u (x)_b)\)

and its counterpart for \(G, v\) have identical Hessians at zero. The finite-period wave estimate is the finite-cutoff bound of Subsubsection 11.21.2: for every forest, every local choice, every two coarse sites and every two polarization amplitudes of modulus at most one, the second derivatives at the origin of the thermal function and of the outgoing comparison function \(\alpha \mathcal{K}_2 +\mathcal{L}\) of the unchanged coarse path, on the corresponding plane-wave directions, differ by at most the stated multiple of \(| \text{Site} (c) | e^{- \gamma c}\), \(c\) being the coarse side. It depends only on these Hessians and the unchanged outgoing classical data. Its bound is therefore preserved with exactly the old scalar correction, gap and tolerance. The other two clauses of the finite-cutoff bound are analyticity of the new thermal function at the origin, which holds because the new graph, the determinant and the chart Jacobian are analytic there, and positivity of the determinant, which is the next remark. Positivity near the new stationary graph follows from continuity and its positive definite Hessian at zero.

Apply this construction only at \(m + 1 = N\); at every lower step keep the old graph, path and scalar correction. The new terminal path is the startup path, while

\(\displaystyle R^{\text{new}}_{N, m} = R^{\text{old}}_{N, m} \quad (m < N), \qquad \alpha^{\text{new}}_{N, m} = \alpha^{\text{old}}_{N, m}, \qquad u^{\text{new}}_{N, m} = u^{\text{old}}_{N, m} \quad (m + 1 < N) .\)

The matching argument above supplies all stationary conditions at the changed step. The revised quadratic horizon supplies its same linear guess and all-period kernel identities. For every fixed level, the changed data consequently agree with the old data on a tail of cutoffs. Their fixed-level limits and eventual extended-domain bounds persist by this exact tail equality. The new terminal graph need not have the old graph's complete analytic expansion, and no such assertion is used.

12.15The terminal Wilson family

The shooting argument needs a continuous initial family on the entire terminal coupling band, with one fixed local dictionary and common summable bounds. We construct it directly from the Wilson density. This also explains why its terminal path can be chosen before any trajectory is selected.

Fix a compact gauge group as in the construction, a clipping profile with direct coefficient \(0 < c_0 \leqslant 1\), linear coefficient \(0 < c_1 < c_0 / 16\), so that in particular \(c_1 \leqslant 1 / 4\), and relaxation length \(\lambda > 0\). Write \(q = 1 - c_0\). For a plaquette holonomy \(u\), put

\(\displaystyle r (u) = \text{chord} (u), \qquad k (u) = \text{curv} (u), \qquad W (u) = \tfrac{1}{2} r (u)^2 .\)

Thus \(0 \leqslant W \leqslant 2\) and \(\|k (u)\| \leqslant r (u)\). The gauge normalization gives \(k (\exp X) = X + O (\|X\|^2)\) near zero.

12.15.1A curvature-coordinate remainder

The analytic inverse function theorem applied to \(X \mapsto k (\exp X)\) gives a local inverse \(v\) near zero. Set \(u (x) = \exp v (x)\) and \(w (x) = W (u (x))\). Conjugation covariance of curvature and uniqueness of the local inverse show \(w (\text{Ad}_g \hspace{0.17em} x) = w (x)\) as germs at zero. The identities \(k (u^{- 1}) = - k (u)\) and \(W (u^{- 1}) = W (u)\) give \(w (- x) = w (x)\). The first and second derivatives at zero are respectively zero and the Euclidean inner product. Thus the fourth Taylor polynomial is

\(\displaystyle T_4 w (x) = \tfrac{1}{2} \|x\|^2 + Q_4 (x), \qquad Q_4 (x) = \frac{1}{4!} D^4 w (0) [x, x, x, x] .\)

The odd terms vanish by evenness. The quartic form \(Q_4\) is adjoint-invariant, by differentiation of the conjugation identity. Define the global smooth residual

\(\displaystyle h (u) = W (u) - \tfrac{1}{2} \|k (u) \|^2 - Q_4 (k (u)) .\)

For every smooth family through the identity its composition with \(h\) has vanishing derivatives through order four. Indeed near the identity \(u = u (k (u))\), so this is the Taylor remainder \((w - T_4 w) (k (u))\); the chain rule preserves the stated vanishing.

We need more than a Taylor estimate at the all-identity link field. For every derivative order \(K\) and spatial power \(P\) there are \(C > 0\) and \(t_0 \in (0, 1]\), independent of the torus, such that

\(\displaystyle | \partial_{a_1} \cdots \partial_{a_l} h (U_0 U_1 U_2^{- 1} U_3^{- 1}) | \leqslant Ct^5, \qquad 0 \leqslant l \leqslant K,\) (12.90)

when the plaquette chord is at most \(t \leqslant t_0\) and the four-link directions have norm at most \(t 5^P\). Derivatives here are successive left increments on the links.

Here is a uniform proof. For fixed links \(U_1, U_2, U_3\) and a small face logarithm \(x\), reconstruct the first link as

\(\displaystyle U_0 (x) = \exp (x) U_3 U_2 U_1^{- 1} .\)

The face holonomy is then \(\exp x\). After \(l\) left differentiations the resulting smooth function of \(x\) vanishes to order \((5 - l)_+ = \max (5 - l, 0)\) at zero. Each differentiation can lower the vanishing order by at most one: introduce its increment parameter as one further variable, differentiate the vanishing Taylor polynomial, and restrict that parameter to zero. All remaining links range over a compact group and the directions, after division by \(t\), range over a fixed compact ball. Taylor's integral remainder therefore gives a common bound \(C_l \|x\|^{(5 - l)_+}\) for the unscaled derivatives. A fixed local logarithm satisfies \(\|x\| \leqslant C_{\log} r (u)\) when \(r (u)\) is sufficiently small. Restoring the \(l\) factors of \(t\) gives \(C_l' C_{\log}^{(5 - l)_+} t^{l + (5 - l)_+} \leqslant C_l'' t^5\), since \(t \leqslant 1\). Take the largest constant and smallest threshold over \(0 \leqslant l \leqslant K\). For a real lattice plaquette, the four links may coincide because of wrapping; substitution into the four-link identity remains valid. Each lies within distance four of any chosen plaquette corner, so the normalized spatially weighted directions obey the required bound \(t 5^P\). This proves (12.90) on every healthy plaquette, including equality in the health threshold.

12.15.2The principal path and the nonnegative debt

Choose the terminal path to have one row \(\sqrt{q} \hspace{0.17em} k (u)\) at each elementary plaquette, quartic term \(qQ_4\), and zero cubic and loop terms. Its zero-momentum metric is \(c_0 I + qI = I\). Each row slot is an elementary plaquette slot with empty walk, hence of length four in the path-length convention of Subsubsection 11.21.2. Each quartic atom has four such legs, all at the same plaquette, and therefore has length sixteen although its geometric support is a single plaquette. Its only nonzero quartic coefficients form a finite list. Hence at any fixed positive strip width \(\sigma\) the weighted path sums are finite: the row sum is \(\sqrt{q} \hspace{0.17em} e^{4 \sigma}\) and the quartic sum is \(e^{16 \sigma}\) times the finite sum of the absolute quartic coefficients. Choosing the stage path allowance above their maximum, and the column allowance above the prescribed factorial, supplies the startup profile before choosing block lengths. Its uncut principal action differs from the Wilson action by \(\sum qh (u)\), so the two actions have identical jets through order four at the identity.

Fix an integer support scale \(B \geqslant 2\) and threshold \(t > 0\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle 2 \leqslant te^{(B - 2) / \lambda} .\) (12.91)

At a plaquette with base site \(x\), the row is kept when every plaquette based at \(y\) has chord at most \(te^{d (x, y) / \lambda}\). Its base action is

\(\displaystyle b (u) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} c_0 r (u)^2 / 2, & r (u) \leqslant t,\\ c_1 tr (u), & r (u) > t, \end{array} \right. +\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{kept}} \hspace{0.17em} q \|k (u)\|^2 / 2.\)

The quartic term is masked by health of the full four-corner plaquette support. Define the debt at that plaquette by

\(\displaystyle d (U) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} W (u) - c_1 tr (u), & r (u) > t,\\ \boldsymbol{1}_{\text{healthy}} \hspace{0.17em} q \|k (u)\|^2 / 2 +\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{not healthy}} \hspace{0.17em} qW (u) -\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{kept}} \hspace{0.17em} q \|k (u)\|^2 / 2, & r (u) \leqslant t. \end{array} \right.\)

A direct split into the healthy, large-chord and remaining cases gives

\(\displaystyle W (u) = b (u) + q\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{healthy}} Q_4 (k (u)) + q\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{healthy}} h (u) + d (U) .\) (12.92)

The debt satisfies \(0 \leqslant d (U) \leqslant 2\), since \(q \|k (u)\|^2 / 2 \leqslant qW (u) \leqslant 2\). On a large-chord plaquette,

\(\displaystyle d (U) \geqslant r (u)^2 / 2 - tr (u) / 4 \geqslant t^2 / 4.\)

If the base site is farther than \(B\) from every flag, the plaquette is healthy and the row is kept. For nearby plaquettes this follows from absence of flags; for the remaining plaquettes (12.91) and the global chord bound \(r \leqslant 2\) make the kept test automatic. Thus its debt vanishes there. The same argument shows that debt at a base site can be determined from the field in its radius-\(B\) neighborhood, with the prescribed boundary flag information.

Average (12.92) over signed coordinate changes. Let \(s_{\ast} = 1 / |\mathcal{S}|\), where \(\mathcal{S}\) is that finite symmetry group, and index the oriented plaquettes by \(i = (s, x, b)\). Their supports are the actual transformed four-corner supports \(Y_i\), with native site \(x\), residual \(h_i\) and debt \(d_i\). The signed average is exact, because the Wilson energy is invariant:

\(\displaystyle \beta E_W (U) = A^{\text{top}}_{\beta} (U) + \beta s_{\ast} q \sum_i \boldsymbol{1}_{\text{healthy} (\text{Y}_{\text{i}})} h_i (U) + \beta s_{\ast} \sum_i d_i (U) .\) (12.93)

This uses the transformed supports and native sites in every channel.

12.15.3Forced packets and the exact Wilson density

Let \(\mathcal{D} (U)\) be the flag set. Join two flags when their closed radius-\(2 B\) balls overlap, and let \(D\) run over the connected components for this relation. Put

\(\displaystyle P (D) = \bigcup_{x \in D} B_{2 B} (x), \qquad C_D (U) = s_{\ast} \sum_{i : \hspace{0.17em} x_i \in B_B (D)} d_i (U) .\)

The regions \(P (D)\) of distinct components are disjoint and \(P (D) \cap \mathcal{D} (U) = D\). Every nonzero debt lies in exactly one of these sums: it lies within \(B\) of a flag, while membership in two such neighborhoods would join the two flag components. Consequently

\(\displaystyle \sum_D C_D (U) = s_{\ast} \sum_i d_i (U) .\)

The energy bound is

\(\displaystyle C_D (U) \geqslant |D| s_{\ast} t^2 / 16.\) (12.94)

To prove it, every flag is a corner of a large-chord plaquette. Such a plaquette has at most four corners and its base is within distance two of the flag, hence in \(B_B (D)\). If \(\mathcal{A}\) is the set of these plaquettes in the identity channel, then \(|D| \leqslant 4 |\mathcal{A}|\). Each contributes at least \(t^2 / 4\); all other debts are nonnegative. Multiplication by \(s_{\ast}\) proves (12.94).

In a fixed dictionary include every nonempty connected flag set \(D\) with address \((P (D), D)\), and put

\(\displaystyle K_D (\beta, U) =\boldsymbol{1}_{\{P (D) \cap \mathcal{D}(U) = D\}} e^{- \beta C_D (U)} .\)

Other addresses have zero coefficient. The forced gas sums products over disjoint supports with total flag inventory \(\mathcal{D} (U)\). Exactly one family can have nonzero product: the components of \(\mathcal{D} (U)\) just constructed. Indeed disjoint connected regions covering that inventory give its component decomposition. Thus the gas equals \(e^{- \beta s_{\ast} \sum_i d_i (U)}\), including the empty product when there are no flags. Put the regular residuals \(\beta s_{\ast} qh_i\) in their healthy supports and take every extra logarithmic attachment to be zero. Equation (12.93) now proves that the full density is exactly \(e^{- \beta E_W (U)}\) at every field.

All coefficients are gauge-invariant and measurable; the local residuals are smooth in left increments. Their value and Hessian at the identity vanish. Permuting the signed channels and lattice sites gives the required translation and signed-coordinate covariance. The packet charge reads only its support with the given flags, since the radius-\(B\) neighborhood of a site in \(B_B (D)\) lies in \(P (D)\). These are the locality conditions of the startup family.

12.15.4Uniform bounds on the whole coupling band

Let \(a > 0\) be the support exponent. A plaquette support has cover number at most \(5^4\). At a fixed native site there are \(|\mathcal{S}|\) times the number \(n_{\square}\) of plaquette directions regular entries. By (12.90) their regular budget for \(0 \leqslant \beta \leqslant v\) is at most

\(\displaystyle |\mathcal{S}| n_{\square} e^{a 5^4} \hspace{0.17em} vCt^5 .\) (12.95)

The constants depend on the stage, not on volume or cutoff.

Choose positive numbers \(\sigma, \kappa, \gamma\) and a lower band endpoint \(l\) such that

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \kappa + \sigma & \leqslant l \hspace{0.17em} s_{\ast} t^2 / 16, \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.96)} \\ (4 B + 1)^4 e^{(a + 2) 5^4 - \sigma} & \leqslant \gamma, \qquad (2 B + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1, \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.97)} \\ (2 B + 1)^4 (e^{\gamma} - 1) & \leqslant 1. \hspace{3cm} \text{(12.98)} \end{array}\)

For \(\beta \geqslant l\), (12.94) implies

\(\displaystyle |K_D (\beta, U) | \leqslant e^{- \kappa} e^{- \sigma |D|} .\)

The elementary ball supports \(B_{2 B} (x)\) have cover number at most \(5^4\), and at most \((4 B + 1)^4\) of them contain a specified site. Thus (12.97) bounds their rooted support sum with exponent \(a + 2\) by \(\gamma\). Apply the connected-support estimate (7.35), with spare exponent one, to these ball supports. It gives the packet majorant

\(\displaystyle \sup_x \sum_{D : \hspace{0.17em} x \in P (D)} e^{- \sigma |D|} e^{(a + 1) \text{cov}_B (P (D))} \leqslant e^{\gamma} - 1.\)

This is one common summable majorant for the entire band \(\beta \geqslant l\). The regular and forced budgets, and the extra one-unit support reserve, have now been proved. After the finite gas grouping the forced cost is at most \(e^{- \kappa} (e^{e^{\gamma} - 1} - 1)\), by the same connected-support estimate with (12.98).

We verify that the actual schedule pays these inequalities. Write \(s = \log \hat{\beta}\) and set

\(\displaystyle M = e^{(\log s)^2}, \quad t = e^{- s / 2} M, \quad B = \lceil s^2 \rceil, \quad \gamma = e^{- M / 2}, \quad \delta_E = e^{- 11 s / 10}, \quad \delta_K = e^{- M^{1 / 5}} .\)

At a fixed stage take \(\sigma = M\), \(l = e^s / 2\) and \(\kappa = M\). Then \(lt^2 s_{\ast} / 16 = s_{\ast} M^2 / 32 \geqslant 2 M\) eventually, giving (12.96). Since \(B\) grows polynomially in \(s\) while \(M\) grows faster than every such polynomial, (12.97) and (12.98) hold eventually. More explicitly \((4 B + 1)^4 \leqslant M / 4\) and \((a + 2) 5^4 \leqslant M / 4\) eventually, so their product with \(e^{- M}\) is at most \(e^{- M / 2}\); also \(e^{\gamma} - 1 \leqslant 2 \gamma\) once \(\gamma \leqslant 1\). Since \(\gamma \to 0\), we may also require \(e^{e^{\gamma} - 1} - 1 \leqslant 1\). The grouped forced cost is then at most \(e^{- M}\) and hence at most \(\delta_K\).

The logarithm of (12.95), with \(v = 2 e^s\), equals

\(\displaystyle C - \frac{3}{2} s + 5 (\log s)^2,\)

where \(C\) is a stage constant. It is at most \(- 11 s / 10\) for large \(s\), because \((\log s)^2 = o (s)\). Also \(t \to 0\), \(B \to \infty\), and \(\log (te^{(B - 2) / \lambda}) = - s / 2 + (\log s)^2 + (B - 2) / \lambda \to \infty\). Thus the residual threshold and (12.91) are paid at the same entry delay.

The stronger startup reserve needed by the later common services is obtained without changing this family. For any fixed additional support exponent \(\eta \geqslant 0\), replace \(a\) by \(a + \eta\) in the preceding bounds and delay the stage further. Take instead \(l = e^s / 4\) and \(\kappa = s_{\ast} M^2 / 128\). Eventually \(M \leqslant \kappa\), and

\(\displaystyle \kappa + M \leqslant 2 \kappa = s_{\ast} M^2 / 64 = lt^2 s_{\ast} / 16.\)

The same packet bounds then give a forced allowance at most \(e^{- s_{\ast} M^2 / 128}\) before grouping, with a still smaller grouped cost after the reserve is paid. These choices depend on the stage and its fixed extra requests, and work for all periods and cutoffs realizing that stage. Taking their entry thresholds together with the other already chosen thresholds therefore preserves the order of choices in the common construction.

12.15.5Continuity and the initial history

The dictionary and the masks above do not depend on \(\beta\). Regular coefficients depend linearly on \(\beta\), so their normalized derivative differences have the bound \(C | \beta - \beta' | t^5\). For a packet, \(0 \leqslant C_D (U) \leqslant 2 s_{\ast} |\mathcal{S}| \hspace{0.17em} | \Lambda | n_{\square}\), and, for \(\beta, \beta' \geqslant 0\),

\(\displaystyle |e^{- \beta C_D (U)} - e^{- \beta' C_D (U)} | \leqslant C_D (U) | \beta - \beta' | .\)

There are finitely many nonzero addresses on a fixed torus. Their weighted difference sums consequently tend to zero with \(\beta' - \beta\), uniformly in \(U\). Together with the band-wide majorants above this proves the required curve property and invariant-atom continuity. No continuity of a health indicator as a function of \(U\) is asserted.

Pack the dictionary into the fixed terminal dictionary by its coupling-independent injection. Zero unused entries contribute neither cost nor logarithm, and every nonzero coefficient, support, inventory, and density is preserved. The finite gas grouping is the absorption of Subsection 8.32 applied to the packed packet activities with all logarithms equal to zero. It is applied to this same dictionary and these same coefficients, and it leaves the regular entries unchanged. Its continuity in \(\beta\) is Lemma 12.1: the first hypothesis is the fixed-address continuity just proved, and the second holds with the band-wide majorants \(e^{- \kappa} e^{- \sigma |D|}\), of which only finitely many are nonzero on the fixed torus. Together with the grouped forced cost computed above, this gives a continuous family on \([\hat{\beta} / 2, 2 \hat{\beta}]\) with exact Wilson density, regular allowance \(\delta_E\) and grouped forced allowance \(\delta_K\). Its scalar constant is zero and its coupling is the input parameter \(\beta\).

We collect the result, separating the numerical conditions from the two identifications with the history.

Proposition 12.6. (The terminal family) Let \(N\) be a level of the scale schedule and let its stage have clip \((c_0, c_1, \lambda)\) with \(c_0 \leqslant 1\), derivative order \(K\), spatial power \(P\) and support exponent \(a\). Let \(t\), \(B\) and \(\hat{\beta}\) be the threshold, radius and reference coupling of level \(N\), and \(\delta_E, \delta_K\) its regular and forced allowances. Assume:

  1. \(B \geqslant 2\) and the relaxation condition (12.91);

  2. \(t \leqslant t_0\), where \(t_0\) is the threshold of (12.90) for \(K, P\);

  3. the regular book (12.95) with \(v = 2 \hat{\beta}\) is at most \(\delta_E\);

  4. there are \(\sigma, \kappa, \gamma\) with \(\kappa \geqslant 0\) satisfying (12.96)–(12.98) for \(l = \hat{\beta} / 2\), and \(e^{- \kappa} (e^{e^{\gamma} - 1} - 1) \leqslant \delta_K\).

Then for every period there is a family \(F_{\beta}\), \(\beta \in [\hat{\beta} / 2, 2 \hat{\beta}]\), of representations at level \(N\) in one fixed dictionary, such that: the path of \(F_{\beta}\) is the startup path of this section in the stage clip; its coupling is \(\beta\) and its vacuum constant is zero; its density is \(e^{- \beta E_W}\) at every field; its regular and forced arrays have the locality, symmetry and jet properties listed above, with regular cost at most \(\delta_E\) and forced cost at most \(\delta_K\); and \(\beta \mapsto F_{\beta}\) is continuous in the labelled regular and forced norms. Moreover the reserved labels are empty. The fixed terminal dictionary contains, for every regular address and every natural number, a reserved regular label with that address, and for every forced support with nonempty inventory and every natural number, a reserved forced label with that support and inventory; these lists do not depend on \(\beta\). The regular and forced address lists of \(F_{\beta}\) are those of the fixed dictionary, and the regular, respectively forced, coefficient of \(F_{\beta}\) on every reserved label is zero. The additional predicate of the reserved forced label with support \(X\) and inventory \(I\) holds at a configuration \(U\) exactly when \(X \cap \text{flags} (U) = I\).

Suppose in addition that the stationary plan of the history has the startup path as its terminal path at \((N, N)\), as in Proposition 12.5, and that the recipe of the history is the default recipe built from that plan and schedule. Then every \(F_{\beta}\) satisfies the retained-history conditions at level \(N\) and the starter relation of that recipe.

Proof. The first part was proved in the preceding subsections: condition (1) is used for the debt and its locality, (2) for the residual bound, (3) for the regular budget on \(0 \leqslant \beta \leqslant 2 \hat{\beta}\), and (4) for the packet majorant on \(\beta \geqslant \hat{\beta} / 2\) and the grouped forced cost. The statement on reserved labels is a property of the fixed packing and grouping, valid for every dictionary and coefficient family to which they are applied. The packing injection takes its values among the unreserved labels, and every entry outside its image is zero. A reserved forced label is the group consisting of one reserved hard label alone. Every grouped label of the packed family consists of packed labels, so a reserved forced label is not among them, and every grouped entry outside the image of the packed family is zero. A reserved forced label is read as the group of one reserved hard label of shape \((X, I)\). Reading it recovers exactly the support \(X\) and the inventory \(I\), and its additional predicate is the original test for that single shape. That test is the flag condition \(X \cap \text{flags} (U) = I\). For the second part, the retained-history conditions at level \(N\) are: the path is the terminal path of the plan, the clip and budget are those of level \(N\), the arrays satisfy the stage bounds at the allowances \(\delta_E, \delta_K\), the path satisfies its stage bounds, and the representation is a start of the recipe. The first is the assumed identity of paths; it is an equality of path data, which agreement of jets through order four would not give. The bounds are the first part and the path bounds of the plan. A start of the default recipe is a representation with coupling in the terminal band which is obtained by the fixed packing and grouping from some dictionary and coefficient family carrying a pre-absorption certificate at the stage allowances, whose raw density on the terminal path of the plan, multiplied by the exponential of its logarithmic corrections, is the Wilson density. Our pre-grouping dictionary and coefficients are such a witness: the regular book and the packet loan give the certificate, the logarithms are zero, and the raw density identity is the one proved above, for the terminal path by the assumed identity of paths. The default recipe imposes no further permission condition on a start. The starter relation is the same statement with the band condition recorded.\(\Box\)

The four numerical conditions hold on the actual schedule, at every level \(N\) including \(N = 0\), for the following reason. The preceding subsection showed that for each fixed stage they hold for all sufficiently large \(s = \log \hat{\beta}\), with \(t, B, \delta_E, \delta_K\) the functions of \(s\) displayed there. The resulting threshold depends only on the stage, so it can be imposed before the schedule is realized. The common selection must include it among the entry requirements of that stage; this inclusion is made in Subsection 13.1 and is an input here. Granting it, a paid schedule enters each stage beyond its entry requirement and has at every level the threshold, radius and allowances given by those functions of its reference coupling, so the four conditions hold at every level. No blocking step is involved in these conditions, so the case \(N = 0\) needs no separate argument. The identification of the terminal path is Proposition 12.5, and the recipe is fixed in Subsection 13.1. With them the proposition gives the initial-family assertion used in Subsection 13.1 for every \(N\) and every period.
