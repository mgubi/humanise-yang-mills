\section{Selecting the coupling and the vacuum constant}
\label{sec:coupling-reset-selection}
An exact local return contains a small correction to the kinetic
coefficient. We must show that this correction lies in the purchased
interval and depends continuously on the incoming representation. Two
healthy configurations suffice to select the two scalar parameters. The
exact density identity at other configurations comes from the local
return, and must be retained separately.

\subsection{A separating configuration in every volume}
Assume the gauge Lie algebra is nonzero, as it is for our simple gauge group.
Let $V_\Lambda=|\Lambda|$ be the number of sites in an even-sided torus.
Let $a_0>0$ be the direct plaquette coefficient of the selected clipped
kinetic action $\mathcal K$, and let $C\ge0$ bound its cubic and quartic
arrays. Fix a healthy-field threshold $0<t\le1$. There is a number
$\eta>0$, depending on the stage bounds but not the torus or $t$, and
an all-healthy configuration $U_*$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reset-separator}
                  \mathcal K(U_*)\ge V_\Lambda\eta t^2.
\end{equation}
Here and below the trivial configuration is denoted by $1$.

To construct it, first choose $\epsilon>0$ such that every chord value
in $[0,\epsilon]$ occurs in $G$. Indeed, take a nonzero Lie-algebra
vector $c$. The derivative at zero of the curvature of $\exp(sc)$ is
$c$, so its chord cannot vanish identically. Continuity and the
intermediate value theorem along that path give the assertion. Set
\[
 r=\min\{\epsilon,1/4,a_0/[512(1+C)]\}>0,
 \qquad h=rt,
\]
and choose $u\in G$ of chord $h$. Set the links in direction $0$ equal
to $u$ on even coordinate-$1$ slices and to $1$ on odd slices; set all
other links equal to $1$. Even side length makes this periodic. Every
$(0,1)$ plaquette has holonomy $u$ or $u^{-1}$ and hence chord $h$.
Every plaquette has chord at most $4h\le t$, by the four-link triangle
inequality. Thus the configuration is healthy.

For each signed lattice symmetry the sum of the direct plaquette terms
is at least $V_\Lambda a_0h^2/2$: the symmetry permutes plaquettes,
and the indicated plane already contributes that amount. The other
quadratic terms are nonnegative on this healthy configuration. The
absolute cubic-plus-quartic contribution per site is at most
\[
 C(4h)^3+C(4h)^4
 \le 128Ch^3\le a_0h^2/4.
\]
The first inequality uses $4h\le1$, and the second uses
$512Ch\le a_0$. Averaging the signed actions preserves the lower
bound $V_\Lambda a_0h^2/4$. This proves
\eqref{eq:reset-separator} with $\eta=a_0r^2/4$.

\subsection{The scalar equation on a purchased interval}
Fix the coarse path, clip and budget. Let $x$ range in an interval $I$,
and let $H_x(U)$ be the actual unnormalized transferred density. Assume
$H_x(1),H_x(U_*)>0$. Write
\[
 c(x)=\log H_x(1)-\log H_x(U_*),\qquad
 b(x)=\beta_f(x)+\alpha_{N,m}.
\]
For a trial correction $d\in[-w,w]$, $0<w\le1$, write its density as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reset-trial-density}
 e^{V_\Lambda v}
 e^{-(b(x)+d)\mathcal K(U)-\mathcal L(U)-A_{x,d}(U)}
                         \mathcal G_{x,d}(U).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal L$ is the fixed loop action, $A_{x,d}$ the regular
activity sum, and $\mathcal G_{x,d}$ the forced gas. The regular
entries vanish at $1$, and $\mathcal K(1)=\mathcal L(1)=0$ because
each path atom vanishes at the trivial configuration. The forced gas
equals $1$ on healthy
configurations. In particular the vacuum parameter is forced to be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reset-vacuum}
                    v(x)=V_\Lambda^{-1}\log H_x(1).
\end{equation}
Put $k=\mathcal K(U_*)>0$ and define the residual
\[
 f(x,d)=(b(x)+d)k+\mathcal L(U_*)+A_{x,d}(U_*)-c(x).
\]
The trial matches $H_x$ at $1$ and $U_*$ exactly when $v$ has
value \eqref{eq:reset-vacuum} and $f(x,d)=0$.

Here are the estimates required for this selection. Let
$\delta\ge0$ be the regular allowance per site and $F\ge0$ the
reset allowance per site. Assume
\begin{align}
 |b(x)k+\mathcal L(U_*)-c(x)|&\le V_\Lambda F,
                                      \label{eq:reset-surplus}\\
 |A_{x,0}(U_*)|&\le V_\Lambda\delta,\nonumber\\
 |A_{x,d'}(U_*)-A_{x,d}(U_*)|
              &\le V_\Lambda F|d'-d|,\nonumber\\
 2(F+\delta)&\le\eta t^2w.\label{eq:reset-purchase}
\end{align}
The arrays have fixed labels, are continuous in their regular and forced
norms along parameter curves, and have locally summable comparison
majorants. These conditions imply continuity of their evaluated sums
along the parameter and dial: truncate a summable majorant, use
continuity for the finite part, and bound the two tails by that majorant.
The same argument works if domination is supplied on a further
subsequence of every convergent sequence. Otherwise a sequence
violating continuity would have a further dominated subsequence which
still violates it. We also assume continuity of the two positive
values of $H_x$ and of $b(x)$.

From \eqref{eq:reset-separator} and \eqref{eq:reset-purchase},
\[
 V_\Lambda(F+\delta)\le kw/2,\qquad V_\Lambda F\le k/2.
\]
Consequently, for $d\le d'$,
\[
 f(x,d')-f(x,d)\ge\tfrac12k(d'-d),\qquad
                         |f(x,0)|\le kw/2.
\]
Thus $f(x,-w)\le0\le f(x,w)$. Continuity in $d$ gives a zero
$d(x)\in[-w,w]$, and the strict slope bound makes it unique in that
interval. It also gives the useful inverse estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reset-inverse}
 |d(x)-d(y)|\le \frac2k|f(x,d(y))-f(y,d(y))|.
\end{equation}
For fixed $y$, the right side tends to zero as $x\to y$; this proves
continuity of $d$ including at endpoints. Substitution into the fixed
labelled arrays preserves their continuity and their budgets. Together
with \eqref{eq:reset-vacuum} this produces a continuous family of
representations with the purchased correction bound $|d(x)|\le w$.

\subsection{Identification with the physical local correction}
In the actual local construction the activity array is independent of
the trial dial. The local projection and compulsory-packet absorption
first give a number $\Delta(x)$ and an array $S_x$ for which
\eqref{eq:reset-trial-density}, at $d=\Delta(x)$ and
\[
 v_0(x)=\tau_x-V_\Lambda^{-1}\sum_i v_x^{\rm reg}(i,1),
\]
is exactly $H_x(U)$ for every $U$. This is the exact local
reorganization; its scalar sign is fixed by evaluating the identity at
$U=1$. Its regular arrays vanish at the flat configuration after
subtraction. With $A_{x,d}$ and $\mathcal G_{x,d}$ independent of $d$,
taking the two healthy evaluations of that exact identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reset-physical-residual}
                         f(x,d)=(d-\Delta(x))k.
\end{equation}
Therefore the selected zero is $d(x)=\Delta(x)$ and $v(x)=v_0(x)$.
This identifies the selected continuous family with the actual
reorganized local return, rather than merely producing a family with
the same two scalar tests.

One may also express the full-field justification as a quantitative
error bound. For fixed $x,U$, put $q=\mathcal K(U)$ and
$r_U=H_x(U)/H_x(1)$. The trial normalized shape equals
$e^{(\Delta-d)q}r_U$. Since $|d|\le1$,
\[
 |e^{(\Delta-d)q}r_U-r_U|
 \le e^{(1+|\Delta|)|q|}|q|\,|r_U|\,|\Delta-d|
 =\frac{e^{(1+|\Delta|)|q|}|q|\,|r_U|}{k}|f(x,d)|.
\]
This follows from $|e^z-1|\le e^M|z|$ for real $z\le M$, $M\ge0$.
It applies also when the forced gas is nontrivial or signed. At the
selected zero it proves exact equality at every configuration; the
constant here may depend on $x,U$ and the finite volume.

The remaining compatibility check uses the actual local construction
of Section~\ref{sec:physical-reset-reorganization}. Write
$H^{\rm early}(x)$ for its early regular price (denoted $H$ there;
here $H_x$ already denotes the density), and $\delta(x),\gamma(x)$
for the regular and forced allowances of its output. Retain the dictionary and coefficients of that construction as they
are before the final forced absorption: the regular entries, the
hard entries and the logarithmic corrections. That construction
supplies three facts about this one batch: the subtraction
estimate $t^2|\Delta(x)|\le H^{\rm early}(x)$ of
\eqref{eq:reset-isotropic-coefficient}; the pre-absorption
certificate at the allowances $\delta(x),\gamma(x)$, consisting of
the regular bound at $\delta(x)$, the hard and logarithmic bounds
and a pair $\mu,\kappa$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:reset-forced-purchase} for the target $\gamma(x)$; and
the normalized density identity, namely that the density of the
batch with vacuum constant zero, multiplied by the exponential of
its logarithmic corrections, equals $H_x(U)/H_x(1)$ at every field.
The history constraints are those of the selected recipe. A step of
that recipe is a batch of this kind together with a number
$\Delta$, satisfying these three conditions with
$H^{\rm early},\delta,\gamma$ replaced by the recipe's own prices,
called its portion and its regular and forced charges; its
permission condition is unrestricted for the default recipe. The
certificate is monotone in its two allowances. Hence the batch
retained above, for an input of the history whose coupling lies
in its band, is a step of the recipe as soon as
\[
 H^{\rm early}(x)\le\operatorname{portion}(x),\qquad
 \delta(x)\le\operatorname{charge}_E(x),\qquad
 \gamma(x)\le\operatorname{charge}_K(x);
\]
the comparisons enlarge the allowances of the same pre-absorption
certificate, and no function, support or label is changed. The
represented output is then obtained from this batch by the fixed
packing and absorption. These three comparisons are
not proved here. They are \eqref{eq:common-recipe-prices} in
Section~\ref{sec:common-funded-history}, for the selected prices.
Granting them, the same local construction is the witness for the
retained-history and next-step constraints: appending its batch to
the incoming finite history gives both. Equality
of densities alone would not imply those constraints for arbitrary
arrays or dictionaries. The forced estimate is retained separately,
including its exponential monitor bound, which is
\eqref{eq:common-final-forcing}.

This proves the scalar selection and its full-field identification
relative to the physical reorganization and the displayed purchases.
In that application the dial-motion estimate is zero. The surplus
estimate comes from the exact all-healthy local expression and the
regular budget, together with the purchased cubic/quartic discrepancy
between the full kinetic action and its quadratic part. The next
construction must verify those purchases on the selected history;
the scalar argument itself does not choose a new path or new prices.
