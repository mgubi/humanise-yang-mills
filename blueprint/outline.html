The public theorem at the top

The main public endpoint is YangMills.jointExistence. At a high level, it asserts the existence, for each compact matrix gauge group with simple Lie algebra, of a Wilson family and suitable refinements along each requested increasing sequence of volumes. The resulting hierarchy packages Wilson convergence, reconstruction, operator-product information, and a trace test. This description is provisional; the exact quantified statement belongs in the statement-audit task.

These are ownership and navigation clusters. Their compiled-graph builds_on summaries contain mutual implementation dependencies and are not a topological proof order. A cluster is not automatically a chapter or a proof task. In particular, Part III contains about 70% of the mapped source, while several clusters mainly organize definitions, parameter choices, analytic utilities, or accounting.

The revised outlines identify III.13 as an alternative common-family construction for YM.SharpGram.habitat. The Wilson family of the public construction is selected in III.14 and assembled in VIII.1. The two families are not identified by that outline. Likewise, VII.1 supplies the free side of the short-distance comparison, while the interacting comparison crosses VI.2 and VIII.1; the free Wick and Gram results of VII.3 support a finite-profile exclusion concluded through VII.2 and VIII.1.

Proposed monograph parts

  1. Setting and construction data. Define the gauge-theoretic, lattice, Wilson-family, field-label, and polymer framework. Quote standard background where possible and state project-specific conventions precisely.

  2. One-step renormalization framework. Develop blocking, symmetric gauge fixing, free kernels, Gaussian changes, and the polymer/cluster expansion.

  3. Effective action and coupling flow. Construct the stationary background, match Wilson energy to fourth order, and derive the marginal flow/reset.

  4. Nonlinear RG estimates. Prove the one-step induction, irrelevant and large-field bounds, determinant/free-Gaussian comparison, and parameter closure.

  5. Uniformity and the construction run. Obtain volume-uniform estimates and spacing limits, then select the funded run and its common Wilson family for the public theorem. Treat III.13's alternative family separately.

  6. Infinite volume. Extract a subsequence for every requested volume sequence and establish the Euclidean hierarchy.

  7. Phases and reconstruction. Decompose the limit into phases and reconstruct the corresponding Wightman theories.

  8. Symmetry and stress tensor. Establish Ward identities, rotations, conservation, and the free-Maxwell comparison used later.

  9. Identification and interaction evidence. Prove the short-distance Maxwell behavior, trace statement, nonfree criterion, and the precisely delimited running-coupling conclusion.

  10. OPE, contacts, and fixed-field consequences. Present the pointwise OPE, contact extensions, and selected scaling/Sharp-Gram consequences.

  11. Assembly and concrete gauge groups. Translate the internal construction into the public theorem and specialize it to SU(2).

This chapter order follows the mathematical transfer of information. It need not follow source-file order. Detailed candidate proof units and dependencies are in unit-plan.md.

Editorial boundaries

  • Standard results should be located and cited before a new proof is written. The consuming unit must check the cited hypotheses and prove any unmatched step.

  • Generated declarations, coercions, instances, record constructors, and tactic artifacts do not receive prose units. Any mathematical content they encode remains.

  • The public bridges receive a correspondence and assembly explanation; they do not cause the underlying proofs to be repeated.

  • The theorem does not acquire a mass-gap, uniqueness of the subsequential limit, clustering, or other physical conclusion merely from the project title. Every such claim requires an exact audited declaration.
