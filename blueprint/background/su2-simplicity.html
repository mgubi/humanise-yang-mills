Result to be used

Put

\(\displaystyle G = \{U \in M_2 (\mathbb{C}) : U^{\ast} U = UU^{\ast} = 1, \det U = 1\}, \qquad L = \{X \in M_2 (\mathbb{C}) : \exp (tX) \in G \text{ for every } t \in \mathbb{R}\} .\)

The scalar field of \(L\) is real, and its bracket is \([X, Y] = XY - YX\). The needed assertion is:

  1. Some \(X, Y \in L\) have \([X, Y] \ne 0\).

  2. Every real vector subspace \(J \subseteq M_2 (\mathbb{C})\) such that \(J \subseteq L\) and \([L, J] \subseteq J\) equals either \(0\) or \(L\).

This is exactly nonabelian simplicity of the real Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{su} (2)\). It is not abstract-group simplicity of \(G\).

The range of mat is exactly \(G\): its wrappers require unitarity and determinant one, and any matrix meeting these requirements supplies the two subtype witnesses. Hence the formal exponential condition is the displayed definition of \(L\), with no additional restriction on its elements.

The commutator is the usual mathematical convention, with no factor of \(i\). Hall's identification therefore applies as stated:

\(\displaystyle L = \{X : X^{\ast} = - X, \text{tr} X = 0\} .\)

The hypotheses here are checked by the concrete matrix group above, not by assuming an arbitrary field named lieAlgebra has the usual meaning. The real submodule and bracket closure are provided by YM.Gauge.lie and YM.Gauge.bracket_mem. The public packaging uses the same carrier.

An ambient real submodule contained in \(L\) is equivalently a real vector subspace of \(L\). Its stated closure under \([L, J]\) is the ordinary Lie ideal condition; antisymmetry also gives \([J, L] \subseteq J\). Thus no closedness of ideals, invariance under the whole group, or complex scalar closure is being silently added.

Application and remaining proof obligations

The following bridge is supplied explicitly rather than identifying real and complex simplicity without justification. Its mathematical reasoning is an application proposed for review; it is not a claim that the Lean proof follows this route.

Identify the complexification

Write \(\mathfrak{s}= \{Z \in M_2 (\mathbb{C}) : \text{tr} Z = 0\}\), with its complex vector-space structure and matrix bracket. For \(Z \in \mathfrak{s}\), put

\(\displaystyle X = (Z - Z^{\ast}) / 2, \qquad Y = (Z + Z^{\ast}) / (2 i) .\)

Taking adjoints gives \(X^{\ast} = - X\) and \(Y^{\ast} = - Y\). Their traces vanish since \(\text{tr} Z = \text{tr} Z^{\ast} = 0\), so \(X, Y \in L\), and \(Z = X + iY\). This expression is unique: if \(X = - iY\) with both \(X, Y\) skew-Hermitian, then \(- iY\) is Hermitian, so \(X\) is both Hermitian and skew-Hermitian and must be zero, as must \(Y\).

Consequently \(\mathfrak{s}= L \oplus iL\) as real vector spaces. The map

\(\displaystyle L \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C} \longrightarrow \mathfrak{s}, \qquad X \otimes z \longmapsto zX\)

is complex linear and bijective by this decomposition. It preserves the bracket because \([zX, wY] = zw [X, Y]\). Thus this is the required complex Lie-algebra identification with \(\mathfrak{sl}_2 (\mathbb{C})\).

Transfer the ideal conclusion back to real scalars

Let \(J \subseteq L\) satisfy the real ideal condition. Inside \(\mathfrak{s}\) form \(J_{\mathbb{C}} = J + iJ\). It is a complex vector subspace: multiplication by \(i\) sends \(a + ib\) to \(- b + ia\). For \(x, y \in L\) and \(a, b \in J\),

\(\displaystyle [x + iy, a + ib] = [x, a] - [y, b] + i ([x, b] + [y, a]) \in J + iJ.\)

Hence it is a complex Lie ideal of \(\mathfrak{s}\). If \(J \ne 0\), then \(J_{\mathbb{C}} \ne 0\). Applying Kirillov's Example 5.38 gives \(J_{\mathbb{C}} =\mathfrak{s}\). For any \(x \in L\), write \(x = a + ib\) with \(a, b \in J\). Uniqueness of the decomposition in \(L \oplus iL\) forces \(a = x\) and \(b = 0\), so \(x \in J\). Thus \(J = L\). If \(J = 0\), the other alternative already holds. This proves exactly the second formal clause.

Check the nonabelian clause and boundary of the application

The matrices

\(\displaystyle A = \left( \begin{array}{cc} i & 0\\ 0 & - i \end{array} \right), \quad B = \left( \begin{array}{cc} 0 & 1\\ - 1 & 0 \end{array} \right), \quad C = \left( \begin{array}{cc} 0 & i\\ i & 0 \end{array} \right)\)

are skew-Hermitian and traceless, so belong to \(L\). Direct multiplication gives \(AB = C\), \(BA = - C\), hence \([A, B] = 2 C \ne 0\). This supplies the first formal clause independently of differences in textbook simplicity conventions. These are also the matrices used in the inspected internal proof.

The combined argument supplies YangMills.su2Simple. The only use inspected in YangMills.su2Theory is passing that proof as the simplicity hypothesis to YangMills.jointExistence. This note establishes no other hypothesis or conclusion of that theorem and gives no reconstruction or mass-gap result.

No quotient by the centre is needed for this real Lie-algebra result. Indeed \(\{1, - 1\}\) is a nontrivial proper central subgroup of \(G\): both scalar matrices have determinant one, and \(A \in G\) is neither of them. Thus an assertion that SU(2) is simple as an abstract group would be false here. Likewise, simple connectedness is a different property. Neither connectedness nor simple connectedness is used in the ideal-transfer argument, and no implication in the reverse direction from real simplicity to complex simplicity is assumed.

There is no unmatched mathematical hypothesis identified for this particular source match. Independent verification of the carrier identification, the complexification bridge, and the references remains required before acceptance.
