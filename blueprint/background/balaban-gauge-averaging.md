## Result to be used

The interface that provisional unit PU-01 needs is the following well-posedness statement. It is established in the pinned source itself and not by any of the papers examined here.

**Setting (S).** A gauge group $G$ is a closed subgroup of $U(N)$, $N\ge1$, with no further hypothesis: `YM.Gauge` (`YM/Gauge/MatrixGroup.lean`) has only the fields `n`, `imageGroup`, `closed_imageGroup`, so finite, abelian and disconnected groups are included. Write $s(u)=1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(u)/N$ (`YM.Gauge.price`) and $\lvert u\rvert_c=\lVert u-1\rVert_{HS}/\sqrt N$ (`YM.Gauge.chord`); then $\lvert u\rvert_c^2=2s(u)$ (`YM.Gauge.chord_price`). A patch (`YM.RG.Patch`) is the cube $\Lambda=[-R,R]^4\cap\mathbb Z^4$ with $R=(J+3)L$, $L\ge3$ odd, $J\ge4$. For $x,y\in\mathbb Z^4$ and a permutation $l$ of the four axes, $\Gamma_l(x,y)$ is the lattice path from $x$ to $y$ that corrects the coordinates in the order $l(0),l(1),l(2),l(3)$, one straight leg per axis (`YM.Lattice.route x y l`, `YM/Lattice/Box.lean`), and $U(\Gamma)$ is the product of link variables along $\Gamma$, first link leftmost (`YM.Lattice.travel`, `YM/Lattice/Path.lean`).

**Frame functional (S).** For $h\colon\Lambda\to G$,

$$
\operatorname{score}(U,h)=\frac1{24\,|\Lambda|^2}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda}\ \sum_{l\in S_4} s\bigl(h(x)\,U(\Gamma_l(x,y))\,h(y)^{-1}\bigr),
$$

which is `YM.RG.Patch.score`. A frame is *minimising* if $h(0)=1$ and it minimises the score among frames with $h(0)=1$ (`YM.RG.Patch.minimizing`). Existence is by compactness (`YM.RG.Patch.min_exists`). The small-holonomy branch (`YM.RG.Patch.usesMin`) is: for all $x,y\in\Lambda$ and all orders $l,k$, $\bigl\lvert U(\Gamma_l(0,x))\,U(\Gamma_k(x,y))\,U(\Gamma_l(0,y))^{-1}\bigr\rvert_c<\texttt{frame\_cut}$.

**Bond functional (S).** For $Y\colon\Lambda\to G$ and $u\in G$, $\operatorname{bondCost}(Y,u)=\sum_{x}w(x)\,s(Y(x)u^{-1})$, where $w$ is the $J$-fold convolution of the uniform probability on the centred block $[-\lfloor L/2\rfloor,\lfloor L/2\rfloor]^4$ (`YM.RG.Patch.weight`, `bondCost`). By `YM.Branch.min_iff` (`YM/Branch/Center.lean`), for weights summing to $1$, $u$ minimises $\sum_i w_i s(f_iu^{-1})$ if and only if $u$ is a Hilbert–Schmidt-nearest point of $G$ to the matrix $\sum_i w_if_i$. The branch `usesBest` is $\operatorname{bondCost}(Y,\texttt{best}\,Y)<\texttt{predictor\_cut}$, with `best Y` a chosen minimiser.

**Statements (S).**

1. `YM.RG.frame_unique` (`YM/Branch/Uniform.lean`): there is $\delta(G)>0$ such that for every `KernelChoices` with `frame_cut` $\le\delta(G)$, every patch and every $U$ on the branch `usesMin`, there is exactly one minimising frame, that is, exactly one minimiser among frames with $h(0)=1$.
2. `YM.RG.predictor_unique` (`YM/Branch/Uniform.lean`): there is $\varepsilon(G)>0$ such that for every `KernelChoices` with `predictor_cut` $\le\varepsilon(G)$, every patch and every $Y$ on the branch `usesBest`, the minimiser of $\operatorname{bondCost}(Y,\cdot)$ is unique.
3. `YM.Axial.eligible` (`YM/Branch/Pick.lean`): if $16\le$ `axial_factor`, then for every patch, every $U$, with no smallness assumption, and **each** of the 24 axis orders $l$ there is a root $r\in\Lambda$ with

$$
\sum_{b}\bigl\lvert a(b_-)\,U(b)\,a(b_+)^{-1}\bigr\rvert_c\ \le\ \texttt{axial\_factor}\cdot(2R+1)\sum_{p}\bigl\lvert U(\partial p)\bigr\rvert_c ,\qquad a(x)=U(\Gamma_l(r,x)).
$$

   Here $b$ runs once over the positively oriented links with both endpoints in $\Lambda$, and $p$ once over the plaquettes in the six coordinate planes $i<j$ all of whose corners lie in $\Lambda$ (`YM.RG.Patch.axialCost`, `facesum`, `eligible`).
4. `YM.Axial.viable` (`YM/Branch/Pick.lean`): for every $G$ and every $\kappa>0$ there is one `KernelChoices` $C$ with penalty $\kappa$ such that `P.Branches C G` holds for **every** patch $P$. The three fields of `Branches` are `frame_unique`, `predictor_unique` and `axial_exists` (non-emptiness of the set of eligible tags). The statement does not expose the other entries of $C$; its proof takes `frame_cut` $=\delta(G)$, `predictor_cut` $=\varepsilon(G)$, `axial_factor` $=16$ (P).

The thresholds $\delta(G),\varepsilon(G)$ do not depend on $L$ or $J$ and are not explicit; they come from a tubular neighbourhood of $G$ in $M_N(\mathbb C)$ (`YM.Gauge.nearest_tube`, `YM.Gauge.nearest_approximate`, `YM/Gauge/Nearest.lean`).

The issue text describes the three fields of `Branches` as obligations "packaged" in the structure. That is how consumers see them: the structure is a hypothesis of many downstream lemmas. They are, however, discharged in the source for every `Gauge` by `YM.Axial.viable`; downstream wrappers such as `YM.Next.Foundations.branches` (`YM/Next/FOUNDATIONS/Primary.lean`) expose that construction. `YM.Axial.eligible` and `YM.Branch.weighted_unique` are marked `used` in `Lean/verification/declaration-usage.tsv`.

## Mathematical reference

Three papers were read in the journal scans, on the pages listed. Nothing outside those pages is asserted. Page numbers are journal pages.

- **[B-RG-I]** T. Bałaban, *Renormalization group approach to lattice gauge field theories. I. Generation of effective actions in a small field approximation and a coupling constant renormalization in four dimensions*, Comm. Math. Phys. **109** (1987), 249–301, [DOI 10.1007/BF01215223](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01215223). Read: pp. 251–255, Eqs. (0.1)–(0.19); p. 256 around Eq. (0.21) in the text layer only.
- **[B-Average]** T. Bałaban, *Averaging operations for lattice gauge theories*, Comm. Math. Phys. **98** (1985), 17–51, [DOI 10.1007/BF01211042](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211042). Read: pp. 17–20, Eqs. (1)–(17); pp. 35–37 with Proposition 3; pp. 42–44 with Propositions 5–7.
- **[B-Gauge]** T. Bałaban, *Spaces of regular gauge field configurations on a lattice and gauge fixing conditions*, Comm. Math. Phys. **99** (1985), 75–102, [DOI 10.1007/BF01466594](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01466594). Read: pp. 81–84 with Theorem 2; pp. 99–100 with Propositions 6–7.

What these pages contain (R). Norms in the papers are the papers' own: [B-Average] distinguishes a Hilbert–Schmidt norm from the operator norm and states most estimates in the latter; they are not the normalised chord $\lvert\cdot\rvert_c$ above.

1. *Setting.* [B-RG-I] pp. 251–252: field configurations take values in a compact Lie group $G$, assumed semisimple and a Lie subgroup of a group of unitary matrices, with the remark that a bigger part of the considerations does not depend on semisimplicity; $L$ is an odd integer $>11$; blocks are centred at coarse lattice points; the action is $\sum_p\varepsilon^{d-4}[1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U(\partial p)]$ with $\operatorname{tr}1=1$, Eq. (0.2). [B-Average] pp. 17–18: $L>1$ is a fixed integer, blocks are $B(y)=\{x: y_\mu\le x_\mu<y_\mu+L\eta\}$, so $y$ is a corner, and $G$ is a Lie subgroup of $U(N)$; p. 20 obtains $G$ by applying $e^{iA}$ to the Lie algebra.
2. *Contours.* [B-RG-I] p. 252: for $x$ in the block $B(y)$, $\mathbf G(y,x)$ is the family of shortest contours from $y$ to $x$ obtained from all permutations of the axes with non-zero displacement, one straight leg per axis.
3. *Averaged link variables.* [B-Average] Eq. (15), p. 19: $\bar U_c=\exp\bigl[i\sum_{x\in B(c_-)}L^{-d}\tfrac1i\log U(\Gamma_{c,x})U(c)^{-1}\bigr]U(c)$, an explicit formula with uniform weight over one block, where $\Gamma_{c,x}$ runs from the block base point $c_-$ through $x$ to $c_+$. It satisfies the covariance requirement (11), $\overline{U^u}=(\bar U)^u$. [B-RG-I] Eq. (0.4), p. 253, is the variant symmetrised over $\mathbf G(c_-,x)$ and $\mathbf G(c_+,x')$ for complexified configurations; Eq. (0.12), p. 254, the variant built on averaged contour variables. [B-Average] p. 42 says that the average is formally defined for all configurations, with good control on configurations satisfying the regularity condition (52).
4. *Group mean.* [B-RG-I] Eqs. (0.5)–(0.9), p. 253: an axiomatic average $M(\{\mathbf U_j\})$, a $G^c$-valued analytic function of finitely many elements of the complexified group $G^c$ of sufficiently small diameter, with $M(\{\mathbf U_j^{-1}\})=M(\{\mathbf U_j\})^{-1}$, $M(\{u\mathbf U_jv\})=uM(\{\mathbf U_j\})v$, permutation invariance, first-order agreement with the arithmetic mean of logarithms, and the property that it maps $G$ to $G$. Eq. (0.10) recalls a definition equivalent to Federbush's: the $\mathbf U\in G^c$ near the $\mathbf U_j$ with $\sum_j\tfrac1i\log \mathbf U_j\mathbf U^{-1}=0$. The paper states that its considerations "are valid universally for all averages satisfying the above properties". The mean is used to define $\mathbf U(y,x)=M(\{\mathbf U(\Gamma)\}_{\Gamma\in\mathbf G(y,x)})$, Eq. (0.11).
5. *Gauge fixing.* [B-RG-I] pp. 254–255: the renormalization integrals are restricted to small-field configurations, $|U(\partial p)-1|<\varepsilon_0$. The residual invariance under $u$ with $u(y)=1$ at coarse points is removed by a Faddeev–Popov argument: one inserts, for $y$ coarse and $x\in B(y)$, $x\ne y$, the normalised factors $z^{-1}\exp[-\tfrac1\alpha(1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U(y,x))]\,\chi(\{|U(y,x)-1|<\varepsilon_0\})$, Eqs. (0.14)–(0.16).
6. *Kernel.* [B-Average] Eq. (10) and [B-RG-I] Eq. (0.17): the transformation is $\int dU\,\prod_c\delta(\bar U(c)V^{-1}(c))\cdots$. [B-RG-I] Eq. (0.13) allows a general gauge-invariant kernel $t(V,U)$.
7. *Regularity of the average.* [B-Average] Proposition 3, p. 36: under the assumptions of that section on $V_0$ and $V_1=e^{iA}$ with $\alpha_0,\alpha_1\le c_3$, where $c_3$ depends on $d$ and $L$, the function $Q(V_0,A)=\tfrac1i\log\bar V_1$ is analytic in $A$ and $Q(V_0,A,c)=L(Q(V_0)A)_c+C(V_0,A,c)$, Eq. (122), with the linear form $Q(V_0)A$ given by (124) and the remainder bounded as in (123). Proposition 6, p. 43: if $U_0$ satisfies (52), then $\overline{U'U_0}^{\,k}$ is analytic in $A'=\tfrac1{i\eta}\log U'$ for $|A'|<\alpha_1$, with the bound (164). Proposition 7, p. 43: under the same assumptions $Q_k(U'U_0,\eta A)$ is analytic in $A'$ and $A$ jointly, and Proposition 4 holds uniformly in $A'$.
8. *Uniqueness in gauge fixing.* [B-Gauge] Theorem 2, p. 83: for $U_0$, $U'U_0$ satisfying (1.33)–(1.35) with $\alpha_0+\alpha_1\le c_1$ there exists exactly one gauge transformation $u$ satisfying the multi-scale averaged constraints (1.29), $(\overline{R_0u}^{\,j})(y)=1$ for $y\in\Lambda_j$, $j=0,\dots,k$, such that the transformed configuration $U_1=U'^{u^{-1}}$ (the action being the background-dependent one of that paper) satisfies (1.36)–(1.39): the bounds on $A=\tfrac1{i\eta}\log U_1$, the averaging equations $Q_j(U_0,\eta A)=B$ and the Landau-type condition $R(U_0)D^{\eta*}_{U_0}A=0$. Page 99 shows that the additional regularity condition in (1.33) can be dropped. Proposition 7, p. 100, is the converse passage to the axial gauge.
9. *A neighbouring result, not examined.* [B-RG-I] p. 256, Eq. (0.21), considers the gauge action restricted to configurations with fixed averages and refers to another paper for a unique critical orbit consisting of minima in a space of regular configurations. This concerns the background field of the action, not the frame or predictor above.

Conclusions, all limited to the passages read.

1. **`min_exists` and `bond_min`** are compactness statements (a continuous function on a compact set attains its minimum). They need no reference.
2. **`Branches` is not supplied by these passages.** None of the statements read concerns a global minimiser of a chordal cost, the nearest-point projection onto $G$, or a bound for an axial gauge on arbitrary configurations. [B-Gauge] Theorem 2 is a uniqueness theorem for a different gauge condition under multi-scale regularity hypotheses that have no counterpart in `usesMin`. The projection $R(U_0)$ there acts on field configurations and is not a nearest-point projection onto the group.
3. **What may be cited** is historical and structural: the permutation-symmetrised contour families ([B-RG-I] p. 252), the requirement that block averaging commute with gauge transformations ([B-Average] Eq. (11)), the idea of a group-valued mean with the invariances (0.5)–(0.7), and gauge fixing normalised at coarse lattice points. These are attributions for an introduction, not steps of a proof.
4. **The predictor with a tagged fallback** has a closer formal antecedent in OpenAI, *Sharp mass bounds for the two-dimensional O(4) model* (preprint, 23 September 2026), Eq. (5.2), p. 39 (R): there the block mean $m_Y$ is normalised when $|m_Y|\ge\tfrac12$ and otherwise replaced by a constituent spin sampled with the block weights, and the kernel is a normalised exponential of the squared chordal distance on $S^3$. This agrees with Scott Armstrong's assessment. That paper treats spins, so it has no frames.

## Application and remaining proof obligations

Proposed treatment for the consuming unit (provisional PU-01, block-spin map and symmetric gauge fixing): **prove in detail**, following the source, and cite Bałaban only for attribution. In the source the argument occupies about 1,030 lines in `YM/Branch/{Pick,Slide,Uniform,Synchronize,Center}.lean`, `YM/Lattice/Foliation.lean` and `YM/Gauge/Nearest.lean`, plus about 550 lines of Lie-group charts in `YM/Gauge/{Chart,Lie,Colors}.lean`. Its mathematical steps, as read from the proof scripts (P):

1. *Nearest-point projection.* For a closed subgroup $G\subset U(N)$ there is a tube around $G$ in $M_N(\mathbb C)$, of radius uniform by invariance, on which the Hilbert–Schmidt-nearest point of $G$ is unique; at $1$ the projection has strict derivative equal to the orthogonal projection onto the Lie algebra (`YM.Gauge.nearest_tube`, `YM.Gauge.focus_deriv`, `YM.Gauge.nearest_approximate`, all in `YM/Gauge/Nearest.lean`; the construction uses the inverse function theorem). This is the tubular-neighbourhood statement for a compact embedded submanifold and is a candidate for **cite and apply**; a precise reference still has to be selected and checked, including for groups that are not connected.
2. *Predictor.* By Jensen's inequality and $2N\,s(uv^{-1})=\lVert u-v\rVert_{HS}^2$ (`YM.Branch.price_diff`), a small value of `bondCost` puts the Euclidean mean in the tube, so its nearest group point is unique (`YM.Branch.weighted_unique`, `YM/Branch/Center.lean`).
3. *Frame.* In `YM/Branch/Synchronize.lean`: any global minimiser of the unnormalised cost satisfies, at each point, a nearest-point equation for an averaged "feedback" matrix (`YM.Branch.Pairs.self_consistent`); comparing with the trivial frame bounds the spread of a minimiser (`YM.Branch.Pairs.compare_energy`); after left translation so that the centroid defect is normal to $G$, the maximal difference $u$ of two minimisers satisfies $u\le u/4+u/4$. Hence two global minimisers differ by a common left factor, which is the statement of `YM.Branch.align`; its threshold does not depend on the number of points or paths. `YM.RG.frame_unique` follows on imposing $h(0)=1$. The final comparison of minimisers is a contraction estimate, not a local uniqueness argument at a critical point.
4. *Axial fallback.* Induction over the axes: choose the layer with least plaquette sum, apply the induction hypothesis there, and transport along the last axis, where each step changes a link by at most one plaquette (`YM.Axial.total_slab` and `YM.Axial.existence` in `YM/Branch/Pick.lean`, with the comparison estimates `variation`, `slide_le`, `window_le` in `YM/Branch/Slide.lean`). This yields the constant $4\cdot4=16$.

Each consuming proof must also check, and this note does not: gauge and lattice covariance of the frame and of the kernel (`YM.RG.Patch.frame_gauged`, `YM.RG.StepData.covary`, `YM.RG.StepData.channel`); normalisation of the kernel (`YM.RG.StepData.density_total`); continuity of the selected frame on its branch (`YM.RG.Patch.selected_cont`). All five have `Branches` as a hypothesis. It must further show how the small-field region of the RG step implies `usesMin` and `usesBest`. For `usesMin` only the sufficient condition `YM.Push.Uniform.minimizing_used` (`YM/Push/STATIONARY_UNIFORM/FrameMin.lean`) was read: if all transports $U(\Gamma_l(i,j))$ between patch points have chord at most $r$ and $3r<$ `frame_cut`, then `usesMin` holds. For `usesBest` nothing was read.

## Search and unresolved questions

- Not read in full: [B-Average] §§B–D and [B-Gauge] §§D–F, of which only the pages listed above were inspected. Not read at all: B-Var (Comm. Math. By the lead's own description B-Var concerns a constrained problem with fixed averages.
- Unverified leads for step 1 and for the frame functional, **not read**: the literature on extrinsic (chordal, projected arithmetic) means on matrix groups and on synchronisation over a group with a complete measurement graph, where functionals of the form $\sum\lVert h(x)W(x,y)-h(y)\rVert^2$ are standard. Whether any published theorem gives uniqueness of global minimisers modulo a common factor, uniformly in the number of points, as `YM.Branch.align` does, is open. No novelty claim is made.
- Open on the Lean side: explicit sizes of $\delta(G),\varepsilon(G)$; the role of $J\ge4$ and $L$ odd, which the proofs of the three fields do not use; regularity of the selected frame beyond continuity, which the header of `YM/Branch/Synchronize.lean` says is not tracked there.
- Whether the linearisation of the Lean predictor at $U=1$ agrees with Bałaban's linear average [B-Average] Eq. (14) was not examined; the weights differ ($w$ against uniform), so exact agreement is not expected.
