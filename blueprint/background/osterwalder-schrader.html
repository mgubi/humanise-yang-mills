Result to be used

The intended application is reconstruction of relativistic fields from the appropriate Euclidean correlations. It has three distinct interfaces:

  1. Coordinate OS construction: YM.Table.Hierarchy supplies YM.OS.realization, whose output is a YM.OS.Chamber: a Hilbert-space Gram construction and compatible time/space evolution.

  2. Selected relativistic reconstruction: the stronger data in YM.Spec.Materializes and its consumers lead to a contour and a field system satisfying YM.Boundary.Wightman.Realizes, including vacuum uniqueness.

  3. Every-character reconstruction and integration: YM.EveryPhase.Completed quantifies over every character, with common labels and boundary mixture laws.

The first output must not be substituted for the second or third. A literature citation for a scalar reconstructed theory does not alone identify the project's chosen spaces, quotient labels, contours, or phasewise choices.

Citation decision: the corrected 1975 OS theorem is a relevant candidate, not yet a checked plug-in replacement. In particular, fixed-arity Capped estimates are not the theorem's uniform-in-arity growth assumption. Stronger Spread.AllCopy estimates are present and are a serious candidate for the missing comparison; they must not be ignored or assumed already converted to the paper's Schwartz seminorms. No failure of the project's complete reconstruction theorem is inferred from an unresolved literature match.

Exact formulas checked against the original scan

The locators in this subsection were checked visually in the original journal scans, not inferred from OCR. OS II uses the joint Schwartz seminorm

\(\displaystyle |f|_p = \sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}^m, | \alpha | \leqslant p} (1 + \sum_{j = 1}^m x_j^2)^{p / 2} |D^{\alpha} f (x) | \qquad \text{(§II, printed p. 284, (2.1)).}\)

Its space \(\mathcal{S}_0 (\mathbb{R}^{4 n})\) has the induced Schwartz topology: all derivatives of its elements vanish whenever any two four-dimensional arguments coincide. Flatness on this set is stronger than just setting the function value to zero there. This is not the space of all Schwartz tests and is not the space of tests supported in one fixed separated compact set.

The named theorem E′ (or E″) → R′ immediately below these formulas gives a uniquely determined Wightman theory from the chosen corrected regularity condition and E1–E4. Part (b), (4.3), gives a positive integer \(w\) and bounds \(|\mathfrak{W}_n (f) | \leqslant \omega_n |f|_{nw}\), where \(0 < \omega_n \leqslant ab^{n^2}\). The output constants are not asserted to have factorial growth. This named theorem must not be confused with Theorem 4.1 on printed p. 288, an intermediate analyticity/estimate theorem.

The inherited hypotheses were checked in OS I §3, printed p. 88 (PDF page 6): E1 is proper Euclidean covariance; E2 is positivity for all finite sequences of ordered positive-time joint tests, using reflection and conjugate reversal; E3 is permutation symmetry; E4 is the spatial-translation cluster limit for two such finite sequences. OS I p. 89, Remark 2, also gives the covariant compact-test cluster formulation (3.1). They are distributional requirements, not assertions about one separated point or one field component.

For the unselected family, there is a documented weaker conclusion rather than a need to assume unique vacuum: OS II p. 283, Remark 6, retains the subset correspondences of OS I p. 89, Remark 3, with the corrected changes. In particular, omitting E4 retains R0–R3 and R5, omitting the cluster axiom R4. This does not remove the corrected regularity, covariance, positivity or symmetry requirements. It does not prove Wightman.Realizes.unique for a mixture.

OS II p. 283, Remark 5, explicitly allows a denumerable collection of arbitrary spinor fields, referring to OS I §6, pp. 102–104. OS I pp. 102–103 specifies the component covariance, adjoint indexing and statistics signs. Consequently the scalar theorem is not the only available version; the project's exact component/representation and mixed-correlation identifications must still be checked before applying that extension. No uniformity across all label degrees is inferred from the scalar statement.

Zinoviev v1 pp. 21–23 was also visually checked. Its E5 is precisely the iterated inverse-Laplace limit in (4.12), with (4.13) and (2.54) defining the test transforms: for each relevant odd distribution there is a threshold \(K\), then all integers \(k > K\), all \(1 \leqslant l \leqslant m\), and all stated test tuples must satisfy the limit. The time-cutoff limits precede the integer-index limits. Theorem 4.1 on p. 23 gives E0–E5 ↔ R0–R5. Merely producing a momentum PVM after reconstruction does not establish this input condition.

The direct source route is already explicit

  3. The result is Surface S and NonTangentialFree, not merely separate-variable convergence. The prerequisite definitions are in Lean/YM/Shift/Ridge.lean; Distn in Lean/YM/Distribution/Tests.lean is a continuous linear map on Schwartz tests.

  4. YM.Org.Show.Rhythm.opens in Lean/YM/Org/Theatre.lean applies that result. YM.Next.BOUNDARY.rhythm and .contour assemble the inputs from Bid.Pantry. .Compatible.vacuum separately adds UniqueHeat; the unselected construction does not borrow it from the selected phase.

This is a source crosswalk, not a new proof or an independent validity judgment. Breathes has quantifiers \(\forall n, v \exists m, C\), so this route alone does not certify OS II's arity-uniform E0′. It instead supplies a precise alternative proof boundary for later LaTeX exposition. A consumer can explain this direct route without claiming to have verified the different E0′ premise.

The paper's scalar Wightman conclusion and the project's richer Realizes record must be compared field by field. No claim of a mass gap, every-phase uniformity, label faithfulness, or continued stress-tensor identities follows just from an OS reference. Stress outputs are outside this literature task.

Application and remaining proof obligations

The following gates specify what a future consuming proof must establish before using the candidate reference. They are proposed mathematical inputs for later LaTeX exposition, not new proved lemmas in this Markdown record.

  1. Fix the family to reconstruct. Name the full table or the selected character table and the original quotient label space. Name each component sequence and its adjoint/rotation action, including compatibility as the level increases. Do not obtain these objects by assuming the final realization.

  2. Construct the distribution input. Extend the compact-separated kernel pairing to the exact flat-diagonal Schwartz space. Establish continuity and the chosen corrected growth condition with the right arity quantifiers. The raw table values on coincident configurations cannot specify an extension.

  3. Match all remaining hypotheses. Verify continuous rotations, translations, reflected adjoints, mixed-label symmetry and positivity in the same extension. If applying the full theorem, establish its cluster hypothesis for that family. A nonclustering reconstruction requires the corresponding corrected subset version, with an explicit weaker conclusion and no unique-vacuum assertion.

  4. Identify the reconstructed object. Match the output distributions with the prescribed contour and prove the component/quotient field identifications, common domain, faithfulness and vacuum convention. A uniqueness statement about distributions is not by itself the needed intertwiner between specified field systems. Stage.lean exposes relevant compatibility and attachment data.

  5. For PU-19, repeat with uniform phase control. Establish hypotheses for every character with uniform constants where integration requires them; prove measurability/continuity and boundary-limit interchange separately. No exceptional-set deletion is allowed if the intended conclusion is all characters.

The strongest quantitative lead in this inspection is Spread.AllCopy, transported uniformly by Almanac.copy. A separately scoped application proof should either derive OS II's precise growth condition from that interface or use the project's direct contour/temperedness route without asserting the OS hypothesis. No conversion proof has been completed here.

An alternative is Zinoviev's Theorem 4.1. To use it one must prove its E5 inverse-Laplace limit condition, not merely point to the existence of the project's eventual momentum PVM. Using a PVM obtained from the reconstruction being justified would be circular. This alternate application is unresolved too.

The selected phase's heat condition and the unselected mixture should therefore remain separate in the proof map.
