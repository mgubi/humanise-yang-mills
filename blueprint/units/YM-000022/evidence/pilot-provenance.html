Statement and notation

The target is YM.Pert.Asymptotics.comparison. It assumes an internal construction family satisfying YM.Family.Makes, a simple internal gauge group, and natural-number sequences N and k with i ⩽ N(i) eventually. The limit is along the natural-number tail, with no monotonicity or divergence requirement on k. There is no requirement to take an infinite-volume limit. The statement uses the representation fixed as part of the gauge-group data.

Proof correspondence

  1. Running input. YM.Family.Makes.wilson gives YM.RG.History.Selected, whose running condition is YM.RG.History.Running. Its comparison coefficient is YM.RG.Calendar.hatBeta. The construction of this history and its running estimate are upstream dependencies of the pilot. The proof states the estimate and retains its threshold uniform in \(N, p\). It proves divergence of the logarithmic scale by bounding the product of the block factors below by \(3^m\).

  2. Uniform ratio estimate. YM.Pert.Asymptotics.zeroUp_bound divides the running estimate by the eventually positive logarithmic scale and bounds the additive intercept divided by that scale. The exposition chooses \(\varepsilon / 2\) for each error budget, with the intercept inequality strict. The source uses \(\varepsilon / 4\).

  3. Positive slope. YM.Gauge.casimir_pos and YM.Gauge.slopeConstant_pos in Gauge/Casimir.lean supply the positivity used by the limit. The exposition gives the invariant-trace argument in terms of the squared norms of brackets of basis vectors. The normalization comes from YM.Gauge.trace_color in Gauge/Colors.lean.

  4. Admissible tail and continuity. The hypothesis on \(N\) makes the uniform ratio bound applicable along the chosen sequences. The exposition infers eventual positivity of the ratio and uses continuity of \(x \mapsto x^{- 1 / 2}\) on \((0, \infty)\). The source composes square root with reciprocal at the positive limit and uses eventual equality of the resulting expression with the scaled proxy.

The comparison is about the trajectory coefficient and includes the bare endpoint as a special case. The identification with a physical X-scheme coupling and the tangent-one test are separate questions. The statement ledger records the scope of this companion result.
