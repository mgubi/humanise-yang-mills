Findings and evidence

R1 (hypotheses of the two propositions): resolved.

  • eq:ex-branch-test-properties states two facts. First, \(L^{\sigma}_d (z) \to \lambda^{\sigma}_d\) in operator norm: the functionals act on a finite-dimensional space, so pointwise continuity implies this. Second, \(\bigcap_{\sigma} \ker \lambda^{\sigma}_4 = \{0\}\). Kernels of finite intersections form a descending chain of subspaces of a finite-dimensional space, so finitely many tests already separate, as the text says. The separation itself is still attributed to field separation of the selected hierarchy plus grid density, and is marked as a retained input. Correct.

  • eq:ex-branch-grade-three-unit holds on a test-dependent small neighbourhood. The proof uses it only at the two endpoints \(0\) and \(n_k a_k e_d\), and \(n_k a_k = h_k \to 0\), so for large \(k\) both lie in the neighbourhood. Correct.

  • eq:ex-branch-recorded-traces makes explicit what the old prose took as exact. On the false branch, \(J^-_{aa} = 0\) (setup, the \(J^-\) display). On the principal branch, \(\sum_a J^0_{aa} = \sum_d \nabla_d Q^{[r]}_d\) (setup, after the \(\varrho_k\) definition). Applying the same linear tested correlation at grade four gives \(\sum_a T^{\sigma, -} = 0\) and \(\sum_a T^{\sigma, 0} = \sum_d G^{\sigma}_d (0)\). Both sides carry the same grade-four normalisation, which is consistent with the \(a_k^{- 1} = a_k^{- 4} / a_k^{- 3}\) remark. Correct.

  • I recomputed the proof of prop:ex-branch-raw-fast against the new hypothesis list.

    • (a) Moving detector: pointwise convergence on a finite-dimensional space is norm convergence. The joint limit map is injective, so it has a positive minimum \(m\) on the unit sphere, and for large \(k\), \(\max_{\sigma} | \Lambda^{\sigma}_k z| \geqslant (m / 2) \|z\|\). This is eq:ex-branch-moving-detector.

    • (b) Telescoping: summing eq:ex-branch-recorded-difference over \(0 \leqslant j < n_k\) gives \(\sum_j G (j) = a_k^{- 1} (F (n_k) - F (0))\). Both endpoint values are within \(E\) of \(L_3 (0) v_{d, k}\) by eq:ex-branch-book-current and eq:ex-branch-grade-three-unit. Hence \(| \Lambda_k w| \leqslant n_k^{- 1} \sum_j |L_4 (ja_k e_d) w - G (j) | + |n_k^{- 1} \sum_j G (j) | \leqslant D + 2 E / (a_k n_k)\), as displayed. With \(h_k \geqslant e^{- \tau s_k}\), the second term is \(O (e^{- (\eta - \tau) s_k})\). Only \(n_k + 3\) ultrafilter-large sets are intersected at each fixed \(k\).

    • (c) False and principal traces follow from the linearity of \(\lambda^{\sigma}_4\), eq:ex-branch-book-tensor, eq:ex-branch-book-difference at \(j = 0\) (where \(L_4 (0) = \lambda_4\)), and eq:ex-branch-recorded-traces.

    Every input the proof uses is now among the cited labels, or in the recording paragraph those labels belong to (radius \(c_{\sigma}\), common ultrafilter, eventual cutoff).

  • eq:ex-branch-transport-bound is now labelled. It is the closed-ball ultrafilter limit of the finite-volume bound at fixed \(k\) and \(v\), followed by a supremum over \(\|v\| \leqslant 1\). No infinite intersection of large sets is used. prop:ex-branch-washout cites it together with \(\ell_k = o (s_k)\) and the affine clock. These are exactly what the proof's step \(\hat{\beta}^p_k B_k r^b_k \to 0\) needs: \(\hat{\beta}^p_k \|B_k \| = O (e^{\epsilon s_k})\) for every \(\epsilon > 0\), and \(\|r^b_k \| = O (e^{- \eta s_k})\). The remaining steps of the washout proof are unchanged and I rechecked them:

    • the diagonal trace of the symmetry average is \(|\mathcal{G}|^{- 1} \sum_g \mathfrak{c} \rho_g (y^b_k)\), because squared signs cancel and the axis maps are bijections;

    • the chart identity applied to \(x = r^b_k\) uses \(\pi R = \text{id}\) and \(\mathfrak{c}q = q\);

    • \(\textsf{P}_i\) is linear, so \(\textsf{P}_i \text{tr} t = \text{tr} \textsf{P}_i t\).
