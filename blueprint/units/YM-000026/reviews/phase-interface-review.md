## Useful next decomposition

### 1. Large-time heat projection

`YM.OS.Semigroup.emerges` in `Lean/YM/OS/Core.lean` supplies the strong limit of the symmetric contraction semigroup E_t to the orthogonal projection P0 onto H0={x:E_t x=x for every t>=0}. This is a suitable standalone elementary lemma after the currently reviewed semigroup construction. Its proof uses decreasing a(t)=||E_t x||², the identity <E_s x,E_t x>=a((s+t)/2), and, for s<=t, ||E_s x-E_t x||²<=a(s)-a(t). The resulting limit is fixed by every E_r, and pairing against fixed vectors identifies it with P0x. No positive gap or quantitative decay rate is concluded.

### 2. Bounded slab insertion operators

`YM.OS.Spread` in `Lean/YM/OS/Slabs.lean` is additional data, not an automatic consequence of the Gram completion. For every slab width d>0 and internally distinct word w with every time in (0,d), it gives a bounded operator B_d(w) on the Euclidean Hilbert space H such that B_d(w)[v]=[w concatenated with tau_d v] for every positive word v. The main construction must prove this operation respects the null quotient and is bounded before extending it.

The quantitative input `YM.OS.Spread.AllCopy` says: for every grade cutoff D and label norm bound R, there are C>0 and integer A>=0 such that, for every admissible slab word of length n, every width d, and every 0<r<=1 with pairwise Euclidean separation at least r and all times between r and d-r,

    ||B_d(w)|| <= C^n (n/r)^(A n).

C and A are uniform over the stated words, widths and buffers, though they can depend on D,R. The empty word is included. `Spread.Laws` also requires operator-norm smoothness on the open slab configuration domain and positional derivatives equal insertion of the specified derivative-label representatives. These are stronger than point correlation smoothness or a separate cap at each arity.

Once bounded operators with the specified action exist, the source proves B_d(empty)=E_d, concatenation, the adjoint formula using reflected/conjugated words translated by d, and transverse covariance by equality on the dense word span. These identities can be grouped in one lemma; the construction and estimates cannot be grouped away as formal scaffolding.

### 3. Compression and commutativity

Let D=H0, let Omega0 be the empty-word vacuum in D, and set A_d(w)=P0 B_d(w)|D in B(D). The algebra A is the norm-closed unital star algebra generated by all admissible A_d(w), with the actual operator norm on B(D). This exact algebra is `YM.OS.Spread.algebra` in `Phase/Mixture.lean`.

`YM.OS.Ground.inert` in `Ground/Lean.lean` proves that transverse translations fix D, assuming the Euclidean hierarchy and full orthogonal covariance with the prescribed label rotations. It does not use only the signed coordinate symmetries already passed by the extraction draft. Its proof first shows that an additional fixed time translation does not change the large-time limit, then rotates a displacement with positive time component onto the time axis, compares the two limits, and finally removes the extra positive time displacement. The transverse unitaries have norm one even when the intervening transverse displacement varies. This is a separate substantive rotation argument.

With that fixed-ground property, `Ground/Compress.lean` proves independence of slab width and of the translation used to place an arbitrary internally distinct whole word into a slab. The resulting M(w) belongs to A. For a positive word v, M(v)Omega0=P0[v]. Therefore Omega0 is cyclic for A on D. The additional bounded map X(v):D->H obtained from B_d(v)|D satisfies M((theta v)w)=X(v)*X(w), with the appropriate whole-word interpretation.

`YM.OS.Ground.adjunction` in `Ground/Permute.lean` proves A commutative. This is not a general fact about compression of local operators. The source compares widely separated trains of four clusters, uses an orthogonal transformation that exchanges time and a transverse coordinate to interchange the middle clusters, then evaluates matrix elements on the dense projected-word span. The identity extends from the generators to the star algebra and its norm closure. The long-train limit is supplied by `Ground/Moments.lean`, subsequently read in full while reviewing phases.tex. Its induction allows unbounded varying transverse shifts because these unitaries fix the limiting ground vector; the contractive error estimate is uniform in the transverse shift. The manuscript must include this argument before the four-cluster exchange.

### 4. Character space and representing measure

`YM.OS.Ground.resolution` in `Ground/Spectrum.lean` takes the hierarchy, full orthogonal covariance, a Spread, and an explicit norm-dense sequence in A. It returns a compact metric space X whose points bijectively parametrize all complex unital star characters of A, and a probability measure mu of full support such that

    integral_X chi(a) dmu(chi) = <Omega0,a Omega0>  (a in A).

The norm-dense sequence is a real hypothesis of this theorem. It is not provided merely by separability of H, since B(H) need not be norm separable. The source proves metrizability of the character space by evaluation on that sequence. Commutativity plus cyclicity of Omega0 makes Omega0 separating for A; the separating property forces full support of the representing measure. A complete article may use precise Gelfand/Riesz theorems for the standard background, but must prove their hypotheses here and the full-support step.

### 5. Phase tables and barycenter

`YM.OS.Spread.Resolution.receives` requires, for every internally distinct whole word w and every translated admissible slab presentation, the identity

    S_chi(w) = chi(A_d(tau_s w)).

It is not a product of character values of single insertions. `Resolution.barycenter` then follows directly by choosing one slab presentation, applying the representing integral identity, evaluating on the vacuum, and using translation invariance of the unselected table. This is a valid short lemma once the phase tables and the compatibility identity are supplied.

The compatibility identity by itself does not establish the Euclidean hierarchy of every S_chi, its full orthogonal covariance, equality of its null label spaces with those of S, its differential/stress identities, or bounded slab operators for its own Hilbert space. The source's `Selection.Certificate` lists these separately. A continuous character applied to a smoothly varying element of A explains some regularity, but positivity and the other properties still need their actual arguments and the relevant identities in A.

### 6. Selecting a unique-vacuum phase

`YM.OS.Cluster.exp_real`, `lifts`, and `selects` in `Phase/Unique.lean` prove a stronger almost-everywhere conclusion before choosing one phase. For one positive word v, let a_chi=P0_chi[v]. The squared norm of E_n[v] decreases to ||a_chi||². The current correlations are continuous in chi, and dominated convergence is justified by the bounded initial current on compact X. The barycenter and compression identities imply the integral of ||a_chi-S_chi(v)Omega_chi||² is zero. Thus this vector difference vanishes almost everywhere for each fixed v.

To conclude that the whole fixed subspace is the vacuum line, the source requires ONE countable sequence of positive words whose complex linear span is dense in EVERY phase Hilbert space. It intersects the corresponding countably many full-measure sets and projects their dense spans. The resulting property is H0_chi=complex span(Omega_chi) almost everywhere; probability then gives at least one selectable chi. An uncountable intersection over arbitrary words is not justified. This common countable dense sequence must be proved from the actual label and configuration spaces, or retained explicitly as an input until proved.

## Reconstruction: concrete analytic and Hilbert-space interfaces

The small interface in `Lean/YM/Boundary/Contour.lean` is sufficiently explicit to organize the next units. E=R^4, configurations are E^n, and tests are the complex Schwartz space S(E^n). A tempered distribution here is a continuous complex-linear map S(E^n)->C, tested without conjugating the test. Each n-point boundary W_n is multilinear in n labels from the common union of graded field quotients and takes values in this tempered-distribution space. The source's internal boundary interface is joint Schwartz testing; the public endpoint only exposes certain product-test consequences.

The time continuation F_n(z,x;v) has n complex time variables z and n fixed real spatial points x in (R^3)^n. Its domain is Im z_1<...<Im z_n. It is jointly continuous in (z,x), holomorphic in z, multilinear in the labels, and at z=i y agrees with the Euclidean table at positions (x_i,y_i). For every strictly increasing real height tuple y and every joint Schwartz test f, its shifted real-time value multiplied by f is absolutely integrable, and its integral at height epsilon*y converges to W_n(f) as epsilon decreases to zero. No positivity of each y_i is required in `Waves.Opens`; strict ordering is the condition. Arity zero is included. The stronger `NonTangentialFree` conclusion gives a limit along every ordered height tuple tending to zero, not merely each fixed ray.

Neither the existence of this F nor tempered boundary limits follows merely from the preceding contraction semigroup. `Org/Show.Rhythm` in `Org/Theatre.lean` packages a concrete small-width operator representation, its polynomial bounds, the common continuation, and additional boundary-growth/line estimates before applying the joint boundary-limit consumer. `Stage.go` then assembles two continuations (unselected and selected) and a selected Wightman realization. Its record fields are producer hypotheses; invoking the consumer is not a proof of them.

A useful minimal Hilbert-space consumer is `YM.Boundary.harvest` in `Lean/YM/Fields/Lo.lean`. Its required objects can be stated without opaque record names:

1. A boundary family W as above, a conjugate-linear involution J on the labels, and a continuous finite-grade Lorentz action K compatible with the prescribed Euclidean rotations and boosts.
2. Multilinearity of word expectations and continuity of the two inserted test variables in each Gram pairing; Gram invariance and continuity under the full proper Poincare group.
3. Spacelike interchange of adjacent smeared letters inside arbitrary words. Supports must be pairwise spacelike for the two interchanged tests.
4. Label faithfulness: a label whose insertion in every word expectation vanishes for every test is zero.
5. For every product-test word b, vectors c_b(t) in the selected Euclidean Hilbert space, t>0, with independent-pair convergence <c_b(s),c_d(t)> -> W(kernel(J,b,d)) as (s,t)->(0,0), plus vacuum convergence and translation intertwining. Convergence along s=t alone is insufficient to infer the Cauchy property used by the source.
6. The selected Euclidean fixed space is the vacuum line.

The source uses the pair convergence to obtain a limit vector for each word, hence a positive boundary Gram kernel. Its Hilbert completion has dense invariant domain equal to the algebraic span of word vectors. Left concatenation defines the field. Null-quotient compatibility is proved using the adjoint-word identity: if u represents zero, pairing its left-concatenation with an arbitrary word vector equals pairing u with the opposite-letter left-concatenation; in particular the squared norm vanishes. This is `Soil.compactor` in `Fields/Stems.lean`; it must not be replaced by a claim that any densely defined insertion automatically descends.

The exact endpoint `Wightman.Realizes` in `Fields/System.lean` includes separately linear label/test fields on that common domain, continuity in each test for each domain vector, the formal adjoint identity and corresponding densely defined adjoint inclusion, a normalized cyclic vacuum, spacelike commutativity, nonzero detection of every nonzero label, a strongly continuous Poincare unitary representation preserving the domain with full field covariance, a projection-valued momentum measure supported in the closed forward cone, and uniqueness of the translation-invariant vacuum. In `Fields/Spectrum.lean` the boundary Hilbert space embeds isometrically and intertwines translations into the Euclidean chamber; positive Euclidean energy plus Lorentz covariance supplies the cone condition, and the selected Euclidean fixed-space property gives vacuum uniqueness. Surjectivity of this embedding is not a hypothesis of the minimal consumer.

The further `EveryPhase.all_characters` theorem is downstream of an existing `Spec.Final`, which already contains selected Wightman reconstruction; it cannot be used as the first construction of that selected theory. Its stronger all-phase conclusions require further source arguments not audited here.

### Concrete vectors and conventions

In Fields/Tall.lean, B_p(v) is a bounded operator for p>=0, linear in the common graded-union label v. For p>0 it is required to equal the actual singleton slab operator of width 2p, with the insertion at spatial origin and time p. For complex z and real spatial displacement x, the leg is

    R(z,q,x) = F_heat(-i z-q) U(x),

where F_heat is the holomorphic semigroup in the right half-plane and U is the transverse unitary representation. It is contractive when Im(z)>q. A shot for labels v_1,...,v_n at real spacetime points (t_i,x_i) and positive strictly ordered heights y_i is the chronological product

    R(t_1+i y_1,p,x_1) B_p(v_1)
      R((t_2-t_1)+i(y_2-y_1),2p,x_2-x_1) B_p(v_2) ... Omega.

The width conditions used by the source are 0<p<1, 4p<y_i, and 4p<y_j-y_i for i<j. They are sufficient rather than sharp. At arity zero the shot is Omega. For the heated word at parameter t>0 use y_i=(i+1)t and p=min(t,1)/8, then integrate the shot against the product of its Schwartz tests over all real spacetime points. This is the actual `heatCup`, not an arbitrary choice of approximating vectors.

Work/C23 proves norm continuity of shots from operator-norm continuity of the interior heat family and joint strong continuity of transverse translation applied to a varying vector. Every leg contracts, so the shot norm is bounded by product_i ||B_p(v_i)|| ||Omega|| for fixed p, independently of real positions. A joint Schwartz test is L1, giving Bochner integrability. No uniform estimate as p decreases to zero is used at this stage. Simultaneously shifting every real spacetime point changes only the first leg; the unitary edge law gives translation covariance. Changing integration variables gives covariance of heated words with the inverse-shifted test convention. The vacuum is invariant under full real translations, including arity zero.

### Mixed point identity and independent heights

Work/C22 proves the mixed identity for two different admissible widths p,q. At purely imaginary times, induction on the shot product identifies it with the actual Euclidean GNS word at heights y. Its inner product with the second shot is the Euclidean correlation with the first tuple reversed, its heights negated, and its labels acted on by J. The real spacetime positions in the resulting boundary formula are only reversed in their first block; they are not reflected.

For arbitrary prescribed real times, the proof continues in a single complex parameter c. Give the first shot times i y_i+c t_i and the second i z_j+c s_j. In the scalar inner product use the first vector at conjugate(c), the second at c; antilinearity makes the scalar function holomorphic. Strict height margins give an open horizontal strip about the real axis where both shots and the common continuation are defined. On an imaginary segment in that strip, all times are again purely imaginary with adjusted real heights, so the preceding GNS identity holds. The one-variable identity theorem extends it to c=1. This avoids any unjustified assertion that the Hilbert space itself has a conjugation and does not require equal widths. The actual `C22.answer` reduces union labels to finite-grade representatives using DiffPlan coverage, and uses receiving-table null inclusion to identify correlations.

Work/C24 integrates this identity. Its merger map is the volume-preserving coordinate permutation (reverse first real tuple, then append second). The product of the conjugated reversed first tests and second tests is one joint Schwartz test. Fixed-height integrability and Fubini give the heated-word inner product as a single tested continuation at heights

    (-n s,...,-s,t,...,m t).

These are strictly ordered for every s,t>0, and tend to zero whenever s and t tend independently to zero. `NonTangentialFree.corner` therefore gives the required product-filter Gram limit. This is the exact place where fixed-ray boundary convergence would be insufficient.

### Nearest substantive analytic producers

The above chain is not a missing formal theorem: actual sources supply the analytic inputs through the following separately substantive proofs. Their mathematical hypotheses still must be established or translated in the monograph.

- Work/C01 constructs B_p by testing centered singleton slab operators against every pair of Euclidean word vectors. Scalar multilinearity proves operator linearity, null-label and grade-inclusion compatibility permit passage to the common union, and the singleton AllCopy bound gives ||B_p(v)||<=C_v p^{-a_v}. p=0 is unused and may be assigned zero.
- Work/C02 proves the padding identity B_{p+u}=E_u B_p E_u and the corresponding padded leg identity. Induction and E_u Omega=Omega prove independence of the scalar matrix element from admissible width. At imaginary times the product is the actual ordered Euclidean correlation.
- Work/C03 patches these width-independent products to a common continuation F, locally choosing one fixed width to prove continuity and holomorphy. The product of the individual B_p norms, with p=k/8, gives |F_y(x)|<=C k^{-m} for every real spacetime configuration x whenever all ordered height gaps are at least k in (0,1]. The constants depend on the fixed label tuple, not on real positions.
- Work/C04 obtains fixed-height integrability and continuity from this uniform bound. Cauchy's estimate gives a local bound on real-time directional derivatives; dominated differentiation and Schwartz integration by parts then prove the exact tested Cauchy--Riemann identity d_a psi_y(f)=psi_y(-i partial_{dawn(a)} f). The sign is minus i on the test derivative.
- Shift/Breath.lean and Osc.lean prove the unrestricted ordered-height boundary. Put r=(0,1,...,n-1) and L=-i partial_{dawn(r)}. For a growth exponent m, form sum_{j=0}^m (-t)^j/j! psi_{y+t r}(L^j f). Its derivative telescopes to (-t)^m/m! psi_{y+t r}(L^{m+1}f). Integrating from 0 to1 expresses psi_y(f) as its polynomial expression at1 minus an integral remainder. This identity first holds for strictly ordered y. For weakly ordered y, y+t r has gaps at least t; the polynomial growth bound cancels t^m, producing an integrable remainder uniformly down to t=0. Dominated convergence makes the expression continuous on the closed ordered cone. Bounds by a finite sum of continuous Schwartz L1-derivative seminorms prove the resulting functional tempered. Linearity and label multilinearity pass by uniqueness of the interior limits. Thus the full ordered-height limit at zero follows, without a ratio restriction.
