Conclusion

The rate is not obtained by merely summing a geometric tail of an already given kernel estimate. The source first proves a one-step propagation estimate for a neutral curvature representative of the difference of two quadratic RG precisions. This estimate yields a recurrence for a positive weighted coefficient cost. A separate, elementary exponential-tilt argument turns that recurrence into a power of the RG scale ratio. A Cauchy argument then constructs the limiting kernel.

The scale-recursion and limit arguments can be explained from explicit scalar inequalities. The first genuinely RG-specific step is the private to_here helper in Lean/YM/RG08/Flat/World.lean, which applies the fully qualified theorem YM.RG08.Flat.toward in Lean/YM/RG08/Packets/FlatStep.lean. Its hypotheses include exact stationarity and quadratic-floor data, atlas bounds on input kernels, an exact tower identity, and neutrality/travel conditions. Its conclusion propagates a curvature representative and its folded cost through a Schur-complement step. I did not prove that mathematical lemma independently. It is the smallest next source declaration to assign for a detailed mathematical proof. A separate scalar proposition is supplied in manuscript/existence/flat-estimate.tex, labels prop:ex-lattice-flat-rate and rem:ex-lattice-flat-rate-scope. It proves only the recurrence-to-power-rate implication. The full flat-sector estimate remains open until the untranslated RG step is proved and its hypotheses are verified on the selected construction data.

Exact local chain

All declarations below are from the pinned source revision. Paths are relative to Lean/.

1. Entrance reshapes one schedule, then returns to the original RG data

YM.RG08.Flat.entrance_gate (YM/RG08/Flat/Entrance.lean) starts with a simple gauge group, kernel choices, a supply sheet, and equality of its kernel with the selected kernel. It obtains a retained linear plan, changes the sheet's stage clips by positive factors, and applies that plan. For an arbitrary compatible consumer level sequence and length floor, it then sets b := shadow a γ HC.fit and invokes YM.RG08.Flat.spacing_choice on b. From the returned separation schedule and a sufficient horizon, it invokes YM.RG08.Flat.supplies and transports the result back to the original horizon using YM.RG08.Flat.same_shadow.

The shadow is defined in YM.RG08.Flat.shadow (YM/RG08/Flat/Shadow.lean). It changes the Stage.clip factorization while preserving the schedule, RG levels, block lengths, couplings, and quadratic precision data. YM.RG08.Flat.same_shadow proves equality of the evaluated flat kernels, conditional on sufficient-horizon hypotheses on both schedules. It does not identify other higher-order or auxiliary data built from the two histories.

YM.RG08.Flat.spacing_choice (YM/RG08/Flat/Length.lean) supplies a Spacing record with width min(1,min(P.α j,P.α(j+1))), exponent θ=1/2, and age function 1+Lambda(a) (where Lambda=2+log(hatBeta)). Its numerical inputs are:

  • length floors at least the ceiling of the explicit entrance cost 2/curve + before(j,j)^2 + before(j,j-1)^2;

  • a delay bound P.Ω(J_i) ≤ log(hatBeta_i) for all i after the supplied threshold;

  • simplicity, used for the positive RG slope that bounds the RG age by the logarithmic coupling.

The source proof of this helper is elementary: the fit condition makes adjacent J_i indices either equal or consecutive; the chosen entrance cost pays both the contraction quote and width-crossing inequalities, while the delay and coupling slope give the polynomial age cap. It is a finite numerical estimate, not the cutoff comparison itself.

2. RowCost is an absolute, exponentially weighted summability bound

YM.Pert.Bulk.RowCost is defined in YM/Pert/Bulk/Atlas.lean. For a kernel K between cover-markets, a side length L, width σ, and cost p, its meaning is

\(\displaystyle \forall u, j, \qquad \sum_{y \in \text{Cover}} \text{ofReal} \hspace{-0.17em} \left( |K (u, (y, j)) | \hspace{0.17em} e^{\sigma \hspace{0.17em} \delta_L (u_{\text{pos}}, y)} \right) \leqslant \text{ofReal} (p) .\)

Here δ_L(x,y)=sep(x,L.val\,y)/L.val, where sep is the Laurent-size distance on the full cover. It is a rescaled cover distance, not finite-torus distance. Thus it controls absolute summability of every row, with an exponential spatial weight; it is stronger than pointwise kernel convergence. The associated Ticket bound has the same weighted absolute-sum form. Flat.output in YM/RG08.Flat.Curl contracts a packet with two fixed coboundary kernels; Flat.sigil_row turns a Folded packet bound into RowCost of this output.

3. One RG step propagates the curvature representative

YM.RG08.Flat.FlatRep in YM/RG08/Flat/World.lean contains four conditions on a packet representing the cutoff difference at a level: Folded weighted absolute summability over all packet coordinates, neutrality (the packet coefficient sum vanishes), town 1 invariance under the simultaneous unit shift, and a Sigil identity saying its exterior-derivative contraction agrees with the difference of the two quadratic precisions on every torus in the period family.

The private to_here helper transports a FlatRep from level i+1 to level i. Its conclusion is a pushed packet, with new folded cost multiplied by the source coefficient quote(a,P,i,u). Its proof invokes YM.RG08.Flat.toward with, among other data:

  • the two histories' exact Diagonal identities and Floor bounds;

  • order-two Atlas bounds and city conditions for the two curvature kernels;

  • exact matching of each history's one-step tower with the destination quadratic update;

  • neutrality, town 1, folded-cost, and travel properties of the previous packet; and

  • the previous level's Sigil identity.

The output of toward preserves neutrality and town 1, multiplies the folded cost by the exact coefficient neutralCoin(L, Faces, Faces, τ, q, p) = |Faces| q (|Faces|(2/τ)p)/L, and proves the next Sigil identity. The Sigil step uses a mixed Schur identity (YM.RG08.Flat.poised) and the exact Diagonal hypotheses. The proof of the folded bound uses the source-specific push_null estimate. These are substantive RG statements; they cannot be replaced by the numerical spacing estimate.

4. World.overlap solves a discrete weighted recurrence

The private helper existsRows in YM/RG08/Flat/World.lean starts from an endpoint packet constructed by Flat.at_end. That endpoint packet is the difference between the two saddle kernels at the top cutoff. Its cost is the top allowance. Working downward one stage at a time, it applies to_here. The explicit scalar cost majorant is

\(\displaystyle f_i = C_{\text{top}} \hspace{0.17em} W_i^{- 1}, \qquad W_i = \exp \hspace{-0.17em} (\theta (t_{\text{top}} - t_i)),\)

where t_i is cumulative logarithmic RG scale. Since W_i^{-1}=W_{i+1}^{-1}\exp(-\theta(t_{i+1}-t_i)), the propagated packet cost is bounded by the next-stage cost times drop_i = exp(-θ(t_{i+1}-t_i)), plus a top-only source. The theorem YM.RG08.Flat.World.overlap combines this recurrence with the paid-quote, block-width, and top-cost receipts in its Funding hypothesis. It applies YM.RG08.Rate.Stair.fade_solve to obtain constants A≥0, integer d, and c>0 such that the folded cost at level i is at most

\(\displaystyle A \hspace{0.17em} (2 + Z + |t_i - t_k |)^d \left( \frac{a_{\text{top}}}{a_i} \right)^c .\)

This is the geometric/decay part of the argument, but only after the one-step packet propagation and all funding receipts have been established.

5. The recurrence-to-rate lemma is separately proved

The source uses YM.RG08.Rate.Stair.fade_solve (YM/RG08/Rate/Ascent.lean). The new manuscript proposition is a finite-interval variant, proved in ordinary mathematical language; it is not a translation of the source theorem statement or a proof of the RG estimate that supplies the recurrence.

For the source application, set the finite interval endpoint to the top RG level, take s to be the reporting scale t_k, and let Z be the nonnegative schedule offset. The source age is exactly 2+Z+|t_i-t_k|. The scalar proposition uses R+Z+|t_i-t_k| with fixed R=max(2,2(d+1)/(theta-mu)), so its constants do not depend on the reporting scale or the schedule offset. The source's polynomial bound in its smaller age therefore implies the proposition's bound in the larger age, provided the forcing and endpoint coefficient are uniform across schedules. The Spacing age-cap and Funding.top_cap fields are the source receipts used to obtain that uniformity in World.overlap. Matching the source's exact recurrence, forcing and endpoint to the proposition remains a correspondence step; it does not discharge the per-step packet estimate.

The scalar proof tilts by exp(mu*(T-t_i)), with mu=min(c_0,theta/2). The resulting contraction is exp(-(theta-mu)*Delta_i). Polynomial age growth is absorbed by choosing an additive age offset at least 2(d+1)/(theta-mu), leaving a uniform geometric factor exp(-(theta-mu)*log(3)/2)<1. Backward induction sums the forcing with constant A/(1-q). Undoing the tilt yields the factor exp(-mu*(T-t_i))=(a_top/a_i)^mu. The full proof, including the finite terminal case and zero-cost case, is in the manuscript proposition.

6. Kernel limit and conversion to the stated estimate

YM.RG08.Flat.Mesh.meshed (YM/RG08/Flat/Mesh.lean) instantiates World.overlap on a road indexed by reporting scale, lower level, and finer cutoff. In addition to a sufficient horizon and Spacing, its hypothesis requires exact equality between each actual tower's transfer matrix and the designated destination update. The theorem turns each FlatRep into a Sigil for the difference of the two cutoffs' quadratic precisions, applies Flat.resolve to identify the output-kernel difference, and then applies Flat.sigil_row. This yields the weighted RowCost Cauchy bound for two cutoffs.

YM.RG08.Flat.Mesh.supplies invokes YM.RG08.Packets.solvent (YM/RG08/Packets/Limit.lean). That completeness lemma has explicit hypotheses: a fixed reporting level, nonnegative spatial expense, a cutoff difference bound tending to zero, nonnegative error bills, and an eventual absolute-cost bound for one cutoff. It proves pointwise convergence to a kernel, retains the error ticket relative to the limit, and passes the cutoff cost bound to the limit. The proof is the Cauchy criterion in the complete target followed by Fatou/finite-sum lower semicontinuity for the weighted absolute costs. This part is not an additional RG estimate.

Mesh.supplies gives a separate limit kernel for each reporting clock label k; uniqueness of pointwise limits proves they agree. It then multiplies the generic kernel cost by the fixed convolution constant from Flat.sigil_row. This yields the public Flat.Quorum fields: frame bound, cutoff convergence, and a cutoff-to-limit RowCost power estimate. The RowCost power estimate is not itself a theorem about the public Wilson measure or its smeared moments.

Smallest next mathematical task

The next task should prove or independently review YM.RG08.Flat.toward (Lean/YM/RG08/Packets/FlatStep.lean) as one coherent one-step lemma. Required work:

  1. Read and state the meanings of Diagonal, Floor, Atlas, city, Matches, neutral, town, Folded, travels, push_null, and town_hall used by its hypotheses and proof.

  2. Derive the propagated Sigil equality from the exact mixed Schur identity YM.RG08.Flat.poised and the inherited precision identities.

  3. Reproduce the push_null weighted coefficient estimate from the stated atlas, floor, and geometry bounds, checking the constants and exponent in neutralCoin and the Folded bound.

  4. Explain why the resulting neutral packet remains within the hypotheses of the next scale step.

Do not expand the assignment to all higher-order flat kernels or the Wilson observable bridge: the local source explicitly limits this chain to the order-zero unmasked quadratic precision. Those additional components are separate requirements for applying the estimate to the full continuum theorem.
