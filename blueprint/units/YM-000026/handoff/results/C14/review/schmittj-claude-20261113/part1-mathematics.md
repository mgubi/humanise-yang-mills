## Findings (ordered by severity)

### F1. `trace-free-rank.tex`, lines 292–318 — INACCURACY (manuscript; pre-existing)

Passage: "Let $\phi_1,\phi_2$ be the two linear functionals on $\mathcal V_5$ obtained by the point-divergence probes $(01,01)$ and $(12,12)$ at $e_0$. … Inserting … and summing $u=0,1,2,3$ gives $\sum_u\partial_uS_w(01,01;u0;e_0)=d_G\nu^2(-256w_1-512w_2+128w_3)$ … Consequently $\phi_1\circ D_0$ and $\phi_2\circ D_0$ are independent".

Reasoning: $\phi_i$ is a functional on the vector table (two-point correlation of a grade-five vector label at $e_0$ with the pair $(ij,ij)$ at $0$), whereas the displayed numbers are $x$-derivatives of the two-point correlation of the stress label. The step $\phi_i(D_0w)=\sum_u\partial_uS_w(ii';u0;e_0)$ needs that the divergence $D_0$ is realised by the derivative plan of the table and that correlations of derivative labels are derivatives of correlations. This is true for the free Gaussian field, and the source makes it a hypothesis (`V.Derivatives (Bel L)`, used through `YM.Further.PhysicalPeriodLimitF933.tested_div` in `free_closure`/`free_rank`), but the chapter never defines $D_0$ beyond "the free divergence" and never states this compatibility. The numbers themselves are correct (recomputed symbolically, see validation).

Requested change: one sentence defining $D_0$ through the derivative plan and stating that, for the free table, the correlation of $\sum_u\partial_u$ applied to a label equals $\sum_u\partial_u$ of its correlation; then $\phi_i\circ D_0$ is the displayed sum.

### F2. `trace-free-rank.tex`, lines 70–82 — INACCURACY (manuscript; pre-existing)

Passage: "Indeed, applying the corresponding curl to the right sides gives the identity on each curl coordinate, and the displayed formulas for the legs then give equality of the potential coefficients."

Reasoning: concluding equality of potentials from equality of curls requires injectivity of the curl on radial (Fock–Schwinger gauge) potentials of the given degree, which is not stated. The identities themselves are correct: with radial gauge $z\cdot A=0$ one has $A_a(z)=\int_0^1 t\,z_iF_{ia}(tz)\,dt$, so the degree-zero part is $\tfrac12 z_iF_{ia}$ and the degree-one part is $\tfrac13 z_kz_r\partial_rF_{ka}$; substituting into $\tfrac12\sum\ell^0_{ij}f_{ij}$ and $\tfrac12\sum\ell^1_{rkl}j_{rkl}$ (with $j_{rkl}=\partial_r(\partial_kA_l-\partial_lA_k)$) reproduces exactly these expressions. I also checked the cycle identity for $\ell^1$ and the radial condition $z_a(\ell^0c)_a=0$.

Requested change: replace the sentence by the direct computation (or cite the radial-gauge reconstruction formula), so that the spanning claim is proved rather than asserted.

### F3. `free-maxwell-ward-flux.tex`, lines 281–310 — INACCURACY (manuscript; pre-existing)

Passages: "The identification between invariant jet polynomials and the reference field table preserves their Wick moments, derivatives and rotations." "Faithfulness of the field table gives $(c_0-c_1)\partial_bv=0$ for every label $v$. The derivative plan is nonzero already in grade four, as detected by the nonzero divergence probes in Section~\ref{sec:ex-lattice-trace-free-rank}." "The reference packaging preserves the hierarchy, derivative plan, rotation and vacuum while selecting this tensor."

Reasoning: these sentences state structural facts of the reference construction (in the source: `YM.Pert.common_stress`, `YM.Maxwell.Free.shines`, `YM.Pert.same_translation` with its hypothesis `leaves D.deriv 4 ≠ 0`, and `S.field_ext` for faithfulness) without proof, precise statement, or a manuscript reference. The uniqueness argument itself is correct: subtracting two translation Ward identities with coefficients $c_0,c_1$ on the same derivative plan, faithfulness gives $(c_0-c_1)D=0$ on grade four, and $D\neq0$ there (for instance $w=(0,1,0,0)$ has nonzero divergence probes, $-256\,d_G\nu^2\neq0$). That last inference again uses the derivative compatibility of F1. "Faithfulness of the field table" is not defined in this chapter.

Requested change: name the inputs (faithfulness of the free table under separated correlations; the identification of invariant jet polynomials with the free table and its compatibility with Wick moments, derivatives and rotations) as precisely stated facts with a reference to where they are proved, or mark them as inputs. Keep the source names in the evidence file.

### F4. `free-maxwell-ward-flux.tex`, lines 205–224 (and 108–144) — INACCURACY / NIT (manuscript; pre-existing)

(a) The drive $v$ in \eqref{eq:maxwell-polynomial-ward} is not specified. The two-enclosed-pulse step (lines 183–203) uses homogeneity of $v$ about the enclosed point ($e=0$ translation, $e=1$ rotation about $x_0$), and $\delta_v$ is defined as the "angular probe for rotation about $x$". The identity is true for $v=e_b$ and $v=A(y-x_0)$ (and by linearity for any affine Killing field), but the statement should say so. (b) In lines 108–144 the letter $c$ is both the colour index of the probe $(c,i,l)$ and the summation index in $T_{ab}$; harmless but confusing when checking \eqref{eq:maxwell-coordinate-charge}.

Requested change: add "where $v=e_b$ or $v(y)=A(y-x_0)$ with $A$ real skew" to the claim; rename the colour index.

### F5. `all-grade-free-detectors.tex`, lines 136–150, 190–199 and 303–309 — INACCURACY (manuscript; pre-existing)

Passage: "let $\operatorname{Leg}_x(P,Q)$ be the one-leg kernel: contract the second derivatives of the scalar Green kernel at separation $x$ with the dual probes of $P$ and $Q$" and "$B_x(\Sigma_xP,Q_k)=(-1)^k\langle\vartheta_x\cdot Q_k,\operatorname{Taylor}_k(\mathcal R_x(\Sigma_xP))(x)\rangle_F$ … The sign $(-1)^k$ is the sign from differentiating the second insertion."

Reasoning: the orientation of the separation is not stated. I computed the covariance directly: the pulse of a grade-$k'$ probe at the origin is $(-1)^{k'}\mathcal R(P)$ (derivatives in the probe point of the even kernel $C(y-x)$). If $\operatorname{Leg}_x$ placed the second probe at $+x$, the sign would be $(-1)^{k'}$ for each component of the first argument, and $\sum_{k'}(-1)^{k'}\operatorname{strength}(k')T^x_{j,k'}P$ would vanish to order $j$ at $-x$, not at $x$, so the triangular transform with $I(x)$ would fail. With the second probe at $-x$ (separation $x=$ first point minus second point, as in $z=x-y$ of the next subsection), using $\mathcal R(P)(-y)=(-1)^{k'}\mathcal R(P)(y)$ one gets exactly the stated $(-1)^k\langle\cdot,\operatorname{Taylor}_k\mathcal R(\cdot)(x)\rangle$, in agreement with the source's `YM.Extra.FD.replyTest`. So the text is correct under the convention it uses implicitly. Similarly, line 303 says "Choose a real orthogonal basis … for the tower Gram form" without saying that this is $B_z$ with $z=x-y$, which is what the contraction weights in \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-script-recurrence} require. Positivity holds for every $x\neq0$, so the theorem is unaffected.

Requested change: state "$\operatorname{Leg}_x(P,Q)$ is the covariance of the probe $P$ at a point $y_0$ with the probe $Q$ at $y_0-x$", and "the tower Gram form at $x-y$" in the Wick subsection.

### F6. `finite-rank-kernel-transport.tex`, lines 25–34 — INACCURACY (manuscript; pre-existing)

Passage: "When $U$ is invariant under the symmetries, put … $A_U=\tfrac12(B_U+JB_UJ)$. Each average still kills $U$ and differs from the identity by a map into $U$."

Reasoning: $JB_UJ$ kills $U$ and has $x-JB_UJx\in U$ only if $JU=U$. "The symmetries" was introduced as the representation $\rho$ of $\Gamma$; $J$ is a separate involution. In the application $U=\ker A_c=L_c\mathcal E$ with $L_c$ intertwining the real involutions and $\mathcal E$ real, so the condition holds. The remaining assertions (idempotence, kernel, commutation, the uniform bound $\|A_U\|\le\|B_U\|$ by isometry of $J$) are correct.

Requested change: "invariant under $\rho(\Gamma)$ and $J$".

The preceding chapter calls the stress $J^0_{ab}$ and writes moments as $\langle\cdots\rangle_0$. Requested change: write $J^0_{ab}$ and $\langle\cdot\rangle_0$, or define $\mathsf T=J^0$ and $\mathcal M$ here.

### F8. `all-grade-free-detectors.tex`, lines 23–24 and 394 — NIT (manuscript; pre-existing)

$I$ denotes both the inversion $I(x)=r_xx$ and the identity matrix in $\vartheta_x=I-2r_x\,x\otimes x$. $\operatorname{Use}_{x,y}$ (line 394) is not defined; from \eqref{eq:ex-lattice-scanner-reflection} it is the free two-point moment $\mathcal W_{x,y}$ restricted to grade $d$. Requested change: write $\mathrm{Id}$ for the identity and define $\operatorname{Use}_{x,y}=\mathcal W_{x,y}$.

### F9. `trace-free-rank.tex`, lines 274–334 — NIT (manuscript; pre-existing)

"Weyl line" for $w=(0,1,-\tfrac14,1)$ is the source's name (`YM.StressFlux.weyl`). Mathematically this is the trace-free Maxwell energy–momentum tensor $\sum_iF_{ai}F_{bi}-\tfrac14\delta_{ab}\sum_{i,j}F_{ij}F_{ij}$, i.e. exactly $J^0$ of `free-maxwell-ward-flux.tex` (I checked the coefficient match, which the later chapter relies on when it says the kernel "is the line through this tensor"). The name suggests the Weyl curvature tensor. Requested change: call it the Maxwell stress line, or add a sentence identifying it with $J^0$. Also, the third free probe $S_w(02,12;01;e_0)$ uses a different representative of the off-diagonal orbit from the raw probe $Q_{02}(01,21)$; harmless, since only injectivity of $w\mapsto$ probes is used.

### F10. `finite-rank-kernel-transport.tex`, lines 141–149 — NIT (manuscript; pre-existing)

The proof that $g_{k,a}|_M=0$ uses continuity of the limiting correlation on the generic configuration domain and its identity with the tested functionals $f_\theta$. This is an input of the same kind as the "smooth representative" listed at lines 242–246, but it is not listed there. Requested change: add it to the list of retained inputs.
