## Findings, ordered by severity

### M1. GAP (minor). Signed-permutation invariance of the level responses is asserted with a one-sentence sketch. `quadratic-lattice-symmetry.tex` lines 51–57; manuscript; pre-existing (not touched by PR #81).

Quoted passage: "Here $D_c$ are the positive direction denominators of the selected finite tower. Their defining alias sums satisfy $D_{s_{\mathrm{perm}}(c)}(sq)=D_c(q)$: a signed permutation bijects the alias indices, preserves their scalar product weights, and permutes the single distinguished axis. The identity also follows term by term through the finite tower recursion."

Verified reasoning: the whole chapter rests on $\rho(sq)=\rho(q)$ and $\widehat P_{pp}(q)=\widehat P_{p'p'}(sq)$, which in turn rest on this invariance. The objects $D_c$ are the level responses $\mathscr D_{v,c}$ of `free-kernel-estimates.tex` lines 341–347, $\mathscr D_{v,a}(p)=\operatorname{feed}_{v,a}(\operatorname{divisor}_1^{-1})(p)+k^{-1}\operatorname{noise}_{v,a}(p)$, with the feed a normalised alias sum of the two-sided drive $D_{v,a}(q)D_{v,a}(-q)$. The invariance needs three separate facts: (a) the reciprocal divisor is invariant under signed permutations of momenta; (b) the two-sided drive satisfies $D_{v,s(a)}(sq)D_{v,s(a)}(-sq)=D_{v,a}(q)D_{v,a}(-q)$; (c) $q\mapsto sq$ permutes the alias set $\{q_r^{(M)}(p)\}$ modulo $2\pi\mathbb Z^4$ (for even $M$ the centred representative set is not sign-symmetric, so periodicity of the summand is used), followed by induction over the nozzle list for the noise. The source proves exactly this chain (`YM.Next.Free.Train.D_signed` in `Lean/YM/Next/FREE_KERNELS/Feed.lean`, via `feed_signed` and `chipSq_signed`). The manuscript names none of (a)–(c); "preserves their scalar product weights" and "the single distinguished axis" are not defined anywhere in the chapter. The fact is elementary and true, so this is a gap in exposition, not a false statement.

Requested change: replace the sentence by a short lemma: state (a) and (b) with a pointer to the definitions of divisor and drive in `free-kernel-estimates.tex`/`free-kernel-components.tex`, prove (c) by the alias reindexing (as done there for the mixed-radix bijection), and conclude by induction on the finite nozzle history. Also call the objects by their name in the producing chapter (level responses $\mathscr D_{v,c}$), since in `free-kernel-estimates.tex` the plain symbol $D_{v,a}$ denotes the drive (see M2).

### M2. INACCURACY. Names and section pointers in the symmetry chapter do not match the producing chapters. `quadratic-lattice-symmetry.tex` lines 40–42, 51, 95–97; manuscript; pre-existing.

Quoted passages: "Assume the blocking penalty is positive and the kernel level is at least four ... Let $P$ be the unamended curvature row from Section~\ref{sec:positive-row-producer}"; "Here $D_c$ are the positive direction denominators"; "The positive amendment of Section~\ref{sec:curvature-horizon-producer} vanishes on exact curvatures: if $M$ is the amended metric, then $d^*M_nd=d^*P_nd$."

Verified reasoning: (i) the row called $P$ here is the pulse $H$ of `positive-row-producer.tex` lines 124–133 (in the source, `YM.Next.Free.pink k v`); (ii) the amendment and the identity $Gd_1=Hd_1$ that gives $d^*M_nd=d^*P_nd$ are defined and proved in `positive-row-producer.tex` (`eq:positive-amendment`, line 153), not in `sec:curvature-horizon-producer`, which only uses them; (iii) "blocking penalty" here is the "positive kernel parameter $k$" of `positive-row-producer.tex` line 125; (iv) $D_c$ is the level response, while $D_{v,a}$ is the drive in `free-kernel-estimates.tex`; the level responses are positive only for $q\neq0$ (they blow up like a reciprocal divisor at $q=0$), which is why the formula is restricted to $q\neq0$. The mathematics is consistent once these identifications are made (I checked them against the source, see `part1-lean.md`), but a reader cannot make them from the text.

Requested change: write $H$ (or say $P=H$), cite `eq:positive-amendment` and the identity $Gd_1=Hd_1$ of `sec:positive-row-producer`, use one name for $k$, and write $\mathscr D_{v,c}$ for the level responses.

### M3. INACCURACY. The Hermitian property of the symbol is presupposed, not stated. `positive-row-producer.tex` lines 38–43; manuscript; pre-existing.

Quoted passage: "Suppose in addition that for every real momentum $p$, $vI\le\widehat A(p)\le MI$, $0<v\le M$, as Hermitian forms."

Verified reasoning: the diagonalisation at line 56 ("Diagonalizing the Hermitian matrix at $q$"), the eigenvalue statement at line 77 and the claim $F_n^{\mathsf T}=F_n$ in `eq:positive-row-periods` all need $\widehat A(p)$ Hermitian for real $p$, equivalently (with real coefficients) $A_{ij}(-z)=A_{ji}(z)$. A two-sided bound on the Hermitian part alone would not suffice. The source states it explicitly (`YM.Craft.Funds` requires `IsHermitian (B.read p)`). With the Hermitian hypothesis, every step of the subsection is correct: I rechecked the derivative bound $|z|_1e^{\sigma|z|_1}\le(\eta-\sigma)^{-1}e^{\eta|z|_1}$, the operator-norm Lipschitz constant $rC/(\eta-\sigma)$ for the max-norm on momenta, $\|M^{-1}\widehat A(p)-I\|\le 1-v/M+v/(2M)$, $\widehat F^2=\widehat A$ and $\widehat B\widehat A=I$ by the binomial and Neumann series, the contour shift giving $|F_{ij}(z)|\le Ke^{-\tau_1|z|_1}$ with the $(2\pi)^{-4}$ normalisation, reality of the coefficients from $\widehat A(-p)=\overline{\widehat A(p)}$, and the transfer to every finite torus by the discrete Fourier transform, including zero frequency and side one.

Requested change: add "Assume $A_{ij}(-z)=A_{ji}(z)$, so that $\widehat A(p)$ is Hermitian for real $p$" before `eq:positive-symbol-band`, and note that $A=B+lX^{\mathsf T}X$ and $R$ in the second subsection satisfy it because $B$ is real with real diagonal symbol.

### M4. INACCURACY (minor). The new lemma `lem:ex-lattice-flat-same-output` uses undefined terms. `flat-step.tex` lines 448–460; manuscript; introduced by PR #81.

Quoted passage: "Fix a level schedule, a cutoff $N$, and two admissible quadratic horizons with the same blocking geometry, blocking mean and penalty at each step. Suppose their terminal one-form precisions at level $N$ agree on every scheduled torus, and their adjacent steps obey the Schur hypotheses above."

Verified reasoning: the proof is correct. Backward induction with `eq:ex-lattice-flat-step-schur` gives $Q_{Y'}-Q_{H'}=N_A^{\mathsf T}(Q_Y-Q_H)M_A=0$ whatever the two transports are; for the cover assertion, with $y_0$ fixed and $n>2|y_0|_\infty$ one has $\ell_n(\pi_ny_0)=y_0$, so the periodisation equals $K(x,y_0)+\sum_{r\ne0}K(x,y_0+nr)$ and the tail is bounded by $\sum_{|y-y_0|_\infty\ge n}|K(x,y)|\to0$ by row summability at the fixed $x$; no uniformity in $x$ is needed. However "admissible" is not defined in the chapter, and "the Schur hypotheses above" must be unpacked: for each step and each scheduled torus, the four identities of lines 330–331 with a common blocking map $B$ and penalty $\lambda$, and symmetry of the precisions of one of the two horizons (only $Q_Y,Q_{Y'}$ are transposed in the derivation; the source `YM.RG08.Flat.poised` also assumes only those two symmetric). "Blocking mean" is ambiguous: in the source it is the blocking map (`YM.Precision.mean S`), while the transports $M_A,N_A$, which may differ, are called means at line 321.

Requested change: delete "admissible", list the hypotheses explicitly as above, and say "the same blocking map $B$" instead of "blocking mean".

### M5. NIT. Positivity of the selected couplings: name its source. `lattice.tex` lines 204–208; manuscript; introduced by PR #81.

Quoted passage: "In the selected construction, every $P_p$ is divisible by four and every $\beta_{N,p}$ is positive."

Verified reasoning: correct. Divisibility is a field of the schedule (`YM.Schedule.period_four`); positivity holds under the construction's selection hypothesis, since `YM.RG.History.Conv.band` gives $\beta_{N,p}\ge\hat\beta_N/2$ and `YM.RG.Calendar.Fits.delayed` gives $\hat\beta_N>1$. A reader of the monograph cannot locate where the selected construction imposes positivity.

Requested change: add a pointer to the section where the coupling band $\beta_{N,p}\in[\hat\beta_N/2,2\hat\beta_N]$ with $\hat\beta>1$ is introduced.

## What was verified as correct (summary of computations redone)

`lattice.tex`. Action and normalisation: each summand $1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_{ij}/r_G\in[0,2]$, pairs $i<j$ counted once, no factor $1/2$; partition function positive and finite for every real $\beta$; gauge covariance $(g\cdot U)_{ij}(x)=g(x)U_{ij}(x)g(x)^{-1}$ by cancellation at the three intermediate vertices; invariance of product Haar measure. Reflection positivity (new in PR #81): I rederived the temporal plaquette holonomy $X_eu_{v+e_j}Y_e^{-1}u_v^{-1}$ from $U_{0j}(x)$ (equality of real traces after inversion), the identity $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(X_eu_{v+e_j}Y_e^{-1}u_v^{-1})=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(X_e(u\cdot Y)_e^*)$ for unitary matrices, positive semidefiniteness of $\exp(c\langle X,Y\rangle)$ for $c=\beta/r_G\ge0$ via $\langle X,Y\rangle^m=\langle X^{\otimes m},Y^{\otimes m}\rangle$, the double gauge average $H(X,Y)=\iint H_0(v\cdot X,u\cdot Y)$ by invariance of $H_0$ under simultaneous gauge transformations and Haar invariance, hence the feature expansion with $M_m(X)$, and the link-cut bookkeeping: positive half $\{0,\dots,h-1\}$, crossing temporal links at $-1\to0$ and $h-1\to h$, boundary slices $0$ and $h-1$ reflected to $-1$ and $h$, symmetric kernels at both cuts, and the case $h=1$ where the two boundary slices coincide. The site-cut factorisation (fixed planes $t=0,h$ invariant under $t\mapsto-t$, reflected pairs of the remaining links, conjugate half integrals because the action is real) is correct, including the empty strict sector at $h=1$. The hypothesis $\beta\ge0$ is used only in the link cut. Gauge invariance of $F$ is not used (it is a hypothesis of the source's `Interior`, so keeping it is harmless). I confirmed the statement numerically on exhaustive small models (`part1-validation.md`): the Gram matrices $\mathbb E[\chi_a\,\overline{\chi_b\circ\Theta}]$ of all characters of the strict sector are positive semidefinite to machine precision for $\mathbb Z_2$ (2D, time periods 2 and 4, both cuts, $\beta\in\{0,0.7,2\}$) and $\mathbb Z_3$ (link cut, time period 2, $\beta=1$), while for $\mathbb Z_3$ with $\beta=-1,-3$ the link cut fails (minimum eigenvalue $-0.083$, $-0.217$), so the check discriminates.
