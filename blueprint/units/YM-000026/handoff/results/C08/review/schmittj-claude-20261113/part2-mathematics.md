## Findings (ordered by severity)

### M1. GAP — `high-degree-cluster-prices.tex` lines 28 and 249–270: the root-price exponent condition below −3/8 is not stated (pre-existing, not touched by PR #93)

Quoted: line 28 "Fix $-1/2<t<0$"; lines 269–270 "The first condition follows in exactly the same way from the strictly smaller than $-3/8$ exponent of $r_0$."

Reasoning. The first expansion condition \eqref{eq:degree-first-smallness} is $4b_BDr_0/\delta_0\le1/2$ with $D=\beta_+^{3/8}$ and $b_B$ of upper exponent zero, so it holds eventually if and only if (up to the usual $\epsilon$) the upper exponent of $r_0$ is below $-3/8$. The chapter defines $r_0$ only as "the original elementary root price" (line 172) and fixes the target exponent $t$ only in $(-1/2,0)$. Nothing in the chapter proves that $r_0$ has exponent below $-3/8$; with $t\in[-3/8,0)$ and $r_0$ of exponent $t$ (which is what the source has: `Target.rate_root` gives the root price rate `Q.rate`, the same $t$), the condition fails. In the source the needed inequality is obtained from two hypotheses that the chapter does not state: `Target.Expense.reach : Q.rate < wanting` and the hypothesis `he : T'.wanting < -(3/8)` of `Height.cruising` (see `part2-lean.md`, L1), so the source effectively requires $t<\varphi<-3/8$. The chapter's later requirement "$q<\varphi$" (line 251) concerns the component price $\omega$, not $r_0$, and the chapter never identifies the exponent of $r_0$ or of $\omega$ with $t$.

Requested change. State that the elementary root price $r_0$ has upper exponent $t$ (the target exponent fixed at line 28) and impose $t<\varphi<-3/8$ (equivalently restrict line 28 to $-1/2<t<-3/8$ and record that $\varphi$ is chosen in $(t,-3/8)$). Then the sentence at lines 269–270 follows from the same exponent count as the second condition.

### M2. GAP — `nonlinear-singleton-inheritance.tex` lines 121–125: producer of the order-six regularity and degree-four moment cancellation not identified (requirement made explicit by PR #93; use of $L^{-5}$ pre-existing)

Quoted: "Fix a purchased derivative order $n\ge6$ … In particular, the degree-four attenuation used below requires the insertion regularity and moment cancellation of Section~\ref{sec:principal-reference-attenuation} through that degree."

Reasoning. The PR #93 repair is correct as far as it goes: Section `sec:principal-reference-attenuation` needs $n\ge\max\{2,d+1\}$ and vanishing polynomial moments through degree $d$; with $d=4$ this gives $n\ge5$ and degree-four cancellation, and $L^{-d-1}=L^{-5}$ is exactly the $d_L$ of line 136. The released text ("$n\ge2$") did not justify $L^{-5}$, so the repair is a genuine correction of a hypothesis (I agree with `ledger.md` lines 406–412). However, the chapter now states the requirement without saying that the old regular activity $w$ satisfies it, or where this is proved. Neither the chapter, nor `outputs.md`, nor `edges.md` names a producer for "the inherited regular activity has vanishing screened polynomial moments through degree four on short supports and regularity through order six". In the source the cancellation is derived (`JointLoad.Heir.Seed.family`, `.four`, `.ghost`, per `ledger.md` lines 421–436) under additional hypotheses (simple gauge group, nonwrapping support, side-size bounds, graded directions in the `Mom.Maps` spaces), and the order six comes from the budget field `BudgetPoint.order_ge`. These are substantive premises of the $L^{-5}$ bound, which is what pays the $L^4$ count at line 187.

Requested change. Add one sentence identifying the section (and its hypotheses: short nonwrapping support, the side-size condition) that proves degree-four cancellation for the inherited regular activity, and that the purchased order is the budget order (at least six). Add the corresponding incoming edge to `edges.md`. If no manuscript section proves it, record it as an explicit outstanding input.

### M3. INACCURACY — `principal-comparison-jets.tex` lines 208–250: the value bound $\|u(0)\|\le v$ of the chart is required but only the derivative scale is justified (pre-existing)

Quoted: line 209 "Let $u$ be a $C^n$ chart … with $\|u(0)\|\le v$ and $\|D^ku(0)\|\le v^k$"; lines 246–249 "This follows by applying the weighted direction bound at $o$ to every read site … An ordered product of $k$ local variations contributes the $k$th power of that scale."

Reasoning. The Taylor lemma \eqref{eq:comparison-quadratic-jet-gain} needs both the value bound and the derivative bounds; I re-derived it and it is correct under both (Faà di Bruno, $r=1$ term $\le C(t+v)v$, $r\ge2$ terms $\le Cv^2$, value $\le Ctv+Cv^2/2$, at most $(n+1)\,\mathrm{Bell}(n)\le n!2^n$ terms). The justification at lines 246–249 covers only the derivatives of $u$ along the variation. The value $u(0)$ is the fixed-gauge logarithm of the base configuration on the read halo; that it is at most $v(x)$ is a statement about the gauge chart of a healthy field (it is presumably why $v$ contains the factor $(1+\mathrm{reach})^p$), and it is delegated without comment to Sections `sec:haar-chart` and `sec:mean-action-jets`.

Requested change. Say explicitly which estimate of `sec:haar-chart` gives $\|u(0)\|\le v(x)$ for the base configuration (the gauge-fixed logarithm of a healthy field on the halo), separately from the derivative scale.

### M4. INACCURACY — `principal-reference-attenuation.tex` lines 114–124: "$\varepsilon\theta_f\le s$" is asserted without the needed comparison of $\theta_f$ with $\theta_c$ (pre-existing)

Quoted: "$z\in\operatorname{supp}\Gamma\Longrightarrow g\|\mathrm{tape}(z)\|\le\varepsilon\theta_f\le s$." and "The chart bound gives $s=C\theta_c$ times a fixed polynomial in its read radius".

Reasoning. The chapter later uses $\theta_c\le2\theta_f$ (line 254) and "bounded ratio" (line 327). The inequality $\varepsilon\theta_f\le s$ needs the opposite direction, $\varepsilon\theta_f\le C\theta_c\cdot\mathrm{poly}$, i.e. a lower bound of $\theta_c$ by a multiple of $\theta_f$. That is plausible (the text calls the ratio bounded) but it is the direction not stated. It is needed so that $\|a(0)\|\le s$ on the cutoff support. What would decide it: the two-sided bound on $\theta_c/\theta_f$ from the threshold definitions, or the source's cutoff hypothesis (`Silences` in `Museum.exchange`), which I did not open.

Requested change. State the two-sided ratio bound (or the lower bound $\varepsilon\theta_f\le C\theta_c$) where $s$ is introduced.
