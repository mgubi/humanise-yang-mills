## Findings, ordered by severity

### F1. GAP (minor), manuscript, pre-existing: the base-point relative-density estimate is only sketched but is consumed quantitatively

- File and lines: `gaussian-differentiation.tex` lines 79–100 (subsection "Polynomial rank costs at the base point"), consumed by `gaussian-moment-prices.tex` lines 86–103 (`eq:moment-ratio-jets`).
- Quoted passage: "composition with the logarithm near one gives a derivative bound polynomial in $N$ ... the product rule applied to $(T_yz)^TC(y)^{-1}(T_yz)$ adds a factor at most $N$ times the inverse row price and a fixed-order polynomial in the derivative scales and $W$. ... The exponential partition formula ... proves a relative-density bound of amplitude one and a derivative scale polynomial in $N$ and these prices."
- Verified reasoning: the minor expansion `eq:gaussian-small-minors`, the vanishing of low-order derivatives of minors of size greater than the order, and the count $(j+1)(1+N)^j j!(1+v)^j$ are correct (re-derived). The remaining steps are correct in substance but are stated rather than proved, and `gaussian-moment-prices` then relies on the precise form $R_\cdot(z)\in\mathcal B_j(1,D(x)F_I(z)^k;y_0)$ with $k$ independent of support size and period. Three things are left implicit. (i) $W$ contains $F_I(z)$ (by `gaussian-moment-prices` line 78), so "polynomial in these prices" means polynomial in $F_I(z)$. Section 2 does not say this. (ii) The quadratic-form derivatives must be bounded by an $\ell^1$–$\ell^\infty$ pairing, $|x^TKy|\le\|x\|_1\|K\|_{\infty\to\infty}\|y\|_\infty$, with $\|z\|_1\le F_I(z)$ and a weighted row price for $D^bC^{-1}$. Without that pairing a Euclidean bound brings in $\|z\|_2$ and an operator norm that has not been priced. (iii) The relative log-density $L$ has $L(y_0)=0$ and $\|D^rL(y_0)\|\le c_r S^r$, where $c_r$ and $S$ are polynomial in $N$ and $F_I$. To convert $e^L$ to amplitude one, absorb the constants $c_r$ (Bell-polynomial count) into the scale. I checked that all three steps work and give some integer $k=k(j)$. So the claim is true, but the derivation that the consumer uses is not written out.
- Requested change: in `gaussian-differentiation` §2 (or in `gaussian-moment-prices` before `eq:moment-ratio-jets`), state the base-point lemma explicitly: inputs (weighted row prices of $C^{-1}(y_0)$ and of the jets of $C$; coordinate jets of $T_yz$ bounded by $W$); output $R_\cdot(z)\in\mathcal B_j(1,c_j(1+N)^{a_j}(1+P)^{b_j}(1+W)^{2};y_0)$ or similar. Write out the $\ell^1/\ell^\infty$ step and the absorption of the constants into the scale.

### F2. INACCURACY, manuscript, made load-bearing by PR #96: the group-only identity is stated without saying that $g$ is a group element

- File and lines: `compensated-variation.tex` lines 27–29.
- Quoted passage: "Also require $\exp\operatorname{Log}_*(\exp(u)g)=\exp(u)g$ for $\|u\|\le d$ and $\|g-1\|\le A$."
- Verified reasoning: before PR #96 the expression used the matrix logarithm, for which $\exp\log X=X$ holds for every matrix near $1$. After the PR, $\operatorname{Log}_*$ is colour-valued, so $\exp\operatorname{Log}_*(X)\in G$. The identity is therefore false for a non-group matrix $X=\exp(u)g$ with $g\notin G$. The Lean field `YM.ConnectedActivityE6.Blaze.Eider.Canary.right` quantifies over `g : G.El` only. The later uses (lines 72–76, and `eq:compensator-held-fine` with $g=M_i(V)$, a group element) apply the identity only to group elements, so no downstream step is affected. The author's account (findings C07-A5: "keeps the group-only inverse identity separate") says this distinction is intended, but line 28 does not state it.
- Requested change: write "for $\|u\|\le d$ and every group element $g\in G$ with $\|g-1\|\le A$" (Frobenius norm).

### F3. INACCURACY, manuscript, introduced by PR #96 (new text): the closed-subgroup fact is used without a reference

- File and lines: `compensated-variation.tex` lines 17–19.
- Quoted passage: "For a group element near the identity this agrees with the local group logarithm: write the element as $\exp(u)$ and use uniqueness in the normal-coordinate chart."
- Verified reasoning: the uniqueness argument is correct once the group element is $\exp(u)$ with $u\in\mathfrak g$ small: then $(u,0)$ is the local preimage, so $\operatorname{Log}_*(\exp u)=u$. That every group element close to $1$ in matrix norm has this form is not elementary for a closed, possibly disconnected subgroup. It is Cartan's closed-subgroup theorem. In the Lean source it is the substantive lemma `YM.Gauge.isolates` used in `YM.Gauge.plog_right` (`Lean/YM/Gauge/Chart.lean`). The manuscript already states and cites this fact precisely in `predictor-branch.tex` lines 40–56 (Hall, *Lie Groups, Lie Algebras, and Representations*, 2nd ed., Thm 3.42). Also, "the real matrix space" should be the space of complex $N\times N$ matrices regarded as a real vector space with $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(A^*B)$, as in `predictor-branch.tex` line 11. "Real matrix space" can be misread as real matrices.
- Requested change: cite Hall Thm 3.42 (or the normal-chart paragraph of Section~\ref{sec:predictor-branch}) for "every $g\in G$ near $1$ is $\exp u$ with small $u\in\mathfrak g$". Replace "real matrix space" with "the matrix space $M_N(\mathbb C)$ regarded as a real inner-product space".
