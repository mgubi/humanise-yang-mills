### M1. `rem:ex-limits-fullmesh`: the sentence on how the main construction proceeds is false (A-II-1, labelled ERROR). Status: CONFIRMED; manuscript; introduced by PR #69; no theorem affected.

Location: `limits.tex` lines 303–305: "The construction used for the main theorem does not take the first selection."

Verified reasoning. The monograph's own assembly chapter says the opposite: `assembly.tex` lines 67–88 (`ex-full-mesh`) combine a hierarchy "constructed by subsequence extraction" along a refined order \((u\circ v,p\circ q)\) with a separately proved full-mesh convergence, and identify the two row limits by uniqueness of limits; lines 119–128 apply this to the order \((\mathrm{id},p)\), whose refinement still has a mesh component \(v\). So the main construction does take a mesh selection for the selected hierarchy and its accompanying data, and in addition proves the full-mesh limit and identifies it with the selected row limits.

What is correct in the remark. I checked this against the proof text. The statements "no full volume limit is asserted", "a diagonal subsequence yields neither iterated limit" and "a mesh subsequence chosen separately for each period would not give the full-mesh assertion" are correct. The remark's description of the public order of limits agrees with the G01 statement ledger (line 147).

Requested change: replace the sentence by a statement of what is done, for instance: "The public statement is not limited to the mesh subsequence \(t\). At each fixed period the construction also proves convergence along the whole mesh sequence, by a different argument: …; Lemma `ex-full-mesh` then identifies the full-mesh row limits with the row limits of the extracted hierarchy." If the author prefers the alternative route (\(t=\mathrm{id}\) from the start), the remark must say that it is an alternative to the route of `assembly.tex` and of the source, and that the properties of the extracted hierarchy which are stated along its own order are not thereby transferred to the full-mesh order. ### M2. The counterfactual sentence about commutation is false; the displayed commutation is stated for one reflection and glossed as "every" (C-I-1, labelled ERROR). Status: CONFIRMED; manuscript; introduced by PR #69; proof of `ex-ordinary-reflection` unaffected.

Location: `reflection-transport.tex` lines 12–27, in particular lines 22–27: "Consequently, if \(\overline v=v\) then \(Jv=R_\rho v\), and for every word \(\theta w\) is the reversal of \(\rho\overline w\) … Without the commutation, a conjugation-fixed label could have a reflected conjugate different from its ordinary reflection, and the first step of the next proof would fail."

Verified reasoning. Requested change: state \(R_s\overline v=\overline{R_sv}\) for every signed coordinate transformation \(s\); say that the two facts used below follow from the definition of \(J\) alone; say that the commutation gives \(J^2=1\) (hence \(\theta^2=1\)) and independence of the order of the two operations; delete the counterfactual sentence. ### M3. `prop:ex-selected-interpolation` omits hypotheses used in its proof (B-I-1 and B-I-2 labelled GAP; B-II-4 labelled GAP). Status: CONFIRMED; manuscript; pre-existing (chapter unchanged by PR #69).

Location: `phase-quantitative-producer.tex` lines 330–339 (the "heard identities"), 504–544 (statement), 614–633 and 648–657 (proof).

Verified reasoning. The listed heard identities are: the played coefficient has \(x\)-coordinate \(I(v)\); the density is complex-linear in \(v\); the density is motion covariant; the charge bound. The proof defines \(Q(v)=\sum_{g,j}\chi_{g,j}(Iv)Z_{g,j}\) with \(Z_{g,j}.\mathrm{density}=g\,\mathcal T_m(V(v_j).\mathrm{density})\), \(v_j=I^{-1}e_j\). The reconstruction formula \(\sum\chi_{g,j}(z)g(e_j)=z\) gives \(V(v)\) only if \(gI^{-1}=I^{-1}g\), that is, if the readout \(I\) intertwines the motion action. This identity is not among the listed assumptions and does not follow from them. The same identity is needed for the asserted covariance of the new readout. For item 5 the exponential entry and forced profile of \(V(v)\) must be recovered from those of the \(V(v_j)\) by the same finite sum; this needs linearity in \(v\) of the forced field of \(V\) (linearity of the exponential entry then follows from linearity of the density, the dressing identity and the nonvanishing evaluation). The second half of B-I-2 (the "coupon-merger theorem" at lines 404–407 is invoked without a statement or reference) is confirmed as to the text; whether an applicable statement exists elsewhere in the manuscript was not checked.

Requested change: add to the enumerated assumptions (i) \(I(gv)=gI(v)\) for every motion, including the conjugation flag; (ii) complex linearity in \(v\) of the coefficient, regular and forced fields of \(V\) and a common support; (iii) \(I(u_N)=u_{m+1}\) for the physical unit; and state or cite the merger lemma with its summability hypotheses. ### M12. The field-quotient edge E4 (A-II-4 and D-II-3 labelled ERROR; B-I-6, C-I-4, D-I-2 labelled GAP). Status: facts CONFIRMED; this is a cross-package finding, not an error of the C03 chapters or of PR #69.
