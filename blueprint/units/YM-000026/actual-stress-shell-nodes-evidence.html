Mathematical distinctions retained

  • The root functional uses the cell-copy set of the centered chamber tiling for the opened reference level p0, as in YM.Cells.Tiling.rootTest; it is not an average over the entire fine torus. Each tiling copy includes its translation and the source's odd-reflection complex conjugation.

  • fuga(m+1) is the physical annulus scale in the fragment; it is not the fine mesh a_{m+1}.

  • The price c_m=exp(scale(m)/32) diverges because scale(m)=log(a_m^{-1})→∞; a_m→0.

  • The finite coefficient output is not only a size estimate: its Format, remainder, and Served density identity are all needed for the later bridge.

  • The two-channel product is handled by the finite mutation/served construction, not by an unproved submultiplicativity claim for the normalized root functional.

  • The source quantifier is: for every fixed collar anchor, the relevant volume-period event is late; common numeric estimates do not imply one common late event for the whole growing collar.

  • The Ribbons.bricks result supplies the fragment and correction at each fixed anchor. The shell manuscript then translates the continuation through Ribbons.sail, including the coefficient ledger, same-history served identity, and pointwise late-set quantifier. The selected menu and transfer assumptions are tied to the reviewed parent prose units listed above.

Rate and density details added in the repaired draft

The repaired manuscript gives the paid width explicitly. With (g_q) the maximal engineering degree at grade (q), its positive price is

\(\displaystyle w_q (v, j) = \frac{\hat{\beta}_j^{- (g_q + 4)}}{vC_j \prod_{\ell < j} C_{\ell + 1}} .\)

This is the ordinary-mathematical restatement of plumR and Mix.width: plumR multiplies (a_j^q) by the width at power (2,\mathrm{winter}(G,q)), hatCoupling is \(\hat{\beta}^{- 1 / 2}\), and Mix.envelope is the label price times the current column count and the product of preceding column counts. The grade-four branch has the further factor (r_m^{-h}).

The rate proof uses the exact channel factors, with both renal factors at m+1 (because the source evaluates the menu at i=m+1):

\(\displaystyle \rho_{m, -} : - dt + 4 (1 - t), \qquad \rho_{m, +} : - 4 t + h + 4 (1 - t),\)

so their product rate is (h+8-(d+12)t=-D+(d+12)(1-t)). The price error rate is (1/32-M-1/8=-5/64), and the marched price comparison has rate gap (1/32-1-M=-61/64), with (M=-1/64). These are ordinary rate calculations spelled out in TeX; the source lemmas pica, quilled, and seeing establish the per-factor rate/growth receipts under the selected-history hypotheses. The proof also records the subexponential column product from the cumulative column-rate receipt, not only the current column count.

The quota itself is indexed by birth level, quota C D m N p z, and the quota constant is birth-level dependent: the source applies quota_stop with ref(D)≤k≤m. It yields a finite (Q_m^{\mathrm{quota}}) at each fixed (m), uniform in volume and period, not a constant uniform over all birth levels. The marched estimate uses fly(D,m)≤1; the source inequality (s_i+1/2) peepers_i ≤ sqrt(betaHat_(i+1)) makes the exponential channel denominator rate zero. The strict lower bound M>−1/32 is sufficient for the correction-price decay, not asserted sharp. Fine response/graft occupancy is (r(m+1)); served transfer occupancy is (r(m)).

The displayed density identity unpacks Accent.Served and Accent.restore: regular coefficients indexed by supports and forced coefficients indexed by boxes remain as higher-degree terms. Accent.field sums full-support- disjoint box families whose flags cover the target. The manuscript does not replace this identity by a single leading coefficient.
